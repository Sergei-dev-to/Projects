# Positive radical rigidity and a degree-four barrier

**Consolidated theorem note**  
**Date:** 2026-07-22  
**Status:** revised once after one hostile proof review

## Abstract

Fix strictly positive Newtonian masses. Suppose a nonconstant,
collision-free, fixed-inertia arc of central configurations has an integral
algebraic curve \(C\) as the Zariski closure of its full labelled
squared-distance image. We prove that every squared-distance function is a
square in the function field of the normalization \(\widetilde C\).
Equivalently, the normalized distance map factors through coordinatewise
squaring. Consequently the pulled-back hyperplane bundle is a square and
\(\deg C\) is even. A positive partial-fraction argument excludes degree two,
so every nonconstant such curve has projective degree at least four.

The theorem is a necessary complexity condition on a possible
positive-mass counterexample to Smale's sixth problem. It is not a finiteness
theorem. Strict positivity is sharp: Roberts's signed five-body continuum
has a linear squared-distance image.

## 1. Set-up and statement

Fix \(n\ge2\), an ambient Euclidean dimension \(d\ge1\), and masses
\(m_1,\ldots,m_n>0\). Put

\[
M=\sum_i m_i,\qquad
E=\{\{i,j\}:1\le i<j\le n\},\qquad
w_{ij}=m_i m_j.
\]

For a collision-free labelled configuration
\(q=(q_1,\ldots,q_n)\in(\mathbb R^d)^n\) with center of mass zero, define

\[
s_{ij}=\lVert q_i-q_j\rVert^2,\qquad
U(q)=\sum_{i<j}\frac{w_{ij}}{\sqrt{s_{ij}}},
\]

and use the centered inertia convention

\[
I(q)=\sum_i m_i\lVert q_i\rVert^2
     =\frac1M\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}s_{ij}.                 \tag{1}
\]

Let \(J\) be a real open interval and let

\[
q:J\longrightarrow(\mathbb R^d)^n
\]

be a real-analytic arc of central configurations with these fixed masses,
center of mass zero, no collisions, and \(I(q(t))=I_0>0\). Assume its full
labelled distance vector is nonconstant.

Consider the inertia-normalized projective distance map

\[
\delta(t)=[1:(s_e(t))_{e\in E}]\in\mathbf P^E(\mathbb R). \tag{2}
\]

The first coordinate is redundant rather than artificial: by (1),

\[
1=\frac1{M I_0}\sum_e w_e s_e
\]

on the arc. Thus (2) is linearly equivalent, on the fixed-inertia
hyperplane, to the usual full projective squared-distance map.

Assume that the projective Zariski closure \(C\) of \(\delta(J)\) is a
geometrically integral curve. Equivalently, one may replace the closure by
the integral component containing a smaller physical subarc. Let

\[
f:\widetilde C\longrightarrow C\hookrightarrow\mathbf P^E
\]

be the normalization map followed by the embedding, and put
\(K=\mathbb R(\widetilde C)\). On the affine chart \(X_0\ne0\), write
\(s_e=X_e/X_0\in K^\times\).

### Theorem 1 (positive-radical square lift and low-degree barrier)

Under the hypotheses above:

1. Every \(s_e\) is a square in \(K\).
2. If

   \[
   \operatorname{Sq}([Y_0:(Y_e)])
   =[Y_0^2:(Y_e^2)]
   \]

   is coordinatewise squaring on \(\mathbf P^E\), there is a real morphism
   \(g:\widetilde C\to\mathbf P^E\) such that

   \[
   f=\operatorname{Sq}\circ g.
   \]

3. On the normalization,

   \[
   f^*\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^E}(1)
   \cong
   \bigl(g^*\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^E}(1)\bigr)^{\otimes2}.
   \]

   In particular, \(\deg C\) is even.
4. The nonconstant curve \(C\) cannot have degree two. Consequently
   \(\deg C\ge4\).

## 2. The potential is constant

### Lemma 2

The Newtonian potential \(U\) is constant on \(J\).

### Proof

On the center-of-mass subspace, a central configuration is a constrained
critical point of \(U\) on a fixed-inertia level. Thus, for a scalar
\(\lambda(t)\),

\[
dU_{q(t)}=\lambda(t)\,dI_{q(t)}.
\]

Differentiating along the arc gives

\[
\frac{d}{dt}U(q(t))
=dU_{q(t)}(q'(t))
=\lambda(t)\frac{d}{dt}I(q(t))
=0.
\]

Hence \(U(q(t))=U_0>0\) on the connected interval \(J\), and

\[
\sum_{e\in E} w_e\sqrt{s_e^{-1}}=U_0              \tag{3}
\]

using the positive physical square roots. \(\square\)

This constancy step is known; in particular, Albouy--Kaloshin formulate an
algebraic version in their Lemma 2. The new work begins with what positivity
forces in (3).

## 3. Positive radical splitting

### Lemma 3 (positive square-class separation)

Let \(K\) be the real function field of a geometrically integral real
algebraic curve with a real open arc. Let
\(a_1,\ldots,a_N\in K^\times\) be regular and strictly positive on that arc,
and let \(c_1,\ldots,c_N>0\). Suppose the positive analytic square-root
branches satisfy

\[
\sum_{j=1}^N c_j\sqrt{a_j}=b,\qquad b\in K,         \tag{4}
\]

on the arc. Then every \(a_j\) is a square in \(K\).

### Proof

Let \(V\) be the finite subgroup of \(K^\times/K^{\times2}\) generated by
the classes \([a_j]\). Choose an \(\mathbb F_2\)-basis
\([b_1],\ldots,[b_r]\) from these generators and put

\[
F=K(\sqrt{b_1},\ldots,\sqrt{b_r}).
\]

After shrinking the physical interval, take every \(\sqrt{b_k}\) to be its
positive analytic branch. Because the classes are independent, the
monomials

\[
B_\epsilon=\prod_{k=1}^r(\sqrt{b_k})^{\epsilon_k},
\qquad \epsilon\in\{0,1\}^r,
\]

form a \(K\)-basis of \(F\).

For each \(j\), there are \(h_j\in K^\times\) and
\(\epsilon(j)\in\{0,1\}^r\) such that

\[
\sqrt{a_j}=h_j B_{\epsilon(j)}.
\]

Choose the sign of \(h_j\) so that this is the positive physical branch.
After shrinking once more, every \(h_j\) is positive on the interval.

The lift of the physical subarc to the chosen sheet of the multiquadratic
cover gives an injective homomorphism from \(F\) to the field of analytic
germs at a generic real point. Thus (4), initially an analytic identity, is
an identity in \(F\).

For every nonzero \(\epsilon\), independence of the \(B_\epsilon\) says

\[
\sum_{j:\epsilon(j)=\epsilon} c_j h_j=0
\]

in \(K\). At a generic physical point, every summand is strictly positive,
which is impossible. Hence no nontrivial square class occurs, so each
\(a_j\) is a square in \(K\). \(\square\)

Apply Lemma 3 to (3) with

\[
a_e=s_e^{-1},\qquad c_e=w_e>0,\qquad b=U_0.
\]

It follows that \(s_e^{-1}\), and therefore \(s_e\), is a square in \(K\)
for every labelled edge \(e\). This proves Theorem 1(1).

## 4. The square lift and even degree

Choose \(\rho_e\in K\) with \(\rho_e^2=s_e\), and set \(\rho_0=1\). These
functions define a rational map

\[
g_0=[1:(\rho_e)]:\widetilde C\dashrightarrow\mathbf P^E.
\]

A rational map from a smooth complete curve to projective space extends
uniquely to a morphism; call the extension \(g\). At the generic point,

\[
\operatorname{Sq}(g)
=[1:(\rho_e^2)]
=[1:(s_e)]
=f.
\]

The two morphisms agree generically and therefore everywhere. Since

\[
\operatorname{Sq}^*\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^E}(1)
\cong\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^E}(2),
\]

we obtain

\[
f^*\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^E}(1)
\cong
\bigl(g^*\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^E}(1)\bigr)^{\otimes2}. \tag{5}
\]

The map \(f\) is the normalization and is generically one-to-one onto
\(C\). Therefore

\[
\deg C
=\deg f^*\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^E}(1)
=2\deg g^*\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^E}(1),
\]

which is even. This proves Theorem 1(2)--(3).

Equation (5) is deliberately a statement on \(\widetilde C\). No descent
of a square root of \(\mathcal O_C(1)\) through singularities of \(C\) is
asserted.

## 5. Degree two is impossible

Suppose for contradiction that \(\deg C=2\), and set

\[
\mathcal A=g^*\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^E}(1).
\]

By (5), \(\deg\mathcal A=1\). Let \(D=g(\widetilde C)\), and let \(k\) be
the generic degree of \(g:\widetilde C\to D\). Then

\[
1=\deg\mathcal A=k\deg D.
\]

Hence \(k=1\), \(D\) is a real projective line, and
\(\widetilde C\cong\mathbf P^1_{\mathbb R}\). In homogeneous coordinates
\([u:v]\), the morphism \(g\) is given by nonzero real binary linear forms
with no common zero:

\[
g=[L_0:(L_e)_{e\in E}].
\]

On a smaller physical interval, change the independent signs of the
\(L_e\), which does not change their coordinatewise squares, so that

\[
\rho_e=\frac{L_e}{L_0}>0.
\]

Constancy of the potential becomes the rational identity

\[
\sum_e w_e\frac{L_0}{L_e}=U_0.                    \tag{6}
\]

Let \(p_0\) be the projective zero of \(L_0\). If some \(L_e\) has a zero
\(p\ne p_0\), group all denominators with zero \(p\). Choose a real linear
form \(\ell_p\) vanishing at \(p\) and oriented so that
\(\ell_p/L_0>0\) on the physical interval. Each member of the group has the
form

\[
L_e=c_e\ell_p,\qquad c_e>0.
\]

The group's contribution to (6) is

\[
\left(\sum_{e:L_e(p)=0}\frac{w_e}{c_e}\right)
\frac{L_0}{\ell_p}.
\]

It has a genuine simple pole at \(p\), with a strictly positive
coefficient. Every other term is regular there, so the pole cannot cancel.
This contradicts the constant right-hand side of (6).

Thus every \(L_e\) vanishes at \(p_0\), so every \(L_e\) is proportional to
\(L_0\). The morphism \(g\), and therefore \(f\), is constant, contrary to
the hypotheses. Degree two is impossible. Odd degrees were already
excluded, so every nonconstant \(C\) has degree at least four. This proves
Theorem 1(4). \(\square\)

## 6. Consequence for Smale's sixth problem

The theorem does not require one to assume in advance that a hypothetical
counterexample is a continuum.

After fixing center of mass and inertia and introducing positive distance
and reciprocal-distance variables, the real collision-free
central-configuration equations are semialgebraic. Their full labelled
distance image is semialgebraic by projection. A zero-dimensional
semialgebraic set is finite. Therefore an infinite set of inequivalent
normalized central configurations has a positive-dimensional distance
image and contains a nonconstant one-dimensional semialgebraic, hence Nash,
subarc. The Zariski closure of such an arc is an algebraic curve; after
passing to the component containing a physical subarc, Theorem 1 applies.

Full labelled distances determine a configuration up to Euclidean
congruence, and passing from \(O(d)\) to \(SO(d)\) introduces at most a
finite ambiguity. Thus infinitely many similarity classes cannot collapse
to finitely many full distance vectors.

### Corollary 4

If Smale's positive-mass finiteness conjecture fails, then the offending
fixed-mass fiber contains an algebraic full squared-distance curve \(C\)
such that:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&s_e\in\mathbb R(\widetilde C)^{\times2}
&&\text{for every labelled edge }e,\\
&f=\operatorname{Sq}\circ g
&&\text{on the normalization},\\
&\deg C\ge4.
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular, neither a merely countably infinite exceptional set nor a
non-algebraic-looking parametrization evades the barrier: semialgebraicity
forces an algebraic curve inside any infinite fiber.

## 7. Positivity is sharp: the Roberts line

Roberts's five-body example has four unit masses on the axes and central
mass \(-1/4\). With \(a^2+b^2=1\), the fixed-inertia potential is

\[
U=4+2\left(M_c+\frac14\right)
\left(\frac1a+\frac1b\right).
\]

At \(M_c=-1/4\), the shape parameter is free and \(U=4\). If \(u=a^2\),
the ten labelled squared distances are

\[
4u,\quad4(1-u),\quad
1,1,1,1,\quad
u,u,\quad
1-u,1-u.
\]

Their projective image is a line. The coefficient of each varying radical
class is

\[
\frac12+2M_c=0.
\]

This is exactly the cancellation that positive weights forbid in Lemma 3.
Roberts therefore supplies both a sharp sign check and a degree-one
counterexample when positivity is removed.

## 8. Literature position and scope

The finite source audit is recorded in PRIOR_ART_MATRIX.md. The closest
known ingredients located there are:

- Albouy--Chenciner's mutual-distance algebraic framework;
- Albouy--Kaloshin's constancy of \(U\) on algebraic continua;
- Roberts and Hachmeister et al. for signed continua;
- Gasull--Lazaro--Torregrosa for case-specific rationalization of radical
  equations.

No audited source states the positive square-class splitting, the induced
coordinatewise square lift of the normalization, or the resulting
degree-four barrier. The defensible wording is therefore "apparently new in
the audited corpus," not a claim of priority.

The limitations are explicit:

- masses are fixed and strictly positive;
- collision freedom and fixed inertia are essential;
- every labelled distance is used, not a coordinate projection;
- the direct theorem concerns an integral algebraic distance curve;
- the line-bundle square lives on the normalization and need not descend to
  a singular image curve;
- the degree is the degree of the full inertia-normalized squared-distance
  image, not the degree of a chosen parametrization;
- the result is a necessary condition and proves no new case of finiteness
  by itself.
