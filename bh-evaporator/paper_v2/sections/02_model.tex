\section{Model Overview}

The evaporator consists of a finite core, a sequence of outgoing radiation bins, and a collision Hamiltonian. The total Hilbert space after $n$ collisions is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{H}_{\rm tot}^{(n)}
  =
  \Hcore\otimes
  \bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{H}_{{\rm rad},j}.
\end{equation}
The core energy is coarse grained into shells. The radiation bins are fresh degrees of freedom, initially in a vacuum label $|0\rangle$, and after one collision they are never coupled again.

\subsection{Core}

The core Hilbert space is a direct sum of energy shells,
\begin{equation}
  \Hcore=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{L}\mathcal{H}_m,
  \qquad
  \dim \mathcal{H}_m=D_m.
\end{equation}
The shell dimensions define a coarse microcanonical entropy,
\begin{equation}
  S(E_m)=\log D_m.
\end{equation}
The central thermodynamic assumption is that $S(E)$ has a convex intruder over a finite interval:
\begin{equation}
  S''(E)>0.
\end{equation}
In that interval the microcanonical temperature and heat capacity are
\begin{equation}
  \beta(E)=\frac{\partial S}{\partial E},\qquad
  T(E)=\beta(E)^{-1},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  C_\mu(E)
  =
  \frac{\partial E}{\partial T}
  =
  -\frac{\beta(E)^2}{S''(E)}<0.
\end{equation}
Thus the core becomes hotter as it loses energy.

In the engineered Hamiltonian model used below,
\begin{equation}
  H_{\rm core}
  =
  \bigoplus_m E_m I_{D_m}.
\end{equation}
This is deliberately minimal. It specifies a finite density of states and an energy ladder, but it does not claim that the core is a natural local many-body Hamiltonian. Finding such a natural core is a separate step.

\subsection{Radiation Channel}

Each collision introduces a fresh radiation bin
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{H}_{{\rm rad},j}
  =
  {\rm span}\{|0\rangle, |1\rangle,\ldots,|M\rangle\}.
\end{equation}
The label $|0\rangle$ means no emitted quantum. The nonzero labels represent independent outgoing modes. The bin Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}
  H_{\rm bin}=\sum_{a=1}^{M}\omega |a\rangle\langle a|.
\end{equation}

The multi-mode structure is not cosmetic. If there is only one emitted label, then a shell-lowering operator
\begin{equation}
  X_m:\mathcal{H}_m\rightarrow \mathcal{H}_{m+1}
\end{equation}
has rank at most $D_{m+1}$. Since evaporation shrinks the shell dimension, $D_{m+1}<D_m$, a one-channel Hamiltonian leaves large dark subspaces. Multiple outgoing labels increase the effective target dimension to $M D_{m+1}$ and are necessary for a robust evaporator.

\subsection{Collision Hamiltonian}

For each fresh bin we evolve the core and bin with
\begin{equation}
  H_{\rm coll}=H_{\rm core}\otimes I
  +I\otimes H_{\rm bin}
  +H_{\rm int}.
\end{equation}
The interaction is
\begin{equation}
  H_{\rm int}
  =
  \sum_{m=0}^{L-1}\sum_{a=1}^{M}
  g\, X_{m,a}\otimes |a\rangle\langle 0|
  +{\rm h.c.},
\end{equation}
where $X_{m,a}:\mathcal{H}_m\rightarrow \mathcal{H}_{m+1}$ are fixed random matrices for each seed. The collision unitary is
\begin{equation}
  U=\exp(-i H_{\rm coll}\Delta t).
\end{equation}
After the collision, the bin is retained as outgoing radiation and a new bin in $|0\rangle$ is introduced.

\subsection{Reduced Core Channel}

For numerical efficiency, the multi-mode Hamiltonian simulations use the exact reduced core channel generated by the collision unitary. If the incoming bin starts in $|0\rangle$, the Kraus operators are
\begin{equation}
  K_a=\langle a|U|0\rangle,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  \rho_{\rm core}\mapsto \sum_{a=0}^{M}K_a\rho_{\rm core}K_a^\dagger.
\end{equation}
This is equivalent to unitary evolution of the core plus all emitted bins, followed by tracing out the emitted radiation. Since the global state is pure, the radiation Renyi-2 entropy equals the core Renyi-2 entropy,
\begin{equation}
  S^{(2)}_{\rm rad}
  =
  -\log {\rm Tr}\,\rho_{\rm core}^2.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Evidence Layers}

We keep two forms of evidence separate:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Thermodynamic evidence.} The shell dimensions define the microcanonical entropy profile and the negative-heat-capacity window.
\item \textbf{Hamiltonian evidence.} A fixed multi-mode collision Hamiltonian produces a weak-coupling accelerating window for convex entropy profiles and a decelerating linear control.
\end{enumerate}

This separation is important. The present framework should not be read as claiming that a natural condensed-matter Hamiltonian has been found. It is an engineered finite Hamiltonian control model.
