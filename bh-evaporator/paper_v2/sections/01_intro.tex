\section{Introduction}

Black-hole evaporation is often discussed as a single phenomenon, but the standard story bundles together ingredients that are logically distinct. Some are intrinsically gravitational: the existence of a causal horizon, the Bekenstein-Hawking area law, semiclassical backreaction, quantum extremal surfaces, islands, and replica-wormhole saddle points. Others are not intrinsically gravitational: thermal-looking reduced states can arise from pure states by typicality or eigenstate thermalization, finite unitary systems generically produce Page-like entanglement curves, and negative heat capacity occurs in finite microcanonical systems with convex entropy regions.

This paper asks how far one can get without geometry. The goal is not to simulate a black hole literally, and not to replace the island formula. The goal is to build a control model: a finite, non-gravitational quantum system that reproduces selected black-hole-like thermodynamic and information-flow signatures while making explicit which assumptions are doing the work.

The model has two layers. The first is a thermodynamic engine. We assume or engineer a finite chaotic core whose microcanonical entropy $S(E)$ has a convex window,
\begin{equation}
  S''(E)>0,
\end{equation}
so that the microcanonical heat capacity is negative. In this window the core heats up as it loses energy. Coupled to a radiation channel with a suitable in-band spectral density, this produces an accelerating evaporation law analogous to the Schwarzschild scaling.

The second layer is a unitary information-flow model. In the minimal Hamiltonian version, the core is coupled repeatedly to fresh outgoing radiation bins. For each bin, a collision Hamiltonian lowers the core by one coarse energy shell while exciting one of several outgoing radiation labels. The full collision is unitary; the reduced core evolves by the Kraus operators obtained from that unitary. This gives a direct computation of the radiation Renyi entropy, rather than an imposed Page curve.

At the level of Hilbert-space bookkeeping, the emission map has the form
\begin{equation}
  \Hcore(E_m)\longrightarrow
  \Hcore(E_m)\otimes |0\rangle
  \oplus
  \Hcore(E_{m+1})\otimes {\rm span}\{|1\rangle,\ldots,|M\rangle\},
\end{equation}
where $|0\rangle$ denotes no emission and the nonzero labels are outgoing radiation modes. This layer keeps the global state pure. Early radiation can be thermal-looking after coarse graining, while late radiation can purify after the effective radiation Hilbert space becomes comparable to the remaining core. The resulting Page-like behavior is a finite-dimensional unitary effect, not a gravitational entropy prescription.

The distinction matters because Page curves alone are not diagnostic of gravity. Random states, chaotic circuits, tensor-network models, and open-system analogues already show that Page-like entropy dynamics can occur without spacetime. What is less often bundled into the same non-gravitational model is the thermodynamic side of black-hole evaporation: negative heat capacity, increasing temperature during energy loss, and accelerating power. Our contribution is to combine these thermodynamic ingredients with unitary information flow in a single control architecture.

The intended lesson is therefore limited but sharp. If this model reproduces a black-hole-like collection of signatures, then those signatures should not be treated as uniquely gravitational. What remains gravitational is the mechanism by which semiclassical gravity computes the fine-grained entropy: the area term, generalized entropy, extremization over quantum extremal surfaces, islands, and replica-wormhole saddles.

\subsection{Contributions}

The paper makes five contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item We define a geometry-free evaporator architecture based on a finite scrambling core with a convex microcanonical entropy window.
\item We derive the thermodynamic scaling dictionary connecting negative heat capacity, $T(E)\propto E^{-1}$, engineered emission spectra, $P(E)\propto E^{-2}$, and $E(t)\simeq(E_0^3-3\beta t)^{1/3}$.
\item We construct an engineered multi-mode collision Hamiltonian whose weak-coupling dynamics shows an accelerating emission window for convex entropy profiles, while a linear-entropy control decelerates.
\item We identify a concrete obstruction in one-channel Hamiltonian evaporators: shrinking shell dimensions create dark subspaces unless the outgoing channel has enough independent modes.
\item We position the model as a control case for black-hole information flow, clarifying what it does not contain: no metric, no causal horizon, no area law, no QES, no islands, and no replica wormholes.
\end{enumerate}
