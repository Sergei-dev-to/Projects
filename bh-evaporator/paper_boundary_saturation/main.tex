\begin{abstract}
How many independent internal sources must feed Hawking radiation in a
Hamiltonian model of Schwarzschild evaporation?  For a weakly coupled
finite quantum system, the operators that emit radiation define a
positive Gram matrix in each microcanonical energy window.  The
participation ratio of this matrix is a basis-independent effective
count of independent source directions.
For four-dimensional massless radiation, a Schwarzschild temperature
law, and smooth ordinary-strength matrix elements, the Schwarzschild
luminosity law requires this count to be of order the
Bekenstein--Hawking entropy.  By comparison, an ordinary local reservoir
with finite-range dynamics and surface-supported emission operators has
effective count bounded by the number of microscopic operators on its
contact surface.  Thus, in this class of models, the degrees of freedom
counted by the black-hole entropy appear in the operators that emit
Hawking quanta.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Toy models of unitary black-hole evaporation often replace the geometry
near the horizon by a finite quantum system coupled to radiation
\cite{HaydenPreskill2007,LeutheusserVanRaamsdonk2017,SlobodovLong,
SlobodovNote}.  Such a system has three separate ingredients.  Its
density of states supplies the black-hole entropy, its
emission operators produce Hawking-like radiation, and its internal
dynamics makes information recoverable from the late radiation.

Consider the emission ingredient.  Suppose a system with
microcanonical entropy $\Smicro(E)$ couples weakly to an exterior field
through operators $O_\mu$.  The label $\mu$ denotes microscopic source
operators; several such operators may feed the same resolved radiation
mode.  In a fixed energy window the matrix of shell-averaged overlaps
of these operators measures how many independent directions in operator
space couple to the exterior.  The standard participation ratio of this
Gram matrix is the effective rank.

In the weak-emission class with massless four-dimensional phase space
and a smooth matrix-element envelope of the kind used in the eigenstate
thermalization hypothesis, the Schwarzschild luminosity law fixes the
effective rank of the emission operator Gram matrix to be of order
\[
  \Smicro(E) \sim S_{\rm BH}(E) .
\]
An ordinary finite-range many-body system with volume-law entropy and
surface-supported coupling has effective rank at most proportional to
its contact surface.  In $d$ spatial dimensions this gives
\[
  \Neff \lesssim S^{(d-1)/d}.
\]

In this sense, the stretched-horizon picture has an algebraic content:
the degrees of freedom counted by the entropy are represented in the
emission operator algebra.  This is familiar in spirit from
complementarity, the membrane paradigm, and holography
\cite{SusskindThorlaciusUglum1993,ThornePriceMacdonald1986,
tHooft1993,Susskind1995,Bousso2002}; the formulation below is tailored
to Hamiltonian evaporation models, where one can inspect the coupling
operators directly.

The same distinction has a standard dynamical expression.  In a local
reservoir, information deposited in the bulk becomes recoverable from
exterior operations only after it has had time to reach the contact
surface, as required by the Lieb--Robinson bound.

\section{Emission Operator Gram Matrix}

Let $\mathcal H_E$ be the microcanonical shell of a finite system and
let $\Pi_E$ be the projector onto this shell.  A weak coupling to an
exterior field has the schematic form
\be
  H_{\rm int}
  =
  \sum_\mu O_\mu\otimes B_\mu ,
\ee
where $O_\mu$ acts on the system and $B_\mu$ creates or annihilates
exterior quanta.  For an emitted quantum of energy $\omega$, define the
shell-averaged Gram matrix
\be
  W_{\mu\nu}(E,\omega)
  =
  \frac{1}{D_E}
  \Tr\!\left[
    \Pi_{E-\omega} O_\mu \Pi_E O_\nu^\dagger
  \right],
\label{eq:gram}
\ee
where $D_E=\Tr\Pi_E$.  This is a positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix.
It is the Hilbert--Schmidt Gram matrix of the transition operators
$\Pi_{E-\omega}O_\mu\Pi_E$.

\begin{definition}[Effective rank]
\label{def:neff}
The effective number of participating emission operators is
\be
  \Neff(E,\omega)
  =
  \frac{(\Tr W)^2}{\Tr W^2}
  =
  \frac{\big(\sum_a\lambda_a\big)^2}{\sum_a\lambda_a^2},
\label{eq:neff}
\ee
where $\lambda_a$ are the eigenvalues of $W$.
\end{definition}

This is the usual participation ratio of the spectrum of $W$.  It is
unchanged by a unitary rebasing of the labels $\mu$ and by an overall
rescaling of the interaction.  If $N$ independent operators contribute
with comparable weight, then $\Neff\simeq N$.  If many microscopic
labels are coherently aligned, the matrix is approximately rank one and
$\Neff\simeq 1$.

The distinction between source operators and radiation modes is
important.  At Schwarzschild temperature the typical wavelength is of
order the black-hole radius, so the number of resolved angular modes per
thermal time is order one.  The effective rank in Eq.~\eqref{eq:neff}
counts independent directions in the system operator algebra, not
instantaneous radiation-mode multiplicity.

\section{Schwarzschild Scaling}

For weak emission, the inclusive power takes the golden-rule form
\be
  P(E)
  \propto
  \int_0^\infty d\omega\,
  \omega^{p+1}\,\Tr W(E,\omega),
\label{eq:power}
\ee
where $p=2$ for four-dimensional massless radiation.  The
microcanonical density of states supplies the thermal factor through
the ratio of final and initial shell dimensions.  If the matrix
elements obey a smooth eigenstate-thermalization-type envelope, the
thermal integral gives the
usual scaling
\be
  P(E) \propto \Neff(E)\,\bar\lambda(E)\,T(E)^{p+2},
\label{eq:power-scaling}
\ee
where
\be
  \bar\lambda(E,\omega)
  =
  \frac{\Tr W(E,\omega)^2}{\Tr W(E,\omega)}
\ee
is the weighted mean eigenvalue of the Gram matrix, evaluated in the
thermal window $\omega\sim T$.

\begin{lemma}[Schwarzschild luminosity and effective rank]
\label{lem:schwarzschild}
Assume four-dimensional massless phase space, $T(E)\propto E^{-1}$,
and an eigenstate-thermalization-type emission envelope in which
$\bar\lambda(E,\omega)$
has no hidden power-law dependence on $E$ across the thermal window.
Then the Schwarzschild luminosity law $P(E)\propto E^{-2}$ is equivalent
to
\be
  \Neff(E) \propto E^2 \propto S_{\rm BH}(E).
\ee
\end{lemma}

\emph{Proof.}
With $p=2$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:power-scaling} gives
$P(E)\propto \Neff(E)\bar\lambda(E)T(E)^4$.  Since
$T(E)\propto E^{-1}$ and $\bar\lambda$ is assumed to carry no additional
power of $E$, the condition $P(E)\propto E^{-2}$ is equivalent to
$\Neff(E)\propto E^2$.  For a Schwarzschild black hole
$S_{\rm BH}\propto E^2$.
\hfill$\square$

The lemma is a statement about the weak-emission class.  Different
models may put the required powers of $E$ into individual matrix
elements, coherent enhancement, phase space, or a different
factorization of the interaction.  Within Eq.~\eqref{eq:power}, the
luminosity fixes the effective rank of the source operator Gram matrix;
the number of labels chosen to write the interaction is basis-dependent.

\section{Local Reservoirs With Surface Coupling}

Consider an ordinary many-body reservoir on a $d$-dimensional lattice
$\Lambda$ with finite local Hilbert space and finite-range Hamiltonian.
Let its entropy be extensive, $S=s|\Lambda|$, in a thermal window.
Assume the exterior field couples through operators supported in a
surface shell $\partial\Lambda$ of fixed microscopic thickness.

\begin{lemma}[Surface-supported emission operators]
\label{lem:surface}
For fixed interaction range and local Hilbert-space dimension,
\be
  \Neff \le C\,|\partial\Lambda|
\ee
for a constant $C$ independent of the reservoir size.  Consequently,
for a regular $d$-dimensional family,
\be
  \Neff \lesssim S^{(d-1)/d}.
\ee
\end{lemma}

\emph{Proof.}
Each emission operator is supported on a bounded-size patch of the
surface shell.  The vector space spanned by all such few-body operators
has dimension at most $C|\partial\Lambda|$, with $C$ depending on the
range and local dimension.  The rank of the Gram matrix
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gram} is bounded by this dimension, and the participation
ratio never exceeds the rank.  For a regular lattice,
$|\partial\Lambda|\sim |\Lambda|^{(d-1)/d}$, while
$S\sim |\Lambda|$.
\hfill$\square$

Lemma~\ref{lem:schwarzschild} gives an entropy-sized effective rank for
Schwarzschild emission.  Lemma~\ref{lem:surface} gives a surface-sized
operator space for a local bulk reservoir with contact coupling.  A very
thin reservoir, or a system whose entropy is already localized on the
emitting surface, may have entropy-sized participation; this is the
stretched-horizon structure in Hamiltonian language.

\section{Dynamical Counterpart}

Let a local reservoir couple to an exterior apparatus through the same
surface shell, with uniformly bounded local interactions.  Deposit a
small quantum register in a bulk region at distance $L$ from that
surface, and allow the exterior apparatus arbitrary measurements,
ancillas, and feedback through the surface coupling.  Lieb--Robinson
bounds \cite{LiebRobinson1972,HastingsKoma2006,NachtergaeleSims2010}
imply that exterior observables have exponentially small commutator with
the deposited register until times of order $L/v$, where $v$ is the
Lieb--Robinson velocity.  Equivalently, the exterior record is nearly
independent of the deposited quantum information until the light cone
reaches the surface.

For a deposit at radius scale in a reservoir with extensive entropy,
$L$ scales as $S^{1/d}$ in thermal-cell units.  The corresponding
exterior recovery time is therefore power-law in $S$.  In the
Hayden--Preskill recovery problem, a small register thrown into an
already scrambled black hole becomes recoverable from the later
radiation after $O(k+\log S)$ emitted quanta, assuming the decoder holds
the earlier radiation and the usual decoupling hypotheses apply
\cite{HaydenPreskill2007,SlobodovLong}.  This comparison uses locality
on the reservoir side and scrambling on the black-hole side.  The
effective-rank calculation above concerns the emission algebra itself.

Recovery has two separable costs: the number of emitted quanta after
which the deposited information is present in the exterior record at
all, and the difficulty of extracting it from that record.  The
comparison above concerns only the first.  The second is an independent
axis: with the dynamics known, the Hayden--Preskill decoder can be made
efficient \cite{YoshidaKitaev2017}, while decoding generic Hawking
radiation is expected to be computationally infeasible
\cite{HarlowHayden2013}, with a conjectured geometric criterion for the
hard instances \cite{BrownGharibyanPeningtonSusskind2020}.  The two
exponents can therefore separate: a system may attain logarithmic
arrival latency while the extraction cost remains exponential.  The
comparison by arrival latency is insensitive to this distinction, which
is set by decoder resources rather than by the emission algebra.

\section{Discussion}

In a weak Hamiltonian emission model, the Schwarzschild luminosity law
fixes the effective rank of the emission operator Gram matrix.  With
ordinary-strength smooth matrix elements and four-dimensional phase
space, that rank is of order $S_{\rm BH}$.  A local reservoir with
surface-supported couplings has effective rank bounded by its surface
area in microscopic units.  The same surface restriction gives the
usual power-law locality bound on exterior recovery of bulk-deposited
quantum information.

The exterior certificate for an entropy-sized participation count is
necessarily temporal.  At Schwarzschild temperature the wavelength is
of order the horizon radius, so the radiation resolves an order-one
number of angular modes per coherence time
\cite{Page1976,BekensteinMayo2001,Pendry1983}: instantaneous emission
statistics have order-one rank however large $\Neff$ may be.  A
stationary horizon offers a second exterior window.  For Rindler
wedges, eternal black holes, and large anti--de Sitter black holes, the
exterior state is thermal in the exact KMS sense, and stationary
response and fluctuation spectra are tied to interior state structure
by the equilibrium dictionary
\cite{BisognanoWichmann1976,Israel1976,GibbonsHawkingAction1977,
HawkingPage1983}.  Schwarzschild evaporation sits on the other side of
that divide: at negative heat capacity the canonical ensemble does not
exist, and there is no stationary KMS state of the exterior field to
which long-time spectral data could be referred.  The exterior instead
receives a single non-stationary record delivering of order one nat per
coherence time over of order $S$ emissions.  Whatever entropy-sized
structure is exterior-visible is therefore visible only in the time
ordering of that record, which is why the exterior counterpart of the
participation statement is the recovery-latency comparison of the
preceding section rather than a flux moment.

The Bekenstein--Hawking density of states and the scrambling dynamics
enter as separate inputs.  Once those inputs are assumed, the entropy
counted by the black hole is also available to the emission operator
algebra.  In stretched-horizon language, the entropy is carried by
degrees of freedom that directly participate in absorption and emission.
In ordinary local-reservoir language, this is a special surface
structure beyond thermality alone.

There is a distinct coherent branch, however, in which the same coarse
flux is carried by a collectively enhanced source rather than by
entropy-sized source participation.  The black-hole $N$-portrait and
memory-burden models provide a useful comparison class
\cite{DvaliGomez2013,DvaliPanchenko2016,DvaliEisemannMichelZell2020,
DvaliValbuenaBermudezZantedeschi2024}.  In the critical condensate
language, equal coupling of an external mode to $K$ memory species is
equivalent to coupling to one collective mode with strength enhanced by
$\sqrt K$, while the remaining $K-1$ modes decouple
\cite{DvaliPanchenko2016}.  In the memory-burden prototype this is the
coherent-enhancement escape from the ordinary-envelope assumption: the
thermal-line Gram matrix may have participation $\Neff=O(1)$, with the
power carried by the large master occupation rather than by $O(S)$
comparable eigenvalues.  The same models also separate participation
from latency.  In the strict prototype the memory-sector occupation is
conserved and fixed-sector diary information has no Hamiltonian route
into the emitted master record; in the black-hole mapping the
memory-burden release channel is instead parametrically long.  Thus
state-count saturation, source-rank saturation, and Hayden--Preskill
latency are separate assumptions.  The rank conclusion above applies
to the weak-emission ordinary-envelope class, not to this collectively
enhanced single-source branch.
