\begin{abstract}
How many independent internal sources must feed Hawking radiation in a
Hamiltonian model of Schwarzschild evaporation?  For a weakly coupled
finite quantum system, the operators that emit radiation define a
positive Gram matrix in each microcanonical energy window.  The
participation ratio of this matrix is a basis-independent effective
count of independent source directions.
For four-dimensional massless radiation, a Schwarzschild temperature
law, and smooth ordinary-strength matrix elements, the Schwarzschild
luminosity law requires this count to be of order the
Bekenstein--Hawking entropy.  Resolved or multi-setting calibrated
emission/absorption measurements, supplemented by photon counting, give
a two-sided test of the main rank-one escapes:
macroscopic occupation drives the ratio above its thermal value, while
a drained thermal collective channel with relaxation bounded by the
temperature is reservoir-starved and drifts below it.  Under the stated
weak-emission, resolution, relaxation, and channel-separation
assumptions, the luminosity and paired response give a quantitative lower bound on source
participation.  By comparison, an ordinary local reservoir
with finite-range dynamics and surface-supported emission operators has
effective count bounded by the number of microscopic operators on its
contact surface.  Thus, in this class of models, the degrees of freedom
counted by the black-hole entropy appear in the operators that emit
Hawking quanta.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Toy models of unitary black-hole evaporation often replace the geometry
near the horizon by a finite quantum system coupled to radiation
\cite{HaydenPreskill2007,LeutheusserVanRaamsdonk2017,SlobodovLong,
SlobodovNote}.  Such a system has three separate ingredients.  Its
density of states supplies the black-hole entropy, its
emission operators produce Hawking-like radiation, and its internal
dynamics makes information recoverable from the late radiation.

Consider the emission ingredient.  Suppose a system with
microcanonical entropy $\Smicro(E)$ couples weakly to an exterior field
through operators $O_\mu$.  The label $\mu$ denotes microscopic source
operators; several such operators may feed the same resolved radiation
mode.  In a fixed energy window the matrix of shell-averaged overlaps
of these operators measures how many independent directions in operator
space couple to the exterior.  The standard participation ratio of this
Gram matrix is the effective rank.

In the weak-emission class with massless four-dimensional phase space
and a smooth matrix-element envelope of the kind used in the eigenstate
thermalization hypothesis, the Schwarzschild luminosity law fixes the
effective rank of the emission operator Gram matrix to be of order
\[
  \Smicro(E) \sim S_{\rm BH}(E) .
\]
An ordinary finite-range many-body system with volume-law entropy and
surface-supported coupling has effective rank at most proportional to
its contact surface.  In $d$ spatial dimensions this gives
\[
  \Neff \lesssim S^{(d-1)/d}.
\]

In this sense, the stretched-horizon picture has an algebraic content:
the degrees of freedom counted by the entropy are represented in the
emission operator algebra.  This is familiar in spirit from
complementarity, the membrane paradigm, and holography
\cite{SusskindThorlaciusUglum1993,ThornePriceMacdonald1986,
tHooft1993,Susskind1995,Bousso2002}; the formulation below is tailored
to Hamiltonian evaporation models, where one can inspect the coupling
operators directly.

The same distinction has a standard dynamical expression.  In a local
reservoir, information deposited in the bulk becomes recoverable from
exterior operations only after it has had time to reach the contact
surface, as required by the Lieb--Robinson bound.

\section{Emission Operator Gram Matrix}

Let $\mathcal H_E$ be the microcanonical shell of a finite system and
let $\Pi_E$ be the projector onto this shell.  A weak coupling to an
exterior field has the schematic form
\be
  H_{\rm int}
  =
  \sum_\mu O_\mu\otimes B_\mu ,
\ee
where $O_\mu$ acts on the system and $B_\mu$ creates or annihilates
exterior quanta.  For an emitted quantum of energy $\omega$, define the
shell-averaged Gram matrix
\be
  W_{\mu\nu}(E,\omega)
  =
  \frac{1}{D_E}
  \Tr\!\left[
    \Pi_{E-\omega} O_\mu \Pi_E O_\nu^\dagger
  \right],
\label{eq:gram}
\ee
where $D_E=\Tr\Pi_E$.  This is a positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix.
It is the Hilbert--Schmidt Gram matrix of the transition operators
$\Pi_{E-\omega}O_\mu\Pi_E$.

\begin{definition}[Effective rank]
\label{def:neff}
The effective number of participating emission operators is
\be
  \Neff(E,\omega)
  =
  \frac{(\Tr W)^2}{\Tr W^2}
  =
  \frac{\big(\sum_a\lambda_a\big)^2}{\sum_a\lambda_a^2},
\label{eq:neff}
\ee
where $\lambda_a$ are the eigenvalues of $W$.
\end{definition}

This is the usual participation ratio of the spectrum of $W$.  It is
unchanged by a unitary rebasing of the labels $\mu$ and by an overall
rescaling of the interaction.  If $N$ independent operators contribute
with comparable weight, then $\Neff\simeq N$.  If many microscopic
labels are coherently aligned, the matrix is approximately rank one and
$\Neff\simeq 1$.

The distinction between source operators and radiation modes is
important.  At Schwarzschild temperature the typical wavelength is of
order the black-hole radius, so the number of resolved angular modes per
thermal time is order one.  The effective rank in Eq.~\eqref{eq:neff}
counts independent directions in the system operator algebra, not
instantaneous radiation-mode multiplicity.

\section{Schwarzschild Scaling}

For weak emission, the inclusive power takes the golden-rule form
\be
  P(E)
  \propto
  \int_0^\infty d\omega\,
  \omega^{p+1}\,\Tr W(E,\omega),
\label{eq:power}
\ee
where $p=2$ for four-dimensional massless radiation.  The
microcanonical density of states supplies the thermal factor through
the ratio of final and initial shell dimensions.  If the matrix
elements obey a smooth eigenstate-thermalization-type envelope, the
thermal integral gives the
usual scaling
\be
  P(E) \propto \Neff(E)\,\bar\lambda(E)\,T(E)^{p+2},
\label{eq:power-scaling}
\ee
where
\be
  \bar\lambda(E,\omega)
  =
  \frac{\Tr W(E,\omega)^2}{\Tr W(E,\omega)}
\ee
is the weighted mean eigenvalue of the Gram matrix, evaluated in the
thermal window $\omega\sim T$.

\begin{lemma}[Schwarzschild luminosity and effective rank]
\label{lem:schwarzschild}
Assume four-dimensional massless phase space, $T(E)\propto E^{-1}$,
and an eigenstate-thermalization-type emission envelope in which
$\bar\lambda(E,\omega)$
has no hidden power-law dependence on $E$ across the thermal window.
Then the Schwarzschild luminosity law $P(E)\propto E^{-2}$ is equivalent
to
\be
  \Neff(E) \propto E^2 \propto S_{\rm BH}(E).
\ee
\end{lemma}

\emph{Proof.}
With $p=2$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:power-scaling} gives
$P(E)\propto \Neff(E)\bar\lambda(E)T(E)^4$.  Since
$T(E)\propto E^{-1}$ and $\bar\lambda$ is assumed to carry no additional
power of $E$, the condition $P(E)\propto E^{-2}$ is equivalent to
$\Neff(E)\propto E^2$.  For a Schwarzschild black hole
$S_{\rm BH}\propto E^2$.
\hfill$\square$

The lemma is a statement about the weak-emission class.  Different
models may put the required powers of $E$ into individual matrix
elements, coherent enhancement, phase space, or a different
factorization of the interaction.  Within Eq.~\eqref{eq:power}, the
luminosity fixes the effective rank of the source operator Gram matrix;
the number of labels chosen to write the interaction is basis-dependent.

\section{Local Reservoirs With Surface Coupling}

Consider an ordinary many-body reservoir on a $d$-dimensional lattice
$\Lambda$ with finite local Hilbert space and finite-range Hamiltonian.
Let its entropy be extensive, $S=s|\Lambda|$, in a thermal window.
Assume the exterior field couples through operators supported in a
surface shell $\partial\Lambda$ of fixed microscopic thickness.

\begin{lemma}[Surface-supported emission operators]
\label{lem:surface}
For fixed interaction range and local Hilbert-space dimension,
\be
  \Neff \le C\,|\partial\Lambda|
\ee
for a constant $C$ independent of the reservoir size.  Consequently,
for a regular $d$-dimensional family,
\be
  \Neff \lesssim S^{(d-1)/d}.
\ee
\end{lemma}

\emph{Proof.}
Each emission operator is supported on a bounded-size patch of the
surface shell.  The vector space spanned by all such few-body operators
has dimension at most $C|\partial\Lambda|$, with $C$ depending on the
range and local dimension.  The rank of the Gram matrix
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gram} is bounded by this dimension, and the participation
ratio never exceeds the rank.  For a regular lattice,
$|\partial\Lambda|\sim |\Lambda|^{(d-1)/d}$, while
$S\sim |\Lambda|$.
\hfill$\square$

Lemma~\ref{lem:schwarzschild} gives an entropy-sized effective rank for
Schwarzschild emission.  Lemma~\ref{lem:surface} gives a surface-sized
operator space for a local bulk reservoir with contact coupling.  A very
thin reservoir, or a system whose entropy is already localized on the
emitting surface, may have entropy-sized participation; this is the
stretched-horizon structure in Hamiltonian language.

\section{A Two-Sided Line-Response Certificate}
\label{sec:static-certificate}

The preceding rank conclusion has two apparent rank-one loopholes.  A
single source can be bright because its occupation is macroscopic, or a
thermal collective mode can be bright because its coupling is enhanced.
In either case the full-source ordinary-envelope assumption in
Lemma~\ref{lem:schwarzschild} fails, and the Gram matrix may have
$\Neff=O(1)$ even though the luminosity is Schwarzschild-sized.

A line-response identity separates the occupation-enhanced branch from
an equilibrium entropy-rank source.  Dynamics is then needed for the
thermal collective branch.  For a resolved line with
system operator $A_i(\omega)$, define the emission and absorption rates
through that channel by
\be
  \Gamma_{{\rm em},i}\propto
  \langle A_i^\dagger A_i\rangle,
  \qquad
  \Gamma_{{\rm abs},i}\propto
  \langle A_i A_i^\dagger\rangle .
\ee
If
\be
  K_i=
  \frac{\langle A_i^\dagger A_i\rangle}
       {\langle [A_i,A_i^\dagger]\rangle},
\ee
then
\be
  r_i\equiv
  \frac{\Gamma_{{\rm em},i}}{\Gamma_{{\rm abs},i}}
  =
  \frac{K_i}{K_i+1}.
\label{eq:line-asymmetry}
\ee
This identity is just the bosonic stimulated-emission algebra written
for an arbitrary channel operator.  In an equilibrium shell, the KMS
condition gives instead
\be
  \langle A_i A_i^\dagger\rangle
  =
  e^{\beta\omega}\langle A_i^\dagger A_i\rangle,
  \qquad
  r_i=e^{-\beta\omega},
\ee
or, more generally, the microcanonical ratio
$\rho(E-\omega)/\rho(E)$; for rotating or charged holes the usual
grand-canonical replacement
$e^{-\beta(\omega-m\Omega_H-q\Phi_H)}$ is used
\cite{HaagHugenholtzWinnink1967,BekensteinMeisels1977,
PanangadenWald1977}.

Large occupation enhancement has $K_i\gg1$ and therefore
$r_i\to1$.  A Schwarzschild thermal line at $\omega\sim T$ instead has
an order-one Boltzmann asymmetry.  Thus a line whose measured
emission/absorption ratio is within $\eta$ of its calibrated
microcanonical value can carry only a small flux fraction through
occupation-enhanced channels.  Writing
\be
  n_\beta = \frac{R}{1-R},
  \qquad
  R=\frac{\rho(E-\omega)}{\rho(E)}
\ee
for the reference thermal occupation, this fraction obeys
\be
  f \lesssim \eta n_\beta ,
\label{eq:f-bound}
\ee
up to the convention used for the experimental or model error $\eta$.

Before adding an envelope assumption, there is an exact observable
floor.  If $\lambda_{\max}=f_{\max}\Tr W$, positivity gives
\be
  \Tr(W^2)\leq \lambda_{\max}\Tr W,
  \qquad
  \Neff\geq \frac{1}{f_{\max}}.
\label{eq:pure-floor}
\ee
Consequently an occupation-enhanced brightest channel obeying
Eq.~\eqref{eq:f-bound} already gives
$\Neff\gtrsim1/(\eta n_\beta)$ without a matrix-element envelope.
This is a floor, not yet the entropy-sized conclusion.

Pure coupling enhancement is different: rescaling $A_i\mapsto cA_i$
leaves Eq.~\eqref{eq:line-asymmetry} invariant in equilibrium.  There
are two physically distinct possibilities.  A channel-selective
elementary coupling lies outside the ordinary gravitational/EFT
coupling class.  A normalized collective mode, however, can acquire a
$\sqrt K$ vertex from $K$ universally coupled constituents without
violating elementary coupling universality.  If that collective mode is
thermal, it has the calibrated ratio and $g^{(2)}(0)=2$ at rank one.
It is therefore an exact instantaneous static mimic.

Persistent emission adds a constraint that the equilibrium snapshot
misses.  Let a collective mode be refilled from the internal system at
thermal rate $\Gamma_{\rm th}$ and drained into empty exterior modes at
rate $\Gamma_{\rm out}$.  In the Markovian thermal-refill model its
steady occupation and response ratio are exactly
\be
 n_* = \frac{\Gamma_{\rm th}n_\beta}
              {\Gamma_{\rm th}+\Gamma_{\rm out}},
 \qquad
 1-\frac{r_*}{R}
 =\frac{x}{n_\beta+1+x},
 \qquad
 x=\frac{\Gamma_{\rm out}}{\Gamma_{\rm th}}.
\label{eq:starvation}
\ee
The time-local assumption is not essential for a stationary linear
Gaussian channel.  With additive frequency-dependent internal and
exterior self-energies, define
$\Gamma_a(\omega)=-2\operatorname{Im}\Sigma_a^R(\omega)$.  The exact
Keldysh relations give, pointwise in a resolved line,
\be
 n_{\rm eff}(\omega)
 =\frac{\Gamma_{\rm int}(\omega)n_\beta(\omega)}
        {\Gamma_{\rm int}(\omega)+\Gamma_{\rm out}(\omega)},
 \qquad
 1-\frac{r_{\rm eff}}{R}
 =\frac{x}{n_\beta+1+x},
 \quad
 x=\frac{\Gamma_{\rm out}}{\Gamma_{\rm int}}.
\label{eq:spectral-starvation}
\ee
Real dispersive self-energy shifts distort the spectral function but
cancel from this ratio.  If $\mathcal A(\omega)=-2\operatorname{Im}
G^R(\omega)$ and
$\delta_-(\omega)=1-r_{\rm eff}(\omega)/R(\omega)$, the outgoing
spectral number current satisfies the exact identity
\be
 j_{\rm out}(\omega)
 =\frac{\mathcal A(\omega)\Gamma_{\rm int}(\omega)
 n_\beta(\omega)[n_\beta(\omega)+1]\delta_-(\omega)}
 {2\pi[1+n_\beta(\omega)\delta_-(\omega)]}.
\label{eq:spectral-current}
\ee
Thus the theorem can be written in terms of an independently calibrated
internal relaxation spectrum; a Planckian ceiling is needed only for a
black-hole corollary.

The drained mode is colder than its refill bath.  Its asymmetry moves
\emph{below} the calibrated ratio, while a macroscopically occupied
source moves above it.  Gaussian drain leaves $g^{(2)}(0)=2$, so the
signed response, rather than photon bunching, is the starvation
diagnostic.

For a line at $\omega\sim T$ carrying number-flux fraction $F_{\rm coll}$
through $m$ equal collective channels, write the total Hawking number
flux as $\dot N_{\rm H}=aT$ with $a=O(1)$ including greybody data.
If the internal relaxation rate is bounded by
\be
  \Gamma_{\rm th}\leq c_P T,
\label{eq:planckian-input}
\ee
Eq.~\eqref{eq:starvation} gives parametrically
\be
  \eta_-
  \gtrsim
  \frac{F_{\rm coll}a}
       {m c_P n_\beta(n_\beta+1)},
  \qquad
  F_{\rm coll}
  \lesssim
  \frac{m c_P n_\beta(n_\beta+1)}{a}\,\eta_- .
\label{eq:collective-flux-bound}
\ee
For Schwarzschild black holes, quasinormal damping supplies the
semiclassical motivation for a thermal-scale relaxation rate
\cite{BertiCardosoStarinets2009}.  A generic Planckian bound on all
many-body relaxation rates is conjectural, not a theorem
\cite{HartnollMackenzie2022}; the chaos bound constrains a different
quantity and is only supporting context
\cite{MaldacenaShenkerStanford2016}.  Equations
\eqref{eq:starvation}--\eqref{eq:collective-flux-bound} are therefore
conditional on Eq.~\eqref{eq:planckian-input} and the stated
single/equal-split channel bookkeeping.  The spectral identities
\eqref{eq:spectral-starvation}--\eqref{eq:spectral-current} remove the
Markov approximation for additive stationary linear Gaussian kernels.
Non-additive, nonlinear/non-Gaussian, unresolved, and mixed-frequency
multiplexed refill remain outside the displayed black-hole bound.  A
total linewidth cannot be substituted for $\Gamma_{\rm int}$ unless
$\Gamma_{\rm out}$ is independently calibrated and the additive split
is valid.

The ordinary sector still requires the smooth-envelope input of
Lemma~\ref{lem:schwarzschild}.  In per-channel form its flux is capped by
$F_{\rm env}$, normalized so that
\be
  \frac{F_{\rm H}}{F_{\rm env}}\sim c\,S_{\rm BH}.
\ee
This is not a new universal numerical constant; it is the ordinary-
sector translation of that lemma.

There is an additional aggregation caveat.  In emission-flux fractions
$f_i=\Gamma_{{\rm em},i}/\sum_j\Gamma_{{\rm em},j}$, the measured total
line ratio is
\be
  r_{\rm tot}
  =\left(\sum_i\frac{f_i}{r_i}\right)^{-1},
  \qquad
  \frac{R}{r_{\rm tot}}
  =\sum_i f_i\frac{R}{r_i}.
\label{eq:aggregate-ratio}
\ee
A HIGH-side and a LOW-side channel can therefore cancel exactly.  Two
such channels can give $r_{\rm tot}=R$ with $\Neff\leq2$, approaching
one for an extreme LOW-side absorber.  One aggregate tolerance cannot
be read as separate high- and low-side bounds.

In the narrow stationary Gaussian class, a two-drain protocol gives an
explicit finite-error separator.  Scale all exterior widths as
$\Gamma_{{\rm out},c}=s\gamma_c$ and write
\be
  \frac{D(s)}{s}=-H+L(s),\qquad
  L(s)=\sum_c\frac{s\gamma_c^2}{g_c+s\gamma_c},
\ee
where $H\geq0$ is the drain-independent HIGH balance weight.  If
$s_2>s_1$, $s_2\gamma_c/g_c\leq X$, and
$|D(s_j)/s_j|\leq\epsilon_j$, monotonicity gives
\be
  L(s_1)\leq
  \frac{s_1(1+X)}{s_2-s_1}(\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2),
  \qquad
  H\leq L(s_1)+\epsilon_1.
\label{eq:two-drain-finite}
\ee
For measured $y_j=r_{\rm tot}(s_j)/R$ with
$|y_j-1|\leq\eta_j<1$, one may take
$\epsilon_j=[E_{\rm tot}(s_j)/(s_jR)]\eta_j/(1-\eta_j)$.
A lower classification threshold
$s_1\gamma_c/g_c\geq x_{\min}>0$ also converts the first bound into
$E_C(s_1)\leq s_1n_\beta L(s_1)/x_{\min}$.  The constants deteriorate
when the scan does not bound the starvation ratios; this is the precise
additional width prior used by this multi-setting realization.

The following
finite-accuracy statement assumes channel/line-shape resolution or a
multi-setting drain, time, resolution, or response-tomography protocol
that separately bounds the two signed sectors.  Under that condition let
\be
  f_{\rm bad}=f_{\rm occ}+F_{\rm coll},
  \qquad
  f_{\rm occ}\lesssim\eta_+ n_\beta,
\ee
where $\eta_+$ and $\eta_-$ are the separately resolved high- and
low-side deviations.  The finite-accuracy pigeonhole bound is then
\be
  \Neff^{\rm ord}
  \gtrsim
  (1-f_{\rm bad})\,\frac{F_{\rm H}}{F_{\rm env}}
  \sim
  (1-f_{\rm bad})c\,S_{\rm BH},
\ee
and
\be
  \Neff
  \gtrsim
  \frac{1}{f_{\rm bad}^2+(1-f_{\rm bad})/(cS_{\rm BH})}
  \sim
  \min\{cS_{\rm BH},f_{\rm bad}^{-2}\}.
\label{eq:finite-eta-neff}
\ee
Exact separately resolved response gives $\Neff\gtrsim cS_{\rm BH}$
within this weak-emission and relaxation class.  Exact aggregate
response alone does not.  At finite accuracy, full total
participation requires $f_{\rm bad}\lesssim S_{\rm BH}^{-1/2}$; at
coarser accuracy Eq.~\eqref{eq:finite-eta-neff} remains a quantitative
floor rather than a saturation measurement.

The response test has one kinematic caveat.  If the bright collective
ladder is anharmonic and fully resolved, a sloped occupation
distribution can make the line ratio look thermal even with
$\Neff=O(1)$.  This fake is resolution-dependent: it fails the same
asymmetry test when the ladder is unresolved, and within a resolved
sub-line its photon counting remains subthermal.  The passive
second-order statistic
\be
  g^{(2)}(0)
  =
  \frac{\langle A^\dagger A^\dagger A A\rangle}
       {\langle A^\dagger A\rangle^2}
\ee
therefore supplies an independent leg of the certificate.  A sharp
collective source gives $g^{(2)}(0)=1-O(1/S)$, while a thermal Hawking
mode gives $g^{(2)}(0)=2$.

More generally, under fourth-moment channel independence, if every
HIGH-side channel obeys $g_i^{(2)}\leq2-\kappa$, then
\be
  Q_{\rm H}\equiv\sum_{i\in H}f_i^2
  \leq \frac{\epsilon_g}{\kappa},
  \qquad
  g_{\rm tot}^{(2)}\geq2-\epsilon_g.
\ee
If a separately resolved spectral protocol bounds the total LOW-side
emitted fraction by $F_C\leq c_-$, then
$Q_C=\sum_{i\in C}f_i^2\leq c_-^2$.  Finally, the ordinary per-channel
envelope $f_i\leq p$ gives
$Q_O=\sum_{i\in O}f_i^2\leq p$.  Hence the paired certificate has the
transparent bound
\be
  \Neff
  \geq
  \frac{1}{\epsilon_g/\kappa+c_-^2+p}.
\label{eq:paired-floor}
\ee
The first two terms are observable/protocol bounds on the enhanced
branches.  The last is still the ordinary-sector smooth-envelope input.
Thus the response calculation closes the dangerous outliers but does
not, by itself, derive the full ordinary Gram tail.  Removing that last
input requires response-kernel tomography or another cumulative bound
on the ordered ordinary eigenvalues.

\section{Dynamical Counterpart}

Let a local reservoir couple to an exterior apparatus through the same
surface shell, with uniformly bounded local interactions.  Deposit a
small quantum register in a bulk region at distance $L$ from that
surface, and allow the exterior apparatus arbitrary measurements,
ancillas, and feedback through the surface coupling.  Lieb--Robinson
bounds \cite{LiebRobinson1972,HastingsKoma2006,NachtergaeleSims2010}
imply that exterior observables have exponentially small commutator with
the deposited register until times of order $L/v$, where $v$ is the
Lieb--Robinson velocity.  Equivalently, the exterior record is nearly
independent of the deposited quantum information until the light cone
reaches the surface.

For a deposit at radius scale in a reservoir with extensive entropy,
$L$ scales as $S^{1/d}$ in thermal-cell units.  The corresponding
exterior recovery time is therefore power-law in $S$.  In the
Hayden--Preskill recovery problem, a small register thrown into an
already scrambled black hole becomes recoverable from the later
radiation after $O(k+\log S)$ emitted quanta, assuming the decoder holds
the earlier radiation and the usual decoupling hypotheses apply
\cite{HaydenPreskill2007,SlobodovLong}.  This comparison uses locality
on the reservoir side and scrambling on the black-hole side.  The
effective-rank calculation above concerns the emission algebra itself.

Recovery has two separable costs: the number of emitted quanta after
which the deposited information is present in the exterior record at
all, and the difficulty of extracting it from that record.  The
comparison above concerns only the first.  The second is an independent
axis: with the dynamics known, the Hayden--Preskill decoder can be made
efficient \cite{YoshidaKitaev2017}, while decoding generic Hawking
radiation is expected to be computationally infeasible
\cite{HarlowHayden2013}, with a conjectured geometric criterion for the
hard instances \cite{BrownGharibyanPeningtonSusskind2020}.  The two
exponents can therefore separate: a system may attain logarithmic
arrival latency while the extraction cost remains exponential.  The
comparison by arrival latency is insensitive to this distinction, which
is set by decoder resources rather than by the emission algebra.

\section{Discussion}

In a weak Hamiltonian emission model, the Schwarzschild luminosity law
fixes the effective rank of the emission operator Gram matrix.  With
ordinary-strength smooth matrix elements and four-dimensional phase
space, that rank is of order $S_{\rm BH}$.  A local reservoir with
surface-supported couplings has effective rank bounded by its surface
area in microscopic units.  The same surface restriction gives the
usual power-law locality bound on exterior recovery of bulk-deposited
quantum information.

Flux moments alone do not certify an entropy-sized participation
count.  At Schwarzschild temperature the wavelength is of order the
horizon radius, so the radiation resolves an order-one number of
angular modes per coherence time
\cite{Page1976,BekensteinMayo2001,Pendry1983}: instantaneous emission
mode counts have order-one rank however large $\Neff$ may be.  The
paired certificate in Sec.~\ref{sec:static-certificate} uses a
different observable: the calibrated stimulated emission/absorption
ratio of a resolved line, supplemented by photon counting to close the
resolved-ladder loophole.  Its sign distinguishes a macroscopically
occupied hot-side outlier from a reservoir-starved cold-side collective
outlier only when the signed components are channel-resolved or
separated by a multi-setting protocol; Eq.~\eqref{eq:aggregate-ratio}
shows that one net ratio permits exact cancellation.  The LOW-side
inference additionally uses an independently calibrated relaxation
spectrum, with Eq.~\eqref{eq:planckian-input} needed only for the
black-hole corollary.  Neither the response nor counting legs remove the
ordinary-sector Gram-tail assumption by themselves.

There remains a separate temporal question.  For Rindler wedges,
eternal black holes, and large anti--de Sitter black holes, the exterior
state is thermal in the exact KMS sense, and stationary response and
fluctuation spectra are tied to interior state structure by the
equilibrium dictionary
\cite{BisognanoWichmann1976,Israel1976,GibbonsHawkingAction1977,
HawkingPage1983}.  Schwarzschild evaporation sits on the other side of
that divide: at negative heat capacity the canonical ensemble does not
exist, and there is no stationary KMS state of the exterior field to
which long-time spectral data could be referred.  The exterior instead
receives a single non-stationary record delivering of order one nat per
coherence time over of order $S$ emissions.  The appropriate reference
for a resolved transition is therefore the microcanonical density-of-
states ratio, not a globally stationary canonical bath.  The two-sided
line-response certificate can bound the source-rank axis within its
weak-emission and relaxation class, but the access latency of deposited
quantum information is still the independent temporal comparison of
the preceding section.

The Bekenstein--Hawking density of states and the scrambling dynamics
enter as separate inputs.  Once those inputs are assumed, the entropy
counted by the black hole is also available to the emission operator
algebra.  In stretched-horizon language, the entropy is carried by
degrees of freedom that directly participate in absorption and emission.
In ordinary local-reservoir language, this is a special surface
structure beyond thermality alone.

There is a distinct coherent branch, however, in which the same coarse
flux is carried by a collectively enhanced source rather than by
entropy-sized source participation.  The black-hole $N$-portrait and
memory-burden models provide a useful comparison class
\cite{DvaliGomez2013,DvaliPanchenko2016,DvaliEisemannMichelZell2020,
DvaliValbuenaBermudezZantedeschi2024}.  The strict emission prototype
uses an ordinary-strength exterior vertex with master occupation
$n_0\sim S$.  It therefore predicts
$r=n_0/(n_0+1)=1-O(1/S)$ and, per resolved mode,
$g^{(2)}(0)=1-O(1/S)$ rather than the thermal value two.  This
occupation-enhanced branch is excluded by the response measurement
within the harmonic/resolution-stable class.

Equal coupling of an external mode to $K$ memory species instead reduces
to one collective mode with strength enhanced by $\sqrt K$, while the
remaining $K-1$ modes decouple \cite{DvaliPanchenko2016}.  If the
collective mode starts thermal, it exactly reproduces the calibrated
ratio and $g^{(2)}(0)=2$ at $\Neff=1$; this confirms that equilibrium
static observables do not determine source participation.  The same
decoupling then causes starvation: only the $O(1)$ occupation of the
bright mode drains, giving a flash with integrated reservoir fraction
$1/K$.  Persistent Hawking-sized emission requires refill, and
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:starvation}--\eqref{eq:collective-flux-bound} turn the
required refill into a low-side response deviation under the
thermal-scale relaxation assumption.  A generic coherent model can
still evade the completed certificate through superthermal refill,
non-additive, nonlinear/non-Gaussian, or nonstationary dynamics, a different
nonthermal refill class, signed cancellation in an insufficiently resolved
protocol, or unresolved multiplexing; the result is a conditional rate bound,
not a refutation of all collective black-hole models.

The same models also separate participation from latency.  In the
strict prototype the memory-sector occupation is conserved and
fixed-sector diary information has no Hamiltonian route into the
emitted master record; in the black-hole mapping the memory-burden
release channel is instead parametrically long.  Thus state-count
saturation, source-rank saturation, and Hayden--Preskill latency are
separate assumptions.
