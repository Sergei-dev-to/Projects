\begin{abstract}
% TODO: finalize once the latency lemma (both halves) and the dS leg
% are proven.
We propose a geometry-free characterization of horizons with two
layers.  The model-side invariant is \emph{boundary saturation}: the
participation number of the emission algebra's channel Gram kernel
scales as the full entropy, $\Neff \sim S$, whereas ordinary local
reservoirs coupled through a contact surface obey
$\Neff \lesssim S^{(d-1)/d}$.  Its exterior certificate is
\emph{recovery latency}: horizons are thermal systems whose full
entropy is exterior-recoverable at logarithmic emitted-record latency
--- a diary of entropy $k$ becomes recoverable from the radiation
within $O(k+\log S)$ emitted quanta in the Hayden--Preskill regime ---
while ordinary reservoirs are bounded below by interior transport, with
power-law latency exponents.  The
criterion is the unique exterior invariant surviving a two-horizon
comparison: for Schwarzschild the state-count input is exotic
(super-Hagedorn, unavailable to local systems) while for de Sitter it
is ordinary --- the cosmological-horizon entropy deficit matches a
generic finite reservoir through second order, with effective heat
capacity equal to the horizon entropy --- yet saturation holds in both
cases and fails for every ordinary thermal system, including the
reservoir that mimics the de Sitter spectrum.  The latency exponent is
dimensionless, independent of coupling normalization, and distinguishes
horizons from systems satisfying every geometric entropy bound,
separating the criterion from the holographic principle, the stretched
horizon, and fast-scrambler connectivity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: the two-horizon contrast}
\label{sec:contrast}

% The contrast engine. Per program decision: this stays foregrounded ---
% without it, saturation reads as a stretched-horizon slogan.

Which exterior property makes a thermal system a horizon?  The
conditional Hamiltonian models of \cite{SlobodovLong,SlobodovNote}
% TODO: self-citations once companion papers are posted
reproduce the operational signature package of a Schwarzschild horizon
--- detailed-balance thermality, area-law emission strength, the
Page/island transition, complementary recovery, state-dependent
mirrors, and the decoding-complexity barrier --- from three inputs: the
state count, boundary accessibility of the leading entropy, and
decoupling.  Each input is measured by one layer of the phenomenology.

Running the same analysis on the cosmological horizon flips one input
and not the other.  For Schwarzschild, the state count is exotic:
$\Smicro(E) = cE^2$ is super-Hagedorn and provably unavailable to local
finite-density systems --- the holographic entropy mismatch
\cite{Bekenstein1981,tHooft1993,Susskind1995,Bousso2002,
CohenKaplanNelson1999,Yurtsever2003}.  For de Sitter, the state count
is ordinary: we show in Section~\ref{sec:reservoir} that the
Schwarzschild--de Sitter horizon-entropy deficit matches the generic
expansion of a large finite thermal reservoir through second order,
with effective heat capacity $C_{\rm eff} = S_0$.  The spectrum input
is thus impossible for one horizon and trivial for the other.

One input survives the comparison unchanged: in both cases the number
of comparably coupled emission channels scales as the \emph{full}
entropy, $N \sim S$, whereas an ordinary local reservoir in $d$ spatial
dimensions, coupled through a contact surface, has volume-law entropy
and only area-law channels, $N \sim S^{(d-1)/d}$.  We propose this
\emph{boundary saturation} as the operational horizon invariant:
\be
  \sigma \;\equiv\; \lim \frac{\log \Neff}{\log S} \;=\; 1
  \qquad \text{(horizon)}, \qquad
  \sigma = \frac{d-1}{d} \quad \text{(ordinary reservoir)}.
\ee
The claim has two layers.  \emph{Boundary saturation is the hidden
model-side condition; recovery latency is its exterior certificate once
mixing is included.}  The invariant itself is model-side:
Section~\ref{sec:participation} defines the accessible channel count
as a spectral invariant of the coupling algebra, immune to channel
rebasing and coupling rescaling.  Its exterior certificate --- which
tests saturation jointly with the mixing input, since a saturated but
slow-mixing system fails it --- is the
recovery-latency exponent (Lemma~\ref{lem:measurement}): horizons
are thermal systems whose full entropy is exterior-recoverable at
logarithmic emitted-record latency, while ordinary reservoirs are
transport-limited to power-law latency.  The certificate, not flux
moments, is what an exterior observer measures: shared outgoing modes
make the channel count invisible to flux statistics --- the same
collective bottleneck that limits the per-quantum record capacity in
\cite{SlobodovLong}.

\section{The participation channel count}
\label{sec:participation}

Let the system shell $\mathcal H_E$ couple to an external continuum
through operators $\{O_\mu\}$.  Define the \emph{channel Gram kernel}
at frequency $\omega$,
\be
  W_{\mu\nu}(E,\omega)
  = \frac{1}{D_E}\,
  \Tr\!\left[\Pi_{E-\omega}\,O_\mu\,\Pi_E\,O_\nu^\dagger\right],
\label{eq:gram}
\ee
a positive-semidefinite Hermitian matrix on channel space: the
shell-averaged Hilbert--Schmidt Gram matrix of the jump operators.

\begin{definition}[Participation channel count]
\label{def:neff}
\be
  \Neff(E,\omega)
  = \frac{(\Tr W)^2}{\Tr W^2}
  = \frac{\big(\sum_a \lambda_a\big)^2}{\sum_a \lambda_a^2},
\ee
the participation ratio of the eigenvalues $\lambda_a$ of $W$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Invariance and dichotomy]
\label{lem:invariance}
(i) $\Neff$ is invariant under unitary rebasing of the channel labels
and under overall coupling rescaling $W \to \lambda W$; it is a
spectral invariant of the coupling algebra's image in shell operator
space.
(ii) Comparable independent channels of rank $N$ give
$\Neff \simeq N$; coherent (Dicke-aligned) coupling gives
$\Neff \simeq 1$ regardless of the microscopic label count.
\end{lemma}

\emph{Proof.}  (i) Both traces are basis-independent, and the ratio is
degree-zero in $W$.  (ii) Flat spectrum of rank $N$:
$(N\lambda)^2/(N\lambda^2) = N$; rank one: $\lambda^2/\lambda^2 = 1$.
\hfill$\square$

saturation criterion.

\section{The compressed exterior channel}
\label{sec:compression}

Why must the certificate be temporal?  Because the horizon condition
itself compresses the exterior channel.  A thermal emitter of size $R$
at temperature $T$ radiates through $\sim (RT)^{d-1}$ modes per
coherence time $\beta$; the horizon condition $T \sim 1/R$ --- the
temperature law for Schwarzschild, Gibbons--Hawking for de Sitter ---
makes this $O(1)$: horizons are one-wavelength systems.  The channel is
moreover critically provisioned: $O(1)$ nats per $\beta$ over
$\tau/\beta \sim S$ emission steps carries exactly the entropy, with no
slack.  This is the channel-capacity face of the statement that black
holes are one-dimensional information conduits
\cite{BekensteinMayo2001,Pendry1983}.  An exterior observer therefore
sees $O(1)$ numbers at any instant; all structure beyond that resides
in correlations across the emitted record, which is why boundary
saturation is invisible to flux moments and why the certificate of
Lemma~\ref{lem:measurement} is a latency exponent.

Compression together with unitarity forces \emph{serialization}, not
mixing: a tape-readout model purifies $S$ bits through a single mode
over $O(S)$ emissions using time-bin orthogonality, with no fast
scrambling.  What forces mixing is the latency demand itself ---

rapid recoverability of arbitrarily deposited information requires the
deposit to reach the few coupling directions within $O(k+\log S)$
emitted records for a diary of entropy $k$, which ordinary local baths
cannot achieve and horizons achieve only when accessibility and mixing
act together.
Compression is thus the reason the two inputs are exterior-visible
only as a joint temporal exponent.

\begin{lemma}[Luminosity measures saturation]
\label{lem:schwarzschild}
Within the weak-emission microcanonical class with massless $p=2$ phase
space, the Schwarzschild luminosity law $P \propto E^{-2}$ holds if and
only if $\Neff(E) \propto E^2 \propto \Smicro(E)$, i.e.\ $\sigma = 1$.
\end{lemma}

\section{The ordinary-reservoir bound}
\label{sec:reservoir-bound}

\begin{lemma}[Contact scaling]
\label{lem:contact}
For a local finite-range Hamiltonian in $d$ spatial dimensions at
temperature $T$, coupled to the continuum through a contact surface of
area $A$: the jump operators are supported on surface sites, so
$\mathrm{rank}\,W \lesssim A/\lambda_T^{d-1}$, while
$S \sim V/\lambda_T^d$.  Hence
\be
  \Neff \;\lesssim\; S^{(d-1)/d},
\ee
strictly below saturation.
\end{lemma}

\subsection{The reservoir lemma}

\begin{lemma}[The de Sitter state count is an ordinary reservoir]
\label{lem:reservoir}
Let a finite system have smooth microcanonical entropy with
$S_R(E_{\rm tot}) = S_0$, $S_R' = \beta$, and heat capacity $C$.  A
patch holding energy $E$ of the shared total sees
\be
  \rho(E) \propto
  \exp\Big[S_0 - \beta E - \frac{E^2}{2T^2C} + \cdots\Big].
\ee
The exact Schwarzschild--de Sitter cosmological-horizon entropy expands
as ($m = M/\ell$, $r_c = \ell(1 - m - \tfrac32 m^2 + \cdots)$)
\be
  S_{\rm hor}(M) = \pi r_c^2 = S_0 - \beta M - 2\pi M^2 + \cdots,
\ee
which is the reservoir form with
\be
  C_{\rm eff} = \frac{1}{4\pi T_{\rm dS}^2} = \pi\ell^2 = S_0 .
\ee
\end{lemma}

The first-order entropic suppression is classical
\cite{GibbonsHawking1977,CLPW2022}; the content here is the
second-order finite-reservoir identification: through this order the
de Sitter state-count input is generic bath thermodynamics with an
unremarkable effective heat capacity of order the entropy.  A checkable
corollary: patch energy fluctuations
$\langle\Delta E^2\rangle = C_{\rm eff}T^2 = S_0 T^2$.

\section{What saturation is not}
\label{sec:ancestors}

% Referee-proofing. Each ancestor differs in kind.

\emph{Stretched horizon} \cite{SusskindThorlaciusUglum1993}: a
postulate about where entropy resides, presupposing the geometry;
saturation is a measurable exponent, statable for systems with no
metric.
\emph{Membrane paradigm} \cite{ThornePriceMacdonald1986}: operational
surface transport; no entropy-versus-channel-count statement.
\emph{Holographic principle}
\cite{tHooft1993,Susskind1995,Bousso2002}: a geometric \emph{bound}
$S \le A/4$; saturation is a dynamical \emph{equality} about the
coupling algebra --- an ordinary bath satisfies every holographic bound
and violates saturation.
\emph{Fast-scrambler connectivity} \cite{SekinoSusskind2008}: a
statement about the internal interaction graph (the mixing input of
\cite{SlobodovLong}); saturation concerns the external coupling
algebra (the emission input).  The two are distinct inputs and the
likeliest point of confusion.
