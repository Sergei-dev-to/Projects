## 1. Core claim

In a horizon-like access channel, the radiation record has two sharply
different layers:

```text
no-hair / conserved / thermodynamic observables
    -> redundantly broadcast into many small record fragments;

microscopic diary or microstate observables
    -> inaccessible to small fragments, recoverable only from large,
       delayed, high-complexity algebras after Page/HP conditions.
```

This is the Quantum-Darwinism split applied to horizons:

```text
pointer algebra = no-hair algebra
quantum residue = diary/microstate algebra
environment fragments = radiation fragments
```

The result should not be phrased as "black holes measure the infaller."
The point is record structure: public classical data versus private
quantum residue.

## 2. Minimal model

Let the finite sector be

```text
H = direct sum_x H_x
```

where `x` labels a small no-hair/pointer algebra: mass bin, charge,
angular momentum, temperature bin, or other coarse conserved data.  Let
`dim H_x = d_x` contain the microscopic degeneracy.  The label `x`
should be understood as a coarse bin with macroscopically
distinguishable emission statistics, not an infinitely precise mass or
spin value.

The radiation record after `n` emissions is split into fragments:

```text
R = R_1 ... R_n .
```

A Darwinian/no-hair channel has approximate form

```text
V: |x, a>_B |0>_R
   -> |B_{x,a}> \otimes |r_x^{(1)}> ... |r_x^{(n)}>
      + microscopic corrections carrying a.
```

The coarse label `x` is inferred redundantly from many fragments because
it controls the statistics of repeated records.  The microscopic label
`a` is not copied into individual fragments; it is encoded in global
correlations among late radiation and remaining interior/core degrees.

This is a schematic channel form, not a claim that exact black-hole
evaporation is classical copying.  Exact unitarity lives in the
`a`-dependent global correlations.

## 3. Diagnostics

For a record fragment `F subset R`, define:

### Coarse distinguishability

```text
D_F(x,x') = 1/2 || rho_F^x - rho_F^{x'} ||_1 .
```

High redundancy means many disjoint fragments `F_i` of sufficient size
satisfy

```text
D_{F_i}(x,x') >= 1 - delta
```

for macroscopically distinct no-hair labels.

### Microscopic distinguishability

For two microstates `a,a'` in the same no-hair sector `x`,

```text
D_F(a,a' | x) = 1/2 || rho_F^{x,a} - rho_F^{x,a'} ||_1 .
```

Cryptographic microscopic access means for all small/subthreshold
fragments,

```text
D_F(a,a' | x) <= epsilon
```

for typical `a,a'`, while the full final radiation, or the
Hayden-Preskill-eligible record plus side information, can recover the
microstate/diary.

### Redundancy

For no-hair observable `X`,

```text
R_delta(X) = max number of disjoint fragments F_i
             with I_acc(X : F_i) >= (1-delta) H(X).
```

For a generic diary `D`, the analogous redundancy should be near zero
below the recovery threshold.

## 4. Theorem-shaped statements

### Theorem 0: Darwinian no-hair / cryptographic residue split

Consider a weak-emission channel whose finite sector decomposes as

```text
H = direct sum_x H_x,
```

where `x` is a coarse no-hair label and `a in H_x` is microscopic data
inside the fixed sector.  Let one resolvable radiation record be
labelled by `m` and let the record algebra be generated by

```tex
A_{mm'} = K_{m'}^\dagger K_m .
```

Assume three conditions.

1. **Within-sector record smoothness.**  For all code states in a fixed
   sector `x`, the diagonal record signatures

```tex
a_{mm'}(\nu)=<nu|A_{mm'}|nu>
```

   vary by at most `eta_x` in the cumulative pre-threshold budget.  This
   is the diagonal-ETH / record-signature condition of
   `weak_emission_eth_bridge.md`.

2. **Between-sector record distinguishability.**  For distinct coarse
   labels `x,x'`, the induced one-record distributions or one-record
   density matrices differ with Chernoff exponent

```text
xi(x,x') > 0
```

   for the chosen coarse bins.

3. **Microscopic decoupling in each sector.**  The channel restricted to
   the microscopic code inside each `H_x` satisfies the Page/HP
   decoupling estimate used in the long paper.

Then the access profile splits:

```text
coarse/no-hair data:
    m-record fragments distinguish x with error
    P_err <= exp[-m xi(x,x')] + O(m eta_x);

microscopic diary data:
    subthreshold fragments inside fixed x have distinguishability /
    recoverability controlled by the decoupling error, while the
    post-Page or HP-eligible record recovers the diary from the global
    radiation algebra plus the appropriate side information.
```

Thus no-hair data are public pointer data for the horizon access
channel, while generic microscopic data are private quantum residue
until the access algebra crosses the Page/HP threshold.

Proof sketch.  Condition 2 and independence or sufficient mixing of
successive records gives the usual Chernoff accumulation for the
coarse classical hypothesis `x`.  Condition 1 prevents microscopic
variation inside `H_x` from contaminating the same small fragments:
the record-signature leakage bound says early fragments can distinguish
code states only through cumulative variation of `a_{mm'}(\nu)`.  With
that leakage controlled, the remaining microscopic information is
governed by the sectorwise decoupling estimate.  Before the relevant
threshold, data processing keeps the diary inaccessible to small
fragments; after the HP/Page threshold, the recovery theorem supplies
the decoder.  The result is a two-layer record: redundant classical
coarse information, delayed global quantum recovery.

This is the precise form of the slogan:

```text
horizon radiation is Darwinian for no-hair data
and cryptographic for diary data.
```

The theorem is intentionally a composition theorem.  The new point is
not Chernoff, no-broadcasting, or Hayden-Preskill separately; it is that
the horizon weak-emission record algebra supplies exactly the split
needed to apply them to different subalgebras.

### Theorem 1: Broadcast no-hair from detailed balance

In a weak-emission model whose record distribution depends on a small
coarse label `x` through temperature/chemical potentials,

```text
p(r | x) != p(r | x')
```

for macroscopically distinct `x,x'`, independent or sufficiently mixing
radiation records accumulate Chernoff information about `x`.  Therefore
the no-hair label is redundantly distinguishable after
`O(log 1/delta / xi)` thermal records per fragment, where `xi` is the
appropriate classical/quantum Chernoff exponent for the chosen bins.

Interpretation: mass/charge/angular momentum/temperature behave like
pointer data because they control the exterior fields and emission
statistics.  The statement is asymptotic/coarse-grained: exact
continuous parameters require binning and enough record samples.

### Theorem 2: No-cloning/no-broadcasting prevents microscopic Darwinism

If `a` ranges over noncommuting microscopic states or arbitrary diary
states in a fixed no-hair sector, no channel can redundantly broadcast
`a` into many independent fragments while preserving arbitrary quantum
superpositions.  Therefore generic diary information cannot be
Quantum-Darwinian pointer data.

This is mostly no-broadcasting/no-cloning, but it cleanly separates
classical no-hair objectivity from quantum microstate recovery.
Commuting classical labels can be redundantly recorded; arbitrary
unknown quantum states cannot.

### Theorem 3: Page/HP cryptographic threshold

In the horizon-class decoupling model, before the Page/HP threshold,
small or subthreshold fragments have negligible recoverability for a
fresh diary `D`; after the threshold, the appropriate large radiation
algebra plus side information recovers `D`.

This imports the decoupling bound from the long paper, but reinterprets
it as a Quantum-Darwinism contrast:

```text
no-hair information: redundant fragment accessibility;
diary information: threshold/global accessibility.
```

### Theorem 4: Ordinary Darwinian environments are not HP channels

A standard decohering environment may redundantly broadcast pointer
data but generically lacks a mechanism for low-latency quantum recovery
of arbitrary newly deposited diaries.  It is Darwinian without being a
horizon-class access channel.

This theorem should probably be stated as a witness/counterexample
rather than a universal theorem.

## 5. Relation to no-hair

The no-hair theorem already says that stationary black holes are
classically characterized by mass, charge, and angular momentum (with
standard caveats about matter content, dimensions, boundary conditions,
and possible hair in extended theories).  The Darwinian split refines
the information-theoretic role of that fact:

```text
no-hair observables are the pointer algebra of the horizon access
channel.
```

They are redundantly visible because the radiation distribution,
geometry, and long-range fields depend on them.  Microscopic data are
not pointer data.  In a unitary evaporation model they survive only in
subtle correlations required by unitarity.

This avoids a common confusion:

```text
classical objectivity of no-hair data
does not imply
classical accessibility of microscopic information.
```

## 6. Heisenberg-cut interpretation

In measurement language:

```text
apparatus/environment records pointer data redundantly;
unrecorded quantum information remains in global correlations.
```

For horizons:

```text
radiation records no-hair data redundantly;
diary information remains behind the operational cut until the access
algebra is enlarged enough: Page radiation, island, mining apparatus,
boundary CFT, or constraint-dressed observables.
```

So the finite horizon is a thermodynamic Heisenberg cut with a pointer
algebra fixed by no-hair data.

## 7. What would be new

Not new:

- no-hair data are public;
- Quantum Darwinism explains pointer objectivity;
- no-cloning/no-broadcasting blocks redundant copying of unknown quantum
  states;
- Hayden-Preskill gives diary recovery after scrambling.

Potentially new:

1. putting these into one access-channel theorem;
2. identifying no-hair observables as the horizon pointer algebra;
3. showing black-hole radiation is simultaneously Darwinian for coarse
   data and cryptographic for microscopic data;
4. using the split to clarify why thermality/no-hair and unitarity/Page
   recovery are compatible but operationally different.

## 8. Why this is worth exploring

It is the shortest bridge from the technical horizon-access program to
the Heisenberg-cut/Quantum-Darwinism idea:

```text
public classical records: redundant no-hair data;
private quantum residue: diary/microstate correlations;
cut movement: enlargement of the access algebra.
```

If the toy theorem works, it gives a concrete answer to "what is
measurement-like about a horizon?" without invoking collapse:

> the horizon channel selects a small pointer algebra for redundant
> public records while preserving the remaining quantum information in
> delayed global correlations.

## 10. Two-sector toy theorem

This is the smallest standalone result worth proving first.

Let

```text
H = H_0 direct-sum H_1
```

and let `X in {0,1}` be the coarse no-hair label.  In sector `x`, a
single resolvable record has state `sigma_x` on the record Hilbert
space.  For a fragment `F` of `m` records, assume

```tex
\rho_F^{x,a}
=
\sigma_x^{\otimes m}
+
\Delta_F^{x,a},
\qquad
\|\Delta_F^{x,a}\|_1 \le \eta_m
```

for all microscopic states `a` in `H_x`.  The correction `eta_m` is the
cumulative record-signature leakage inside the fixed sector.

Define the quantum Chernoff exponent

```tex
\xi(0,1)
=
-\log \inf_{0\le s\le 1}
\mathrm{Tr}\,\sigma_0^s\sigma_1^{1-s}.
```

Then the optimal error for distinguishing the coarse label from `F`
satisfies, up to the standard finite-`m` Chernoff prefactor,

```tex
P_{\rm err}(F)
\lesssim
e^{-m\xi(0,1)}
+
\eta_m .
```

So any collection of disjoint fragments of size

```tex
m \gtrsim \xi^{-1}\log(1/\delta)
```

each carries the coarse label with error `delta + eta_m`, and the
redundancy scales like the number of such fragments in the emitted
record.

The two-layer interpretation requires a nonempty window: the fragment
must be long enough to resolve the sector but short enough that
within-sector leakage remains small.  If `eta_m \sim m gamma`, this is
the resolution gap `gamma << xi`.

Now add a diary system `D`, with purifier `R_D`, encoded into the
microscopic states inside a fixed sector `x`.  If the sectorwise
decoupling estimate gives

```tex
\|\rho_{R_DF}-\rho_{R_D}\otimes\rho_F\|_1
\le
\epsilon_{\rm dec}
```

for all subthreshold fragments `F`, then no decoder acting on such a
fragment recovers the diary with error smaller than the corresponding
information-disturbance bound.  After the Page/HP threshold, the
existing recovery theorem applies to the appropriate global radiation
record plus side information.

Therefore the same emitted record is Darwinian for `X` and
cryptographic for `D`:

```text
X: redundant fragment access;
D: no fragment access below threshold, global recovery above threshold.
```

This toy theorem is deliberately agnostic about gravity.  The
horizon-specific content enters when `X` is the no-hair algebra, the
records are collective Hawking records, and boundary saturation plus
mixing supply the Page/HP latency.

### Proof

Use equal priors for the two coarse sectors.  For the ideal sector
records `sigma_0^{\otimes m}` and `sigma_1^{\otimes m}`, the optimal
binary discrimination error is

```tex
P_{\rm err}^{(0)}(m)
=
{1\over2}\left(
1-{1\over2}
\|\sigma_0^{\otimes m}-\sigma_1^{\otimes m}\|_1
\right).
```

The quantum Chernoff bound gives

```tex
P_{\rm err}^{(0)}(m)
\le
C_m\,e^{-m\xi(0,1)},
```

with `C_m` subexponential in `m`; for the exponent statement this is
the advertised `exp[-m xi]`.

Now restore the microscopic corrections.  Let

```tex
\rho_F^{x,a_x}
=
\sigma_x^{\otimes m}
+
\Delta_F^{x,a_x},
\qquad
\|\Delta_F^{x,a_x}\|_1\le \eta_m .
```

Take the POVM that is optimal for the ideal pair
`sigma_0^{\otimes m}, sigma_1^{\otimes m}` and apply it to the actual
states.  For any POVM element `0 <= M <= I`,

```tex
|\mathrm{Tr}\,M\Delta_F^{x,a_x}|
\le
\|\Delta_F^{x,a_x}\|_1 .
```

Thus the actual error changes by at most an additive `O(eta_m)`:

```tex
P_{\rm err}(F;a_0,a_1)
\le
C_m e^{-m\xi(0,1)} + O(\eta_m).
```

This proves public accessibility of the coarse label.  If the total
record has `n` approximately independent or mixing records, it can be
partitioned into `r=floor(n/m)` disjoint fragments of size `m`.  Each
fragment identifies `X` with error at most `delta` once

```tex
m \ge \xi(0,1)^{-1}\log(C_m/\delta)
```

up to the leakage term.  This is Darwinian redundancy for the coarse
observable: many disjoint fragments carry the same classical label.

For the diary side, fix `x` and let `R_D` purify the diary system `D`.
The subthreshold privacy hypothesis is

```tex
\|\rho_{R_DF}-\rho_{R_D}\otimes\rho_F\|_1
\le
\epsilon_{\rm dec}.
```

For any decoder `\mathcal D:F -> \widehat D`, trace distance is
monotone under channels, so

```tex
\|
(\mathrm{id}_{R_D}\otimes\mathcal D)(\rho_{R_DF})
-
\rho_{R_D}\otimes\mathcal D(\rho_F)
\|_1
\le
\epsilon_{\rm dec}.
```

The second state is product across `R_D|\widehat D`; it cannot contain
the diary entanglement.  For a `d_D`-dimensional maximally entangled
diary, its fidelity with the target `|\Phi\rangle_{R_D\widehat D}` is at
most `1/d_D`.  Therefore

```tex
F_{\rm rec}(D|F)
\le
{1\over d_D}+O(\epsilon_{\rm dec}),
```

because entanglement fidelity against the fixed maximally entangled
target is a linear overlap and changes by at most the trace-norm error,
up to the convention for the factor in trace distance.  Equivalently,
high-fidelity recovery from `F` would violate the decoupling hypothesis
by the standard information--disturbance duality.

After the Page/HP threshold, the sectorwise recovery theorem supplies a
decoder on the larger radiation algebra plus side information.  The
same physical record therefore has two access profiles:

```text
coarse label X:
    many small fragments distinguish it by Chernoff accumulation;

diary D inside fixed X:
    small fragments are decoupled from it, while the large delayed
    access algebra recovers it by Page/HP.
```

This proves the two-sector split.

### Lemma: resolution gap

Let the public-sector discrimination error for an `m`-record fragment
obey

```tex
P_{\rm pub}(m)\le C_m e^{-m\xi}+\eta_m,
```

with `C_m` subexponential, and let the within-sector leakage budget
satisfy

```tex
\eta_m\le m\gamma
```

over the record window of interest.  For fixed target errors
`delta_pub, delta_leak`, there exists a fragment size `m` such that

```text
P_pub(m) <= delta_pub,
eta_m <= delta_leak
```

whenever

```tex
{1\over \xi}\log {C_m\over \delta_{\rm pub}}
\lesssim
{\delta_{\rm leak}\over \gamma}.
```

In scaling form, the two-layer public/private window is nonempty when

```text
gamma << xi.
```

The finite-`m` prefactor is harmless at this level provided `C_m` is
subexponential in `m`: the public-resolution scale
`xi^{-1}\log(C_m/delta_pub)` is then self-consistent in the same
parametric window, up to subleading corrections.

Proof.  Public distinguishability requires
`m \gtrsim xi^{-1}\log(C_m/delta_pub)`.  Block leakage control requires
`m \lesssim delta_leak/gamma`.  A common `m` exists exactly when the
lower public-resolution scale lies below the upper leakage scale.  For
order-one error thresholds and subexponential `C_m`, this is the
signal-dominates-leakage condition `gamma << xi`.

For horizons, `xi` is set by the chosen no-hair binning and one-record
thermal/greybody statistics.  `gamma` is the per-record within-sector
record-signature or timing leakage.  The public no-hair/private diary
interpretation therefore requires no-hair information to accumulate
parametrically faster than microscopic leakage inside a fixed no-hair
sector.

## 11. Horizon specialization

The toy theorem becomes a horizon statement by choosing the sector label
to be a coarse no-hair bin and the microscopic label to be a diary or
microstate inside that bin.

```text
X       = coarse no-hair bin
          (mass/temperature, charge, angular momentum, or chemical
          potential bin);

H_X     = microscopic degeneracy space compatible with that bin;

D       = diary or arbitrary code subspace inside H_X;

F       = fragment of the emitted Hawking record;

sigma_X = one-record state/distribution of the resolvable radiation
          record in bin X.
```

For Schwarzschild, the simplest `X` is a mass or temperature bin.  The
one-record distribution is fixed by the density-of-states ratio:

```tex
p_X(\omega,\lambda)
\propto
\Gamma_\lambda(E_X,\omega)\,
\exp[S(E_X-\omega)-S(E_X)] ,
```

with greybody factor `Gamma_lambda` and with the record label
`(\omega,\lambda,t)` after time binning.  For charged or rotating
horizons, `X` includes the corresponding coarse conserved labels and
the Boltzmann factor is replaced by the usual chemical-potential form.

The between-sector Chernoff exponent is nonzero whenever the chosen
bins produce distinguishable emission statistics:

```text
xi(X,X') > 0
```

for the fragment resolution under consideration.  This is the public
layer: enough radiation fragments estimate the no-hair parameters
because those parameters control the thermal spectrum, greybody
weights, and long-range fields.

The within-sector leakage parameter is supplied by the record algebra
of the weak-emission note.  For eigenstates `nu,nu'` inside the same
coarse sector,

```tex
a_{mm'}(\nu)
=
\langle\nu|K_{m'}^\dagger K_m|\nu\rangle .
```

The sector privacy condition is precisely that these record signatures
are smooth inside the sector:

```text
sum over pre-threshold records of
Var_{code,j}[a_j] <= eta.
```

Equivalently, small fragments may learn the coarse bin `X`, but they do
not learn which microscopic state or diary inside `H_X` was present.

The private layer is then inherited from the long paper's decoupling
input.  Conditioned on fixed `X`, the diary reference `R_D` is decoupled
from subthreshold fragments:

```tex
\|\rho_{R_DF}-\rho_{R_D}\otimes\rho_F\|_1
\le
\epsilon_{\rm dec}(F|X).
```

After the Page transition, or in the Hayden-Preskill regime where the
decoder already holds the prior radiation record, the larger access
algebra recovers `D` with the usual decoupling budget.

Thus the horizon specialization of the two-sector theorem is:

```text
no-hair data:
    public, redundantly fragment-accessible, Chernoff accumulated;

diary/microstate data:
    private to small fragments inside fixed no-hair sector,
    recoverable only from delayed/global/Page-HP access.
```

This is the clean access-channel form of classical no-hair plus unitary
evaporation.  Classical no-hair data become pointer data because they
control the record distribution.  Microscopic data remain quantum
residue because the record algebra is smooth inside the no-hair sector
and decoupling controls recovery.

In the algebraic definitions of Section 16, the horizon profile reads:

```text
code algebra:
    A_code = direct sum_X B(H_X), with X a finite no-hair bin.

center-public:
    Hawking fragments distinguish X through their thermal/greybody
    record statistics, with Chernoff exponent set by the chosen binning
    and observation window.

block-private:
    after conditioning on X, subthreshold fragments are decoupled from
    the diary reference, provided within-sector record-signature and
    timing leakage are controlled.

anonymous compressed record:
    collective jumps give record-label anonymity because the resolvable
    Hawking record carries only frequency/channel/time-bin data at
    limited bandwidth; source-symmetry anonymity requires ETH/randomness
    or symmetry of the source-to-mode map.

block-recoverable:
    Page/HP radiation, island reconstruction, boundary access, or
    constraint-dressed access supplies the enlarged algebra, depending
    on the model and protocol.
```

### Relation to anonymity and saturation

The collective-jump result matters here.  The emitted Hawking record
contains frequency, channel, and time-bin data, but no microscopic
source address.  The public data are therefore no-hair data, not
which-cell labels.  This is record-label anonymity.  The stronger claim
that repeated record statistics do not reconstruct source addresses is
the source-symmetry anonymity condition.

Boundary saturation and latency add the horizon-strength condition.
An ordinary Darwinian environment can redundantly broadcast pointer
data; that alone does not make it horizon-like.  A horizon-class
channel also couples to an entropy-sized source algebra and makes
arbitrary diary information recoverable from the appropriate radiation
record at Page/HP latency.  The combined signature is therefore:

```text
Darwinian no-hair public records
+ anonymous compressed emission
+ saturated source-side access
+ delayed Page/HP microscopic recovery.
```

This is the current best standalone result from the constrained-access
turn.  The earlier operational-horizon and boundary-saturation papers
are useful here because they supplied the record algebra, collective
emission structure, and decoupling estimates; the direction of travel is
now result-first rather than paper-first.

### Validity window

The horizon specialization is a finite-resolution statement.

1. **Coarse bins, not exact continuous parameters.**  The public label
   `X` is a bin in mass/temperature, charge, angular momentum, or
   chemical potential space.  Exact real-valued parameters are estimated
   from records; they are not literally copied into each fragment.

2. **Quasi-static fragments.**  For Schwarzschild evaporation, a fragment
   used to infer a mass/temperature bin must lie in a window over which
   the energy changes negligibly compared with the bin width.  Equivalently,
   one can write the theorem stepwise with labels `X_j` along the
   evaporation trajectory.

3. **Microscopic privacy has two ingredients.**  Page/HP decoupling alone
   is not the whole privacy condition for small fragments.  Diagonal
   record-signature leakage and timing leakage must also be controlled
   inside the fixed coarse sector:

```text
within-sector record smoothness
+ subthreshold Page/HP decoupling
= microscopic privacy for small fragments.
```

4. **The theorem is about passive radiation records.**  Active mining,
   boundary-CFT access, or constraint-dressed/Gauss-law observables
   change the accessible algebra and therefore move the cut.

These restrictions are not defensive caveats; they are the operational
content of the result.  The theorem classifies what a specified access
channel makes public or private.

## 12. Algebraic public/private access theorem

The binary and horizon versions are instances of a finite-dimensional
algebra statement.  Let the code algebra be

```tex
\mathcal A_{\rm code}
=
\bigoplus_{x\in\mathcal X}\mathcal B(\mathcal H_x),
```

with center

```tex
Z(\mathcal A_{\rm code})
=
\bigoplus_{x\in\mathcal X}\mathbb C\,P_x .
```

The center is the candidate public algebra.  The noncommutative matrix
blocks `B(H_x)` are the private quantum residue inside each classical
sector.

Consider a passive record channel with fragments `F`.  For a fragment
made of `m` elementary records, assume:

1. **Center distinguishability.**  Each sector `x` has an ideal fragment
   record state `sigma_x^{(m)}`, and the family
   `{sigma_x^{(m)}}_{x in X}` has minimum pairwise Chernoff exponent

```tex
\xi_* =
\min_{x\ne y}
\left[
-{1\over m}\log
\inf_{0\le s\le 1}
\mathrm{Tr}\,
(\sigma_x^{(m)})^s(\sigma_y^{(m)})^{1-s}
\right]
>0 .
```

   For independent elementary records, `sigma_x^{(m)} =
   sigma_x^{\otimes m}` and `xi_*` is independent of `m`.

2. **Weak block smoothness for small fragments.**  For basis/code states
   inside a fixed sector, the diagonal record signatures or recorded
   outcome statistics vary only at cumulative scale `eta_m`.  Equivalently,
   the fragment record law can be compared with an ideal sector law up
   to weak within-sector leakage:

```tex
\|q_F^{x,a}-p_x^{(m)}\|_{\rm TV}\le \eta_m
\quad\text{or}\quad
|a_{mm'}(\nu)-a_{mm'}(\nu')|\le \eta_m .
```

   This is the physically useful record-signature condition: small
   fragments can see the central projection `P_x` while their diagonal
   statistics are nearly insensitive inside the block.  It is weaker
   than full decoupling of arbitrary superpositions.

3. **Block decoupling for diaries.**  If a diary/reference system `R_D`
   purifies a code state inside a fixed block `H_x`, then every
   subthreshold fragment satisfies

```tex
\|\rho_{R_DF}-\rho_{R_D}\otimes\rho_F\|_1
\le
\epsilon_{\rm dec}.
```

4. **Delayed correctability.**  For a larger or later access algebra
   `L`, there is a recovery map that reconstructs the diary/block
   algebra with error `epsilon_rec`.

Then the access profile is:

```text
center:
    public.  Fragments distinguish x with error
    P_err <= (|X|-1) C_m exp[-m xi_*] + O(|X| eta_m).

blocks:
    private to subthreshold fragments.  No fragment decoder recovers
    diary entanglement beyond the decoupling-error bound.

delayed algebra:
    recovers the block information with error epsilon_rec.
```

The public/private split requires a nonempty resolution window.  If
`\eta_m \sim m\gamma` and `\xi_*` is the per-record between-sector
Chernoff information, the useful regime is

```text
1/xi_* <~ m <~ 1/gamma,
equivalently gamma << xi_*.
```

This signal-dominates-leakage condition is part of the horizon
specialization for any chosen no-hair binning.

Proof.  The public-center statement is finite-alphabet hypothesis
testing.  For each candidate `x`, compare it against every `y != x`
using the binary test for the ideal pair.  The quantum Chernoff bound
gives an error `C_m exp[-m xi_*]` for each pair; a union bound gives
the factor `|X|-1`.  Replacing the ideal records by actual records
changes every test probability by the weak leakage budget, giving the
displayed additive `O(|X| eta_m)` term.  A sharper
multiple-hypothesis Chernoff theorem can improve constants, but the
exponent and access interpretation are already fixed by the pairwise
bound.

For the private-block statement, trace distance is monotone under any
decoder acting on `F`.  Thus a decoder applied to a decoupled
`rho_{R_DF}` produces a state close to
`rho_{R_D}\otimes\mathcal D(\rho_F)`, which is product across the
diary reference and the proposed output.  A product state cannot carry
the diary entanglement, so high-fidelity recovery from `F` would violate
the decoupling hypothesis, equivalently the standard
information-disturbance duality.  The delayed-correctability assumption
then says the private block information is hidden from small fragments,
not destroyed.

This theorem is the algebraic core of the constrained-access idea:

```text
the passive record broadcasts the center
and hides the noncommutative blocks until the access algebra is enlarged.
```

For horizons, the proposed effective center is the binned
no-hair/thermodynamic algebra, with microscopic degeneracy sectors as
blocks.  This is a dynamical claim about the passive record channel: it
requires the Hawking record to be block-scalar inside no-hair sectors
and distinguishable across them.  For ordinary measurement
environments, the analogous center is the pointer algebra.  For
gauge/gravity questions, changing dressings, edge modes, islands,
mining, or boundary access changes which algebra is available, and
therefore changes the public/private split.

## 14. Definitions: public center, private blocks, recoverable blocks

Let

```tex
\mathcal A_{\rm code}
=
\bigoplus_{x\in\mathcal X}\mathcal B(\mathcal H_x)
```

and let `F` denote a fragment of the passive record.

### Center-public at scale `m`

The center is `(delta,m)`-public if every central sector label `x` can be
identified from any allowed fragment of `m` elementary records with
error at most `delta`, uniformly over states inside the block:

```tex
\sup_{\rho_x\in\mathcal S(\mathcal H_x)}
P_{\rm err}(x|F;\rho_x)
\le
\delta .
```

For redundancy, require many disjoint fragments `F_i` of size `m` to
satisfy the same bound.  This is the algebraic form of Darwinian
objectivity: the center is not merely accessible somewhere; it is
accessible repeatedly from many small records.

### Block-private at scale `m`

The blocks are `(epsilon,m)`-private if, after conditioning on a fixed
sector `x`, every allowed fragment of `m` elementary records is decoupled
from the reference `R_D` purifying any diary/code state inside
`\mathcal H_x`:

```tex
\|\rho_{R_DF}-\rho_{R_D}\otimes\rho_F\|_1
\le
\epsilon .
```

Equivalently, all measurements on `F` have negligible distinguishing
power for generic noncommuting block information, and every decoder on
`F` has entanglement-fidelity error controlled by `epsilon`.

### Block-recoverable from an enlarged algebra

The block algebra is `(epsilon,L)`-recoverable if an enlarged or delayed
access algebra `L` admits a recovery channel `\mathcal R_L` whose
entanglement fidelity for the diary/code state inside each fixed sector
is at least `1-epsilon`.

```tex
F_e\!\left(
(\mathrm{id}_{R_D}\otimes\mathcal R_L)(\rho_{R_DL}),
|\Phi\rangle_{R_DD}
\right)
\ge
1-\epsilon .
```

This definition separates privacy from loss.  A private block is hidden
from small fragments; a recoverable private block is still available
from the correct larger algebra.

### Anonymous compressed passive record

Record-label anonymity means the elementary passive record labels do
not include microscopic source addresses inside `\mathcal H_x`.
Source-symmetry anonymity means the record statistics do not reconstruct
those addresses over time.  In the Schwarzschild case, collective jumps
give record-label anonymity because the outgoing mode carries
frequency/channel/time-bin data but no emitter index.  Source-symmetry
anonymity also requires the usual ETH/randomness, permutation symmetry,
or typicality condition on the source-to-mode map.

The passive record is compressed when the number of resolvable record
degrees per coherence time is parametrically smaller than the
source-side access participation.  For one-wavelength Schwarzschild
emission, `\lambda\sim R` gives the one-thermal-cell compression.

### Source-side saturation

Let `W` be the Gram kernel of the physically specified source-side
jump/coupling operators that feed the passive access channel in the
relevant shell or code subspace, with normalization fixed by channel
strengths.  The source-side participation is

```tex
N_{\rm eff}(W)
=
{(\mathrm{Tr}\,W)^2\over \mathrm{Tr}\,W^2}.
```

The access channel is source-saturated in a finite sector of entropy
`S` when

```text
N_eff(W) = Theta(S)
```

under the comparable-channel strength condition appropriate to the
model.  It is invariant under strength-preserving unitary mixing of the
physical operator family, not under arbitrary reweighting.  This is a
source-algebra statement, not an instantaneous radiation-mode count; the
emitted coherence matrix can have much lower rank after the shared
outgoing modes compress the source operators.

### Lemma: source participation and emitted-mode compression

Let the physical source-side jump family be `{J_\alpha}` in the relevant
shell or code subspace, with Hilbert-Schmidt Gram kernel

```tex
W_{\alpha\beta}
=
\langle J_\alpha,J_\beta\rangle_{\rm shell}.
```

The source participation

```tex
N_{\rm eff}(W)={(\mathrm{Tr}\,W)^2\over \mathrm{Tr}\,W^2}
```

is invariant under a unitary change of physical jump basis
`J_\alpha -> \sum_\beta U_{\alpha\beta}J_\beta`, because
`W -> U W U^\dagger` preserves both `Tr W` and `Tr W^2`.  It is not
invariant under arbitrary reweighting of the jump operators; such
reweighting changes the physical channel strengths.

If the resolvable outgoing record modes are linear combinations of the
source jumps,

```tex
L_i=\sum_\alpha C_{i\alpha}J_\alpha,
```

then the emitted-mode coherence kernel is

```tex
\Gamma=C W C^\dagger.
```

Flux and HBT-style observables built from instantaneous outgoing modes
probe `\Gamma`, not `W` itself.  Therefore a many-dimensional
source-side access algebra can appear as a low-rank emitted radiation
coherence matrix when the map `C` compresses many source directions into
few resolvable outgoing modes.

This is the formal content of the flux obstruction:

```text
source-side saturation is a property of W;
instantaneous radiation statistics see C W C^\dagger.
```

For horizon-class channels the proposed saturation condition is
`N_eff(W)=Theta(S)` for the physical source jump family, while
one-wavelength Hawking emission may still have only order-one
instantaneous radiation-mode participation.

### Low-latency recovery

A channel has `(k,S,epsilon)` low-latency recovery if an arbitrary
`k`-qubit diary deposited into a typical sector becomes recoverable from
the allowed enlarged record algebra after

```text
O(k + log S + log(1/epsilon))
```

elementary emitted records, after whatever Page/HP side-information
condition is part of the protocol.  This is a record-count or access-
depth statement, not merely a coordinate-time delay.  It should be
tagged by the protocol class: passive radiation, active mining,
boundary reconstruction, or dressed/Gauss-law access.

### Complexity-disciplined recovery

Recovery is complexity-disciplined when the statement specifies the
decoder class:

```text
information-theoretic:
    a recovery channel exists;

efficient:
    the decoder is polynomial or otherwise feasible in the stated
    resources;

high-complexity:
    recovery exists but falls under Harlow-Hayden/Python's-lunch-type
    barriers, conditional on the hypotheses of those results.
```

This prevents algebraic recoverability from being mistaken for practical
decodability.

### Source-local versus nonlocal/dressed access

The passive access channel is source-local when each elementary record
operator is generated from bounded-size or geometrically local source
operators before internal routing/mixing.  Fast recovery in such a
model requires the diary information to reach the accessible operators.

The access channel is nonlocal or dressed when the allowed exterior
operator already has support on global source degrees of freedom, or
when gauge/gravitational constraints make the exterior algebra
nonfactorizing.  Then fast access can be supplied by the access algebra
itself rather than by internal mixing.  This is the finite-dimensional
analogue of the Gauss-law/holography-of-information branch.

These definitions make the theorem non-tautological.  The hypotheses
are operational statements about record states, decoupling, and recovery
maps; "public" and "private" are the resulting access properties.

## 17. Direction 1: naturalness of center broadcast

The algebraic theorem assumes the important structure:

```text
records distinguish the central label x,
records are smooth inside each block H_x.
```

The naturalness question is whether ordinary passive monitoring tends to
produce exactly that structure.

### Candidate lemma: block-scalar monitoring broadcasts the center

Let

```tex
\mathcal A_{\rm code}
=
\bigoplus_x \mathcal B(\mathcal H_x)
```

and suppose one elementary record is produced by an instrument whose
effects are approximately block-scalar:

```tex
E_m = K_m^\dagger K_m
=
\bigoplus_x p(m|x)\,I_{\mathcal H_x}
+
\Delta_m,
\qquad
\|\Pi_x\Delta_m\Pi_x\|\le \eta .
```

Assume the classical distributions `p(.|x)` are distinguishable between
sectors:

```text
xi_* = min_{x neq y} Chernoff(p(.|x),p(.|y)) > 0.
```

Then repeated passive records broadcast the center and are insensitive
to the blocks:

```text
center:
    m records identify x with error ~ exp[-m xi_*] + O(m eta);

blocks:
    record probabilities vary by at most O(m eta) among states inside
    fixed H_x.
```

This is essentially einselection/Darwinism in center-block language.  The
monitored observable is the center; the environment learns `x`; states
inside `H_x` remain invisible to this record up to the block-scalar
error.

### What this would explain

For horizons, the weak-emission record algebra supplies the same
structure if:

```text
between no-hair bins:
    Hawking/greybody record statistics differ;

inside a no-hair bin:
    diagonal record signatures are ETH-smooth.
```

Then no-hair-as-pointer-algebra is not an extra postulate.  It is the
center selected by the passive emission instrument.

### What this does not explain

This lemma does not give:

```text
source-side saturation;
anonymous source erasure;
logarithmic recovery latency;
fast routing/mixing;
complexity barriers;
constraint-dressed access.
```

It explains only the public-center part of the access profile.

### Promising aspect

This looks genuinely promising because the theorem is almost forced by
standard measurement theory:

```text
commuting/central data can be redundantly measured;
noncommuting block data cannot be redundantly broadcast;
block-scalar instruments do not see inside the blocks.
```

The black-hole contribution would be to identify the passive Hawking
instrument as approximately block-scalar inside no-hair sectors and
distinguishing across them.

### Proposition: block-scalar monitoring broadcasts the center

Let

```tex
\mathcal H=\bigoplus_{x\in\mathcal X}\mathcal H_x,
\qquad
\mathcal A_{\rm code}
=
\bigoplus_x\mathcal B(\mathcal H_x).
```

Consider a finite-outcome passive record instrument applied for `n`
steps.  Assume:

1. **Sector non-demolition.**  The record instrument does not move the
   state between sectors during the fragment:

```tex
K_m\Pi_x=\Pi_xK_m\Pi_x .
```

2. **Block-scalar effects.**  For every outcome `m`,

```tex
E_m\equiv K_m^\dagger K_m
=
\bigoplus_x p(m|x)I_{\mathcal H_x}
+
\Delta_m,
\qquad
\|\Pi_x\Delta_m\Pi_x\|\le \eta .
```

3. **Distinguishable central laws.**  The sector distributions
   `p(.|x)` have minimum pairwise Chernoff exponent

```tex
\xi_*=\min_{x\ne y}
-\log\inf_{0\le s\le 1}
\sum_m p(m|x)^s p(m|y)^{1-s}
>0 .
```

Let `q_x^{\rho}(\mathbf m)` be the actual distribution of an
`n`-outcome record string `\mathbf m=(m_1,\ldots,m_n)` starting from
any state `rho` supported in `H_x`, and let

```tex
p_x^{(n)}(\mathbf m)=\prod_{j=1}^n p(m_j|x)
```

be the ideal product distribution.  If the outcome alphabet has size
`r`, then

```tex
\|q_x^\rho-p_x^{(n)}\|_{\rm TV}
\le
{1\over2}nr\eta + O(n^2\eta^2)
```

for small `eta`.  Hence the sector label can be inferred from the
record with error

```tex
P_{\rm err}
\le
(|\mathcal X|-1)e^{-n\xi_*}
+
{1\over2}nr\eta
+
O(n^2\eta^2),
```

up to the usual finite-`n` Chernoff prefactor.  Meanwhile, for any two
states `rho,rho'` in the same block,

```tex
\|q_x^\rho-q_x^{\rho'}\|_{\rm TV}
\le
nr\eta+O(n^2\eta^2).
```

Thus the repeated passive record broadcasts the center while remaining
block-insensitive over the leakage window `n r eta << 1`.

This is the classical-record version of the lemma.  It controls the
statistics available in the recorded outcomes.  If the observer has
access to a quantum environmental fragment rather than only the outcome
register, the block-scalar hypothesis should be strengthened to a
channel statement:

```tex
\left\|
\mathcal E_F(\rho_x)-\sigma_x^{(n)}
\right\|_1
\le \eta_n
\quad\text{uniformly for }\rho_x\in\mathcal S(\mathcal H_x),
```

or to the corresponding completely-bounded/diamond-norm condition on
the fragment channel.  That stronger hypothesis is what the algebraic
public/private theorem uses for full block privacy.  The effect-level
lemma is still useful: it shows how ordinary passive monitoring makes
the center public at the level of observed records.

### Proof

For an input state `rho_x` supported in one block `H_x`, the probability
of one record outcome is

```tex
\Pr(m|x,\rho_x)
=
\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho_x E_m
=
p(m|x)+\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho_x\Delta_m .
```

Thus

```tex
|\Pr(m|x,\rho_x)-p(m|x)|\le \eta .
```

Since the instrument is sector non-demolition, after any history
`\mathbf h=(m_1,\ldots,m_{j-1})` the conditional state remains in
`H_x`.  Therefore every conditional one-step probability satisfies

```tex
|q(m_j|\mathbf h,x,\rho_x)-p(m_j|x)|\le \eta .
```

Equivalently, the conditional total-variation distance from the ideal
one-step law is bounded by

```tex
\|q(\cdot|\mathbf h,x,\rho_x)-p(\cdot|x)\|_{\rm TV}
\le {1\over2}r\eta .
```

The chain rule for total variation, or a step-by-step coupling of the
actual and ideal processes, gives

```tex
\|q_x^\rho-p_x^{(n)}\|_{\rm TV}
\le
1-\left(1-{1\over2}r\eta\right)^n
\le
{1\over2}nr\eta+O(n^2\eta^2).
```

The ideal product laws distinguish sectors by the Chernoff exponent
`\xi_*`.  A pairwise maximum-likelihood test plus a union bound over
wrong sectors gives

```tex
P_{\rm err}(x|\mathbf m)
\lesssim
(|\mathcal X|-1)e^{-n\xi_*}
+
{1\over2}nr\eta
+
O(n^2\eta^2),
```

again up to the standard finite-`n` Chernoff prefactor.

For block-insensitivity, apply the same comparison to two initial states
`rho,rho'` in the same sector:

```tex
\|q_x^\rho-q_x^{\rho'}\|_{\rm TV}
\le
\|q_x^\rho-p_x^{(n)}\|_{\rm TV}
+
\|q_x^{\rho'}-p_x^{(n)}\|_{\rm TV}
\le
nr\eta+O(n^2\eta^2).
```

Thus the record can learn the central sector exponentially well in `n`,
while its ability to distinguish block states grows only with the
within-sector leakage budget.

The quantum version replaces probability distributions by fragment
states `sigma_x` and the classical Chernoff exponent by the quantum
Chernoff exponent.  The logic is the same: sector distinguishability
accumulates across records, while block variation contributes only the
within-sector leakage budget.

### Stronger naturalness question

The lemma is a sufficient condition.  The more interesting question is
whether passive monitoring tends to become block-scalar after coarse
graining because noncentral record components are either unstable,
nonredundant, or forbidden by no-broadcasting.

Candidate route:

```text
redundant records select an approximately commuting algebra;
the maximal redundantly recorded algebra is the effective center;
noncommuting block information is pushed into nonredundant correlations.
```

If true, the center would not be chosen by hand.  It would be the
commuting algebra selected by redundant accessibility.

## 18. Direction 1b: records select the effective center

The block-scalar lemma still assumes the center/block split.  The
stronger access claim reverses the logic:

```text
the public center is the algebra selected by redundant records.
```

This is not a new theorem from scratch.  It is the constrained-access
translation of no-broadcasting and spectrum-broadcast/strong-Darwinism
results.

### Exact statement: broadcastable algebras are commutative

Suppose a channel broadcasts a family of states into two or more
independent fragments, in the sense that each fragment has the same
distinguishing power for the encoded alternatives.  The no-broadcasting
theorem says this is possible for a family of density matrices only when
the family commutes.  Equivalently, exact redundant public records can
copy classical information, not a noncommuting algebra.

Access translation:

```text
exactly redundant public information
=> an effectively commutative algebra
=> a candidate center.
```

This is the finite-dimensional reason the public layer should be a
center/pointer algebra.  Noncommuting block information can be present
globally, but it cannot be redundantly broadcast into many independent
small fragments without destroying the quantum structure.

### Spectrum-broadcast structure

The stronger Darwinism literature packages objective records as states
of the schematic form

```tex
\rho_{S E_1\cdots E_N}
=
\sum_x p_x\,|x\rangle\!\langle x|_S
\otimes \rho_{E_1}^{(x)}
\otimes \cdots
\otimes \rho_{E_N}^{(x)},
```

with the fragment states for different `x` distinguishable, ideally
orthogonal in each fragment.  In algebraic language, the redundant
records have selected the commutative algebra generated by the
projectors `|x><x|`.

For the center/block decomposition, replace `|x>` by the central
projection `P_x`:

```tex
\mathcal A_{\rm public}
\simeq
Z(\mathcal A_{\rm code})
=
\bigoplus_x \mathbb C P_x .
```

Everything not resolved by the redundant records is block information.
If later recoverable, it is private residue rather than lost
information.

### Approximate version needed here

The exact theorem is too sharp for physical records.  The useful
working claim is approximate:

```text
if many fragments redundantly and stably encode the same alternatives,
then the accessible public algebra is approximately commuting;
the unresolved degrees define approximate blocks.
```

This approximate statement should be treated as imported/standard in
spirit, with constants supplied by the relevant no-broadcasting or
spectrum-broadcast stability theorem if needed.  For the present
program, it justifies the direction:

```text
redundant accessibility -> effective center,
not assumed center -> redundant accessibility.
```

### Horizon reading

For horizons, this suggests a sharper no-hair statement:

```text
passive Hawking records redundantly select only the commuting
no-hair/thermodynamic algebra;
microscopic diary data remain in nonredundant block correlations.
```

The black-hole-specific work is then to identify the redundantly
selected algebra with binned no-hair data and to bound leakage of
block information by record-signature smoothness plus decoupling.

### Darwinian-to-horizon upgrade map

The result target is an upgrade map from ordinary public records to the
horizon-class access profile.  Each added property has a distinct job:

```text
redundant public center:
    selected classical/no-hair alternatives become objective records;

block privacy:
    noncommuting microscopic data are absent from small fragments;

anonymous compressed passive record:
    records carry no microscopic source address and have limited
    outgoing bandwidth per coherence time;

source-side saturation:
    the coupling/access algebra participates in order the full finite
    entropy, rather than only a small contact subspace;

fast routing or nonlocal access:
    arbitrary newly deposited information reaches the accessible algebra
    at horizon latency;

enlarged-algebra recovery:
    the private block information becomes recoverable after the
    Page/HP condition, or through islands/boundary/dressed access;

complexity discipline:
    algebraic recovery is tagged by the decoder class needed to realize
    it.
```

In this form, Quantum Darwinism supplies the first line.  The horizon
claim begins when the public-center layer is combined with private
block recovery, anonymous compression, source-side saturation, and a
specified latency/complexity law.

The public-center line is an import, not a new theorem of this program.
The relevant standard structure is:

```text
no-broadcasting:
    noncommuting families cannot be redundantly broadcast;

Quantum Darwinism / Brandao-Piani-Horodecki:
    observers accessing a system indirectly through fragments have
    access to classical information about one measurement, and many
    observers agree on that information;

strong Quantum Darwinism / SBS:
    objective records are equivalent, under the stated independence
    conditions, to spectrum broadcast structure.
```

Translated into the access algebra:

```text
many redundant stable records
=> effectively commuting public information
=> center selected by the record channel.
```

The remaining public-side work is quantitative stability: choose an
approximate objectivity criterion and import constants for how close the
selected algebra is to commuting.

This is the larger horizon-interface theorem shape:

```text
Darwinian public records
+ private-block decoupling
+ anonymous compressed source-saturated access
+ fast routing or nonlocal/dressed access
+ Page/HP recovery
= horizon-class constrained access profile.
```

The separation witnesses below test that each added clause is doing
real work.

### Access-latency classification theorem

The private-block recovery side now has a sharper theorem.  Consider a
finite-dimensional source with uniformly bounded finite-range dynamics
and an emitted record generated by source-local coupling near an access
region `X`.  If an arbitrary diary can be deposited in a region `Y`, then
the source-local reduced-record theorem gives

```text
finite LR velocity + source-local emission
=> emitted record channel near-constant before the Y -> X routing time.
```

Therefore a system with location-uniform fast recovery of arbitrary
deposits must realize one of two mechanisms:

```text
fast internal routing / scrambling:
    the diary reaches the source-local emission algebra in sub-power-law
    time, so the finite-velocity obstruction is absent for the relevant
    dynamics;

nonlocal or dressed access:
    the emitted, boundary, or dressed algebra is not source-local in the
    factorized source variables, so the access map itself reaches the
    private block globally.
```

This is not a complexity statement.  The obstruction is
information-theoretic: before the routing time the reduced record
channel is close to a constant channel, so no measurement or decoder on
the emitted record can recover the diary.

Mechanism dictionary:

```text
ordinary local reservoir:
    finite LR velocity + source-local contact -> power-law latency;

stretched horizon / fast scrambler / Hayden-Preskill:
    fast-routing branch, with recovery floor set by scrambling plus
    emitted-record budget, e.g. O(log S + k);

holography of information / Gauss-law dressing:
    dressed-access branch, where exterior observables are not
    source-local in a factorized bulk Hilbert-space description;

AdS boundary reconstruction:
    nonlocal/dressed from the bulk-source viewpoint, even if the
    boundary theory has its own local structure;

Rindler wedge:
    thermal wedge access without a finite-sector Page/HP recovery law,
    so it tests thermality rather than the full horizon-class profile.
```

The classification theorem is the private-information half of the
horizon-as-quantum/classical-interface picture.  It says why ordinary
local reservoirs do not become horizons merely by being thermal or
Darwinian: they lack both fast routing and dressed/nonlocal access.

### Canonical witness matrix

The canonical witness matrix is now in
`notes/constrained_access_review_memo.md`.  This theorem note keeps the
proof-relevant witnesses and separation statements only.  The matrix's
job is to show that the predicates are not synonyms.  The two
load-bearing checks are:

```text
saturated slow-router:
    source-side participation is extensive, but recovery latency is
    power-law because information must route through local dynamics;

nonlocal anonymous encoder:
    records are anonymous and can be fast, but only because the access
    operator is nonlocal/dressed rather than source-local.
```

### Candidate separation statements

1. **Public center does not imply block recovery.**

Witness: ordinary Quantum Darwinism.  Pointer data are public, while
generic quantum information has no Page/HP recovery law.

2. **Block recovery does not imply public center.**

Witness: erasure/QEC code with trivial center.  Authorized regions
recover quantum information, but no classical pointer/no-hair algebra is
redundantly broadcast unless one is added.

3. **Compression does not imply scrambling or fast recovery.**

Witness: tape emitter.  A single serial record can release all
information over time without fast routing.  Compression gives
serialization, not mixing.

4. **Recovery does not imply anonymity.**

Witness: tape emitter or detector array.  The record can identify the
source/time slot while still eventually releasing the state.

5. **Anonymity does not imply mixing.**

Witness target: a nonlocal anonymous encoder that reads global
collective functions directly.  It preserves anonymity by hiding source
addresses but moves the burden into a nonlocal emission/access map.

6. **Saturation does not imply logarithmic latency.**

Witness: saturated but slow-routing system.  All degrees couple in
principle, but a newly deposited diary reaches the coupling directions
only after a long internal time.

7. **Thermality does not imply finite-sector horizon access.**

Witness: Rindler wedge.  Modular/thermal behavior exists, but without a
finite unregulated horizon sector and Page/HP recovery law.

8. **Source-side saturation is not visible in instantaneous flux
moments.**

Witness: shared-mode Hawking emission.  HBT sees radiation-mode
participation, not source Gram-kernel participation.

### Promising aspect

This direction is valuable because it turns the program into a
classification rather than a slogan.  Each witness prevents a common
false inference:

```text
Darwinism -> horizon
QEC -> horizon
compression -> scrambling
thermality -> finite entropy
flux area -> source channels
anonymity -> mixing
```

The pre-sharp-turn work already found several witnesses.  The two
load-bearing witnesses have now been sharpened into:

```text
saturated but slow-routing system;
nonlocal anonymous encoder.
```

With those witnesses in place, the access-profile properties are
demonstrably independent enough to be worth treating separately.

The slow-router side is now supported by the source-local reduced-record
theorem below: locality makes the reduced record state nearly
diary-independent before the routing time, so high-fidelity recovery is
information-theoretically impossible in that window.  The theorem does
not rely on decoder complexity or on bounding the full generated record
algebra.

### Source-local record model

For the slow-router branch, use the following finite-dimensional model.
Let the source be a bounded-degree graph or lattice `\Lambda`, with
bounded local Hilbert dimension and finite-range, uniformly bounded
Hamiltonian

```tex
H_\Lambda=\sum_Z h_Z,
\qquad
\sup_x\sum_{Z\ni x}\|h_Z\|<\infty .
```

Let `X\subset\Lambda` be the access/contact region and `Y\subset\Lambda`
the region where a diary is deposited, with distance `L=d(X,Y)`.

The record system is an environment or field locally coupled to the
source only through `X`:

```tex
H_{\rm tot}(s)=H_\Lambda+H_R(s)+H_{\rm int}(s),
\qquad
\mathrm{supp}_\Lambda H_{\rm int}(s)\subset N_R(X).
```

The emitted record `R_t` is the part of the environment collected up to
time or record depth `t`.  The diary code is a finite-dimensional
local-unitary family supported in `Y`:

```tex
\rho_U = U_Y\rho_0 U_Y^\dagger,
\qquad U_Y\in\mathcal U(\mathcal H_Y).
```

The channel from the diary to the emitted record is

```tex
\mathcal N_t(\rho_D)
=
\mathrm{Tr}_{\neg R_t}\!\left[
W_t(\rho_D\otimes\rho_{\rm rest}\otimes |0\rangle\langle0|_R)W_t^\dagger
\right],
```

where `W_t` is generated by `H_tot(s)`.  Optional side information `Z`
may be included in the output if it is prepared before the diary is
inserted, dynamically frozen or separately evolved, and
diary-independent in the sense that the local diary operation on `Y`
does not change the initial reduced state on `Z`.  If `Z` already
contains the diary, its purifier, or a reference system that
distinguishes the diary operation at `t=0`, the recovery question is
trivial and outside this latency test.

This is the source-locality assumption.  The theorem below does not need
compression or anonymity.  Those are separate horizon-profile
predicates; locality of dynamics and source-locality of emission are
the latency inputs.

### Theorem: source-local reduced-record latency bound

In the source-local record model above, let `\mathcal C_t` be the
constant channel that maps every diary state to the record state produced
by a fixed reference diary preparation.  Then, for `t<L/v`,

```tex
\|\mathcal N_t-\mathcal C_t\|_\diamond
\le
\varepsilon_{\rm LR}(t),
\qquad
\varepsilon_{\rm LR}(t)
\le
C\,|X|\,t\,e^{-\mu(L-vt)} ,
```

with constants determined by the local dimensions, interaction range,
operator-norm bounds, and the chosen Lieb-Robinson estimate.  If
side information `Z` satisfying the diary-independence condition above
is supplied to the decoder, the same statement holds for
`\mathcal N_t^Z:D\to R_tZ`, with constants depending on the support and
preparation of `Z`.

Consequently every decoder on `R_t` satisfies

```tex
F_e(D|R_t)
\le
{1\over d_D}
+O(\varepsilon_{\rm LR}(t)).
```

High-fidelity recovery of a diary deposited distance `L` from the
source-local access region therefore requires

```text
t >= L/v - O(log(C|X|t/epsilon)).
```

The logarithm contains `t` only through a slowly varying prefactor; for
fixed error threshold this implicit fixed point shifts the ballistic
scale only logarithmically.

For a `d`-dimensional local reservoir with `S=Theta(|\Lambda|)` and
worst-case deposits at distance `L=Theta(S^{1/d})`, location-uniform
recovery latency is power-law:

```text
t_rec = Omega(S^{1/d})
```

up to logarithmic and geometry-dependent factors.

Proof sketch.  Use the standard reduced-state corollary of
Lieb-Robinson locality.  Discretize the record field, if necessary, as
environment ancillas coupled only near `X` during the interval
`[0,t]`.  A perturbation or diary unitary supported in `Y` can influence
the reduced state on the collected environment only through operators
whose backwards light cone reaches `Y`.  The LR estimate bounds that
influence by the spacetime boundary factor times
`e^{-\mu(L-vt)}`, giving

```tex
\| \mathcal N_t(\rho)-\mathcal N_t(\rho')\|_1
\le
\varepsilon_{\rm LR}(t)
```

uniformly over diary states, and in the ancilla-stable version over
diary-reference states.  This is a statement about the reduced record
state itself, so it already controls the supremum over all measurements
on `R_t`, including computationally unbounded decoders.  Lemma A below
then converts channel closeness to a constant channel into the
entanglement-fidelity recovery bound.

The path being bounded is the combined source-plus-environment path
`Y -> X -> R_t`.  Since `H_{\rm int}` has source support only near `X`,
there is no direct `Y -> R_t` shortcut; the source part of the influence
must first enter the LR light cone of `X`.

This theorem proves the saturated slow-router witness: extensive
source-side participation can coexist with power-law recovery latency.
Source-side saturation is an access-capacity statement; logarithmic
latency additionally requires fast routing/mixing or nonlocal/dressed
access.

Equivalently, the theorem gives the latency dichotomy:

```text
location-uniform sub-ballistic recovery of arbitrary deposits
=> no finite LR velocity / fast routing
   OR emission/access is not source-local.
```

The two branches have different floors.  The fast-routing branch still
pays the internal routing or scrambling time, such as `O(log S+k)` in a
Hayden-Preskill-type regime.  The nonlocal/dressed-access branch can
move the speed into the access map itself, with no source-local routing
wait.  The dichotomy only asserts the need for a sub-power-law escape
from finite-velocity source-local propagation.

Compression and anonymity are not used in this proof.  They remain part
of the horizon-class access profile, not part of the locality/latency
obstruction itself.

Scope.  The theorem is stated for uniformly bounded finite-range
dynamics.  Sufficiently fast power-law interactions should be handled by
the appropriate long-range LR estimate, with the recovery exponent
changed accordingly.  Couplings whose range or strength grows with the
system size belong to the fast-routing branch rather than to the
finite-velocity source-local class.

### Witness construction A: saturated but slow-routing

Goal: show that source-side saturation does not imply logarithmic
latency.

Construct a system with `S` qubits whose source-side emission algebra
contains `O(S)` comparably weighted local jump operators, so the
Gram-kernel participation is extensive:

```text
N_eff ~ S.
```

This is the source-participation lemma applied to `O(S)` normalized
local source jumps: `N_eff(W)=(Tr W)^2/Tr W^2=Theta(S)` when the
eigenvalues of `W` are comparable.

But let the internal dynamics be slow: geometrically local on a line or
`d`-dimensional lattice, or otherwise lacking rapid operator growth
into the emission algebra.  Suppose a diary is deposited in a region
whose operator has small overlap with the jump operators that dominate
the next `n` records.  Even though the full source algebra is extensive,
the diary operator must grow into the actually sampled coupling
directions before the record can carry it.  A Lieb-Robinson/operator-
growth bound gives

```text
latency >= distance / v,
```

so for deposits at distance `L ~ S^{1/d}` the latency is power-law.

This witness has:

```text
finite sector: yes
source-side saturation: yes, N_eff ~ S for the source Gram kernel
block recovery: eventual, if the readout is complete
log latency: no
reason: slow internal routing
```

The point is that saturation is an access-capacity statement, while
latency is a routing statement.  They coincide for horizon-class systems
only when mixing/routing is fast enough.

There are two versions worth keeping distinct:

```text
parallel high-bandwidth version:
    all cells are directly imageable; saturation is present but the
    record is not compressed/horizon-like.

compressed random-sampling version:
    the source algebra is extensive, but each record samples only a
    small anonymous slice; without fast mixing, arbitrary deposits wait
    for routing or sampling, giving long latency.
```

Both show that saturation alone does not imply the horizon recovery law.

### Witness construction B: nonlocal anonymous encoder

Goal: show that anonymity does not imply mixing, and that fast recovery
can hide in the emission/access map itself.

Let the source have `S` microscopic cells, and let a `k`-qubit diary be
inserted into an arbitrary cell or small region.  Define a one-step
record map by applying a dense nonlocal encoder `G` to the whole source
and emitting a small record subsystem `R`.  For instance, `G` may be a
Haar-random or unitary-design encoder on the full source sector, with
record size

```text
log dim R = k + O(log(1/epsilon))
```

in the usual decoupling/HP regime with the appropriate side information.

Make the construction anonymous by choosing the encoder ensemble to be
permutation-covariant over source cells:

```tex
G \mapsto G U_\pi^\dagger
```

only relabels the hidden microscopic source convention and does not put
the source address into the emitted record label.  Equivalently, one can
twirl the ensemble over cell permutations while keeping the decoder
informed of the public encoding convention.  The individual record
operators are not source-local: their Heisenberg pullbacks have support
on the full source sector.

The emitted record is anonymous in the source-label sense: no passive
label identifies the cell where the diary was deposited.  It can also be
fast, because the access map directly applies a global encoder before
emission.  But the speed did not come from internal local mixing; it was
inserted into a nonlocal emission/access map.

This witness has:

```text
anonymous passive record: yes
fast recovery: possible
source-local access: no
internal routing/mixing forced: no
```

This is the finite-dimensional analogue of the Gauss-law / holography of
information branch: the exterior algebra was never source-local in the
factorized sense.  It is not a counterexample to the routing theorem; it
is the second branch of the access-latency classification theorem.

The important distinction is between permutation covariance and literal
permutation invariance.  Literal invariance under all cell permutations
would erase too much information for arbitrary diary recovery.  The
witness needs the record labels to hide the source address while the
access operators remain dense enough to encode the diary globally.

### Alternatives theorem setup

The theorem is stated contrapositive-first because the proof engine is a
reduced-state Lieb-Robinson bound.

Model class.  The source is a bounded-degree graph or lattice with
uniformly bounded finite-range dynamics.  The emitted record is produced
by an environment/field coupling supported near an access region `X`.
A diary is deposited in a region `Y`, with `L=d(X,Y)`.  The source-local
condition is on the physical coupling that creates the record, not on a
chosen basis of later record observables.

Reduced-record input.  Locality gives, for the diary-to-record channel,

```tex
\|\mathcal N_t-\mathcal C_t\|_\diamond
\le
C|X|t\,e^{-\mu(L-vt)}
\qquad (t<L/v),
```

where `\mathcal C_t` is the constant channel producing the record for a
reference diary preparation.  This is a statement about the reduced
state on the record itself.  It therefore controls all measurements and
all decoders on the emitted record, including computationally unbounded
ones.

Conditional theorem.  In a geometrically local, source-local model,

```text
location-uniform fast recovery of arbitrary deposits
=> fast internal routing / no finite LR velocity.
```

Contrapositive:

```text
finite LR velocity + source-local emission
=> recovery latency is at least the source-to-access routing time.
```

Alternative branch.  If a model has location-uniform fast recovery
without fast internal routing, then the emitted-access map was not
source-local in this sense.  The speed resides in a nonlocal or
constraint-dressed access map, as in the finite-dimensional nonlocal
encoder witness and in the Gauss-law/holography-of-information branch.
Compression and anonymity are not hypotheses of this latency theorem;
they are additional predicates of the horizon-class profile.

#### Lemma A: constant-channel closeness gives recovery-latency bound

Let `\mathcal N_t:D\to R_t` be the channel from a diary system `D` to the
accessible record algebra collected up to record depth/time `t`.  Suppose
there is a constant channel `\mathcal C(\rho_D)=\tau_{R_t}` such that

```tex
\|\mathcal N_t-\mathcal C\|_\diamond\le \varepsilon(t).
```

Then no decoder acting on `R_t` can recover a `d_D`-dimensional diary
with high entanglement fidelity.  More explicitly, for every recovery
map `\mathcal R:R_t\to \widehat D`,

```tex
F_e\!\left(
(\mathrm{id}_{R_D}\otimes\mathcal R\mathcal N_t)(\Phi_{R_DD}),
\Phi_{R_D\widehat D}
\right)
\le
{1\over d_D}+O(\varepsilon(t)).
```

Proof.  Composing with a decoder cannot increase diamond distance, so
`\mathcal R\mathcal N_t` is `\varepsilon(t)`-close to the constant
channel `\mathcal R\mathcal C`.  Applied to half of a maximally
entangled state, the constant channel produces
`\rho_{R_D}\otimes\mathcal R(\tau)`, a product state across
`R_D|\widehat D`.  Its fidelity with `\Phi_{R_D\widehat D}` is at most
`1/d_D`.  Entanglement fidelity is a linear overlap with the target
maximally entangled state, so trace-norm closeness changes it by at most
`O(\varepsilon(t))`, up to the convention for the factor in the trace
distance.

#### Lemma B: source-local emission gives constant-channel closeness

Under the source-local record model and finite-range dynamics,

```tex
\|\mathcal N_t-\mathcal C_t\|_\diamond
\le
\varepsilon_{\rm LR}(t)
\le
C|X|t\,e^{-\mu(L-vt)}
```

for `t<L/v`.

Proof sketch.  This is the reduced-state form of the Lieb-Robinson
bound.  Model the emitted record as environment degrees of freedom
coupled to the source only near `X`.  A change in the diary preparation
supported in `Y` can affect the reduced state of the collected record
only through the LR light cone connecting `Y` to the interaction region
`X` during the interval `[0,t]`.  The influence is bounded by the
spacetime boundary factor `|X|t` times the usual exponential tail.
Because the conclusion is a trace/diamond-norm bound on the reduced
record channel, it already applies to arbitrary record measurements and
decoders; no bounded-degree observable restriction is needed.

Together, Lemma A and Lemma B prove the slow-router witness and supply
the contrapositive engine for the access-latency classification theorem:

```text
finite LR velocity + source-local emission
=> record channel near-constant before the routing time
=> no high-fidelity recovery before the routing time.
```

Consequently, location-uniform fast recovery of arbitrary deposits must
come from one of two places:

```text
fast internal routing / no finite LR velocity,
or
nonlocal, non-source-local, or constraint-dressed access.
```

### Updated separation lesson

The clean implication is not

```text
anonymity + compression -> mixing.
```

The clean implication is:

```text
source-local emission + finite LR velocity
-> no location-uniform fast recovery of arbitrary deposits.
```

Equivalently:

```text
location-uniform fast recovery of arbitrary deposits
-> fast routing/mixing OR nonlocal/dressed access.
```

Compression and anonymity still matter for the horizon-class profile,
but the latency dichotomy itself is stronger: it does not use them.
