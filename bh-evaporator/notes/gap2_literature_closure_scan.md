# Gap 2 Literature Closure Scan

## Question

Can existing literature close the remaining deterministic mixer gap?

The reduced target is:

```text
sectorwise cumulative second-moment decoupling
for the composed evaporation map,
generated by a fixed homogeneous sparse Cayley-expander Hamiltonian.
```

## Short Answer

No single paper appears to close this exact target.

The literature does close several nearby targets:

```text
random circuits -> second-moment decoupling,
random circuits on arbitrary architectures -> approximate designs,
sparse/expander graphs -> fast operator growth/scrambling evidence,
symmetry-aware Hayden-Preskill -> charge-sector leakage/remnants,
Hamiltonian-design ensembles -> abstract Hamiltonian design generation.
```

The missing exact link remains:

```text
one fixed homogeneous Cayley-expander Hamiltonian
    -> sectorwise two-replica gap / cumulative second-moment contraction.
```

## Closest Useful Literature

### Brown-Fawzi: decoupling with random quantum circuits

Brown and Fawzi prove decoupling for random quantum circuits with
`O(n log^2 n)` gates and polylogarithmic depth when broad interactions are
allowed.  This is the closest theorem to our information-flow need, because it
is explicitly a decoupling theorem rather than only an OTOC/operator-spreading
statement.

Use:

```text
second-moment decoupling technology,
contraction/iterate intuition,
Page-like information hiding from random circuits.
```

Does not supply:

```text
single deterministic Cayley-expander Hamiltonian.
```

Reference:

- Brown and Fawzi, "Decoupling with random quantum circuits,"
  arXiv:1307.0632.

### Brandao-Harrow-Horodecki and later design-gap papers

Brandao, Harrow, and Horodecki prove local random quantum circuits form
approximate designs, using moment operators and spectral gaps of associated
local Hamiltonians.  Later work improves these spectral gap bounds.

Use:

```text
the two-replica/moment-operator language,
the connection between approximate designs and local Hamiltonian gaps.
```

Does not supply:

```text
deterministic fixed Hamiltonian dynamics.
```

References:

- Brandao, Harrow, Horodecki, "Local random quantum circuits are approximate
  polynomial-designs," arXiv:1208.0692.
- Haferkamp, "Random quantum circuits are approximate unitary t-designs in
  depth O(n t^{5+o(1)})," arXiv:2203.16571.

### Mittal-Hunter-Jones: arbitrary circuit architectures

Mittal and Hunter-Jones extend approximate-design results to random quantum
circuits on arbitrary connected graphs.  For `k <= 4` they obtain design
generation on certain bounded-degree graphs with circuit size controlled by
graph and Hamiltonian spectral-gap methods.

This is especially relevant because a Cayley expander is a graph architecture.

Use:

```text
random two-qudit gates on a Cayley/expander architecture should have
second-moment mixing controlled by graph/moment gaps.
```

Does not supply:

```text
time-independent homogeneous Hamiltonian on that graph.
```

Reference:

- Mittal and Hunter-Jones, "Local random quantum circuits form approximate
  designs on arbitrary architectures," arXiv:2310.19355.

### Sparse/expander fast scrambling

Barbon-Magan propose local quantum systems on expander graphs as fast
scramblers.  Bentsen-Gu-Lucas connect sparse graph structure to fast operator
growth and scrambling.

Use:

```text
physical motivation for expander geometry,
operator-spreading support for O(log N) scrambling.
```

Does not supply:

```text
sectorwise decoupling theorem for our fixed K_N.
```

References:

- Barbon and Magan, "Fast Scramblers, Horizons and Expander Graphs,"
  arXiv:1204.6435.
- Bentsen, Gu, Lucas, "Fast scrambling on sparse graphs," arXiv:1805.08215.

### Symmetry-aware Hayden-Preskill / scrambling

Nakata, Wakakuwa, and Koashi study Hayden-Preskill with symmetry and show
that conservation laws can cause leakage delays and information remnants.
This supports our sectorwise phrasing and prevents overclaiming charge-blind
decoupling.

Use:

```text
exact symmetry charges must be tracked,
decoupling should be sectorwise or charge records must be included in R.
```

Does not supply:

```text
Cayley group charge-emission lemma for our interaction.
```

Reference:

- Nakata, Wakakuwa, Koashi, "Black holes as clouded mirrors: the
  Hayden-Preskill protocol with symmetry," arXiv:2007.00895.

### Hamiltonian-design ensemble work

Recent Hamiltonian-design papers show that Hamiltonian dynamics can generate
unitary designs under ensemble, temporal-ensemble, polylog-local, or quench
assumptions.  They help justify the abstract/theorem-backed mixer but do not
solve the fixed deterministic sparse Hamiltonian problem.

Use:

```text
the abstract mixer can be Hamiltonian-realized under broader assumptions.
```

Does not supply:

```text
one explicit homogeneous sparse K_N.
```

References:

- Nakata, Hirche, Koashi, Winter, PRX 2017.
- Cui, Schuster, Mao, Huang, Brandao, arXiv:2510.08434.
- Zhou, Lowenberg, Sonner, "Realizing Unitary k-designs with a Single Quench,"
  2026.

## Best Closure Prospects

### Near-term theorem-backed closure

Use random circuits or Brownian/random Hamiltonian dynamics on an expander
architecture:

```text
RQC/Brownian expander dynamics
    -> second-moment decoupling by known methods
    -> fixed graph and sparse interactions, but random gates/noise/ensemble.
```

This would close a strong nearby result, not the deterministic Cayley result.

### Deterministic target

Try to adapt the random-circuit moment-operator proof to a single homogeneous
Cayley Hamiltonian:

```text
derive the two-replica generator for K_N,
decompose by Cayley symmetry sectors,
prove a gap/contraction bound using the expander gap.
```

This is original work.  The literature provides the method and target, but not
the result.

## Assessment

There is a real chance to make the gap substantially smaller by importing
random-circuit/second-moment machinery.  There is not, based on this scan, an
existing theorem that directly closes the deterministic homogeneous
Cayley-expander Hamiltonian.

The honest statement is:

```text
The remaining deterministic gap is not vague.  It is the absence of a
sectorwise two-replica gap/contraction theorem for a fixed homogeneous
Cayley-expander Hamiltonian.  Existing random-circuit and symmetry-aware
scrambling literature gives the right toolkit and closes nearby stochastic
versions.
```
