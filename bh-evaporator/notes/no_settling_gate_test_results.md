# No-Settling Gate Test Results

## Purpose

Test the two non-settling routes identified from the reviewer desiderata:

```text
1. stripped matrix clump;
2. connector-mode softness.
```

These are not final evaporator simulations. They are kill tests for whether the
routes contain the missing natural mechanisms.

## Gate 1: Stripped Matrix Clump

Script:

```text
sim/matrix_clump_gate_scan.py
```

This improves the earlier classical matrix-clump diagnostic by requiring an
escape event to satisfy both:

```text
1. large radial separation;
2. weakened off-diagonal connection to the remaining clump.
```

Then it checks the post-event signature:

```text
remaining clump energy decreases;
remaining clump temperature proxy increases.
```

### Small N=4, D=2 scan

Command:

```text
python sim/matrix_clump_gate_scan.py
```

Output:

```text
sim/data/matrix_clump_gate_scan.csv
```

Result:

```text
seed  pass   ratio_max  dE_cl   dT_cl

401   mixed  3.791      -0.505  -0.643
402   mixed  4.778      -0.877  -0.392

```

Summary:

```text
pass = 0/6

```

The model generates separation-like events, but the post-event heating
signature is absent. Most candidate events lose clump energy and cool. One
event heats, but it also gains clump energy.

No stricter escape candidates appear.

### Matrix-Clump Verdict

The stripped classical matrix clump does not currently pass the gate.

What survives:

```text
matrix variables give a non-imposed notion of separation;
small systems can show radial separation events.
```

What fails:

```text
robust post-escape heating of the remaining clump.
```

This does not rule out BFSS-like or more structured matrix models. It does say
that the stripped bosonic matrix Hamiltonian is not an immediate no-settling
evaporator.

## Gate 2: Connector Softness

Script:

```text
sim/connector_softness_gate.py
```

The connector model needs microscopic hard emissions with energy scale

```text
omega_typ ~ T_N ~ 1/N.
```

Pairwise connector counting gives

```text
S ~ N^2,
```

but the Hamiltonian also needs soft connector transitions. This gate tests
whether simple incidence-local connector spectra supply them.

Models tested:

```text
independent fixed-gap connectors;
unscaled line graph L(K_N) connector hopping;
Kac-normalized line graph connector hopping, 1/N;
line graph hopping scaled by 1/N^2;
Kac-normalized complete-graph site hopping.
```

Here `L(K_N)` is the line graph whose vertices are connectors of the complete
graph and whose edges join connectors sharing one endpoint.

### Strict target eta = 1

Command:

```text
python sim/connector_softness_gate.py
```

Output:

```text
sim/data/connector_softness_gate_summary.csv
```

Result at `N=128`:

```text
model                              pass  gap power  gap       target   soft frac
independent_fixed_gap              no    0.000      1         0.0078   0.000
line_graph_unscaled                no    1.000      128       0.0078   0.000
line_graph_kac_1_over_N            no    0.000      1         0.0078   0.000
line_graph_soft_by_hand_1_over_N2  no   -1.000      0.0078    0.0078   0.016
site_complete_kac_1_over_N         no    0.000      1         0.0078   0.000
```

The natural line-graph choices do not produce `1/N` soft transitions. The only
`1/N` gap appears when the Hamiltonian is scaled by `1/N^2`, which inserts the
desired softness.

### Constant-factor check eta = 2

Command:

```text
python sim/connector_softness_gate.py --target-eta 2 \
  --summary-csv sim/data/connector_softness_gate_summary_eta2.csv \
  --rows-csv sim/data/connector_softness_gate_rows_eta2.csv
```

Result:

```text
line_graph_soft_by_hand_1_over_N2 passes;
all natural/unscaled/Kac-normalized variants fail.
```

This confirms the issue. The connector spectrum can be made soft by choosing a
stronger N-dependent scaling, but that is not generated by the simple
incidence-local Hamiltonian.

### Connector Verdict

The connector model passes the entropy-counting gate but fails the softness
gate in its simplest complete-graph incidence Hamiltonian forms.

What survives:

```text
S ~ N^2 from pairwise connectors;
T ~ 1/N at counting level.
```

What fails:

```text
natural microscopic emission scale omega ~ 1/N.
```

Pairwise connector counting alone is not enough.

## Gate 2b: Collective Critical Connector Spectra

The simple connector failure was not the end of the branch. A broader spectral
search was run:

```text
sim/collective_connector_softness_search.py
notes/collective_connector_softness_results.md
```

This tests connector sectors with `O(N^2)` collective critical modes rather
than complete-graph incidence modes.

Result:

```text
model                         soft gate  power gate  <omega>/T  P_local power
complete_line_graph_kac       no         no          128.000    -49.420
connector_grid_NxN_crit       yes        yes         1.110      -1.816
connector_rect_exactish_crit  yes        yes         1.238      -1.738
connector_ring_crit           yes        yes         0.503      -1.512
connector_ring_linear_crit    yes        yes         1.050      -1.967
powerlaw_alpha1               yes        yes         1.008      -1.999
powerlaw_alpha2               yes        no          0.500      -1.500
```

Interpretation:

```text
complete-graph connector incidence is too hard;
critical collective connector spectra can produce omega ~ T ~ 1/N;
with local spectral-weight normalization they can also give P ~ N^-2.
```

So the connector route is alive again, but only if the connector sector has a
critical/gapless collective spectrum.

## Gate 2c: Critical Connector Heating

The next gate was:

```text
after the core emits energy of order T and the active connector sector changes
from N to N-1, does the remaining core heat?
```

Scripts:

```text
sim/critical_connector_heating_gate.py
sim/critical_connector_heating_robustness.py
notes/critical_connector_heating_results.md
```

Robustness scan:

```text
eta = 0.5, 1.0, 2.0
emitted energy = kappa T, with kappa = 0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0
energy proxies = Bose thermal energy and classical active-mode cutoff
N = 8 to 128
```

Result:

```text
model                         status    strict pass  mean heating  min ratio at Nmax
connector_grid_NxN_crit       fails     0.04         0.18          0.8348
connector_rect_exactish_crit  fails     0.00         0.06          0.6655
connector_ring_crit           fragile   0.42         0.93          1.0034
connector_ring_linear_crit    fails     0.21         0.46          0.9435
powerlaw_alpha1               fragile   0.75         0.81          0.9884
powerlaw_alpha2               robust    1.00         1.00          1.0083
```

For `N = 32` to `128`, restricted to the survivors:

```text
model                 status    strict pass  mean heating  min ratio at Nmax
connector_ring_crit         mostly    0.88         0.97          1.0034
connector_ring_linear_crit  fails     0.21         0.46          0.9435
powerlaw_alpha1             fragile   0.75         0.81          0.9884
powerlaw_alpha2             robust    1.00         1.00          1.0083
```

Interpretation:

```text
generic criticality is not enough;
grid-like critical connector spectra fail the heating gate;
linear one-dimensional critical connector spectra fail the heating gate;
quadratic one-dimensional critical connector spectra are the live branch;
alpha=2-like spectra heat robustly but have a power-scaling caveat;
alpha=1-like spectra have good power scaling but fragile heating.
```

## Combined Interpretation

Both no-settling routes remain conceptually useful. The matrix route has not
passed its first dynamical gate. The connector route fails in its simplest
incidence form, passes a broader critical-spectrum gate, and then narrows under
the heating gate.

```text
matrix clump:
  natural separation, no robust heating yet;

connector model:
  natural area-like counting, soft scale only with critical collective spectra,
  heating only in quadratic one-dimensional or alpha=2-like critical spectra.
```

This is sharper than the previous status. The missing mechanism is not merely
"area entropy" or "radiation split." It is:

```text
area-like relational degrees that also have a dynamical soft spectrum and
post-emission heating.
```

## Consequence For The Search

The next no-settling model cannot be a plain complete-graph connector spectrum
or a stripped commutator-squared matrix model.

It needs at least one additional physical ingredient:

```text
1. a collective or critical mechanism producing soft relational modes;
2. a stabilizing matrix-clump mechanism that still permits escape;
3. a finite relational Hamiltonian whose connector gaps depend on separation;
4. a known long-range/nonadditive model with both convex DOS and compatible
   emission operators.
```

The current best next idea is a quadratic critical connector ring/clump. It borrows the
connector counting mechanism and adds a more specific spectral ingredient:

```text
O(N^2) active relational modes arranged into a one-dimensional quadratic
critical band.
```

The next hard gate is to find or build a microscopic Hamiltonian whose active
connector sector naturally has that band, then measure state count, energy
scaling, emission scale, heating, power, and information flow in the same run.
