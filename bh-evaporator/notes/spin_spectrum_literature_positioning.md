# Spin-Spectrum Autonomous Evaporator: Literature Positioning

## Question

How should the current autonomous spin-spectrum multiband model be read relative to existing black-hole evaporation toy models and many-body thermalization literature?

Current model:

```text
H_total =
  direct_sum_L [4 sigma L + H_spin(L)]
  + H_rad_multiband
  + H_erosion
```

with shell erosion defined in the site basis and transformed into the spin-energy eigenbasis.

## Closest Literature Buckets

### Qubit evaporation models

Examples:

```text
Avery, "Qubit Models of Black Hole Evaporation", arXiv:1109.2911.
Osuga and Page, "Qubit Transport Model for Unitary Black Hole Evaporation
without Firewalls", arXiv:1607.04642.
Broda, "Causal unitary qubit model of black hole evaporation", arXiv:2104.14901.
```

What they cover well:

```text
unitarity;
information transfer;
Page-like entanglement;
qubit bookkeeping;
firewall/information-flow constraints.
```

What they usually do not try to cover quantitatively:

```text
negative heat capacity;
accelerating evaporation from a core equation of state;
hard spectral thermality from an autonomous Hamiltonian;
emission rates generated by density of states.
```

Osuga and Page explicitly describe their model as qualitatively Hawking-like and too crude for the detailed spectrum. That is directly relevant: our new work is pushing exactly on the part qubit transport models usually bracket away.

### Hayden-Preskill and fast scrambling

Examples:

```text
Hayden and Preskill, "Black holes as mirrors", arXiv:0708.4025.
Sekino and Susskind, "Fast Scramblers", arXiv:0808.2096.
```

What they say:

```text
If the black-hole interior is unitary and rapidly mixing, information thrown in
after the Page time can come out quickly in the radiation.
Black holes are expected to be among the fastest scramblers.
```

Relevance to our model:

```text
scrambling is needed for information-flow diagnostics;
scrambling is not enough to guarantee hard spectral thermality.
```

Our multiband failure fits this distinction. The model can emit, shrink, and entangle without producing the correct hard spectrum.

### ETH and many-body thermalization

Example:

```text
Deutsch, "Eigenstate Thermalization Hypothesis", arXiv:1805.01616.
```

What ETH contributes:

```text
individual many-body eigenstates can look thermal for simple observables;
chaotic systems can justify microcanonical/canonical typicality;
operator matrix elements in the energy basis have structured, thermal-looking
statistics.
```

Relevance to our model:

```text
the spin-spectrum version is aligned with ETH reasoning;
the shell-removal operator should be treated like a physical operator whose
matrix elements sample many-body states;
the integrable/no-transverse-field control is important.
```

Our results are consistent with this expectation at small size:

```text
chaotic-ish spin-spectrum run:
  TV ~ 0.365;

integrable control:
  TV ~ 0.420;
```

The difference is not decisive, but it points in the expected direction.

### Microcanonical black-hole radiation

Example:

```text
Casadio and Harms, "Microcanonical Description of (Micro) Black Holes",
Entropy 13, 502 (2011).
```

Relevant idea:

```text
for finite-energy black-hole radiation, the canonical thermal description can
be replaced by a microcanonical description based on energy conservation and
black-hole density of states.
```

Why this matters:

```text
the hard spectrum should be controlled by state-count ratios,
roughly exp[S(E - omega) - S(E)],
rather than by assigning a Boltzmann factor to the coupling.
```

This is exactly the issue exposed by our multiband runs. Flat couplings plus an artificial ladder gave too-hard emission. A spin-derived density of states improved the spectrum but did not solve it at current size.

### Page-like open-system dynamics

Local note:

```text
notes/glatthard_positioning.md
```

Core lesson from Glatthard-style open-system work:

```text
Page-like entropy dynamics is generic in ordinary relaxing systems.
```

Impact on our project:

```text
Page-like behavior alone is not enough.
The differentiator is Page-like information flow tied to negative heat
capacity and autonomous emission.
```

## What The Literature Would Probably Say About Our Current Model

### Strong points

The model has a clearer physical spine than a pure qubit transport model:

```text
finite Hilbert space;
time-independent Hamiltonian;
spin-derived internal spectrum;
site-basis shell-removal operator;
multiband outgoing radiation;
energy conservation;
measured emission spectrum;
measured entanglement diagnostics.
```

The site-basis shell-removal construction is important. It avoids random eigenstate-to-eigenstate transitions and gives the erosion operator a geometric register meaning.

The current result addresses a gap that qubit/Page models often leave open:

```text
whether hard-radiation spectral thermality can emerge in the same autonomous
unitary model that handles shrinkage and information flow.
```

### Weak points

The model is still small:

```text
L0 = 3;
two-shell evaporation;
five radiation bands;
short waveguide.
```

The spin spectrum is rescaled:

```text
H_spin(L) eigenvalues are centered and width-controlled.
```

That is less artificial than assigned bin degeneracies, but still a modeling choice.

The hard spectrum remains too hard:

```text
best spin-spectrum TV ~ 0.246;
high omega bands remain overweighted.
```

So the model cannot yet claim Hawking-like spectral thermality.

The entropy split is sector-aware:

```text
core label = (sector, core index)
radiation label = (sector, shell records, hard chain)
```

This is natural for a direct-sum staged Hamiltonian, but a fixed tensor-product embedding would be cleaner.

### Likely reviewer pressure points

```text
1. Is H_spin(L) chosen because it is natural, or because it gives the desired
   density of states?

2. Does rescaling the spin spectrum preserve the claimed mass law?

3. Is the thermal target pure Boltzmann or should it include phase-space /
   greybody factors?

4. Is the shell-removal operator local enough after transforming to the
   energy basis?

5. Are the results stable under seeds, spin parameters, and larger waveguides?

6. Can the Page-like entropy diagnostics be separated from sector-label
   bookkeeping?
```

## Novelty Assessment

The individual ingredients are standard:

```text
qubit evaporation models;
Page-like random-unitary information flow;
fast scrambling;
ETH;
microcanonical emission ratios;
finite spin Hamiltonians;
waveguide radiation models.
```

The combination appears less standard:

```text
autonomous non-gravitational evaporator
with negative-heat-capacity droplet equation of state,
spin-derived internal spectrum,
site-basis shell removal,
multiband hard radiation,
and measured spectral / entropy diagnostics.
```

The present result is not yet a finished result because hard spectral thermality remains incomplete. But the failure is informative and literature-aligned:

```text
autonomous evaporation and Page-like information flow are easier than
Hawking-like hard spectral thermality.
```

## Best Current Positioning

The safest claim:

```text
We construct an autonomous non-gravitational quantum evaporator that combines
black-hole-like thermodynamic state counting with unitary shell radiation.
When the hard-radiation sector is made multiband, a spin-derived many-body
spectrum improves the emitted spectrum relative to an artificial internal
ladder, but finite-size emission remains too hard. This isolates hard spectral
thermality as the main remaining non-gravitational bottleneck.
```

This is stronger than a pure Page-curve toy model and more honest than claiming the full black-hole phenomenology package is already reproduced.

## Next Literature-Aware Step

The next test should address the main reviewer pressure point:

```text
Is the remaining spectral hardness a finite-size / density-of-states issue,
or is it telling us that a simple spin droplet cannot generate Hawking-like
spectral thermality?
```

Best practical options:

```text
1. run seed/parameter robustness for the spin-spectrum model;
2. compare pure Boltzmann target to omega^p exp(-omega/T) targets;
3. compute the actual spin-sector density-of-states slopes and see whether
   the emitted spectrum follows them;
4. attempt a slightly larger effective density-of-states model only after
   the spin-spectrum behavior is understood.
```
