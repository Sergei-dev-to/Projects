# DSSYK detector-backreaction resource audit

Date: 2026-07-20

Status: **complete negative feasibility gate; WP2 remains closed.** The
bounded primary-source pass finds a constraint-preserving DSSYK worldline
contact, standard machinery that can turn a chosen contact into an
instrument, an observer-energy cap, and a recent Euclidean metric-
susceptibility bound. It does not find a construction that derives these
ingredients jointly as a Lorentzian sequential observer process with a
physical bound on diary-sensitive detector action, contact count, or retained
memory.

The strongest conclusion is an analytic nonimplication, not merely an absence
claim: the observer-energy cap currently used in DSSYK does not bound the
normalization or integrated action of the model detector contact. The separate
ingredients therefore cannot be composed into the missing WP2 resource law
without additional physics.

## 1. Bounded question and decision rule

The follow-up question was:

> Does existing de Sitter observer or detector-backreaction literature derive
> both a constraint-preserving worldline interaction and a bound on one of the
> resource parameters needed by the observer-access problem?

The relevant resource is not detector response by itself. In the notation of
the intrinsic-bandwidth gate, it is a bound on, or a physical relation for,

$$
G_D(T)=\sum_j\int_{I_j}dt\,
\left\|H_j(t)-H_j^{(0)}(t)\right\|,                         \tag{1.1}
$$

where the comparison interaction is diary blind but has the same interfaces,
clock, and public data. Equivalent useful inputs would be a derived contact
count, switching-work budget, retained-memory rule, or a common bulk
implementation cost that bounds (1.1).

A source reopens WP2 only if it supplies all of the following:

1. a Lorentzian interaction preserving the physical constraint;
2. probe preparation, readout, state update, and retained memory sufficient
   to define a sequential instrument;
3. a physically derived bound on coupling, duration, repetitions, action, or
   another resource controlling record formation;
4. a rule that can be applied to the one-copy and doubled descriptions before
   the diary answer is known.

## 3. The interaction prong is partly satisfied

Let

$$
Q=H_L-H_R
$$

denote the equal-energy constraint. Narovlansky and Verlinde construct
physical dressed operators $O_\Delta^\pm$ satisfying
$[Q,O_\Delta^\pm]=0$ and write a model observer interaction

$$
S_{\rm int}=\int d\tau\,
\left[X^+(\tau)O_\Delta^-(\tau)
      +X^-(\tau)O_\Delta^+(\tau)\right].                    \tag{3.1}
$$

Here $X^\pm$ raise and lower the internal energy of an Unruh detector along
its proper-time worldline. The corresponding transition rate is

$$
\dot P(E_i\to E_j)=|X_{ij}|^2G_\Delta(E_j-E_i),              \tag{3.2}
$$

with a de Sitter detailed-balance ratio. Thus it is too strong to say that no
worldline interaction exists in the doubled DSSYK proposal. Equation (3.1)
is a constraint-preserving contact on the DSSYK physical space.

It is not a complete instrument. The matrix elements $X_{ij}$ are not fixed,
there is no switching envelope or finite contact schedule, and the paper does
not specify a final probe POVM, post-measurement update, retained detector
memory, or gravitational cost of using the contact.

Primary source: Narovlansky and Verlinde,
[*Double-scaled SYK and de Sitter Holography*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16994),
especially Eqs. (27), (64)--(67).

## 4. Standard machinery completes a chosen contact, not its price

There is no formal obstacle to deriving an instrument once a contact is
fully specified.

- Fewster and Verch derive completely positive measurement maps and
  Davies--Lewis instruments from a localized system--probe coupling, initial
  probe state, and probe readout, including causal composition of multiple
  coupling regions.
- Polo-Gomez, Garay, and Martin-Martinez derive the POVM and field-state
  update induced by an Unruh--DeWitt-type detector with a declared switching
  function and final detector measurement.
- Jorgensen and Pollock show how a Feynman--Vernon influence functional
  determines a process tensor, including temporal correlations and memory.

These results explain how to obtain the positive multitime object WP2 needs.
They do not select the coupling, switching, readout, reset rule, or cost from
de Sitter gravity. Applying them to (3.1) would therefore produce a family of
valid completions, not a uniquely derived observer process.

The established detector-backreaction literature adds another useful but
limited ingredient. Raval, Hu, and Anglin derive influence-functional and
Langevin descriptions containing the field's self-consistent backreaction on
accelerated detectors. Lin and Hu solve a detector--field model
nonperturbatively and show explicitly that this backreaction generates
entanglement, transients, and non-Markovian reduced dynamics. Here
"backreaction" is detector--field mutual influence on a prescribed spacetime,
not a bound derived from dynamical de Sitter geometry. It can determine the
memory kernel after a coupling has been chosen, but it neither selects that
coupling nor prices its gravitational use.

Primary sources:

- Fewster and Verch,
  [*Quantum Fields and Local Measurements*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.06512).
- Polo-Gomez, Garay, and Martin-Martinez,
  [*A Detector-Based Measurement Theory for Quantum Field Theory*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02793).
- Jorgensen and Pollock,
  [*Exploiting the Causal Tensor Network Structure of Quantum Processes to Efficiently Simulate Non-Markovian Path Integrals*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00315).
- Raval, Hu, and Anglin,
  [*Stochastic Theory of Accelerated Detectors in a Quantum Field*](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9510002).
- Lin and Hu,
  [*Backreaction and Unruh Effect: New Insights from Exact Solutions of Uniformly Accelerated Detectors*](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0611062).

## 5. The available bounds do not price the contact

### 5.1 Observer energy

Tietto and Verlinde treat the combined static patch and observer in a fixed
deficit-angle sector. Their energy partition obeys

$$
E_{\rm obs}=E,
\qquad E_{\rm SdS}=E(\psi)-E,
\qquad 0\leq E\leq E(\psi).                                 \tag{5.1}
$$

Localized physical operators may change the partition while preserving the
sector. Equation (5.1) is a genuine cap on the observer subsystem's free
energy. It does not constrain $X_{ij}$ in (3.1), the switching work, or the
number and duration of detector contacts.

Primary source: Tietto and Verlinde,
[*A Microscopic Model of de Sitter Spacetime with an Observer*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03869),
Eqs. (25)--(26).

### 5.2 Euclidean metric susceptibility: the closest near-hit

Espindola and Ali construct a recent Euclidean Gaussian observer with
internal oscillator variables $q_I(\tau)$, a gap $\Omega_0$, and a smeared
worldtube source $K_{\rm clk}Ph$ for metric perturbations. Defining the source
strength $\Lambda_{\rm clk}$, they obtain

$$
\left\|A_{\rm clk}^{-1/2}K_{\rm clk}\right\|_{\rm op}
\leq\frac{\Lambda_{\rm clk}}{\Omega_0},
\qquad
\frac{\Lambda_{\rm clk}^2}{\Omega_0^2}<\delta_P            \tag{5.2}
$$

as a sufficient condition for a previously stable projected Gaussian
gravitational channel to remain coercive.

This is a real backreaction inequality, but it is not yet the WP2 inequality:

1. the source couplings, smearing profiles, and detector gap are chosen model
   inputs rather than derived DSSYK observer data;
2. the construction is Euclidean and quadratic, with no Lorentzian readout or
   completely positive sequential instrument;
3. it couples the observer to a metric channel, not the diary-sensitive matter
   operator in (3.1);
4. no result relates (5.2) to $G_D(T)$, switching work, contact count, or
   recovery fidelity;
5. the numerical threshold depends on gravitational-Hessian normalization,
   gauge fixing, and Newton's constant.

It is therefore best treated as a candidate *admissibility constraint* for a
future microscopic observer, not as a present operational access budget.

Primary source: Espindola and Ali,
[*Spectral Admissibility of Real Observers in Euclidean de Sitter Gravity*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30423),
especially Eqs. (45)--(56).

### 5.3 Lorentzian gravitational backreaction remains correlator-level

The July 2026 Lorentzian papers do incorporate genuine gravitational effects,
but still stop on the other side of the operational gap. Cui and Kolchmeyer
write the ordinary forward coupling
$H_{\rm int}(t)=X(t)\phi(t)$ and observe that the reduced observer density
matrix is insensitive to the anti-scrambling OTOCs because its Dyson
expansion remains on one timefold. Chen, Stanford, Tang, and Yang incorporate
gravitational shock backreaction and observer recoil in worldline
correlators. Neither work supplies a detector POVM, post-measurement
instrument, retained-record rule, or contact budget.

Primary sources:

- Cui and Kolchmeyer,
  [*A de Sitter Anti-Scrambling Algebra*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13665).
- Chen, Stanford, Tang, and Yang,
  [*Negative Shocks versus Static Patch Holography*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14042).

## 6. Energy-cap/action-budget nonimplication

The failure to combine (3.1) and (5.1) can be stated exactly.

> **Proposition (observer-energy cap does not price detector action).**
> Consider a class of observer models that fixes the free detector
> Hamiltonian and an upper bound $E_{\rm obs}\leq E_{\max}$, and admits a
> nonzero constraint-preserving diary-sensitive interaction difference
> $\Delta H(t)=H(t)-H^{(0)}(t)$. If the class remains admissible under
> positive rescalings of $\Delta H(t)$ because the same physical construction
> imposes no normalization bound, then the energy cap implies no finite upper
> bound on
> $G_D(T)=\int_0^Tdt\,\|\Delta H(t)\|$.

**Proof.** Write one admissible interaction difference as

$$
\Delta H(t)=g(t)X\otimes\Delta O_{\rm phys}(t),
\qquad [Q,\Delta O_{\rm phys}(t)]=0.                         \tag{6.1}
$$

For any $c>0$, replace $g(t)$ by $cg(t)$, or equivalently replace $X$ by
$cX$. This preserves the free detector Hamiltonian, its energy spectrum, the
cap $E_{\rm obs}\leq E_{\max}$, and constraint preservation. But

$$
G_D^{(c)}(T)=cG_D^{(1)}(T).                                  \tag{6.2}
$$

Since $c$ is unrestricted by the stated energy cap, no finite function of
$E_{\max}$ alone can upper-bound $G_D(T)$. In the NV response formula, the
same rescaling multiplies transition rates by $c^2$. QED.

The proposition does not claim that gravity can never price a detector.
Rather, it identifies the extra statement a future derivation must supply.
For example, a dynamical total-energy or backreaction condition might bound
the interaction norm. Even then, a pointwise bound does not by itself limit
integrated action: unrestricted duration or repeated contacts can make
$G_D(T)$ arbitrarily large. A full resource law must therefore control both
strength and use.

## 7. Isometric transport still kills a free doubled advantage

Let $W:\mathcal H_1\to\mathcal H_{\rm eq}$ be the equal-energy isometry. The
NV contact transports to one copy as

$$
H_{{\rm int},1}(t)
=(I_D\otimes W^\dagger)H_{{\rm int},{\rm eq}}(t)
 (I_D\otimes W).                                             \tag{7.1}
$$

On the physical image, (7.1) preserves operator norms, detector transition
probabilities, every instrument obtained from the same probe preparation and
readout, the resulting process tensor, and $G_D(T)$. Thus the explicit
worldline contact does not evade the WP1 no-free-access result.

The Euclidean metric-susceptibility criterion has no established one-copy
image because the checked literature does not give a DSSYK dictionary for
that metric-source channel. That absence cannot be counted as a doubled
advantage; it means the comparative resource rule is not yet defined.

## 8. Verdict and allowed continuation

The answer to the bounded question is **no jointly, yes separately**:

- a constraint-preserving model detector contact exists;
- standard open-system theory can complete a chosen contact into an
  instrument or process tensor;
- observer-energy and Euclidean backreaction bounds exist;
- no checked source ties those bounds to the Lorentzian diary-sensitive
  contact, its accumulated action, or its record memory.

WP2 therefore remains closed. No DSSYK scaling-operator numerics, exact OTOC,
or detector-response computation is authorized by this audit. Such a
calculation would still evaluate a chosen operator family under an unpriced
protocol.

The detector branch may reopen only on a source-derived relation of the form

$$
G_D(T)\leq
F(E_{\rm obs},G_N,R_{\rm dS},\text{backreaction},\text{memory},\ldots),
                                                                    \tag{8.1}
$$

or an equivalent bound on contacts or implementation cost, together with a
Lorentzian instrument and a common one-copy/doubled comparison. If (8.1) is
found, the generic recovery converse already proved by the program can be
applied immediately. Until then, the nonimplication proposition is the
endpoint rather than an invitation to choose a budget by hand.
