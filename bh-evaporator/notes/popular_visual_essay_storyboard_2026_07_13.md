# How Does a Black Hole Give Information Back?

## Timed visual-essay storyboard

Target runtime: approximately 15–17 minutes, depending on narration speed,
pauses, and diagram animation.

The status labels keep provenance visible during production:

- **Literature:** established result being explained from prior work.
- **Project:** control construction or comparison performed in this program.
- **Open:** question that requires a theory-specific quantum-gravity account.

## Visual pacing rules

### Keep the first four minutes physical

Do not show a Page curve, entropy label, or reconstruction circuit before the
audience has seen the two evaporators and understood their different diary
outcomes. The opening should feel like an experiment, not a lecture.

### Introduce one technical word at a time

Use this order:

1. radiation record;
2. unitary evolution;
3. Page curve;
4. scrambling;
5. island;
6. reconstruction.

Terms such as “isometry,” “operator gas,” “Petz map,” “code subspace,” and
“entanglement wedge” belong in the companion article or optional end-card
references, not in the main narration.

### Make provenance visual, not apologetic

The literature/project/open distinction should appear as a calm production
ledger. Avoid repeated phrases such as “this is not a claim that…” in the
narration. State the boundary once, show it on screen, and move on.

### Let diagrams carry repeated explanations

Once the viewer has seen information distributed across three emissions, do not
repeat the full quantum-code explanation. Reuse the same colored-thread visual
when islands and later reconstruction appear.

## On-screen text budget

Use no more than one major phrase at a time. Recommended text cards:

- **Does the record depend on what went in?**
- **Final destination**
- **State-dependent radiation**
- **Reconstructible encoding**
- **Decoder**
- **Thermal appearance ≠ information loss**
- **Compatibility: demonstrated in a control model**
- **Derivation from quantum gravity: open**

Avoid putting full sentences or caveats on screen while the narrator is making
another point. The provenance map is the one deliberate exception.

## Source and claim handling

The final video description and companion article should link the primary papers
for Hawking radiation, the Page curve, Hayden–Preskill, islands, reconstruction,
and the dynamical bridge models. The narration need not read citations aloud.

At the editing stage, maintain a claim ledger with three columns:

| Spoken claim | Source category | Required qualification |
|---|---|---|
| The Page curve rises and falls for a pure total state under typicality assumptions. | Literature | It does not specify an emission-by-emission diary route. |
| A diary can be recoverable from later radiation in the old-black-hole setup. | Literature | Requires scrambling and early-radiation access. |
| A unified ordinary quantum process can combine Hawking-like thermodynamics and information return. | Project | It is a control model, not a microscopic black hole. |
| Dynamical models connect state-dependent emission to reconstruction. | Literature | Each model has stated idealizations. |
| A fundamental black-hole derivation remains open. | Open | Requires a theory-specific account of observables and dynamics. |
