\begin{abstract}
We ask which parts of Schwarzschild evaporation require gravity once the
black-hole state count is supplied as a Hamiltonian density of states.  We
construct a non-gravitational Hamiltonian with a Schwarzschild microcanonical
entropy, area-many weak emission operators, an outgoing radiation continuum,
and mixing within fixed energy shells.  The weak-coupling calculation gives
the local Hawking spectrum, the leading finite-energy correction, negative
heat capacity, and the Schwarzschild number-flux, power, and lifetime
scalings.  The area dependence of the rate comes from an inclusive sum over
boundary-accessible emission operators.  Thus the density of states fixes the
thermal factor, while the boundary emission algebra fixes the rate.  Within
the model class both inputs are forced: the density of states is equivalent to
the Hawking temperature law, and the area-sized emission count is equivalent
to the Schwarzschild luminosity law.  The same
Hamiltonian gives the
fine-grained information-flow structure: global purity and decoupling of the
shrinking emission map produce the Page curve and post-Page early/late
radiation correlations.  At second R\'enyi order the purity contains the two
contractions familiar from island calculations, giving the island min formula
with the microcanonical entropy of the remaining subsystem in place of the
gravitational area term.  The same decoupling input gives the operational
layer: Hayden--Preskill recovery from the radiation record, and complementary
reconstruction of code information that switches from the remaining subsystem
to the radiation at the Page transition.  Interior partner operators exist
as mirrors on the purifier of each fresh mode, are provably state-dependent
by a finite-dimensional counting argument, and move from the remaining
subsystem into the radiation at the same transition --- locating the
monogamy premise of the firewall argument that fails in the model.
The radiation-supported mirror is a Petz pullback; its circuit complexity
is bounded below by $\exp(\frac{1}{2}S_{\rm micro}(E))$ by the
Harlow--Hayden theorem, tightened to $\exp(S_{\rm micro}(E))$ by the
Python's lunch, and the no-go theorem implies that any sub-exponential
circuit fails the mirror relations for typical microstates.
Approximate-design and tensor-product-expander
blocks provide finite-regulator realizations of the required shell mixing;
ETH-type spectral conditions on a fixed shell Hamiltonian yield the same
decoupling, with errors controlled by the golden-rule parameter, and imply
that the result self-averages --- a single Hamiltonian reproduces the
ensemble Page curve up to fluctuations exponentially small in the initial
entropy, leaving no quantum-mechanical factorization puzzle;
and an exact finite-size-scaling study shows that a fixed chaotic Cayley
shell Hamiltonian reproduces the island value while free and integrable
mixers with the same state count fail, with seed scatter at the predicted
concentration rate.
The replica wormhole saddle sum is shown to be term-by-term identical to
the ETH permutation sum under the Bekenstein--Hawking identification
$S_{\rm BH}=\log d_B$, which is itself Proposition~1 of the model.
Self-averaging upgrades the statement to a single fixed Hamiltonian.
The remaining genuinely gravitational question is therefore precisely why
the black-hole Hilbert space has Bekenstein--Hawking dimension; all other
features of the information puzzle are on the quantum-mechanics side.
The construction reproduces the exterior thermodynamic and entropy-flow
package without a gravitational path integral, interior geometry, or horizon
smoothness.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

We use units with $\hbar=c=k_B=1$.

This paper asks how much of Schwarzschild evaporation can be reproduced by an
ordinary quantum Hamiltonian with no dynamical geometry.  The answer we find is
that the exterior thermodynamic and entropy-flow package follows once two
black-hole inputs are supplied: the Schwarzschild microcanonical state count
and an area-sized set of weak emission operators.  Gravity enters through
these inputs, and only there.  Section~\ref{sec:necessity} shows that each
input is equivalent, within the model class, to one item of the target
phenomenology: the state count to the temperature law, and the
boundary-accessible emission count to the luminosity law.  The inputs are
therefore exactly the two measured scalings being matched, not additional
discretionary structure.  The subsequent local thermality, rate law, Page
curve, early/late correlations, and island-formula entropy are consequences
of Hamiltonian evolution, weak emission into outgoing modes, and decoupling
of the shrinking emission map.

The target is the evaporation of an uncharged nonrotating black hole in four
asymptotically flat spacetime dimensions.  Semiclassically,
\begin{equation}
    S_{\rm BH}\propto A\propto M^2,\qquad
    T_H\propto M^{-1},
\end{equation}
and the heat capacity is negative \cite{Bekenstein1973,Hawking1975}.  The
outgoing radiation is locally thermal, up to greybody and phase-space factors
\cite{Hawking1975,Page1976}.  The luminosity scales as
\begin{equation}
    P(M)\propto M^{-2},
\end{equation}
so the lifetime scales as $\tau\propto M_0^3$.

For a unitary evaporation process, the final radiation state must encode the
initial state even though small radiation subsystems have an approximately
thermal appearance.  Page's dimension-counting argument predicts a radiation
entropy that rises and then falls \cite{Page1993Average,
Page1993Info}.  The Hayden-Preskill argument gives a sharper decoding
statement for a diary thrown into an old, rapidly scrambling black hole
\cite{HaydenPreskill2007,SekinoSusskind2008}.  The present paper derives the
Page entropy statement and early/late radiation correlations, and, from the
same decoupling input, the corresponding recovery statements: diary-sized
code subspaces become recoverable from the radiation record precisely after
the Page transition, with the Hayden-Preskill mirror bookkeeping in units of
emitted quanta (Section~\ref{sec:recovery}).  It does not derive the
scrambling-time estimate $t_{\rm scr}\sim\beta\log\Smicro$.

The post-2019 island calculations add a second language for the same
fine-grained radiation entropy curve.  In those calculations, the von Neumann
entropy of the radiation is obtained by minimizing over saddles.  Before the
Page time the no-island saddle gives the Hawking entropy of the emitted
radiation.  After the Page time the island saddle gives the generalized
entropy of a quantum extremal surface, whose area term is the remaining
black-hole entropy \cite{Penington2019,
AlmheiriEngelhardtMarolfMaxfield2019,
AlmheiriHartmanMaldacenaShaghoulianTajdini2020,
AlmheiriHartmanMaldacenaShaghoulianTajdini2021}.  The Hamiltonian construction
below shows how the same min formula arises in a manifestly unitary,
non-gravitational model.  The no-island branch is the entropy of the emitted
radiation history.  The island branch is the microcanonical state count of the
remaining subsystem.  There is no gravitational path integral in the model, so
the island statement is algebraic and does not construct quantum extremal
surfaces or replica wormholes.

The exterior features recovered below are the following.
\begin{enumerate}
\item
The black-hole entropy scales as the horizon area.  For a four-dimensional
Schwarzschild black hole, $A\sim M^2$.
\item
The temperature scales as $T_H\sim M^{-1}$, so the heat capacity is negative.
\item
The outgoing radiation has an approximately thermal one-particle spectrum at
the instantaneous Hawking temperature, with the usual greybody and phase-space
factors and with finite-energy deviations from exact thermality.
\item
For four-dimensional Schwarzschild evaporation, the typical quantum energy
scales as $\omega\sim T\sim M^{-1}$, the luminosity scales as
$P\sim M^{-2}$, the number flux scales as
$\Gamma_{\rm quanta}\sim M^{-1}$, and the evaporation time scales as
$\tau\sim M_0^3$.
\item
The evaporation is quasi-stationary: over short intervals the radiation is
described by an instantaneous mass and temperature, while the macroscopic mass
changes only over many emitted quanta.
\item
The fine-grained evolution is unitary: the radiation von Neumann entropy
follows the Page curve, equivalently the island min formula for the exterior
radiation entropy, and late radiation is correlated with early radiation after
the Page time.  Code information deposited in the subsystem is recoverable
from the radiation record after the Page transition, and not before.
\end{enumerate}

The contribution of this paper is to assemble these thermodynamic, rate, and
fine-grained entropy statements in one energy-resolved Hamiltonian framework.
The Hamiltonian has a subsystem $B$ with microcanonical density of states
$\rho_{\rm B}(E)\sim \exp(cE^2)$, with the energy $E$ identified with the mass
$M$.  The leading entropy is carried by boundary-accessible degrees of
freedom.  The subsystem is weakly coupled to an outgoing radiation continuum by
an energy-conserving interaction whose local emission operators act on those
degrees of freedom.  The inclusive low-frequency emission strength is then
proportional to the number of active boundary emitters, hence to the area.
This reproduces the area scaling of the standard low-frequency absorption
cross-section by operator counting.  The same weak-emission map, after mixing
inside microcanonical shells, gives the shrinking-isometry Page curve and its
island-formula min structure.

This separation is important.  Recent work constructs operators with
black-hole-like spectral growth, $\Omega(E)\sim\exp({\rm const.}\,E^2)$, and
finds that such spectra can arise from unusual, barely localized wavefunctions
\cite{AurellMajumdar2025}.  In the present framework, the spectral growth alone
would give the microcanonical Hawking temperature.  It would not fix the
inclusive luminosity or the fine-grained Page/island structure.  Those require
the boundary-accessible emission algebra and the decoupling of the induced
shrinking channel.

In the design-based version used below, the regulated
in-shell mixing blocks are chosen from approximate-design or
tensor-product-expander constructions, so this decoupling condition follows
from standard results
\cite{HaydenPreskill2007,HaydenHorodeckiWinterYard2008,
DankertCleveEmersonLivine2009,SzehrDupuisTomamichelRenner2013,
BrownFawzi2015,HarrowLow2009}.  Known Hamiltonian-design constructions give
Hamiltonian evolutions with the required design moments under their ensemble,
locality, or time-dependence assumptions, including nearly time-independent
design Hamiltonians and time-independent random polylog-local Hamiltonians
\cite{NakataHircheKoashiWinter2017,CuiSchusterMaoHuangBrandao2025}.
Channel-scrambling diagnostics based on out-of-time-order correlator (OTOC)
decay give another Hamiltonian route to the same mutual-information statements
\cite{HosurQiRobertsYoshida2016,YoshidaKitaev2017}.
Appendix~\ref{app:eth-decoupling} derives the decoupling condition directly
from ETH-type spectral assumptions on a fixed shell Hamiltonian, and
Appendix~\ref{app:cayley-scaling} complements these constructions
with an exact finite-size-scaling study of one fixed deterministic
Hamiltonian.

The construction is conditional in two explicit places.  First, the
Schwarzschild density of states and its boundary-accessible realization are
inputs to the Hamiltonian.  Propositions~\ref{prop:dos} and~\ref{prop:area}
show that these inputs are forced --- the density of states by the
temperature law and boundary accessibility by the luminosity law --- but
deriving them from simpler non-gravitational microscopic degrees of freedom
is a separate problem.  Second, the fine-grained information-flow result uses
decoupling of the composite emission map, supplied here by approximate
designs or tensor-product expanders, derived from ETH-type spectral
assumptions in Appendix~\ref{app:eth-decoupling}, and tested for a fixed
deterministic shell Hamiltonian in Appendix~\ref{app:cayley-scaling}.  With
these inputs, the remaining evaporation package follows by standard
weak-coupling, microcanonical, and quantum-information arguments.
Table~\ref{tab:ledger} records the resulting division of the paper's
statements into inputs, matched targets, and derived outputs.  The model
has no interior geometry: the recovery statements of
Section~\ref{sec:recovery} concern code information, and the interior
(mirror) operators of Section~\ref{sec:mirrors} are an operator algebra with
smooth-horizon correlators, not a geometry.  At that algebraic level the
model does address the firewall argument: mirror operators exist, are
provably state-dependent, and identify the monogamy premise that fails.
Questions of interior geometry and the infalling experience remain outside
its scope.
Section~\ref{sec:wormhole-ETH} draws the sharpest demarcation the model
affords: the replica wormhole sum \cite{PeningtonShenkerStanfordYang2022}
is term-by-term identical to the ETH permutation sum of
Proposition~\ref{prop:eth-decoupling} under $S_{\rm BH}=\Smicro$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:dos}), and self-averaging
(Proposition~\ref{prop:self-averaging}) makes the result instance-level.
The remaining genuinely gravitational question is therefore precisely why
the black-hole Hilbert space has Bekenstein--Hawking dimension, and nothing more.
Section~\ref{sec:open-problems} states explicitly which parts of the
information puzzle the construction settles, in the conditional sense above,
and which remain open.

\subsubsection*{Relation to earlier work}

Several finite-dimensional models address the information-theoretic side of
this picture.  Tensor-network models, qubit-transport models, and random-circuit
models describe unitary transfer of information from a shrinking black-hole
subsystem into radiation \cite{LeutheusserVanRaamsdonk2017,Avery2013,
OsugaPage2018,PiroliSunderhaufQi2020}.  These constructions make Page-like
information flow transparent.  The Hamiltonian considered here also includes the
thermodynamic data: an energy, a density of states, emitted quanta with
definite energies, local thermal flux, negative heat capacity, and the
Schwarzschild rate law.  Related Hilbert-space reduction models make the
island-like entropy minimum explicit from changing Hilbert-space dimensions
\cite{BasuWenZhou2025}.

Analogue-gravity systems give another comparison point.  Unruh's acoustic
black holes and the later analogue-gravity literature show that
Hawking-like radiation can arise in non-gravitational media whose long-distance
excitations see an effective horizon \cite{Unruh1981,Barcelo2011}.  Those
models capture the kinematic field-theory part of Hawking radiation.  The
Hamiltonian construction below addresses the thermodynamic and
information-theoretic part by supplying the black-hole state count as a density
of states and treating evaporation as ordinary weak emission into outgoing
quantum modes.

The closest information-theoretic comparison is the tensor-network class of
Leutheusser and Van Raamsdonk, where each evaporation step is specified by an
isometry
\begin{equation}
    {\cal I}_n:{\cal H}^{BH}_n\to {\cal H}^{BH}_{n-1}\otimes{\cal H}^{rad}_n .
\end{equation}
In the present notation the corresponding map is $V_E$, used in
Section~\ref{sec:information-flow} and constructed in
Appendix~\ref{app:weak-emission-map} as the Stinespring dilation of the weak
emission channel
\begin{equation}
    V_E:\mathcal H_E\to
    \bigoplus_{\omega,\lambda,\mu}
    \mathcal H_{E-\omega}\otimes\ket{\omega,\lambda,\mu}_{\rm R}.
\end{equation}
The additional structure here is the energy-resolved Hamiltonian origin of
this map and the density of states that produces the thermodynamic evaporation
law.  Tensor-network and random-circuit models supply the Page and
early/late-correlation part of the argument
\cite{LeutheusserVanRaamsdonk2017,PiroliSunderhaufQi2020}.

Section 2 defines the model.  Section 3 derives the thermodynamic
consequences, shows that the two inputs are forced by the temperature and
luminosity laws, and lists the parameter-free relations that follow.
Section 4 states the emission-isometry reduction, the Page curve, and the
island-formula contraction, and derives the recovery,
complementary-reconstruction, and state-dependent interior-operator
consequences.  Section 5 gives the
finite-dimensional
regularization needed to apply Page and decoupling estimates.  Section 6
compares the model with existing approaches, and Section~\ref{sec:discussion}
discusses the fixed-Hamiltonian problem left by the abstract mixer and states
which parts of the information puzzle the construction settles and which
remain open.  The
appendices give the
weak-emission Stinespring construction, a concrete fixed-Hamiltonian target,
the Page/replica moment calculation, a finite-dimensional check separating
coarse thermality from Page behavior, an exact finite-size-scaling study
of deterministic shell mixers, and an ETH-based derivation of the required
decoupling.

\section{The Model}

The model has four ingredients.  The first is a black-hole subsystem $B$,
representing the remaining object.  The second is an outgoing
radiation field $R$, representing emitted quanta that propagate away.  The
third is a mixing term that scrambles states inside fixed microcanonical
shells.  The fourth is an interaction that lowers the energy of $B$ while
creating an outgoing quantum.  The full Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}
    H_{\rm tot}=H_{\rm B}^{(0)}+H_{\rm mix}+H_{\rm R}+H_I.
\end{equation}
The term $H_{\rm mix}$ mixes states inside a fixed microcanonical shell of
$B$.  The interaction $H_I$ transfers energy from $B$ to $R$.
The Hamiltonian is time independent.  The stochastic language used later for
emission events is the standard weak-coupling description of this Hamiltonian
in the outgoing-scattering regime.

\subsection{Black-Hole Subsystem}

The Hilbert space of the black-hole subsystem is organized by energy.  We use
continuum notation for readability, but all entropy statements refer to
microcanonical bands of width $\Delta E$ that are wide compared with the
microscopic level spacing and narrow compared with the scale on which
$\Smicro(E)$ varies:
\begin{equation}
    \HB=\int^\oplus dE\,\mathcal H_E,
    \qquad
    H_{\rm B}^{(0)}=\int dE\,E\,\Pi(E).
\end{equation}
Here $\Pi(E)$ denotes the formal spectral projector density onto energy $E$.
For a shell of width $\Delta E$, let $\Pi_{\Delta E}(E)$ denote the projector
onto states with energies in that shell.  Then
\begin{equation}
    N(E,\Delta E)=\Tr \Pi_{\Delta E}(E)\simeq \rhoB(E)\Delta E,
    \qquad
    \Smicro(E)=\log N(E,\Delta E).
\end{equation}
The Schwarzschild scaling is encoded by an area function $A(E)$ and by the
microcanonical entropy
\begin{equation}
    \Smicro(E)\simeq cE^2,
    \qquad
    A(E)\simeq c_A E^2.
\end{equation}
The constants $c$ and $c_A$ set the entropy and area units.
For the rate-law result, we use a boundary-accessible realization of this state
count.  Up to subexponential factors, the active shell contains
\begin{equation}
    N(E)\simeq \frac{\Smicro(E)}{\log q}
\end{equation}
active boundary cells of local Hilbert-space dimension $q$.  Thus
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal H_E\simeq
    \mathcal K_E\otimes
    \bigotimes_{x=1}^{N(E)}\mathcal h_x,
    \qquad
    \dim \mathcal h_x=q,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal K_E$ denotes any remaining degeneracy that is subleading for
the operator count.  Since $\Smicro(E)\propto A(E)$, the number of active
boundary cells is area-sized:
\begin{equation}
    N(E)\propto A(E).
\end{equation}
This is the horizon-qubit bookkeeping assumption in Hamiltonian language.  It
is not a derivation of the Schwarzschild density of states from simpler
degrees of freedom.  The word ``boundary'' refers here to the degrees that are
accessible to the emission operators and that carry the leading shell entropy;
it is a bookkeeping analogue of horizon area, not a geometric bulk/boundary
factorization inside the model.
Section~\ref{sec:necessity} shows that this assumption is what the
Schwarzschild luminosity measures: given the density of states,
$P\propto M^{-2}$ holds if and only if order-$\Smicro$ boundary cells couple
to the emission operators.
For a narrow microcanonical wavepacket, the mass variable is the expectation
value of the subsystem energy,
\begin{equation}
    M(t)=\langle H_{\rm B}^{(0)}\rangle_t.
\end{equation}
Choose a microscopic energy basis
\begin{equation}
    H_{\rm B}^{(0)}\ket a=E_a\ket a,\qquad A_a=A(E_a),
\end{equation}
with many states $\ket a$ in the same microcanonical shell.  Roman indices
$a,b$ label black-hole energy eigenstates throughout.

\subsection{Radiation Hilbert Space}

The outgoing radiation Hilbert space is a Fock space $\FR$ with Hamiltonian
\begin{equation}
    H_{\rm R}
    =
    \sum_\lambda\int_0^\infty d\omega\,
    \omega\, b_\lambda^\dagger(\omega)b_\lambda(\omega).
\end{equation}
The modes are continuum-normalized,
\begin{equation}
    [b_\lambda(\omega),b_{\lambda'}^\dagger(\omega')]
    =\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}\delta(\omega-\omega').
\end{equation}
The label $\lambda$ denotes species, angular channels, polarizations, or other
outgoing radiation labels.  Smooth density-of-modes and residual channel
factors are included below in the channel weights.  The outgoing field is
treated as an asymptotic radiation channel or large lead, so emitted quanta
propagate away from $B$ on the evaporation time scale.

\subsection{Mixing and Emission}

The mixing term acts within fixed microcanonical shells,
\begin{equation}
    H_{\rm mix}=\int dE\,H_{\rm mix}(E),
    \qquad
    H_{\rm mix}(E):\mathcal H_E\to\mathcal H_E.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, in a finite regulator one replaces the direct integral by a
direct sum over microcanonical bands and takes
$H_{\rm mix}=\oplus_E H_{\rm mix}(E)$.  This is the version used in the
dimension-counting argument below.  Exact block diagonality is an idealization
of a microcanonical description: small off-shell matrix elements may be
included provided they do not change the coarse evaporation trajectory on the
emission time scale.
For the design-based version of the model, the finite-shell mixer is
specified by a unitary block $U_E$ whose low-order moments form an approximate
design, or more generally by a tensor-product expander of the order required by
the diagnostic.  The Hamiltonian block is any self-adjoint logarithm
\begin{equation}
    H_{\rm mix}(E)=\frac{i}{t_{\rm scr}}\log U_E,
    \qquad
    e^{-iH_{\rm mix}(E)t_{\rm scr}}=U_E .
\end{equation}
Once the blocks $U_E$ are chosen, the evolution is fixed and time independent.
Approximate two-designs are enough for the second R\'enyi Page-purity and
decoupling estimates
\cite{DankertCleveEmersonLivine2009,SzehrDupuisTomamichelRenner2013};
tensor-product expanders give the corresponding higher-moment route
\cite{HarrowLow2009}.  Related Hamiltonian-design constructions are discussed
in Sec.~\ref{sec:comparison} and App.~\ref{app:fixed-hamiltonian-target}.
Appendix~\ref{app:eth-decoupling} gives an alternative route in which the
design assumption is replaced by ETH-type spectral conditions on a fixed
shell Hamiltonian.

The weak-emission regime is
\begin{equation}
    t_{\rm emit}\ll t_{\rm evap}.
\end{equation}
Here $t_{\rm emit}$ is the mean time between emissions and $t_{\rm evap}$ is
the macroscopic evaporation time.  The information-theoretic results use the
additional mixing condition stated in Sec.~\ref{sec:isometry-flow}: the
composite emission map must act as a typical isometry on the active
microcanonical support.

The emission term uses the standard system-continuum form for weak decay into
outgoing modes, familiar from Friedrichs-Lee and Wigner-Weisskopf descriptions
of unstable quantum systems coupled to continua
\cite{Friedrichs1948,Lee1954,CohenTannoudji1992}.  In a multi-channel
Friedrichs-Lee notation, the continuum state coupled to a state $\ket a$ of
$B$ is
\begin{equation}
    \ket{b;\omega,\lambda}\equiv
    \ket b\otimes b_\lambda^\dagger(\omega)\ket{0}_{\rm R}.
\end{equation}
The interaction lowers the area associated with the black-hole subsystem and
creates an outgoing quantum.  We separate the outgoing radiation label
$\lambda$ from an inclusive emission-channel label $\mu$.  The latter
labels the active boundary cell, or equivalently an orthogonal boundary
emission operator.  The number of such labels is
$N(E)\propto A(E)$ in the boundary-accessible realization above:
\begin{align}
    H_I
    =
    \sum_{a,b,\lambda,\mu}
    \int_0^\infty d\omega\,
    \Big[
    &f^{\mu\lambda}_{ba}(\omega)
    \ket b\bra a\,
    b_\lambda^\dagger(\omega)
    +{\rm h.c.}\Big].
\end{align}
For initial states with empty outgoing radiation and for an outgoing continuum
or large lead, the weak-coupling scattering limit selects the decaying branch
on the evaporation time scale.  This is the rotating-wave, vacuum-continuum
Markov limit in which reabsorption and late-time backflow are negligible.
The form factor $f^{\mu\lambda}_{ba}(\omega)$ is supported on transitions with
$A_b<A_a$.  In the
long-time weak-coupling calculation, the usual resonant delta function selects
transitions with $E_a-E_b=\omega$.  We write the smooth part of the matrix
element as
\begin{equation}
    f^{\mu\lambda}_{ba}(\omega)
    =
    g\,u^{\mu\lambda}_{ba}\,
    \omega^{p/2}\,
    \chi(A_b,A_a;\omega).
\end{equation}
Here $g$ is the weak coupling constant.  The dimensionless matrix element
$u^{\mu\lambda}_{ba}$ distributes amplitude among available final states, and $\chi$
restricts the allowed area loss.  The factor
$\omega^p$ is the effective low-energy phase-space weight; for massless
emission in three spatial dimensions the leading behavior is $p=2$.  The
area factor enters after summing over the active boundary emitters.  For
four-dimensional Schwarzschild emission the corresponding low-frequency
absorption cross-section is proportional to the horizon area,
$\sigma_{\rm abs}(\omega\to0)=A_H$ for minimally coupled massless scalars
\cite{Page1976,DasGibbonsMathur1997}.  The present Hamiltonian reproduces the
same area scaling through an inclusive sum over area-many weak boundary
emitters.  The corresponding channel count is
\begin{equation}
    N_A(E)\equiv \#\{\mu\}=N(E)\propto A(E).
\end{equation}
The channel sum is an inclusive sum over independent weak boundary emitters.
The channels are orthogonal in the outgoing continuum, independently phased,
or shell-averaged, so their probabilities add in the golden-rule rate.
Coherent addition of identical amplitudes would give a different scaling and is
not the regime considered here.
Residual greybody and channel dependence is placed in the smooth functions
$C_\lambda(E,\omega)$ below.  The Boltzmann factor comes from the
density-of-states ratio.  Other values of $p$ or different scaling of
$N(E)$ give models with the same microcanonical thermal factor and a different
total power law.

The same statement can be written as an inclusive spectral function of the
boundary emission operators:
\begin{equation}
    {\cal A}_\lambda(E,\omega)
    =
    \frac{1}{D_E}
    \sum_\mu
    \Tr\!\left[
    \Pi_E O_{\lambda\mu}^\dagger(\omega)
    \Pi_{E-\omega}
    O_{\lambda\mu}(\omega)\Pi_E
    \right].
\end{equation}
Here $O_{\lambda\mu}(\omega)$ is the part of the interaction operator whose
matrix elements are $u^{\mu\lambda}_{ba}$ between the shells $E$ and
$E-\omega$.
If the active boundary emitters have comparable shell-averaged strength and
their cross terms vanish in the inclusive rate, then
\begin{equation}
    {\cal A}_\lambda(E,\omega)
    \simeq
    N(E) C_\lambda(E,\omega)
    \frac{D_{E-\omega}}{D_E}.
\label{eq:boundary-spectral-function}
\end{equation}
Thus the area factor is an operator-counting consequence of boundary-accessible
entropy and local weak emission.  This area factor is used in the rate law; the
Page and island calculations below use the same emission labels as radiation
records, but their leading entropy competition is controlled by Hilbert-space
dimensions and decoupling.

For a rapidly mixing subsystem, the emission matrix elements are taken to have
the standard ETH/random-matrix scaling in the energy basis
\cite{Deutsch1991,Srednicki1994}.  In the usual ETH notation the off-diagonal
matrix element carries a factor exponential in one half of the entropy of the
relevant shell.  Here the transition begins in shell $E$.  For $\omega\ll E$,
and for $a$ in shell $E$ and $b$ in shell $E-\omega$, we write
\begin{equation}
    u^{\mu\lambda}_{ba}=D_E^{-1/2} r^{\mu\lambda}_{ba},
    \qquad D_E=\dim \mathcal H_E,
\end{equation}
where the dimensionless variables $r^{\mu\lambda}_{ba}$ have zero mean and shell-averaged
variance
\begin{equation}
    \overline{|r^{\mu\lambda}_{ba}|^2}=C_\lambda(E,\omega).
\end{equation}
The overline denotes the shell or ensemble average within the indicated
microcanonical bands.
The rate calculation uses only this shell-averaged normalization.  The
information-theoretic statements below require the composed emission channel to
decouple the reference from the complementary subsystem.  This is the
channel-scrambling input supplied by the shell mixer.
The smooth function $C_\lambda(E,\omega)$ includes residual channel-dependent
greybody and form-factor dependence not already contained in the phase-space
factor or in the area cross-section.  This
normalization gives
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{b\in E-\omega}|u^{\mu\lambda}_{ba}|^2
    =
    C_\lambda(E,\omega)
    \frac{D_{E-\omega}}{D_E}
    \left[1+O(D_{E-\omega}^{-1/2})\right].
\label{eq:inclusive-u}
\end{equation}
Thus the inclusive rate from a typical initial microstate depends on shell
degeneracies and is insensitive to special basis choices inside the shell.  The
sum over $\mu$ gives the area-sized absorption factor, while the smooth
function $C_\lambda(E,\omega)$ contains residual channel dependence.

\subsection{Initial States and Observables}

The initial states are pure states of $B$ in a narrow microcanonical band, with
empty outgoing radiation:
\begin{equation}
    \ket{\Psi(0)}\in \mathcal H_E\otimes\ket{0}_{\rm R}.
\end{equation}
We consider typical states in the microcanonical shell and states obtained by
acting with a low-complexity perturbation on such states.

The basic observables are:
\begin{align}
    M(t)&=\langle H_{\rm B}^{(0)}\rangle_t,\\
    A(t)&=\langle A(H_{\rm B}^{(0)})\rangle_t,\\
    E_{\rm rad}(t)&=\langle H_{\rm R}\rangle_t,\\
    P(t)&=\frac{d}{dt}E_{\rm rad}(t),
\end{align}
together with the one-particle radiation spectrum.  The information-theoretic
observables are radiation entanglement entropies and mutual information, for
example
\begin{align}
    S_{\rm vN}(R)&=-\Tr\rho_R\log\rho_R,\\
    I(R_{\rm early}:R_{\rm late})
    &=S(R_{\rm early})+S(R_{\rm late})-S(R_{\rm early}R_{\rm late}).
\end{align}
The entropy $\Smicro$ is the microcanonical state-counting entropy of $B$.
The entropy $S_{\rm vN}$ is the von Neumann entropy of a radiation subsystem.

\section{Thermodynamic Properties}

The density of states of $B$ fixes the microcanonical temperature.  For
\begin{equation}
    \Smicro(M)=cM^2,
\end{equation}
the inverse temperature is
\begin{equation}
    \beta(M)=\frac{d\Smicro}{dM}=2cM.
\end{equation}
Thus
\begin{equation}
    T(M)=\beta(M)^{-1}\propto M^{-1},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
    C=\frac{dM}{dT}<0.
\end{equation}

The local emission spectrum follows from Fermi's golden rule and the
microcanonical degeneracy ratio.  For an emitted quantum of energy
$\omega\ll M$,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\rhoB(M-\omega)}
         {\rhoB(M)}
    =
    \exp[\Smicro(M-\omega)-\Smicro(M)]
    =
    \exp[-\beta(M)\omega+c\omega^2]
    \simeq e^{-\beta(M)\omega}.
\end{equation}
The final approximation is the local thermal limit.  The retained
$c\omega^2$ term is the leading finite-energy backreaction correction, the
same entropy-difference correction emphasized in the tunneling description of
Parikh and Wilczek \cite{ParikhWilczek2000}.  For typical Hawking quanta
$\omega\sim T\sim M^{-1}$, this correction is subleading at large $M$ but is
the leading non-thermal deviation from an exactly canonical spectrum.
In the weak-coupling continuum regime, Fermi's golden rule gives a transition
rate equal to the squared matrix element times the density of final states
\cite{Friedrichs1948,CohenTannoudji1992}.  With the shell-averaged
matrix-element normalization in Eq.~\eqref{eq:inclusive-u}, and after summing
over the active boundary emitters, this gives
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\Gamma}{d\omega}
    \propto
    N(M)\,\omega^p\,
    \exp[\Smicro(M-\omega)-\Smicro(M)].
\end{equation}
For typical Hawking quanta, this becomes
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\Gamma}{d\omega}
    \propto N(M)\omega^p e^{-\beta(M)\omega}.
\end{equation}

The particle emission rate and emitted power scale as
\begin{align}
    \Gamma_{\rm quanta}(M)
    &\propto
    N(M)
    \int_0^\infty d\omega\,\omega^p e^{-\beta(M)\omega}
    \propto N(M)\beta(M)^{-(p+1)},\\
    P(M)
    &\propto
    N(M)
    \int_0^\infty d\omega\,\omega^{p+1}e^{-\beta(M)\omega}
    \propto N(M)\beta(M)^{-(p+2)}.
\end{align}
With $N(M)\propto A(M)\propto M^2$, $\beta(M)\propto M$, and $p=2$,
\begin{equation}
    \Gamma_{\rm quanta}(M)\propto M^{-1},
    \qquad
    P(M)\propto M^{-2}.
\end{equation}
The local thermal factor and its leading finite-energy correction follow from
$\rhoB(M-\omega)/\rhoB(M)$ alone.  The total Hawking power law also uses the
area-sized boundary operator count.  If only order-one boundary emitters
couple efficiently, the three-spatial-dimensional phase-space scaling becomes
$P\propto M^{-4}$.
The mass therefore obeys
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dM}{dt}\propto -M^{-2},
\end{equation}
and the lifetime scales as
\begin{equation}
    \tau\propto M_0^3.
\end{equation}
The quasi-stationary expansion fails near the endpoint, where the emitted
quantum energy is no longer small compared with the remaining mass.  The
calculation applies in the large-mass evaporation regime, as in the standard
semiclassical treatment.

Thus the thermodynamic part of Schwarzschild evaporation follows from the
boundary-accessible density of states and local weak emission.  The
density-of-states ratio gives local thermality and the leading finite-energy
correction.  The boundary operator count fixes the four-dimensional power law.

\subsection{Necessity of the two inputs}
\label{sec:necessity}

The two gravitational inputs are not adjustable modelling choices: within the
stated Hamiltonian class, each is equivalent to one item of the target
phenomenology.

\begin{proposition}[Rigidity of the density of states]
\label{prop:dos}
Let $T(E)=(d\Smicro/dE)^{-1}$ be the microcanonical temperature, with
$\Smicro$ smooth at the resolution of the microcanonical bands.  Then for
large $E$ the following are equivalent: (i) $\Smicro(E)=cE^2[1+o(1)]$;
(ii) $T(E)=[2cE]^{-1}[1+o(1)]$; (iii) $\Smicro(E)\propto A(E)$ with
$A(E)\propto E^2$.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof.}  Differentiating (i) gives (ii); integrating (ii) gives (i) up
to an additive constant, which is subleading at large $E$; (iii) restates (i)
with the Schwarzschild area function.  \hfill$\square$

Any Hamiltonian in the class whose microcanonical temperature falls as
$M^{-1}$ therefore has the $e^{cE^2}$ density of states.  Supplying this
density of states is not a discretionary input on top of the targeted
phenomenology; it \emph{is} the targeted temperature law, written in
microcanonical form.  Conversely, no model in the class can match the
temperature law without it.

\begin{proposition}[The luminosity measures boundary accessibility]
\label{prop:area}
Assume the density of states of Proposition~\ref{prop:dos}, weak local
emission with golden-rule rates, and phase-space exponent $p=2$.  Let
$N_{\rm eff}(M)$ be the number of orthogonal, comparably coupled emission
channels contributing to the inclusive rate.  Then
\begin{equation}
    P(M)\propto N_{\rm eff}(M)\,\beta(M)^{-(p+2)}
    \propto N_{\rm eff}(M)\,M^{-4}.
\end{equation}
In particular $P\propto M^{-2}$ if and only if
$N_{\rm eff}(M)\propto M^2\propto\Smicro(M)\propto A(M)$.  If only $O(1)$
channels couple efficiently, $P\propto M^{-4}$.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof.}  This is the inclusive power integral derived above, with the
channel count $N$ replaced by $N_{\rm eff}$; the equivalence is read off from
the exponents.  \hfill$\square$

The structural gravitational input is therefore a single qualitative
statement: the leading shell entropy is boundary-accessible to the emission
operators.  Given Proposition~\ref{prop:dos}, the count of entropy-bearing
cells is $N(E)\simeq\Smicro(E)/\log q$ automatically; what the input adds is
that all of them emit.  The two luminosity laws $M^{-2}$ and $M^{-4}$ are the
two sides of this dichotomy, so the Schwarzschild power law is precisely the
measurement of the input.  The same input is used a second time, in
Section~\ref{sec:information-flow}, as the operator algebra on which the
decoupling of the shrinking emission map acts.

It is worth being explicit about what this input imports.  In holographic
theories the restriction of the accessible algebra to area-many boundary
degrees of freedom is a consequence of bulk locality and the gravitational
Gauss law \cite{Harlow2016}; a generic non-gravitational Hamiltonian has no
reason to organize its leading entropy this way.  Postulating boundary
accessibility therefore imports a semblance of geometric locality --- the
statement that the system radiates through a surface whose cell count tracks
its entropy --- without supplying the geometry that explains it.
Proposition~\ref{prop:area} is the quantitative form of this admission: the
imported structure is exactly one scaling law's worth, no more and no less,
because the assumption is equivalent to the measured luminosity exponent.
Why such a restricted emission algebra arises microscopically without bulk
locality is part of open problem~1 in Section~\ref{sec:open-problems}.

The two inputs are necessary for the thermodynamic package, but they do not
contain the fine-grained package.  Appendix~\ref{app:cayley-scaling} exhibits
shell Hamiltonians with identical state count, shrinking-dimension
bookkeeping, and emission-record structure whose radiation entropy
nevertheless fails the Page/island formula --- diverging from it for free
dynamics and plateauing above it for integrable dynamics --- while a fixed
chaotic Hamiltonian reproduces it.  The ingredient separating success from
failure is dynamical, not gravitational.

\subsection{Parameter-free relations}
\label{sec:cross-relations}

Once the two inputs are calibrated --- the constant $c$ by the temperature
law and boundary accessibility by the luminosity law --- the remaining
package contains no further adjustable parameters.  The following relations
are then fixed.
\begin{enumerate}
\item
\emph{Spectrum correction.}  The exact single-emission factor is
$\exp[\Smicro(M-\omega)-\Smicro(M)]=e^{-\beta(M)\omega+c\omega^2}$.  The
coefficient of $\omega^2$ is the same $c$ measured by the temperature law,
$c=\beta(M)/2M$, so the leading non-thermal distortion of the spectrum is
predicted rather than fitted \cite{ParikhWilczek2000}.
\item
\emph{Mean quantum energy.}  The thermal phase-space integrals give
$\langle\omega\rangle=(p+1)T=3T(M)$, hence
$P/\Gamma_{\rm quanta}=3T(M)$, up to greybody weighting in
$C_\lambda(E,\omega)$.
\item
\emph{Entropy per quantum.}  The total number of emitted quanta is
$N_{\rm quanta}=\int\Gamma_{\rm quanta}\,dt
\propto\int_0^{M_0}M^{-1}\,M^2\,dM\propto M_0^2\propto\Smicro(M_0)$:
the radiation carries order-one entropy per quantum.
\item
\emph{Lifetime--entropy relation.}  $\tau\propto M_0^3=(S_0/c)^{3/2}$, so
$\tau\propto S_0^{3/2}$ at fixed $c$.
\item
\emph{Page point.}  The radiation entropy attains its maximum $S_0/2$ when
$\Smicro(E)=S_0/2$, that is, at $M_{\rm Page}=M_0/\sqrt2$, reached at
$t_{\rm Page}=(1-2^{-3/2})\,\tau\simeq0.65\,\tau$; after this time
$I(R_{\rm early}{:}R_{\rm late})>0$.
\end{enumerate}
Relations 1--4 lie in the thermodynamic sector.  Relation 5 ties it to the
fine-grained sector of Section~\ref{sec:information-flow}: the time at which
late quanta become correlated with early quanta is fixed by the same two
calibrations that set the temperature and the luminosity.  Within the model
class the package is rigid --- the inputs cannot be adjusted to change one
item without moving the others.

\section{Isometry Reduction and Information Flow}
\label{sec:isometry-flow}
\label{sec:information-flow}

The thermodynamic calculation uses inclusive transition rates.  Fine-grained
information flow uses the corresponding maps between microcanonical Hilbert
spaces.  In the weak-coupling Markov regime, conditioning on one emitted
quantum gives an emission isometry
\begin{equation}
    V_E:\mathcal H_E\longrightarrow
    \bigoplus_{\omega,\lambda,\mu}
    \mathcal H_{E-\omega}\otimes \ket{\omega,\lambda,\mu}_{\rm R}.
\label{eq:emission-isometry-main}
\end{equation}
The labels record the emitted energy, outgoing channel, and boundary emission
operator.  The branch weights are the golden-rule weights derived above,
including the density-of-states ratio
$D_{E-\omega}/D_E$ and the area-sized sum over active boundary emitters.  The
standard quantum-jump construction of Eq.~\eqref{eq:emission-isometry-main}
and the waiting-time record are given in Appendix~\ref{app:weak-emission-map}.

For a narrow coarse-grained evaporation trajectory
\begin{equation}
    E_0\to E_1\to \cdots \to E_m=E,
\end{equation}
the one-step maps compose to
\begin{equation}
    V_{E_0\to E}:
    \mathcal H_{E_0}\longrightarrow
    \mathcal H_E\otimes \mathcal H_{R(E_0\to E)} .
\label{eq:composite-isometry-main}
\end{equation}
The radiation Hilbert space in Eq.~\eqref{eq:composite-isometry-main} carries
the emitted energies, channel labels, boundary labels, and time-bin labels
along the trajectory.  Energy conservation fixes the remaining shell, while
the density-of-states ratio fixes the thermodynamic branching weights.

The fine-grained assumption is a typical-encoding condition for the composite
map on the active microcanonical support.  In the regulated model this
condition is supplied by the approximate-design or tensor-product-expander
mixing blocks described in Section~2.  Page's theorem and standard decoupling
estimates then apply to the factorization
$\mathcal H_E\otimes \mathcal H_{R(E_0\to E)}$
\cite{Page1993Average,Page1993Info,HaydenHorodeckiWinterYard2008}.
Appendix~\ref{app:eth-decoupling} derives this condition from ETH-type
assumptions on a fixed shell Hamiltonian, with errors controlled by the
weak-coupling parameter, and Appendix~\ref{app:cayley-scaling} tests it
directly, by exact finite-size scaling, for a fixed deterministic shell
Hamiltonian and for free and integrable counterexamples.
Operationally, this means the following.  Let $X_E$ be the smaller factor in
the bipartition
$\mathcal H_E\otimes\mathcal H_{R(E_0\to E)}$.  For the code subspace under
consideration, the reduced state on $X_E$ is close to the corresponding
Page-typical state: maximally mixed in the equiprobable-history idealization,
or Hawking-weighted on the radiation branch when the golden-rule history
weights are kept.  Denote the trace-distance error by $\epsilon_{\rm Page}(E)$.
Then the entropy errors are bounded by the usual continuity estimates and are
absorbed in the $O(1)$ term below in the large-entropy regime.  Equivalently,
for code states purified by a reference, the complementary subsystem is
decoupled from that reference, up to the same error.

Let
\begin{equation}
    \Delta S_{\rm rad}(E)=\Smicro(E_0)-\Smicro(E),
    \qquad
    D_{\rm B}(E)=e^{\Smicro(E)}.
\end{equation}
Here $\Delta S_{\rm rad}$ is the coarse-grained thermodynamic entropy carried
by the emitted Hawking history, and $D_{\rm B}$ is the remaining core state
count.  Under the typical-encoding condition, the exterior radiation entropy is
\begin{equation}
    S_{\rm vN}(R;E)
    =
    \min\{\Delta S_{\rm rad}(E),\Smicro(E)\}
    +O(1).
\label{eq:pagecurve}
\end{equation}
The post-Page decreasing branch also follows directly from global purity:
$S_{\rm vN}(R)=S_{\rm vN}(B)\le \Smicro(E)$ once the remaining subsystem is the
smaller factor.  The typical-encoding condition is what saturates the smaller
factor on both sides of the Page transition.

The same statement has a replica form.  At second R\'enyi order, for
equiprobable radiation histories with effective dimension
$D_{\rm rad}=e^{\Delta S_{\rm rad}}$,
\begin{equation}
    \Tr\rho_R^2
    \simeq
    e^{-\Delta S_{\rm rad}(E)}
    +
    e^{-\Smicro(E)} .
\label{eq:renyi-two-island}
\end{equation}
For Hawking-weighted histories, labelled by probabilities $p_\alpha$, the
first term is replaced by $\Tr p^2$:
\begin{equation}
    \Tr\rho_R^2
    \simeq
    \Tr p^2+e^{-\Smicro(E)} .
\label{eq:weighted-renyi-two-main}
\end{equation}
Appendix~\ref{app:page-replica} gives the Page and permutation-moment
calculation behind Eqs.~\eqref{eq:renyi-two-island}
and~\eqref{eq:weighted-renyi-two-main}.

Equation~\eqref{eq:pagecurve} is the island formula in microcanonical
variables.  The first entry is the no-island Hawking entropy of the emitted
radiation history.  The second entry is the island branch, written here as the
logarithm of the remaining microcanonical Hilbert-space dimension.  In the
gravitational quantum-extremal-surface formula this second term is the
generalized entropy, with area contribution $A/4$ equal to the black-hole
entropy at the later mass \cite{Penington2019,
AlmheiriEngelhardtMarolfMaxfield2019,
AlmheiriHartmanMaldacenaShaghoulianTajdini2021}.  Here it is
$\log D_{\rm B}(E)$.  The second R\'enyi contraction is the corresponding
non-geometric replica contraction: replicas connect through the smaller
remaining core factor, with no spacetime saddle.

The correspondence is therefore:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
gravitational island calculation & Hamiltonian model \\
\midrule
radiation region & emitted radiation record \\
no-island saddle & Hawking-history contraction \\
island saddle & remaining-core contraction \\
area term $A/4G$ & $\log D_{\rm B}(E)=\Smicro(E)$ \\
QES minimization & minimization over the remaining shell \\
replica wormhole saddle & non-geometric replica contraction \\
entanglement wedge of the radiation & subsystems recoverable from the
radiation \\
island reconstruction & decoupling/Petz recovery from the radiation \\
smooth horizon at a fresh mode & thermal entanglement with its purifier \\
interior partner operators & state-dependent mirrors on the purifier \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
This table is a dictionary for the entropy, recovery, and mirror statements
only.  The Hamiltonian model has no spacetime region bounded by a quantum
extremal surface and no gravitational path integral.  The last four rows are
derived in Sections~\ref{sec:recovery} and~\ref{sec:mirrors}.

The distinction the dictionary encodes is between an \emph{operational}
island and a \emph{geometric} one.  By an operational island we mean a
subsystem whose Hilbert-space dimension bottlenecks the radiation entropy
--- the second branch of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pagecurve} --- and whose content
becomes recoverable from, and whose partner operators become supported on,
the radiation after the transition.  By a geometric island we mean a
spatially localizable region bounded by a quantum extremal surface.  The
model derives the operational island in full; it does not produce a
geometric one, and the dictionary above is the precise extent of the
correspondence.  The claim is therefore that the geometric island is
sufficient but not necessary for the entropy and recovery structure: any
dynamics with the stated state counting and decoupling produces the
operational island, with or without a spacetime realization.

Combining the finite-dimensional Page estimate with the regulated
weak-emission construction gives the schematic accuracy statement
\begin{equation}
    S_{\rm vN}(R;E)
    =
    \min\{\Delta S_{\rm rad}(E),\Smicro(E)\}
    +O(1)
    +O\!\left(\epsilon_{\rm Page}(E)\log D_{\rm min}(E)\right)
    +\delta_{\rm shell}+\delta_{\rm gb}+\delta_{\rm end}.
\end{equation}
Here $D_{\rm min}(E)=\min\{D_{\rm B}(E),e^{\Delta S_{\rm rad}(E)}\}$.
The first correction is the finite-dimensional Page correction.  The second is
the error from imperfect decoupling of the composite emission map.  The terms
$\delta_{\rm shell}$, $\delta_{\rm gb}$, and $\delta_{\rm end}$ denote,
respectively, finite shell-width errors, greybody/history-weighting errors,
and the breakdown of the quasi-stationary large-mass expansion near the
endpoint.

The Page time is determined by
\begin{equation}
    \Smicro(E_{\rm Page})
    =
    \frac12\Smicro(E_0),
\end{equation}
up to corrections set by shell width and greybody-weighted branching.  After
this time, a late radiation block is locally close to thermal while its
purifier is mostly the early radiation.  Thus
\begin{equation}
    I(R_{\rm early}:R_{\rm late})
    =
    S(R_{\rm early})+S(R_{\rm late})-S(R_{\rm early}R_{\rm late})
\end{equation}
becomes nonzero after the Page time, because
$S(R_{\rm early}R_{\rm late})=S(B)$ by global purity and $S(B)$ is bounded by
the smaller remaining Hilbert space.  Section~\ref{sec:recovery} upgrades
this correlation statement to an operational one: a recovery channel on the
radiation record exists for diary-sized code subspaces precisely after the
Page transition, and the Hayden-Preskill mirror bookkeeping follows from the
same decoupling estimate.

The fine-grained result therefore has two layers.  The Hamiltonian and
shell-averaged matrix elements give the density-of-states ratio in the
inclusive rates.  Global purity and the shrinking remaining Hilbert space give
the post-Page entropy bound.  Saturation of the rising branch, saturation of
the post-Page bound, and early/late correlations use decoupling of the
composite emission map on the active microcanonical support.  The weak-coupling
and quasi-stationary derivation applies while the emitted quantum energy is
small compared with the remaining energy.

\subsection{Recovery and complementary reconstruction}
\label{sec:recovery}

The entropy statements above have an operational layer.  Decoupling does not
only fix the radiation entropy: by Uhlmann's theorem it implies the existence
of recovery channels on one factor, and their provable absence on the
complementary factor.  This subsection records both directions.  The
statements are conditional on the same typical-encoding input as
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pagecurve} and use nothing beyond it.

Fix a code subspace $\mathcal C\subset\mathcal H_{E_0}$ with
$\dim\mathcal C=e^k$ --- the ``diary,'' a low-complexity perturbation of
shell states in the sense of Section~2.4 --- and let a reference $Q$ be
maximally entangled with $\mathcal C$.  Apply the composite emission map of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:composite-isometry-main}.  The global state is then pure on
$Q\otimes\mathcal H_E\otimes\mathcal H_{R(E_0\to E)}$, and we write $B$ for
the remaining core and $R$ for the radiation record.  Under the
typical-encoding condition, the standard second-moment decoupling estimate
gives, up to order-one prefactors and the additive design error,
\begin{equation}
    \big\|\rho_{QB}-\pi_Q\otimes\rho_B\big\|_1
    \lesssim
    \exp\!\Big\{-\tfrac12\big[\Delta S_{\rm rad}(E)-\Smicro(E)-k\big]\Big\},
\label{eq:decoupling-bound}
\end{equation}
where $\pi_Q$ is the maximally mixed reference state
\cite{HaydenPreskill2007,HaydenHorodeckiWinterYard2008,
SzehrDupuisTomamichelRenner2013,Dupuis2014}, together with the same estimate
with $B$ and $R$ exchanged and the exponent negated.

\begin{proposition}[Recovery from the radiation]
\label{prop:recovery}
If $\Delta S_{\rm rad}(E)\ge\Smicro(E)+k+2\log(1/\epsilon)$, there exists a
channel $\mathcal R$ acting only on the radiation record whose output,
together with $Q$, is $O(\epsilon)$-close in trace norm to the maximally
entangled state.  The code is recoverable from the radiation with error
$O(\epsilon)$.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof.}  Equation~\eqref{eq:decoupling-bound} makes $\rho_{QB}$
$\epsilon$-close to $\pi_Q\otimes\rho_B$.  The global state purifies
$\rho_{QB}$, and $\pi_Q\otimes\rho_B$ has a purification in which $Q$ is
maximally entangled with an ancilla.  Uhlmann's theorem supplies an isometry
on $R$ relating the two purifications up to error $O(\sqrt\epsilon)$;
composing with the partial trace gives $\mathcal R$.  The Petz channel of the
core restriction provides an explicit near-optimal recovery map
\cite{Petz1986,BarnumKnill2002}.  \hfill$\square$

\begin{proposition}[Complementary recovery]
\label{prop:complementary}
Let $w=k+2\log(1/\epsilon)$.
(i) If $\Delta S_{\rm rad}(E)\le\Smicro(E)-w$, the code is recoverable from
the remaining core with error $O(\epsilon)$, and no channel acting on the
radiation alone produces a state whose overlap with the maximally entangled
state exceeds $e^{-2k}+O(\epsilon)$ --- the value achieved by discarding the
radiation and guessing.
(ii) If $\Delta S_{\rm rad}(E)\ge\Smicro(E)+w$, the code is recoverable from
the radiation with error $O(\epsilon)$, and no channel acting on the core
alone does better than $e^{-2k}+O(\epsilon)$.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof.}  The recovery halves are Proposition~\ref{prop:recovery} and
its mirror under $B\leftrightarrow R$.  For the converses: in regime (i) the
exchanged form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:decoupling-bound} makes $\rho_{QR}$
$\epsilon$-close to $\pi_Q\otimes\rho_R$, and for any channel $\Lambda$ on
$R$ the overlap of $({\rm id}\otimes\Lambda)(\pi_Q\otimes\rho_R)$ with the
maximally entangled state is exactly $e^{-2k}$; trace-norm perturbations
change it by $O(\epsilon)$.  Regime (ii) is the same with the factors
exchanged.  \hfill$\square$

For diary-sized codes, $k=O(1)$, the two regimes meet in a window of width
$O(\log(1/\epsilon))$ nats around the island transition
$\Delta S_{\rm rad}=\Smicro$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pagecurve}: in Schwarzschild
variables, at $M_{\rm Page}=M_0/\sqrt2$ and $t_{\rm Page}\simeq0.65\,\tau$
(Section~\ref{sec:cross-relations}).  Before the transition the code
information sits in the core and the radiation record carries none of it;
after the transition the assignment is reversed.  This is complementary
recovery in the sense of the error-correction formulation of holographic
reconstruction \cite{AlmheiriDongHarlow2015,DongHarlowWall2016}, with the
recovery channel on the radiation playing the role of entanglement-wedge
reconstruction of the island
\cite{CotlerHaydenPeningtonSaltonSwingleWalter2019,Penington2019}.  The
correspondence rows added to the dictionary above are these two statements.

The same estimate gives the information-mirror statement for an old black
hole \cite{HaydenPreskill2007}.  If the diary is deposited at a shell $E_1$
past the Page transition and the decoder holds the early radiation that
purifies the core at the deposit time, the decoupling exponent counts only
the radiation emitted after the deposit, so $k+2\log(1/\epsilon)$ additional
nats suffice.  By relation~3 of Section~\ref{sec:cross-relations}, each
emitted quantum carries order-one entropy, so a diary of $k$ nats becomes
radiation-recoverable after $O(k+\log(1/\epsilon))$ additional quanta.
Explicit decoders are known in the design setting \cite{YoshidaKitaev2017}.
What the model does not supply is the dynamical scrambling-time estimate
$t_{\rm scr}\sim\beta\log\Smicro$ \cite{SekinoSusskind2008}: the bookkeeping
here is in emitted quanta, conditional on per-step mixing, not in an
independently derived mixing time.

Three qualifications.  First, what is reconstructed is code information ---
the microstate data of the diary --- not interior geometry; the model has no
interior, and these statements are the algebraic content of island
reconstruction, not a geometric one.  Section~\ref{sec:mirrors} extends this
algebraic layer to interior partner operators, with the same caveat.  Second,
the statements are conditional
on the same decoupling input as the entropy results, with the same error
budget; they are corollaries, not new assumptions.  Third, for
Hawking-weighted histories the flat dimensions are replaced by one-shot
entropies of the weighted history distribution, and the one-shot decoupling
theorem gives the corresponding statement with $\Delta S_{\rm rad}$ replaced
by a smooth min-entropy \cite{Dupuis2014}.

\subsection{Interior operators and state dependence}
\label{sec:mirrors}

The recovery statements above concern code information.  This subsection
extends them to the model's analogue of interior mode operators.  The
gravitational questions are where the partner of a late Hawking mode lives,
and whether operators describing it can be chosen independently of the
microstate \cite{AMPS2013,PapadodimasRaju2013,PapadodimasRaju2014,
VerlindeVerlinde2013,Harlow2016}.  Both have sharp answers here: partner
(mirror) operators exist for every microstate and move from the core to the
early radiation at the Page transition, and no state-independent choice is
possible.  The first statement is a corollary of decoupling; the second is a
finite-dimensional counting argument requiring no analytic care.

Let $\mathsf b$ denote the record register of the most recent emission step,
with Hilbert space of dimension $d_b$, and let $P$ denote everything else ---
the remaining core $B$ together with the earlier radiation record $R$ --- so
that the global pure state lives on
$\mathcal H_{\mathsf b}\otimes\mathcal H_P$.  Call a global pure state
$\ket\Psi$ \emph{smooth at the fresh mode} if its reduced state on
$\mathsf b$ is the full-rank thermal state $\rho_\beta$ of the mode at the
instantaneous temperature.  This is the model analogue of the
infalling-vacuum condition: the freshly emitted mode is thermally entangled
with the rest, not pure.  The weak-emission channel produces exactly this
marginal, with the detailed-balance weights of Section~3, up to the same
$O(\Gamma_E/\Delta E)$ and finite-energy corrections as the spectrum.

For a smooth state, finite-dimensional modular theory --- here, the
$\rho_\beta^{1/2}$-twisted transpose on the Schmidt partner --- supplies, for
each operator $X$ on the mode, a unique \emph{mirror} operator
$\widetilde X_\Psi$ supported on the Schmidt partner of $\mathsf b$ inside
$P$, characterized by
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde X_\Psi\ket\Psi
    =
    \big(\rho_\beta^{1/2}X^\dagger\rho_\beta^{-1/2}\otimes
    \mathbb 1_P\big)\ket\Psi,
    \qquad
    [\widetilde X_\Psi,\,Y\otimes\mathbb 1_P]=0
    \quad\text{for exterior }Y.
\label{eq:mirror-relation}
\end{equation}
Write $X^\sharp\equiv\rho_\beta^{1/2}X^\dagger\rho_\beta^{-1/2}$ for the
twisted adjoint appearing in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mirror-relation}.  For a mode
annihilation operator $a$ at frequency $\omega$ the first relation
reads $\tilde a\ket\Psi=e^{-\beta\omega/2}a^\dagger\ket\Psi$, the defining
relation of the state-dependent interior constructions
\cite{PapadodimasRaju2013,PapadodimasRaju2014}, and the induced two-point
functions $\langle Y\widetilde X\rangle_\Psi
=\Tr[\rho_\beta^{1/2}Y\rho_\beta^{1/2}X^\dagger]$ are the two-sided thermal
correlators of a smooth horizon.  The construction visibly depends on
$\ket\Psi$ through the Schmidt basis and weights.  The next proposition shows
the dependence is not removable.

\begin{proposition}[No state-independent interior]
\label{prop:no-go}
Fix a mode operator $X$ with $[X^\sharp,X]\neq0$ (any raising or
lowering operator qualifies).  No single operator $\widetilde X$ satisfies
the action relation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mirror-relation} for every smooth state
while commuting with the exterior algebra: any $\widetilde X$ satisfying the
action relation on all smooth states equals
$X^\sharp\otimes\mathbb 1_P$ --- an exterior operator --- whose commutator
with the exterior algebra is of order one.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof.}  Smooth states span the full Hilbert space: fixing one smooth
$\ket{\Psi_0}$, the states $(\mathbb 1\otimes U)\ket{\Psi_0}$ are smooth for
every unitary $U$ on $P$ (the mode marginal is unchanged), unitaries span
the operator algebra of $P$ linearly, and
$(\mathbb 1\otimes\mathcal B(\mathcal H_P))\ket{\Psi_0}
=\mathcal H_{\mathsf b}\otimes\mathcal H_P$ because $\rho_\beta$ has full
rank.  The action relation is linear in $\ket\Psi$, so holding on a spanning
set it forces the operator identity
$\widetilde X=X^\sharp\otimes\mathbb 1_P$.
For $X=a$ the commutator is
$[e^{-\beta\omega/2}a^\dagger,a]\otimes\mathbb 1_P\neq0$.  \hfill$\square$

The spanning family is dynamically legitimate --- same fresh emission,
different past --- and the argument is robust: averaging over the unitary
family shows that an operator satisfying the action relation to accuracy
$\epsilon$ on typical smooth microstates equals the exterior operator up to
$\epsilon$ in a thermally weighted two-norm, leaving the order-one
commutator defect intact.  This is the finite-dimensional form of the
state-dependence argument of \cite{PapadodimasRaju2013,PapadodimasRaju2014}:
demanding one fixed linear operator forces $\tilde a=e^{-\beta\omega/2}
a^\dagger$, which is not behind the horizon at all.

\begin{proposition}[Location of the mirror]
\label{prop:mirror-location}
Let $w=\log d_b+2\log(1/\epsilon)$.
(i) If $\Delta S_{\rm rad}(E)\le\Smicro(E)-w$, mirror operators for the
fresh mode supported on the core exist for every smooth microstate, with
one fixed core operator serving all states produced by the emission isometry
from the shell, and the relations of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mirror-relation} holding
on products of exterior operators applied to the state up to
$O(\sqrt\epsilon)$; no operator supported on the radiation record reproduces
the mirror correlators to better than $O(\epsilon)$.
(ii) If $\Delta S_{\rm rad}(E)\ge\Smicro(E)+w$, then for each decoupled code
subspace there is a fixed operator on the early radiation record satisfying
the mirror relations, in the same sense, for every state of the subspace ---
including superpositions --- up to $O(\sqrt\epsilon)$; no core-supported
operator does better than $O(\epsilon)$.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof.}  Pre-Page the rising branch of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pagecurve} is
additive over radiation factors, so $I(\mathsf b:R)=O(\epsilon)$ and the
purifier of the fresh mode embeds in the core up to $O(\sqrt\epsilon)$ by
Uhlmann's theorem; conjugating the modular mirror through the embedding gives
$\widetilde X_B$.  It is fixed across the shell because the accessible smooth
states span only the image of the emission isometry, a subspace of dimension
$D$ inside the larger target space, so the spanning hypothesis of
Proposition~\ref{prop:no-go} is not met --- which is precisely why a fixed
choice is possible there.  The converse follows because any operator
satisfying the mirror correlators on $R$ would make the connected
mode--radiation correlators order one, while they are bounded by
$\|\rho_{\mathsf bR}-\rho_{\mathsf b}\otimes\rho_R\|_1=O(\epsilon)$.
Post-Page the purifier of a fresh block is the early radiation
(Section~\ref{sec:information-flow}), $I(\mathsf b:B)=O(\epsilon)$, and the
same Uhlmann conjugation lands the mirror on $R$; the isometry can be chosen
once per decoupled code subspace because the decoupling bound
Eq.~\eqref{eq:decoupling-bound} holds uniformly on it, and the relations are
then linear over the subspace.  The converse exchanges $B$ and $R$.
\hfill$\square$

The two regimes meet in the same $O(\log(1/\epsilon))$ window around the
Page transition as complementary recovery: the mirror's support tracks the
entanglement wedge.  Post-Page, the interior partner of a fresh mode is a
subsystem of the early radiation in the code encoding --- the algebraic
content of the $A=R_B$ and ER=EPR proposals
\cite{VerlindeVerlinde2013,MaldacenaSusskind2013}, with the island
reconstruction realized by the Petz pullback of
Proposition~\ref{prop:recovery}.

This dissolves the monogamy step of the firewall argument within the model.
The argument needs three premises for an old black hole \cite{AMPS2013}:
(P1)~unitarity makes the fresh mode strongly correlated with the early
radiation; (P2)~smoothness makes it thermally entangled with an interior
partner; (P3)~the partner is a system independent of the early radiation.
Premises (P1) and (P2) hold here --- (P1) is derived and (P2) is the
constructed mirror --- while (P3) fails, and by
Proposition~\ref{prop:no-go} it \emph{must} fail: post-Page the partner is
supported inside the early radiation, so the two entanglements of (P1)
and~(P2) are the same entanglement counted twice, and no state-independent
operator independent of the radiation could have done the mirror's job.  The
model thus locates the premise at which the argument breaks and shows the
break is forced, not optional.

The cost of state dependence is also quantified.  The objection to
state-dependent interiors is that quantum mechanics acts linearly, so
observables should not depend on which superposition of microstates is
considered \cite{AMPSS2013,MarolfPolchinski2016}.  In the model the boundary
is explicit: one fixed reconstruction serves an entire decoupled code
subspace of dimension $e^k$ with
$k\le\Delta S_{\rm rad}-\Smicro-2\log(1/\epsilon)$, superpositions within the
subspace included, while Proposition~\ref{prop:no-go} forbids extending any
fixed choice to families spanning the shell.  State dependence here is
subspace dependence with the decoupling budget; linearity violations can
arise only across code subspaces exceeding the budget.

Two further objections to state-dependent constructions can be checked
directly because the model is explicit.  For superpositions of
\emph{macroscopically distinct} microstates --- components in different
energy shells --- the construction is sectorwise: components of different
mass emit distinguishable radiation histories, so the record decoheres the
sectors, and any sectorwise-defined mirror returns the Born-weighted mixture
of per-sector correlators.  Linearity is not violated for cross-shell
superpositions; it is enforced by the same record that carries the Hawking
flux.  For the frozen-vacuum problem \cite{Bousso2014} --- if mirrors are
defined relative to whichever state is handed to us, even an excited interior
is declared vacuum --- the model's answer is that the reference is not chosen
by hand: smoothness is a property, the full-rank thermal marginal of the
fresh record, which the golden-rule channel produces dynamically through
detailed balance.  Non-smooth states exist in the Hilbert space, are
distinguishable by exterior measurements on the mode, and the construction
simply does not apply to them; excitations on top of a smooth state within
the code subspace are counted by the mirror algebra in the ordinary way.
The caveat is that this resolves the ambiguity for the model's marginal
notion of smoothness; the gravitational frozen-vacuum problem concerns the
infalling mode structure, which the model does not represent.

\begin{proposition}[Complexity of the radiation mirror]
\label{prop:mirror-complexity}
Let $\widetilde X_R$ be the post-Page mirror of
Proposition~\ref{prop:mirror-location}(ii), supported on the early radiation.
The quantum circuit complexity of implementing $\widetilde X_R$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal C(\widetilde X_R)
    \;\geq\;
    \exp\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\Smicro(E)\bigr)
    \label{eq:mirror-complexity}
\end{equation}
for any smooth microstate and any shell energy $E$ in the post-Page era
($\Delta S_{\rm rad}(E)>\Smicro(E)$).
The bound decreases monotonically along the evaporation trajectory,
reaching zero only at the endpoint where $\Smicro(E)\to0$.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof sketch.}
By the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:mirror-location}, the mirror is
constructed from the Uhlmann isometry $W:\mathcal H_{\mathsf b}\to
\mathcal H_R$ that embeds the Schmidt partner of the fresh mode into the
radiation.  This isometry equals the adjoint of the Petz recovery channel of
Proposition~\ref{prop:recovery} applied to the partner observable, which
involves $\rho_R^{-1/2}$ on the $e^{\Delta S_{\rm rad}}$-dimensional
radiation register.  The Harlow--Hayden theorem
\cite{HarlowHayden2013} states that any decoder implementing such
a recovery from scrambled Hawking radiation has circuit complexity at least
$\exp(\frac{1}{2}S_{\rm BH})$; in the model $S_{\rm BH}=\Smicro(E)$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:dos}), giving~\eqref{eq:mirror-complexity}.  The
Python's-lunch bound of \cite{BrownGharibyanPeningtonSusskind2020} tightens
the exponent to $\Smicro(E)$.
The monotone decrease follows from $\Smicro(E)$ decreasing along the
trajectory as $E$ shrinks; the bound vanishes only when $\Smicro\to0$.
\hfill$\square$

\smallskip
Propositions~\ref{prop:no-go} and~\ref{prop:mirror-complexity} are
complementary.  Proposition~\ref{prop:no-go} shows that any operator
simple enough to be state-independent --- implementable by a fixed circuit
of sub-exponential depth --- has an $O(1)$ commutator defect and fails to
be a genuine mirror for typical smooth microstates.  Proposition~\ref{prop:mirror-complexity}
quantifies the cost: the minimal circuit depth sufficient for the mirror
relations to hold is $\exp(\frac{1}{2}\Smicro)$.  The complexity barrier is
not an accident; it is the price of forced state dependence.  The interior
partner exists in the radiation without being feasibly accessible ---
the same separation as for the recovery channel
(Section~\ref{sec:open-problems}, item~4).

\begin{corollary}[Accessibility--complexity trade-off]
\label{cor:accessibility-complexity}
Along the post-Page trajectory, the decoupling budget
$k_{\rm max}(E)=\Delta S_{\rm rad}(E)-\Smicro(E)-O(\log(1/\varepsilon))$
(Propositions~\ref{prop:recovery} and~\ref{prop:mirror-location})
and the decoder complexity lower bound
$\mathcal C_{\rm min}(E)=\exp(\frac{1}{2}\Smicro(E))$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:mirror-complexity})
are related by $\Delta S_{\rm rad}(E)=S_0-\Smicro(E)+O(\Gamma_E/\Delta E)$, giving
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:accessibility-complexity}
  k_{\rm max}(E) = S_0 - 2\Smicro(E) - O\!\left(\log\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\right),
  \qquad
  \mathcal C_{\rm min}(E) = \exp\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\Smicro(E)\bigr).
\end{equation}
A computational budget $D$ is sufficient only when $\Smicro(E)\le 2\log D$,
at which stage $k_{\rm max}(E)\ge S_0-4\log D$.
For a polynomial-time observer ($D=S_0^c$, fixed $c\ge1$), the threshold
$\Smicro(E)\le 2c\log S_0$ is met only when the black hole has evaporated
to within $2c\log S_0$ bits of the endpoint; at that stage
$k_{\rm max}=S_0-O(\log S_0)$.
The complexity barrier recedes as the interior evaporates: nearly all
interior information becomes feasibly accessible, but only once the black
hole has nearly ceased to exist.
\end{corollary}

As with the recovery statements, what is constructed here is an operator
algebra with smooth-horizon correlators, not interior geometry or an
infalling experience; these are the algebraic conditions any gravitational
interior description must reproduce
(Section~\ref{sec:open-problems}, item~2).

\section{Finite-Dimensional Regulator}

This section makes the finite-dimensional part of the argument explicit.  The
Hamiltonian calculation uses continuous energy and radiation labels, while
Page and decoupling estimates apply to finite Hilbert spaces.  The regulator
below replaces the continuous labels by finite bands and wave packets while
preserving the weak-coupling emission branch.  Appendix~\ref{app:finite-check}
gives a small finite-dimensional check of the Page-theorem step and of the
distinction between coarse energy-bin thermality and fine-grained radiation
entropy.

The continuum labels are replaced by finite labels in two steps.  Black-hole
energies are grouped into microcanonical bands.  Outgoing radiation modes are
grouped into wave packets with finite frequency, channel, and time-bin labels.
After this replacement, each emission branch is a finite map from one
microcanonical Hilbert space to a smaller microcanonical Hilbert space tensored
with a radiation label.  The continuum formulas are recovered by refining the
bands and wave packets.

Choose energy bands $I_i=[E_i,E_i+\Delta E]$, with $\Delta E$ large compared
with the microscopic level spacing and small on the scale over which
$\Smicro(E)$ changes.  The black-hole Hilbert space becomes
\begin{equation}
    \HB^{\rm reg}
    =
    \bigoplus_i \mathcal H_i,
    \qquad
    \dim\mathcal H_i
    =
    D_i
    \simeq \rhoB(E_i)\Delta E .
\end{equation}
The radiation continuum is regulated by wave-packet modes labelled by a
frequency bin $\alpha$, an outgoing radiation label $\lambda$, an inclusive
emission-channel label $\mu$, and an outgoing time bin $\tau$,
\begin{equation}
    \ket{\omega,\lambda,\mu}_{\rm R}
    \quad\longrightarrow\quad
    \ket{\alpha,\lambda,\mu,\tau}_{\rm R}.
\end{equation}
The bin dimensions and weights are chosen so that sums over
$\alpha,\lambda,\mu,\tau$ converge to the continuum integrals in the
weak-coupling rate formula.

With this regulator, the emission branch of one weak-coupling time step is a
finite Stinespring map
\begin{equation}
    V_i^{\rm reg}:
    \mathcal H_i
    \longrightarrow
    \bigoplus_{\alpha,\lambda,\mu}
    \mathcal H_{i-\alpha}\otimes
    \ket{\alpha,\lambda,\mu,\tau}_{\rm R},
\end{equation}
where $\omega_\alpha$ is a representative frequency in bin $\alpha$, and
$i-\alpha$ denotes the band containing $E_i-\omega_\alpha$.  Its branch
weights are the discretized versions of $w_E(\omega,\lambda,\mu)$, including
the density-of-states ratio and the area-sized emission-channel count.
Refining the energy bands, frequency bins, and time bins recovers the
continuum rate calculation, while keeping finite-dimensional spaces at every
intermediate stage.

The regulated map is the finite-dimensional object to which Page's theorem is
applied.  The regulator preserves the weak-coupling emission branch and replaces
the continuous energy and radiation labels by finite bands and wave packets.
This is the only role of the regulator in the main argument.  It supplies the
finite-dimensional setting in which Page and decoupling statements are
applied to the Hamiltonian emission map.

\section{Comparison with Existing Models}
\label{sec:comparison}

Tensor-network, qubit-transport, and random-circuit models give explicitly
unitary descriptions of information transfer from a shrinking black-hole
subsystem into radiation
\cite{LeutheusserVanRaamsdonk2017,Avery2013,OsugaPage2018,
PiroliSunderhaufQi2020,HottaNambuYamaguchi2018}.  Their basic dynamical object
is an isometry between black-hole and radiation Hilbert spaces.  Here that
isometry is the weak-coupling emission map of an energy-resolved
Hamiltonian.

Recent non-gravitational models show that Page-like entropy dynamics can occur
in ordinary quantum systems, including open-system models, exactly solvable
free-fermion dynamics, and interacting spin chains
\cite{Glatthard2024,Glatthard2025,Kehrein2024,RayDharKulkarni2025}.  These
models separate Page-like entropy dynamics from strictly gravitational
mechanisms.  The present construction ties the same information-flow logic to
a Schwarzschild density of states, an energy-conserving emission channel,
and the Schwarzschild rate scalings.

Quantum-optical models of unitary black-hole evaporation track energy transfer
and approximate thermality in explicitly unitary models
\cite{Alsing2025,Alsing2026}.  Related recent protocol models use
coherently branched evaporation histories or time-dependent interior-radiation
pair dynamics to maintain microscopic unitarity
\cite{AkilGiannelliModestoDahlstenChiribellaBrukner2025,Abutaleb2026}.  The
thermal factor here comes from the microcanonical density-of-states ratio of
the black-hole subsystem.
Local spin-chain models with a dynamically changing system/environment split
also reproduce Page-curve behavior and show how much of that behavior can be
kinematic \cite{JonesAltaieVarcoe2026}.  The Hamiltonian here connects the
shrinking state count to emitted energy and rate data as well as to the
radiation entropy curve.
Matrix-theory black-hole models provide a more microscopic route to clump
evaporation and decreasing active degrees of freedom, with a different
holographic motivation
\cite{BanksFischlerKlebanovSusskind1998,BerkowitzHanadaMaltz2016}.

The Hamiltonian class uses four ingredients: boundary-accessible Schwarzschild
state counting, an outgoing continuum, local weak emission from the active
boundary cells, and decoupling of the composite emission map, supplied by
designs and checked for a fixed deterministic mixer in
Appendix~\ref{app:cayley-scaling}.
The area-sized inclusive strength follows from the boundary operator count.
This combination ties Page's isometry logic to the Schwarzschild thermodynamic
scalings.  The radiation entropy also has the island-formula structure, and
its second R\'enyi purity has the corresponding two-term replica competition,
with the gravitational area term replaced by the microcanonical entropy of the
remaining subsystem.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The model separates the exterior evaporation package into a small number of
Hamiltonian ingredients.  The density of states fixes the thermodynamic
behavior: temperature, negative heat capacity, the local thermal factor, and
the leading finite-energy correction.  Boundary accessibility and local weak
coupling turn that state count into an area-sized inclusive emission strength,
which fixes the Schwarzschild number flux, power, and lifetime scalings.  The
fine-grained entropy statements use global purity, the shrinking remaining
Hilbert space, and decoupling of the composite emission map.

The island comparison is algebraic.  The no-island branch is the entropy of
the emitted Hawking history.  The island branch is the logarithm of the
remaining microcanonical state count.  In gravitational calculations that
second term is the generalized entropy of a quantum extremal surface; in this
Hamiltonian model it is $\log D_{\rm B}(E)$.  The second R\'enyi calculation
has the same two replica contractions: the post-Page contraction connects
replicas through the smaller remaining core factor.
Section~\ref{sec:recovery} adds the operational layer: complementary
recovery of code information, switching from the core to the radiation at
the island transition, which is the model's version of entanglement-wedge
reconstruction.  Section~\ref{sec:mirrors} extends this to interior partner
operators: mirrors with smooth-horizon correlators exist for every
microstate, are necessarily state-dependent, and cross from the core to the
radiation at the same transition, which dissolves the monogamy step of the
firewall argument at the algebraic level.

The construction is conditional in two places.  First, the Schwarzschild
density of states is an input to the Hamiltonian.  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:dos} it is forced by the targeted temperature law, and
by Proposition~\ref{prop:area} its boundary-accessible realization is forced
by the targeted luminosity law, so the model takes exactly two measured
scalings and no further gravitational structure
(Table~\ref{tab:ledger}).  A more microscopic
non-gravitational model would derive this boundary-accessible state count from
simpler degrees of freedom.  Second, the main text uses approximate-design or
tensor-product-expander mixing to supply the decoupling
needed for the Page curve and early/late correlations.  Known
Hamiltonian-design constructions provide related Hamiltonian-generated
versions under their own ensemble, locality, or timing assumptions
\cite{NakataHircheKoashiWinter2017,CuiSchusterMaoHuangBrandao2025,
ZhouZhouSonner2026}.  Appendix~\ref{app:eth-decoupling} replaces the design
assumption by ETH-type spectral conditions on a fixed shell Hamiltonian ---
smooth jump-operator matrix elements, fourth-moment factorization, and a
nondegenerate spectrum imply the required second-moment decoupling, with
errors controlled by the same weak-coupling parameter as the golden rule ---
and shows that the result self-averages: under the eighth-moment extension
of the same hypothesis, a single fixed Hamiltonian reproduces the ensemble
Page curve with fluctuations $O(e^{-S_0/2})$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:self-averaging}).
Appendix~\ref{app:cayley-scaling} adds
finite-size-scaling evidence that a fixed, time-independent,
vertex-transitive shell Hamiltonian supplies the same decoupling at exactly
solvable sizes, with seed scatter falling at the predicted concentration
rate.

The natural fixed-Hamiltonian problem is more specific than asking for a
generic scrambler.  The weak-emission block must decouple the code while it
maps one shell to a smaller shell.  Appendix~\ref{app:fixed-hamiltonian-target}
spells out one concrete Cayley-expander target.  Vertex transitivity makes the
boundary channels equivalent by symmetry; the remaining dynamical requirement
is sectorwise second-moment decoupling for the open weak-emission channel,
with exact symmetry charges tracked or recorded in the radiation.  This is the
main route by which the abstract mixer could be replaced by a simple
deterministic sparse Hamiltonian.  Appendix~\ref{app:cayley-scaling} carries
out the first finite-size step of this programme, for a circulant Cayley
mixer and a scramble-then-reveal step channel.

The mixing assumption should also be compared with the dynamical signature
that defines black holes as fast scramblers.  A Schwarzschild black hole
emits roughly one quantum per time $\beta$ while scrambling in
$t_{\rm scr}\sim\beta\log\Smicro$ \cite{SekinoSusskind2008}, so order
$\log\Smicro$ quanta leave per scrambling time; per-step mixing is therefore
\emph{stronger} than the physical rate, not weaker.  Weakening it to the
physical rate changes little: the entropy package needs decoupling only on
the accumulated radiation, so a mixing lag of $O(\log\Smicro)$ quanta widens
the Page window by a relative $O(\log S_0/S_0)$, while the information-mirror
delay of Section~\ref{sec:recovery} grows from $O(k)$ quanta to
$O(\log\Smicro)$ --- which \emph{recovers} the original Hayden--Preskill
estimate rather than spoiling it.  On the mechanism side, nonlocal or
expander interaction graphs are precisely the architecture the fast-scrambling
conjecture requires \cite{LashkariStanfordHastingsHaydenOsborne2013}, and the
vertex-transitive circulant family of Appendix~\ref{app:cayley-scaling} is in
this class; the chaos bound $\lambda_L\le2\pi/\beta$
\cite{MaldacenaShenkerStanford2016} caps the rate at which any Hamiltonian
can discharge the assumption.  What is missing is a derivation of
$t_{\rm scr}\sim\beta\log\Smicro$ from the shell Hamiltonian itself ---
e.g.\ OTOC decay for the Cayley family --- which would tie the mixing rate to
the temperature; this is addressed in
Proposition~\ref{prop:scrambling-time} below, with the remaining
undetermined ingredient identified in open problem~4 of
Section~\ref{sec:open-problems}.

\begin{proposition}[Scrambling time from the chaos bound]
\label{prop:scrambling-time}
Let $H_E$ be a shell Hamiltonian with $\Smicro$ effective qubits satisfying
the ETH spectral conditions of Appendix~\ref{app:eth-decoupling}.  The
in-shell scrambling time satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:scrambling-time}
  t_{\rm scr} = \frac{\log\Smicro}{\lambda_L}
  \;\geq\; \frac{\beta\log\Smicro}{2\pi},
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_L\le2\pi/\beta$ is the Maldacena--Shenker--Stanford chaos
bound \cite{MaldacenaShenkerStanford2016}, and equality is achieved by
maximally chaotic Hamiltonians ($\lambda_L=2\pi/\beta$).  The emission
rate is one quantum per $O(\beta)$ time, so the shell re-scrambles in
at least $(\log\Smicro)/(2\pi)$ emission steps.  The per-step mixing assumption of
Section~\ref{sec:isometry-flow} is therefore an overapproximation by a
factor of $O(\log\Smicro)$; relaxing it to the physical mixing rate
shifts the Page window by a relative $O(\log S_0/S_0)$ and converts the
Hayden--Preskill $O(k)$-step information delay of
Section~\ref{sec:recovery} into the physical $O(\log\Smicro)$-step
estimate of \cite{HaydenPreskill2007}.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof sketch.}
For a chaotic ETH Hamiltonian, the squared-commutator out-of-time-order
correlator $C(t)=-\langle[W(t),V]^2\rangle_\beta$ for generic local
operators $W$, $V$ at separation $>1$ vanishes at $t=0$ (spatial
locality) and grows in the Lyapunov window $t_{\rm eq}\ll t\ll t_{\rm
scr}$ as
\[
  C(t) \;\sim\; \frac{c}{\Smicro}\,e^{\lambda_L t},
\]
where the $1/\Smicro$ prefactor reflects the $O(1)$ initial reach of a
local operator in an $\Smicro$-qubit system.  Scrambling is complete when
$C(t_{\rm scr})=O(1)$, giving $t_{\rm scr}=(\log\Smicro)/\lambda_L$.
The chaos bound then supplies the upper bound on $\lambda_L$ and the
lower bound on $t_{\rm scr}$.  For a maximally chaotic Hamiltonian
($\lambda_L=2\pi/\beta$), this is the Sekino--Susskind fast-scrambling
estimate \cite{SekinoSusskind2008}.  The emission lag follows because one
quantum is emitted per $O(\beta)$ time (Section~\ref{sec:isometry-flow}),
so $t_{\rm scr}/t_{\rm emit}\ge(\log\Smicro)/(2\pi)$.~$\square$

\smallskip\noindent\emph{What Proposition~\ref{prop:scrambling-time} derives and what it assumes.}
The formula $t_{\rm scr}=(\log\Smicro)/\lambda_L$ follows from ETH and
the definition of scrambling.  The chaos bound gives the lower bound
$t_{\rm scr}\ge(\beta\log\Smicro)/(2\pi)$.
What is not derived is whether $\lambda_L=2\pi/\beta$ for the specific
shell Hamiltonian: this is the saturation of the chaos bound, which holds
for black holes by gravitational shock-wave arguments \cite{ShenkerStanford2014}
and for SYK in the large-$N$ limit, but is not a consequence of ETH
alone.  The Cayley family's OTOC saturation at $t_{\rm mix}=5$
steps for $n=6$--$12$ qubits (Appendix~\ref{app:cayley-scaling}) is
consistent with $t_{\rm scr}\sim O(\log n)$ for those sizes, but does
not determine $\lambda_L$ from a time series.  Extracting $\lambda_L$
from OTOC vs.\ $t$ for the Cayley family is the remaining open question
(item~4 below); if it saturates the bound, the scrambling-time estimate
becomes a derived consequence of the shell dynamics.

\subsection{Replica wormholes as permutation contractions}
\label{sec:wormhole-ETH}

The gravitational replica path integral for $\Tr\rho_R^n$, summed over
$n$-replica topologies, assigns each permutation $\sigma\in S_n$ a saddle
weight proportional to $b^{C(\sigma)}\,r^{C(\tau\sigma)}/(br)^n$, with
$\tau=(12\cdots n)$ the cyclic boundary permutation, $b=e^{S_{\rm BH}}$,
and $r=e^{S_{\rm rad}}$
\cite{PeningtonShenkerStanfordYang2022,AlmheiriHartmanMaldacenaShaghoulianTajdini2020}.
The identity saddle ($\sigma=e$, weight $r^{-1}$) is the disconnected
geometry giving the Hawking/no-island answer; the dominant connected saddle
($\sigma=\tau^{-1}$, weight $b^{-1}$) is the Euclidean wormhole giving the
island answer; the full sum reproduces the Page curve.

This is precisely the permutation sum~\eqref{eq:renyi-n-perm} of
Appendix~\ref{app:page-replica}, derived there from the Haar-random replica
formula and already used in Section~\ref{sec:information-flow}.
Under the identification $b=D_{\rm B}(E)=e^{\Smicro}$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:dos}, the gravitational saddle weight and the
Haar replica weight are the same function of $\sigma$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:eth-decoupling} establishes that a single fixed
Hamiltonian satisfying the ETH spectral conditions produces the same
second-moment structure as a Haar isometry, grounding the permutation sum
in non-random Hamiltonian dynamics.
Proposition~\ref{prop:self-averaging} then upgrades the statement from the
ETH ensemble to a single fixed Hamiltonian: the instance purity deviates from
the permutation sum by $O(e^{-S_0/2})$.
Taken together:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:wormhole-ETH-chain}
  \underbrace{
    \frac{1}{(br)_n}
    \sum_{\sigma\in S_n}
    b^{C(\sigma)}\,r^{C(\tau\sigma)}
  }_{\text{replica saddle sum (gravity, Prop.~\ref{prop:dos})}}
  \;=\;
  \underbrace{\E_{\rm ETH}\,\Tr\rho_R^n}_{\text{Prop.~\ref{prop:eth-decoupling}}}
  \;=\;
  \Tr\rho_R^n\big|_{\text{fixed }H}+O\!\left(e^{-S_0/2}\right).
\end{equation}

Three consequences follow.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{The wormhole is a geometric Wick contraction.}
The topology change of the replica wormhole implements the same combinatorial
pairing as the ETH Wick contraction of the emission-channel matrix elements:
the identity versus the cyclic inverse of $\tau$.  The geometry encodes
the statistics; once those statistics are fixed by ETH and the spectrum,
the wormhole adds no computational information beyond what follows from
non-gravitational quantum mechanics.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Half-wormholes are the connected ETH classes.}
The sub-leading connected saddles of \cite{SaadShenkerStanfordYao2021}
correspond to the twenty connected permutation classes of
Proposition~\ref{prop:self-averaging}, each of size $O(e^{-S_0})$.
A single fixed Hamiltonian already accounts for them through self-averaging;
there is nothing to restore, because the instance entropy is already the
ensemble answer at this order.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{The only gravitational input is the spectrum.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:wormhole-ETH-chain} holds by non-gravitational quantum
mechanics once $b=e^{\Smicro}$ is given.  The identification
$S_{\rm BH}=\Smicro$ --- Bekenstein--Hawking entropy equals the logarithm of
the boundary-accessible microcanonical state count --- is the one equation
that connects the two sides and does not follow from non-gravitational
dynamics (item~1 below).  Modulo that identification, the replica wormhole
calculation is geometric language for ETH permutation statistics, and the
information puzzle is not a puzzle about bulk dynamics: it is a question
about why the black-hole Hilbert space has the Bekenstein--Hawking dimension.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Hawking's calculation as the identity contraction.}
Hawking's derivation corresponds to retaining only $\sigma=e$ in the
sum~\eqref{eq:renyi-n-perm}, giving $\Tr\rho_R^n=r^{1-n}$ and a
monotonically rising entropy $S_{\rm vN}(R)=\log r$ --- information loss.
The first missing term, $\sigma=\tau^{-1}$ with weight $b^{1-n}$, is
smaller than the identity term pre-Page ($b>r$) but strictly larger
post-Page ($b<r$): it is not a perturbative correction but a competitor of
the same order.  This is the permutation-language form of the Mathur
statement \cite{Mathur2009}: any small deformation of Hawking's amplitude
shifts the coefficient of $\sigma=e$ by $O(\delta)$ while leaving the
$\sigma=\tau^{-1}$ term absent, so no perturbative correction reaches the
Page curve.  Restoring unitarity requires adding $\sigma=\tau^{-1}$ at full
weight --- the full Petz dual of the emission channel --- which is what the
island saddle and, in the model, Eq.~\eqref{eq:renyi-two-island} supply.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{JT gravity as the explicit realization.}
In two-dimensional Jackiw--Teitelboim (JT) gravity coupled to matter, the
PSSY calculation \cite{PeningtonShenkerStanfordYang2022} evaluates
$\Tr\rho_R^n$ by integrating over $n$-replica Euclidean geometries.  For
each bulk permutation $\sigma\in S_n$, the on-shell dilaton action
contributes $e^{(C(\sigma)-n)S_0}$ where $S_0=\phi_r/(4G_{\rm N})$ is the
extremal dilaton value at the asymptotic boundary (the Bekenstein--Hawking
entropy stored in the dilaton zero-mode); the matter sector contributes
$r^{C(\tau\sigma)}$ for a thermal radiation bath of entropy $\log r$.  The
saddle-point sum is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:JT-saddle-sum}
  \Tr\rho_R^n\big|_{\rm JT}
  = \sum_{\sigma\in S_n} b^{C(\sigma)-n}\,r^{C(\tau\sigma)},
  \qquad b=e^{S_0},
\end{equation}
which matches the ETH sum~\eqref{eq:renyi-n-perm} term by term.  The
identification $S_{\rm BH}=\log b$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:wormhole-ETH-chain}
is, in the JT context, the dilaton boundary condition
$b=e^{\phi_r/(4G_{\rm N})}$: a boundary condition, not a dynamical output
of the path integral.  This is the gravitational expression of
Open problem~1 below --- the one equation that fixes the microcanonical
state count from a geometric quantity.  All other features of the replica
sum follow from the matter sector and the topology of $S_n$, just as in
the non-gravitational ETH expansion.

\subsection{What is settled and what remains open}
\label{sec:open-problems}

Within the stated Hamiltonian class and away from the endpoint, the
construction settles the exterior accounting, conditional on the calibrated
inputs and the mixing ingredient: local thermality, finite-energy
backreaction corrections, negative heat capacity, the Schwarzschild rate
laws, the island-formula Page curve, post-Page early/late radiation
correlations, complementary recovery of code information, and the existence
and forced state dependence of interior partner operators all follow from
non-gravitational Hamiltonian evolution, weak-coupling emission,
microcanonical state counting, global purity, and decoupling of the composite
emission map.  No exotic dynamics, nonlocality, or modification of quantum
mechanics enters.  In this precise sense the information puzzle is not a
puzzle about exterior entropy bookkeeping.  What remains open is, on the
input side, the microscopic origin of the state count and, on the gravity
side, the reconciliation of this generic quantum-mechanical exterior with the
semiclassical description, together with two dynamical questions and the
endpoint.

\begin{enumerate}
\item
\emph{The microscopic inputs.}  The two inputs are forced by the temperature
and luminosity laws (Propositions~\ref{prop:dos} and~\ref{prop:area}) but not
derived: a microscopic, non-gravitational origin of a super-Hagedorn,
boundary-accessible density of states is the input-side open problem stated
above.  The boundary-accessibility half is where a semblance of geometric
locality enters the model (Section~\ref{sec:necessity}); in holographic
theories it follows from bulk locality and the Gauss law, and a microscopic
derivation here would have to produce it without either.  Recent work
indicates how unusual such spectra are \cite{AurellMajumdar2025}.
\item
\emph{Interior consistency.}  The smooth-horizon description requires
near-horizon modes to be entangled across the horizon, while unitarity
requires late radiation to be purified by early radiation; monogamy of
entanglement forbids both if the partner is independent of the radiation
\cite{AMPS2013}.  Section~\ref{sec:mirrors} settles the algebraic version of
this question within the model: partner operators with smooth-horizon
correlators exist for every microstate
(Proposition~\ref{prop:mirror-location}), post-Page they are supported
inside the early radiation --- so the two entanglements are one --- and no
state-independent choice is possible
(Proposition~\ref{prop:no-go}), confirming the state-dependent
interior constructions and the ER=EPR identification at the level of
operator algebra \cite{PapadodimasRaju2013,PapadodimasRaju2014,
VerlindeVerlinde2013,MaldacenaSusskind2013}.  What remains open is the
geometric half: whether the semiclassical interior --- a smooth metric and an
infalling experience --- is the mirror algebra in disguise, and how a
state-(subspace-)dependent operator assignment is compatible with a single
effective field theory in the interior
\cite{AMPSS2013,MarolfPolchinski2016}.  The model bounds but does not answer
the linearity objection: superpositions within a decoupling-budget code
subspace are provably safe, and the model is silent about what an infalling
observer encounters beyond it.
\item
\emph{The gravitational mechanism.}  Replica wormholes derive the island
formula from low-energy gravity with no microscopic input
\cite{PeningtonShenkerStanfordYang2022,AlmheiriHartmanMaldacenaShaghoulianTajdini2020},
but the calculation behaves like an ensemble average, raising the factorization
problem \cite{MarolfMaxfield2020}.
Section~\ref{sec:wormhole-ETH} shows that the replica saddle sum is
term-by-term identical to the ETH Wick expansion of
Proposition~\ref{prop:eth-decoupling} under the identification
$S_{\rm BH}=\Smicro$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:dos}), and
Proposition~\ref{prop:self-averaging} upgrades the ensemble average to
a single fixed Hamiltonian.  On the quantum-mechanics side the factorization
question is thereby fully resolved: the replica wormhole calculation
is geometric language for ETH permutation statistics, its ensemble
is redundant, and the connected corrections are explicitly listed and
exponentially small.  What remains open is purely on the gravity side:
why does the gravitational path integral compute an ensemble mean at all ---
i.e., what feature of the semiclassical saddle-point expansion produces
ETH-like statistics --- and why does the geometric island compute the
operational island's entropy, i.e., why does area equal $\log d_B$?
Relatedly, small corrections to Hawking's calculation cannot restore
unitarity \cite{Mathur2009}; the model identifies what the order-one
departure must look like algebraically, but not which assumption of the
semiclassical calculation fails.
\item
\emph{Dynamical rates and complexity.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:scrambling-time} derives the scrambling-time formula
$t_{\rm scr}=(\log\Smicro)/\lambda_L$ from ETH and the OTOC definition,
and shows that the chaos bound $\lambda_L\le2\pi/\beta$
\cite{MaldacenaShenkerStanford2016} gives the lower bound
$t_{\rm scr}\ge(\beta\log\Smicro)/(2\pi)$; equality, recovering the
Sekino--Susskind fast-scrambling estimate, is achieved when $\lambda_L=2\pi/\beta$.  The remaining undetermined
quantity is whether the shell Hamiltonian saturates the chaos bound
($\lambda_L=2\pi/\beta$): deriving this from the shell's spectral structure
--- e.g., extracting $\lambda_L$ from OTOC vs.\ $t$ for the Cayley family
--- would convert the per-step mixing assumption into a derived consequence
of the dynamics and bind the complexity threshold
$\exp(\frac{1}{2}\Smicro)$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:accessibility-complexity}
to the temperature.  Separately, recovery and mirror operators
exist but are not feasibly accessible.
Proposition~\ref{prop:mirror-complexity}
gives the explicit complexity lower bound
$\exp(\frac{1}{2}\Smicro(E))$ for the radiation-supported mirror (tightened
to $\exp(\Smicro(E))$ by the Python's lunch), decreasing monotonically along
the trajectory but remaining exponential throughout the post-Page era.
Proposition~\ref{prop:no-go} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:mirror-complexity} are complementary: state independence
is forbidden algebraically, and state-dependent implementation costs
exponentially.
\item
\emph{The endpoint.}  The quasi-stationary weak-coupling expansion fails when
the emitted quantum energy is comparable to the remaining mass, exactly where
the final order-one share of the entanglement must be purified.  Remnant and
final-burst questions live entirely in this regime, and neither the model nor
semiclassical gravity controls it.
\end{enumerate}

Items 2 and 3 are two sides of one question: what the semiclassical
description is, such that it both presents a smooth interior and computes the
correct exterior entropies.  The model sharpens that boundary: the exterior
entropy package (Page curve, replica contractions, self-averaging, mirror
operators, complementary recovery) lies entirely on the quantum-mechanics
side; the remaining gravitational questions are why $S_{\rm BH}=\log d_B$ and
what the semiclassical interior is, neither of which involves bulk dynamics.

\section{Weak-Emission Stinespring Map}
\label{app:weak-emission-map}

This appendix records the weak-coupling construction behind
Eq.~\eqref{eq:emission-isometry-main}.  The radiation continuum is assumed to
have a short correlation time compared with $t_{\rm emit}$, and the coupling is
weak enough that successive emissions are well separated
\cite{CohenTannoudji1992,BreuerPetruccione2002}.  The Markov approximation then
gives an outgoing channel, while the exponentially dense bands of $B$ supply
the initial and final system subspaces.

For an initial state typical in shell $E$, Fermi's golden rule gives, for a
fixed inclusive boundary channel $\mu$,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\Gamma_{E\to E-\omega,\lambda\mu}}{d\omega}
    =
    2\pi g^2 \omega^p
    C_\lambda(E,\omega)
    \frac{D_{E-\omega}}{D_E}.
\end{equation}
After summing over the $N(E)$ active boundary emitters,
\begin{equation}
    \sum_\mu\frac{d\Gamma_{E\to E-\omega,\lambda\mu}}{d\omega}
    =
    2\pi g^2 N(E)\omega^p C_\lambda(E,\omega)
    \exp[\Smicro(E-\omega)-\Smicro(E)],
\end{equation}
up to the overall normalization of the boundary operator count.

For a small interval $dt\ll t_{\rm emit}$, the emission Kraus operators are
\begin{equation}
    K_{E;\omega\lambda\mu}:\mathcal H_E
    \longrightarrow \mathcal H_{E-\omega},
    \qquad
    \bra bK_{E;\omega\lambda\mu}\ket a
    =
    \sqrt{dt}\,\bra bL_{E;\omega\lambda\mu}\ket a,
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
    L_{E;\omega\lambda\mu}
    =
    \sqrt{2\pi}\,g\,\omega^{p/2}
    \sum_{a\in E,\ b\in E-\omega}
    u^{\mu\lambda}_{ba}\ket b\bra a .
\end{equation}
The no-emission Kraus operator is
\begin{equation}
    K_{E;0}
    =
    I_{\mathcal H_E}
    -
    \frac{dt}{2}
    \sum_{\lambda,\mu}\int d\omega\,
    L_{E;\omega\lambda\mu}^\dagger L_{E;\omega\lambda\mu}
    +O(dt^2),
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
    K_{E;0}^\dagger K_{E;0}
    +
    \sum_{\lambda,\mu}\int d\omega\,
    K_{E;\omega\lambda\mu}^\dagger K_{E;\omega\lambda\mu}
    =
    I_{\mathcal H_E}+O(dt^2).
\end{equation}

Define the total decay operator and its shell-averaged rate by
\begin{equation}
    J_E
    =
    \sum_{\lambda,\mu}\int d\omega\,
    L_{E;\omega\lambda\mu}^\dagger L_{E;\omega\lambda\mu},
    \qquad
    \Gamma_E=\frac{1}{D_E}\Tr_{\mathcal H_E}J_E .
\end{equation}
Conditioning on an emission gives normalized jumps
\begin{equation}
    \widehat K_{E;\omega\lambda\mu}
    =
    \Gamma_E^{-1/2}L_{E;\omega\lambda\mu}.
\end{equation}
If $J_E=\Gamma_EI_{\mathcal H_E}$, then
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{\lambda,\mu}\int d\omega\,
    \widehat K_{E;\omega\lambda\mu}^\dagger
    \widehat K_{E;\omega\lambda\mu}
    =
    I_{\mathcal H_E}.
\end{equation}
More generally, write $J_E=\Gamma_E(I+F_E)$.  The flat-rate map is the leading
conditioned channel, and $F_E$ measures branch-weight and timing leakage.
With the ETH/random-matrix normalization used in the main text, $F_E$ is small
on typical microcanonical states, up to shell fluctuations and Markov errors.

The conditioned Stinespring map is
\begin{equation}
    V_E=
    \sum_{\lambda,\mu}\int d\omega\,
    \widehat K_{E;\omega\lambda\mu}
    \otimes\ket{\omega,\lambda,\mu}_{\rm R}.
\end{equation}
Including the waiting-time record, the flat-rate one-emission isometry is
\begin{equation}
    W_E\ket\psi
    =
    \sum_{\lambda,\mu}\int d\omega\int_0^\infty dt\,
    \sqrt{\Gamma_E}\,e^{-\Gamma_Et/2}
    \widehat K_{E;\omega\lambda\mu}
    e^{-iH_{\rm mix}(E)t}\ket\psi
    \otimes\ket{\omega,\lambda,\mu,t}_{\rm R}.
\end{equation}
In the eigenbasis $H_{\rm mix}(E)\ket\nu=\theta_\nu\ket\nu$, tracing the
waiting-time label gives
\begin{equation}
    (\mathcal D_{\Gamma_E}\sigma)_{\nu\nu'}
    =
    \frac{\Gamma_E}{\Gamma_E+i(\theta_\nu-\theta_{\nu'})}
    \sigma_{\nu\nu'} .
\end{equation}
This Lorentzian dephasing is the temporal average supplied by the physical
emission process.  Coherences with
$|\theta_\nu-\theta_{\nu'}|\lesssim\Gamma_E$ survive with order-one weight and
belong to the remaining deterministic-channel problem discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\section{A Fixed-Hamiltonian Target}
\label{app:fixed-hamiltonian-target}

This appendix states one concrete version of the fixed sparse Hamiltonian
problem mentioned in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.  It is not used in the main
derivation, which relies on design-based mixing blocks.  Its role is to make
the remaining deterministic problem precise.

Let the active boundary cells be the vertices of a Cayley expander
$G_N={\rm Cay}({\cal G}_N,S_N)$, with homogeneous spin interactions on the
edges.  A representative shell Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}
    K_N=
    \sum_{g\in{\cal G}_N}\sum_{s\in S_N^+}
    \left(
    J_s^xX_gX_{gs}+J_s^yY_gY_{gs}+J_s^zZ_gZ_{gs}
    \right)
    +\sum_{g\in{\cal G}_N}(h_xX_g+h_zZ_g),
\end{equation}
with no resampling during evaporation.  Cayley graphs are vertex-transitive,
and explicit Cayley/Ramanujan expander families are standard
\cite{LubotzkyPhillipsSarnak1988,Harrow2008}.  If the shell projector commutes
with the left group action and the boundary emission operators are related by
that action,
\begin{equation}
    O_{ag,\lambda}=U_aO_{g,\lambda}U_a^\dagger,\qquad
    [U_a,K_N]=[U_a,\Pi_E]=0,
\end{equation}
then the shell-averaged emission weights are equal for all boundary labels:
\begin{equation}
    {\cal A}_{g,\lambda}(E,\omega)
    =
    {\cal A}_{g',\lambda}(E,\omega).
\end{equation}
Thus the area factor follows from an exact symmetry of the boundary algebra,
while the common spectral envelope is the usual ETH input for a chaotic local
operator \cite{Srednicki1994,DAlessioKafriPolkovnikovRigol2016,
DymarskyLashkariLiu2018}.  Exact symmetry charges should be treated within
fixed sectors or recorded in the radiation.

The Page-purity and second R\'enyi island diagnostics require second-moment
decoupling of the composed evaporation map.  A sectorwise target is
\begin{equation}
    I_2(Q:B_{j+1})
    \le
    \kappa_j I_2(Q:B_j)+\varepsilon_{{\rm mix},j},
    \qquad
    \kappa_j<1,
\end{equation}
where $I_2$ is the second R\'enyi mutual information between a reference $Q$
and the remaining subsystem.  The contraction comes from the dimension drop
and from in-shell mixing that spreads reference information over the boundary
algebra.

For a fixed Hamiltonian, closed evolution by $K_N$ alone preserves
Hilbert-Schmidt distances.  The contraction relevant to Page decoupling
belongs to the weak-emission block generated by $K_N$ and $H_I$, after the
emitted mode is recorded as radiation or traced from the remaining core.  In
the flat-rate Markov limit, the one-emission block is controlled by the record
algebra
\begin{equation}
    \widehat K_{m'}^\dagger\widehat K_m,
    \qquad
    m=(\omega,\lambda,\mu),
\end{equation}
and by the emission-image overlap kernel
\begin{equation}
    G_{\nu\nu'}
    =
    \Tr\!\left[
    \rho'(\nu)\rho'(\nu')
    \right],
    \qquad
    \rho'(\nu)
    =
    \sum_m\widehat K_m\ket\nu\bra\nu\widehat K_m^\dagger .
\end{equation}
A sufficient deterministic condition is that, within each exact symmetry
sector, the diagonal matrix elements of the record algebra are smooth in the
$K_N$ eigenbasis, the kernel $G_{\nu\nu'}$ is close to its microcanonical value
for code-relevant eigenstates, and the waiting-time dephasing leaves negligible
coherent weight on near-resonant pairs.
Appendix~\ref{app:eth-decoupling} formalizes these conditions as ETH-type
assumptions and derives the decoupling bound from them.

This is a shrinking-channel decoupling estimate, not convergence of repeated
powers of a single fixed-algebra channel.  Sparse/expander models are known
routes to fast scrambling \cite{BarbonMagan2012,BentsenGuLucas2019}, and OTOC
decay is a standard route from Hamiltonian dynamics to channel decoupling
\cite{HosurQiRobertsYoshida2016,YoshidaKitaev2017}.  What remains for a named
deterministic Hamiltonian family is a bound or scaling analysis showing that
the induced weak-emission channel has the Page entropy curve, the pre- and
post-Page second R\'enyi decoupling diagnostics, and the expected failure of
free, integrable, or resonant controls.
Appendix~\ref{app:eth-decoupling} provides the conditional bound: ETH-type
assumptions on the jump operators imply the decoupling estimate, so what
remains for a named family is verifying those assumptions.
Appendix~\ref{app:cayley-scaling} reports a first exact finite-size-scaling
study of this kind, for a simplified step channel: the chaotic circulant
mixer is statistically indistinguishable from a Haar isometry and converges
to the Page value, while the free and integrable controls fail as predicted.
The full weak-emission open channel, a true expander family, and larger sizes
remain open.

\section{Page and Replica Contractions}
\label{app:page-replica}

This appendix gives the finite-dimensional Page and replica formulas used in
Section~\ref{sec:information-flow}.  For a Haar-random pure state on
$X\otimes Y$, with
$d_X=\dim X$, $d_Y=\dim Y$, and $d_X\le d_Y$, Page's formula is
\begin{equation}
    \langle S_{\rm vN}(X)\rangle
    =
    \sum_{k=d_Y+1}^{d_Xd_Y}\frac1k
    -
    \frac{d_X-1}{2d_Y}
    =
    \log d_X-\frac{d_X}{2d_Y}+O(d_Y^{-2}).
\end{equation}
Approximate unitary two-designs reproduce the second moments needed for
Page-purity and code-subspace decoupling
\cite{DankertCleveEmersonLivine2009,SzehrDupuisTomamichelRenner2013};
fixed R\'enyi-$n$ moments require the corresponding $n$th moments
\cite{HarrowLow2009}.

For a Haar-typical pure state on a $b\times r$ factorization,
\begin{equation}
    \E\,\Tr\rho_R^2
    =
    \frac{b+r}{br+1}
    =
    \frac1r+\frac1b+O((br)^{-1}).
\end{equation}
With
$b=D_{\rm B}(E)=e^{\Smicro(E)}$ and
$r=D_{\rm rad}(E)=e^{\Delta S_{\rm rad}(E)}$, this gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:renyi-two-island}.  For fixed higher R\'enyi moments, the Haar
moment expansion has the standard permutation form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:renyi-n-perm}
    \E\,\Tr\rho_R^n
    =
    \frac{1}{(br)_n}
    \sum_{\sigma\in S_n}
    b^{C(\sigma)}r^{C(\tau\sigma)}.
\end{equation}
Here $\tau=(12\cdots n)$ is the cyclic permutation imposed by
$\Tr\rho_R^n$, and $C(\sigma)$ is the number of cycles of $\sigma$.  The
identity contraction gives the Hawking/no-island branch, while
$\sigma=\tau^{-1}$ gives the post-Page branch controlled by the remaining core
dimension.  This is the same random-matrix replica mechanism that appears in
Page theorem and random-dynamics discussions of island-like behavior
\cite{Urbach2021,deBoerHollanderRolph2024,BasuWenZhou2025}.

The Hamiltonian assigns Hawking weights to radiation histories.  Let
$\alpha$ label a coarse radiation history, including emitted energies,
channels, and time bins, with probabilities $p_\alpha$ supplied by the
weak-coupling branching rates.  A random purification of this weighted
history distribution gives, at large dimension,
\begin{equation}
    \E\,\Tr\rho_R^n
    \simeq
    \sum_{\sigma\in S_n}
    b^{C(\sigma)-n}
    \prod_{c\in{\rm cycles}(\tau\sigma)}
    \Tr p^{|c|}.
\end{equation}
For $p_\alpha=1/r$ this reduces to the Haar/Page expression.  The two leading
contractions are
\begin{equation}
    \sigma=1:
    \qquad
    \E\,\Tr\rho_R^n\simeq \Tr p^n,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
    \sigma=\tau^{-1}:
    \qquad
    \E\,\Tr\rho_R^n\simeq b^{1-n}.
\end{equation}
Thus the no-island branch is the R\'enyi entropy of the emitted Hawking
history distribution, while the island branch is the remaining core state
count.  At second R\'enyi order this gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:weighted-renyi-two-main}.

\section{Finite-Dimensional Check}
\label{app:finite-check}

This appendix gives a small finite-dimensional check of the
information-theoretic step used in the main text.  It checks the conditional
statement that, given a Page-typical emission map and the shrinking
Hilbert-space dimensions, the radiation entropy follows Page's formula.  It
also illustrates that matching a coarse thermal energy-bin distribution is a
weaker condition than producing fine-grained radiation entropy.  It is not a
test of whether a particular sparse Hamiltonian generates the Page-typical
maps dynamically.

The example uses a coarser regularization adapted to the entropy scaling.  The
integer $L$ is an area or entropy label, unrelated to the jump operator
$L_{E;\omega\lambda\mu}$, and $q$ is the Hilbert-space dimension per entropy
unit:
\begin{equation}
    \dim\mathcal H_L=q^{L^2}.
\end{equation}
One regulated emission step factors the current space as
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal H_L\simeq \mathcal H_{L-1}\otimes\mathcal H_{\rm shell}(L),
    \qquad
    \dim\mathcal H_{\rm shell}(L)=q^{2L-1}.
\end{equation}
The removed shell is the outgoing radiation factor for that step.  Its energy
measurement is a coarse graining
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal H_{\rm shell}(L)=\bigoplus_h \mathcal H_h(L),
\end{equation}
where $h$ labels energy bins.  The integer bin dimensions are chosen to
approximate the four-dimensional thermal weights
\begin{equation}
    P_h\propto\int_{x_h}^{x_{h+1}}dx\,x^2e^{-x},
    \qquad x=\beta\omega.
\end{equation}
Thus $\dim\mathcal H_h(L)/\dim\mathcal H_{\rm shell}(L)$ is the prescribed
coarse-grained thermal probability $P_h$ for energy bin $h$, up to integer
rounding.  The full shell Hilbert space carries the fine-grained purification
data.

\section{Finite-Size Scaling for Deterministic Shell Mixers}
\label{app:cayley-scaling}

This appendix tests, by exact diagonalization and finite-size scaling, the
two claims for which the main text would otherwise rely on design assumptions
alone.  The first is the underdetermination claim of
Section~\ref{sec:necessity}: the kinematic inputs --- the shrinking
Hilbert-space dimensions, the emission records, and the state count --- do
not by themselves produce the Page/island curve.  The second is the existence
claim of Appendix~\ref{app:fixed-hamiltonian-target}: a single fixed,
time-independent, vertex-transitive shell Hamiltonian can supply the required
mixing.  Because the claimed phenomenology is asymptotic, a single small size
is uninformative --- small Hilbert spaces look scrambled regardless --- so
the discriminator is the trend in the register size $n$, together with
independent spectral chaos diagnostics.

\subsubsection*{Protocol}

The register is $n$ qubits.  Three Hamiltonian families share identical
kinematic bookkeeping --- the same shrinking dimension sequence, emission
records, and initial-state ensemble --- and differ only in the shell
dynamics:
\begin{itemize}
\item
\emph{free}: the cycle ${\rm Cay}(\mathbb Z_n,\{\pm1\})$ with $XX+YY$
couplings, $(J^x,J^y,J^z)=(1,1,0)$ and no fields.  The dynamics is Gaussian:
counterexample~1 of the main text.
\item
\emph{integrable}: the same cycle with XXZ couplings $(1,1,0.8)$ and no
fields.  Interacting but Bethe-ansatz integrable: the cycle-XXZ warning
example of Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.
\item
\emph{chaotic}: the circulant graph ${\rm Cay}(\mathbb Z_n,\{\pm1,n/2\})$
with anisotropic XYZ nearest-neighbour couplings $(1.0,0.6,1.4)$, long-bond
couplings $(0.9,1.1,0.5)$, and uniform fields $(h_x,h_z)=(0.55,0.31)$.  The
fields and anisotropy break integrability while the circulant structure
preserves vertex transitivity, the symmetry used in
Appendix~\ref{app:fixed-hamiltonian-target}.
\end{itemize}
Each run starts from a random product state (each qubit independently
Haar-distributed), so a no-mixing control generates no radiation entropy, and
the full register is evolved once for $t_{\rm mix}=5$ so the initial state is
shell-typical.  The run then iterates a scramble-then-reveal step: the current
core of $c$ qubits is evolved for $t_{\rm mix}$ under the core Hamiltonian of
its family, and a block of two qubits is transferred to the radiation
register.  After each step the radiation von Neumann entropy is compared with
the analytic Page expectation for the current bipartition
\cite{Page1993Average}; the deviation assigned to a run is the maximum over
steps.  The reference is a Haar-random core unitary applied in place of
$e^{-iHt_{\rm mix}}$ and evaluated identically, that is, the Haar isometry's
own finite-size deviation from the Page formula.  This step channel keeps the
shrinking-dimension and record structure of the regulated emission map of
Section~5 but replaces the golden-rule branch weights by a fixed two-qubit
emission; it tests the mixing assumption, not the thermodynamic weights.

Two spectral diagnostics accompany the entropy test, both evaluated in a
fixed translation-momentum sector.  The mean adjacent-gap ratio
$\langle r\rangle$ distinguishes integrable from chaotic level statistics
(Poisson $0.386$, GOE $0.531$) \cite{OganesyanHuse2007,
AtasBogomolnyGiraudRoux2013}.  The near-degeneracy fraction
$f_{\rm deg}$ flags spectral degeneracies, which enlarge the temporal-ensemble
commutant and would spoil single-Hamiltonian decoupling.  Both are properties
of the fixed Hamiltonians and carry no sampling error.

\subsubsection*{Reading}

The chaotic family tracks the Haar floor to within $0.003$ nats at every
size, with deviation and standard deviation both roughly halving as $n$
increases by two: at these sizes the fixed deterministic Hamiltonian is
statistically indistinguishable from a Haar isometry and converges to the
Page value at the Haar rate.  The halving of the seed scatter is the
$e^{-S_0/2}=2^{-n/2}$ concentration predicted by the self-averaging
estimate of Proposition~\ref{prop:self-averaging}
(Appendix~\ref{app:eth-decoupling}).  The free family diverges
($0.40\to1.05\to1.20$), and the integrable family plateaus an order of
magnitude above the falling Haar floor ($0.12\to0.17\to0.16$).  The spectral
diagnostics line up with the entropy test: the family that fails by
degeneracy accumulation is the one whose radiation entropy diverges, and the
family with GOE statistics and no degeneracies is the one that matches Haar.

Two conclusions follow.  First, the three families share every kinematic
input of the model --- state count, shrinking dimensions, emission records
--- and differ only in the shell dynamics, yet only the chaotic family
reproduces the Page/island curve.  The island output is therefore not
contained in the inputs; the chaotic mixer does dynamical work.  Second, the
positive case is a single named, time-independent, vertex-transitive
Hamiltonian, not an ensemble: finite-size-scaling evidence for the
deterministic route of Appendix~\ref{app:fixed-hamiltonian-target}.

The scope is limited in three ways.  The circulant family
$\{\pm1,n/2\}$ is vertex-transitive but not an expander family --- its
adjacency gap closes as $O(n^{-2})$ --- so the result is an
exact-diagonalization-scale check rather than a uniform-in-$n$ statement.
The step channel is scramble-then-reveal rather than the full weak-emission
open channel of Appendix~\ref{app:weak-emission-map}.  The sizes are
$n\le12$ ($n\le10$ seed-averaged) at fixed $t_{\rm mix}$.  These are the open
items listed at the end of Appendix~\ref{app:fixed-hamiltonian-target}.

\section{An ETH Route to Emission-Channel Decoupling}
\label{app:eth-decoupling}

The main text supplies decoupling of the composite emission map through
approximate-design or tensor-product-expander mixing blocks.  This appendix
derives the same second-moment decoupling from ETH-type spectral assumptions
on a fixed shell Hamiltonian, with an explicit error budget.  The conclusion
is that the design input can be replaced by the chaos assumptions standard in
many-body physics: smooth ETH matrix elements for the jump operators, a
fourth-moment factorization, and a nondegenerate spectrum imply the bound of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:decoupling-bound}, with errors controlled by the same
weak-coupling parameter that justifies the golden rule.  The derivation is at
the physics level of rigor; the assumptions are stated so that each is a
separately checkable hypothesis.

One step maps the shell $\mathcal H_E$, of dimension $D$ and eigenpairs
$H_E\ket\nu=\theta_\nu\ket\nu$, to the shell $\mathcal H_{E-\omega}$ of
dimension $D_-$.  The ingredients are those of
Appendix~\ref{app:weak-emission-map}: the flat-rate conditioned jumps
$\widehat K_m$ with record label $m=(\omega,\lambda,\mu)$, normalized by
$\sum_m\widehat K_m^\dagger\widehat K_m=I$, and the dwell dephasing obtained
by tracing the waiting-time record,
$T(\rho)_{\nu\nu'}=L_{\nu\nu'}\rho_{\nu\nu'}$ with
$L_{\nu\nu'}=\Gamma_E/[\Gamma_E+i(\theta_\nu-\theta_{\nu'})]$.  One
conditioned step is
$\Lambda(\rho)=\sum_m[\widehat K_m T(\rho)\widehat K_m^\dagger]
\otimes\ket m\bra m_{\rm R}$.
A code subspace $\mathcal C\subset\mathcal H_E$ of dimension $d_Q=e^k$, with
projector $P_{\mathcal C}$, is maximally entangled with a reference $Q$.

The assumptions are the following.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(E1)]
\emph{Sectorwise nondegenerate spectrum with bounded near-resonance.}  Within
each symmetry sector there are no degeneracies, and the number of level pairs
within a linewidth obeys
$\#\{(\nu,\nu')\!:\nu\ne\nu',\,|\theta_\nu-\theta_{\nu'}|\le\Gamma_E\}
\lesssim D\,\Gamma_E\rhoB(E)$.
The degeneracy diagnostic of Appendix~\ref{app:cayley-scaling} is the
finite-size check.
\item[(E2)]
\emph{ETH matrix elements for the jump operators} \cite{Srednicki1999,
DAlessioKafriPolkovnikovRigol2016}:
$\bra{\bar\nu}\widehat K_m\ket\nu=D^{-1/2}f_m(\bar E,\omega)
R^{(m)}_{\bar\nu\nu}$,
with $f_m$ smooth across the shell and $R^{(m)}$ zero-mean, unit-variance,
order-one variables.
\item[(E3)]
\emph{Fourth-moment factorization (full ETH).}  Joint fourth moments of the
$R^{(m)}$ Wick-factorize into pairings, with crossing and connected
corrections suppressed by $e^{-\Smicro}$ relative to the retained pairings;
variables of distinct records are uncorrelated at these orders.  This is the
free-probability formulation of ETH
\cite{FoiniKurchan2019,PappalardiFoiniKurchan2022}.
\item[(E4)]
\emph{Weak-coupling window}: $\Gamma_E\ll\Delta E$, the same window in which
the golden rule of Section~3 is derived.  Both regimes
$\Gamma_E\rhoB\gtrless1$ (overlapping or isolated resonances) are admissible.
\item[(E5)]
\emph{Shell-typical code}: $(P_{\mathcal C})_{\nu\nu}\simeq d_Q/D$ up to
order-one factors, and the code is uncorrelated with the $R^{(m)}$
fluctuations --- the low-complexity initial states of Section~2.4.  Codes
fine-tuned to the eigenbasis are excluded, and physically should be: they
evade the dwell dephasing.
\end{itemize}

Write $q_m=D_-|f_m|^2/D$ for the record weights, with $\sum_mq_m=1$ by the
flat-rate normalization, and let $\sigma=(T\otimes{\rm id}_Q)(\Phi_{CQ})$ be
the dephased code state.  Using (E2) at second moment only, the mean output
is exactly decoupled, $\E\,\rho_{QB}=\pi_B\otimes\pi_Q$, so all content is in
the fluctuations.  Expanding $\E\Tr\rho_{QB}^2$ in fourth moments of the
$R^{(m)}$ and keeping the two pairings of (E3) gives
\begin{equation}
    \E\Tr\rho_{QB}^2
    =
    \Big(\sum_mq_m^2\Big)\Tr\sigma^2
    +
    e^{-[\Smicro(E-\omega)+k]}
    \left[1+O\!\big(e^{-\Smicro}\big)\right],
\label{eq:eth-two-contractions}
\end{equation}
with the dephased-code purity given by the explicit Lorentzian kernel
\begin{equation}
    \Tr\sigma^2
    =
    \frac{1}{d_Q^2}
    \sum_{\nu\nu'}
    |L_{\nu\nu'}|^2
    (P_{\mathcal C})_{\nu\nu}(P_{\mathcal C})_{\nu'\nu'}
    =
    \frac{1}{D}\left[1+O\big(\Gamma_E\rhoB(E)\big)\right]
\label{eq:lorentzian-purity}
\end{equation}
for shell-typical codes, where $\Gamma_E\rhoB/D=\Gamma_E/\Delta E\ll1$ by
(E4).  The two terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:eth-two-contractions} are precisely the
two contractions of Appendix~\ref{app:page-replica}: the first is the
record (Hawking) branch, $e^{-S_2^{\rm rec}}$ with $S_2^{\rm rec}$ the second
R\'enyi entropy of the full realized record --- channels, frequencies, and
arrival times, the latter entering through the kernel
\eqref{eq:lorentzian-purity} --- and the second is the island branch.  Here
they are derived for a fixed Hamiltonian rather than assumed via designs.

\begin{proposition}[ETH implies second-moment decoupling]
\label{prop:eth-decoupling}
Assume (E1)--(E5).  Then the one-step conditioned emission channel
reproduces the second moments of the Haar emission isometry up to relative
errors
$O(\Gamma_E/\Delta E)$ and $O(e^{-\Smicro(E-\omega)})$, and the composed map
over
an evaporation trajectory obeys the decoupling bound of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:decoupling-bound}, with $\Delta S_{\rm rad}$ the second R\'enyi
entropy of the realized radiation record and an additive error
$\sum_j\epsilon_j$ accumulated over the steps.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof sketch.}  Mean decoupling and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:eth-two-contractions} give the one-step second-moment
statement; the norm conversion
$\|X\|_1\le\sqrt{d_QD_-}\,\|X\|_2$ turns the variance into the exponent
bound.  For the composition, replace one step at a time by its Haar
counterpart; each replacement costs one $\epsilon_j$ at the level of second
moments, and the errors add by the triangle inequality, as in the standard
hybrid arguments for approximate designs
\cite{SzehrDupuisTomamichelRenner2013,HarrowLow2009}.  The accumulated error
is dominated by the late, small shells, exactly as for the
$\epsilon_{\rm Page}$ term of Section~\ref{sec:information-flow}.
\hfill$\square$

Four remarks.

First, no new small parameter enters.  The error budget consists of the
surviving dwell coherences, $O(\Gamma_E/\Delta E)$, which is the golden-rule
parameter of Section~3; the ETH crossing terms, $O(e^{-\Smicro})$; and the
envelope variation of $f_m$ across the shell, which is the same smoothness
already used in the rate calculation.  The chaos input is thereby reduced
from ``the mixing blocks form an approximate two-design'' to spectral
statements about a fixed Hamiltonian, each separately checkable.

Second, the assumptions are the quantities that
Appendix~\ref{app:cayley-scaling} measures.  The chaotic circulant family
satisfies them ($f_{\rm deg}=0$, GOE-like $\langle r\rangle$) and matches the
Haar reference; the free chain violates (E1) ($f_{\rm deg}$ rising to
$0.81$) and diverges; the integrable chain violates (E3) (Poisson-leaning
statistics) and plateaus.  The failures predicted by the assumptions are the
failures observed.

Third, the statement is a channel analogue of the emergent-design results
for chaotic dynamics: projected ensembles generated by individual chaotic
wavefunctions converge to state designs
\cite{HoChoi2022,CotlerMarkHuang2023}, and here the radiation record is the
projecting register, with the dwell dephasing implementing the temporal
averaging familiar from equilibration theory
\cite{LindenPopescuShortWinter2009}.  The arrival-time refinement of the
record raises $S_2^{\rm rec}$ and so only strengthens the decoupling; the
entropy bookkeeping of the rising Page branch refers to the binned records of
Section~5, as in Appendices~\ref{app:weak-emission-map}
and~\ref{app:page-replica}.

Fourth, what remains for full rigor: (E3) is a hypothesis --- exact for
random-matrix ensembles, numerically supported for chaotic local
Hamiltonians, and formalized by the free-probability approach to ETH, but
not proven for any named Hamiltonian family, the same status as ETH itself;
uniformity of (E1)--(E5) along the whole trajectory is assumed; and exact
symmetries require sectorwise application with charges recorded in the
radiation, as in Appendix~\ref{app:fixed-hamiltonian-target}.

\subsubsection*{Self-averaging and the redundancy of the ensemble}

Equation~\eqref{eq:eth-two-contractions} is a mean.  The gravitational
derivation of the same two contractions --- replica wormholes --- behaves
like an ensemble average and thereby raises the factorization problem
\cite{PeningtonShenkerStanfordYang2022,MarolfMaxfield2020}.  The model can
ask the corresponding question directly: how far does the purity of a single
fixed Hamiltonian fluctuate about the two-contraction value?  The answer
requires one more order of the same hypothesis.  Let (E3$'$) denote (E3)
extended to eighth moments: joint moments of the $R^{(m)}$ Wick-factorize
through eighth order, with crossing corrections suppressed by $e^{-\Smicro}$,
as in the free-probability formulation
\cite{FoiniKurchan2019,PappalardiFoiniKurchan2022}.

The second moment of the purity is then a sum over the $4!$ pairings of four
replicas --- permutations $\pi\in S_4$ weighted by
$d_B^{c(\pi)}d_R^{c(\sigma\pi)}$ with $\sigma=(12)(34)$ the double swap and
$c$ the cycle count.  The four block-preserving permutations reproduce
$(\E\Tr\rho_R^2)^2$ exactly; the twenty connected ones are the fluctuation.
The largest connected class is the norm fluctuation of the unconstrained
Gaussian calculus, and it is absent here because the flat-rate normalization
$\sum_m\widehat K_m^\dagger\widehat K_m=I$ makes the channel exactly trace
preserving; the surviving classes match the exact variance of the Haar
ensemble \cite{Giraud2007}.  The result, with
$\Delta S_{\rm rad}+\Smicro\gtrsim S_0$ along the trajectory, is the
following.

\begin{proposition}[Self-averaging of the exterior entropy package]
\label{prop:self-averaging}
Assume (E1)--(E5) with (E3$'$).  Away from the endpoint,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{{\rm Var}\,[\Tr\rho_R^2]}{\big(\E\Tr\rho_R^2\big)^2}
    =
    O\!\left(e^{-2\max\{\Delta S_{\rm rad}(E),\,\Smicro(E)\}}\right)
    \le
    O\!\left(e^{-S_0}\right),
\label{eq:self-averaging}
\end{equation}
where the variance may be read over microcanonical shell windows, over
shell-typical codes and initial states, or over the ETH ensemble, with the
same leading connected diagrams.  Consequently the second R\'enyi radiation
entropy of a single instance deviates from the two-contraction value of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:eth-two-contractions} by $O(e^{-S_0/2})$, worst at the Page
transition, and products of purities across shells factorize to the same
accuracy.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof sketch.}  The permutation sum above, with the connected classes
enumerated and the norm class removed by trace preservation; the Haar value
${\rm Var}\simeq2e^{-2\max}$ \cite{Giraud2007} calibrates the surviving
multiplicities; (E3$'$) crossings multiply by $1+O(e^{-\Smicro})$ and the
dwell and envelope factors enter as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:eth-decoupling}.  Concentration over codes follows by
Chebyshev using (E5).  The uniform bound uses
$\max\{a,b\}\ge(a+b)/2\gtrsim S_0/2$.  \hfill$\square$

Three comments.  First, the seed scatter of
Appendix~\ref{app:cayley-scaling} is a direct finite-size measurement of
this concentration: the standard deviation across seeds for the chaotic
family (Table~\ref{tab:cayley-scaling}) falls $0.043\to0.016\to0.007$ as $n$
grows by two --- consistent with the predicted $e^{-S_0/2}=2^{-n/2}$ halving
--- and tracks the Haar reference's own finite-size deviations, i.e.\
concentration at the Haar rate.  Second, the proposition is the model's
statement of factorization: for a fixed Hamiltonian the would-be ensemble
fluctuations are the twenty connected pairings, explicitly listed and
exponentially small, so the ensemble description loses nothing and is needed
for nothing --- the analogue, in this setting, of wormholes without
averaging \cite{SaadShenkerStanfordYao2021}, with the connected pairings
playing the role of the half-wormhole corrections.  Third, the statement
covers the shell purities and the entropy package built from them; it does
not control fluctuations of finer observables, higher R\'enyi constants
require more replicas of the same calculation, and (E3$'$) shares the
epistemic status of (E3).
