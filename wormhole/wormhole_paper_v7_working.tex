\title{Can a Wormhole Induce a Monopole?}

\author{Sergei Slobodov}
\email{sergei@slobodov.com}
\noaffiliation

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Could a body orbiting near one mouth of a traversable wormhole make the mass
seen near the other mouth rise and fall?  Monopole rigidity precludes this possibility.  In the Maxwell case, source motion cannot induce a time-varying monopole across the throat, though higher time-varying multipoles can be present.  The same holds for gravity at linear order.  This argument is local and requires neither a compact interior nor a specific asymptotic structure.  Remaining cumulative monopole effects are limited to matter transit and, at second order, to gravitational radiation.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

% ============================================================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
% ============================================================

A wormhole has two mouths.  If a star orbits close to one of them, does the
mass an observer measures at the other rise and fall with the orbit?  We show
that it does not.  So long as nothing crosses the sphere where the measurement
is made, the mass measured there is constant, however the star moves.

Dai and Stojkovic (DS) proposed that it does
\cite{DaiStojkovic2019Observing}.  They join two Schwarzschild exteriors at a
sphere of radius $R$, place a body of mass $\mu$ at radius $A$ on one side, and
solve the static matching problem.  The far side then carries a mass $\mu R/A$.
Letting $A$ trace an eccentric orbit makes that mass rise and fall, which would
appear as a periodic $1/r^2$ acceleration near Sgr~A*; sensitivity estimates
for such a signal followed \cite{SimonettiEtAl2021SensitiveSearches}.  But the
solutions at different $A$ are separate static
solutions, not successive states of one evolution, and moving the star does not
carry the system from one to the next.  See also
\cite{Krasnikov2020CommentObserving,DaiStojkovic2020ReplyObserving}.

In electromagnetism, field lines can thread a wormhole, so each mouth looks
charged although there is no charge anywhere---Wheeler's ``charge without charge''
\cite{MisnerWheeler1957ClassicalPhysics}.  The flux through a sphere around one
mouth is then fixed, and changes only when charge crosses that sphere
\cite{FrolovNovikov1990PhysicalEffects}.  In
the DS model, which joins two separate asymptotic regions, the electric
version gives a position-dependent charge only because both ends are held at
the same potential, as for a grounded conductor rather than an isolated one.  Higher
multipoles are not fixed this way, and they do change as the star moves.  In general
relativity the same holds for first-order perturbations of a Schwarzschild
background, whose monopole carries no dynamics of its own.

The paper is organized as follows.  Section~\ref{sec:maxwell} works the
electric case, where everything can be solved exactly.
Section~\ref{sec:ds-model} applies the same test to the DS metric.
Section~\ref{sec:gravity-charge} proves the gravitational balance law for
perturbations of any shape, and Section~\ref{sec:gravity-application} shows
that the DS coefficient is the charge that law holds fixed.

We use geometrized units $G=c=1$, denote the background mass by $M$ and write
$r_g=2M$, and work to first order in the small mass ratio
\begin{equation}
  \epsilon=\frac{\mu}{M}\ll1.
\end{equation}

% ============================================================
\section{Static families and time evolution}
\label{sec:maxwell}
% ============================================================

We take the electrostatic solution DS write down, let the charge move without
traversing the wormhole, and ask which parts of the field can change with it.
We show that the monopole part stays constant, first by a concrete
calculation in the DS setup and then for any measuring surface, with no
assumption about what it encloses.

\subsection{The DS solution}

DS glue two copies of flat space outside a sphere of radius $R$, with the
charge $q$ on the axis at radius $r_1=A$ in one copy and the observer at
$(r_2,\theta_2)$ in the other \cite{DaiStojkovic2019Observing}.  Let $F$ be
the Maxwell field, $j$ the electric current, and for a closed two-surface $S$
\begin{equation}
  Q[S]=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_S\star F.
  \label{eq:maxwell-charge}
\end{equation}
The potential in each copy solves Laplace's equation away from the charge,
and each is defined only up to a constant, $c_1$ and $c_2$.  Matching the potential and its normal
derivative at $r_1=r_2=R$ gives
\begin{equation}
  V_2=c_2+\sum_{\ell\ge0}\frac{B_\ell}{r_2^{\ell+1}}P_\ell(\cos\theta_2),
  \quad
  B_{\ell\ge1}=q\,\frac{2\ell+1}{2(\ell+1)}\,\frac{R^{2\ell+1}}{A^{\ell+1}},
  \quad
  B_0=\frac{qR}{2A}+\frac{R}{2}(c_1-c_2).
  \label{eq:ds-multipoles}
\end{equation}
The coefficients with $\ell\ge1$ are those of
Ref.~\cite{DaiStojkovic2019Observing}.  Their expansions drop both constants.
One of the two can be absorbed into the zero of potential; the other cannot,
and it enters $B_0$.
In this static problem every multipole with $\ell\ge1$ is fixed by the position
of the charge.  The monopole is not.  A constant is itself an $\ell=0$ field, so
it shifts $B_0$ and nothing else, and one free parameter survives there.

The same choice appears with an ordinary conductor: one may fix its charge or
fix its potential, not both \cite[Ch.~2]{JacksonClassicalElectrodynamics}.  A
grounded sphere takes on an induced charge proportional to $1/A$; an isolated
one keeps its charge and lets its potential float.  The two mouths here behave
as the plates of a capacitor.  Setting $q=0$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ds-multipoles}
leaves $B_0=-\tfrac12R(c_2-c_1)$, so the capacitance is $R/2$, and $c_2-c_1$ is
the work needed to carry a unit charge from one end to the other.

Dropping both constants puts the two ends at the same potential, and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ds-multipoles} then gives%
\footnote{DS use an unnormalized flux and a charge parameter differing by the
same factor of $4\pi$; the ratio $Q_2/q$ is unchanged.}
\begin{equation}
  Q_2(A)=B_0=\frac{qR}{2A}.
  \label{eq:ds-electric}
\end{equation}
Imposing that condition afresh at every $A$ makes the charge change with the
orbit.

Maxwell's equations preserve the charge, not the potential difference.  Now
let the charge move, so that $A$ depends on time.  With $S$ at rest,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{dQ_2}{dt}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\oint_S\partial_t\mathbf E\cdot d\mathbf S
  =\frac{1}{4\pi}\oint_S(\nabla\times\mathbf B)\cdot d\mathbf S
   -\oint_S\mathbf J\cdot d\mathbf S
  =-\oint_S\mathbf J\cdot d\mathbf S,
  \label{eq:maxwell-concrete}
\end{equation}
the curl term vanishing because $S$ is closed.  The charge remains in the
other space, so $Q_2$ is constant, and by Eq.~\eqref{eq:ds-electric} so is
$B_0$.  The other coefficients can vary, and
Fig.~\ref{fig:fixed-sector-field} shows the field they produce.

\begin{figure}[!ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{figures/fig_thinshell_fixed_monopole_flux_v7.pdf}
  \caption{The zero-flux member of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ds-multipoles}: $B_0=0$,
  which requires $c_2-c_1=q/A$.  The two panels are the two exterior charts,
  and the black circles are the same throat sphere, identified.  The charge
  still produces a field in the opposite chart, from the $B_{\ell\ge1}$ terms
  alone; these are unchanged from DS.  Lines that cross the throat return
  through it at other angles, so the net flux is zero.  Matching colors
  mark the same flux tube in the two charts; the pale source-side lines
  escape to infinity without reaching the throat.}
  \label{fig:fixed-sector-field}
\end{figure}

The constants $c_1$ and $c_2$ are the potentials of two asymptotic regions,
and they fix which member of the static family DS write down.
Equation~\eqref{eq:maxwell-concrete} uses only that $S$ is closed and free
of crossing current.  A wormhole at Sgr~A* would have both mouths in one
region, where there is no pair of end potentials to compare and a
single-valued potential becomes an assumption of its own: the loop through
the throat and back bounds no surface, so $\oint\mathbf E\cdot d\mathbf l$ need
not vanish.  Assuming that it does is an extra condition, like $c_1=c_2$
with two ends.  Equation~\eqref{eq:maxwell-concrete} needs neither, so the
monopole stays constant in either geometry.

\subsection{The balance law}

The same balance holds for any measuring surface.
Let $S_t$ be a smooth family of measuring surfaces and let
$\mathcal T=\bigcup_{t\in[t_1,t_2]}S_t$ be the timelike three-surface they
sweep out.

Frolov and Novikov proved the corresponding balance for a static wormhole,
concluding that the flux through the handle cannot change until charged
particles pass through it, and that motion of charges in the external space
leaves it unaltered \cite{FrolovNovikov1990PhysicalEffects}.  We restate the
identity for an arbitrary measuring family.

\begin{theorem}[Measuring-surface balance, after Frolov and Novikov]
\label{thm:maxwell}
If $\partial\mathcal T=S_{t_2}-S_{t_1}$, then
\begin{equation}
  Q[S_{t_2}]-Q[S_{t_1}]=\int_{\mathcal T}\star j.
  \label{eq:maxwell-balance}
\end{equation}
In particular, the measured flux is constant whenever no electric current
crosses $\mathcal T$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Integrate $d\star F=4\pi\star j$ over $\mathcal T$ and apply Stokes' theorem.
Each $S_t$ is arbitrary, and may bound no compact region.
\end{proof}

Figure~\ref{fig:worldtube} shows which surface is meant: $\mathcal T$ is the
history of the observer's measuring sphere, not a cut through the throat.

\begin{figure}[!ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.72\textwidth,trim=0 35 0 55,clip]{figures/fig_worldtube_stokes.pdf}
  \caption{A measuring surface $S_t$ is compared at $t_1$ and $t_2$ through
  its spacetime history $\mathcal T=\bigcup_tS_t$.  If no current crosses
  $\mathcal T$, Stokes' theorem gives equal Maxwell fluxes on the endpoint
  surfaces even when the source moves near the other mouth.  The gravitational
  argument uses the same geometry with the first-order Killing charge in place
  of the Maxwell flux.}
  \label{fig:worldtube}
\end{figure}

In a source-free region $d\star F=0$, so the flux is set by the initial data
and does not change on its own.  Searches for wormhole candidates already
treat a mouth's flux as fixed data
\cite{PiotrovichEtAl2024WormholeCandidates}.%
\footnote{Being fixed is not the same as being protected.  The flux is subject
to
Schwinger-type processes of the kind Gibbons described for charged black holes
\cite{Gibbons1975VacuumPolarization}, and classically to ionization of any
polarizable matter supporting the throat.  Both move charge across the
measuring surface, and are therefore instances of the balance law above rather
than exceptions to it.}

Which surface one measures on matters.  A charge that crosses the throat
changes the flux through a cut at the throat, but not through an observer
sphere further out, so long as it stays between the two.  Once it crosses the
observer sphere as well, Eq.~\eqref{eq:maxwell-balance} changes the measured
flux by the ordinary current term.

The fixed-$A$ fields of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ds-multipoles} are valid static
boundary-value solutions; they are not successive states of a single Maxwell
evolution.  This is the local worldtube form of the fixed wormhole-charge
prescription used in Ref.~\cite{Krasnikov2008ElectrostaticInteraction}.

Theorem~\ref{thm:maxwell} applies to a sphere around either mouth, so each
carries a flux of its own, changed only by current crossing that sphere.

\section{The DS mass perturbation}
\label{sec:ds-model}
% ============================================================

We repeat the check for their gravitational construction, now against the
linearized field equations, asking whether its monopole part can evolve in
time.

DS join two copies of the Schwarzschild exterior,
\begin{equation}
  d\bar s^2=-f(r)dt^2+f(r)^{-1}dr^2+r^2d\Omega^2,
  \qquad f(r)=1-\frac{r_g}{r},
  \label{eq:background}
\end{equation}
at spheres $r=R>r_g$.  A small source of mass $\mu$ moves at radius
$r_1=A(t)>R$ in the ``other'' exterior, and $r_2$ denotes the observer-side
radius.  Matching at fixed $A$ gives the one-parameter family
\cite{DaiStojkovic2019ObservingV2,
DaiStojkovic2019ObservingV3}
\begin{equation}
  h^{\mathrm{our}}_{tt}=\frac{2b}{r_2},
  \qquad
  h^{\mathrm{our}}_{rr}=\frac{2b r_2}{(r_2-r_g)^2},
  \qquad
  b(A)=\frac{\mu R}{A},
  \label{eq:ds-observer-mode}
\end{equation}
a vacuum mass perturbation that leaves the predicted acceleration unchanged,
$\simeq-b/r_2^2$ far from the mouth.  DS identify $b$ as an effective mass at
the observer mouth.%
\footnote{The journal field instead uses
$b_{tt}=\mu R/A$ and
$b_{rr}=\mu A(R-r_g)^2/[R(A-r_g)^2]$
\cite{DaiStojkovic2019Observing}.  For $A>R$,
$\delta G^{r}{}_{r}=2(b_{tt}-b_{rr})/[f(r_2)r_2^3]\ne0$, so this field is off
shell; later revisions set $b_{rr}=b_{tt}$.  Their displayed source-side
annulus $R<r<A$, with $h_{tt}=2\mu/A$ and $h_{rr}=0$, is also off shell,
$\delta G^{r}{}_{r}=2\mu r_g/[Af(r)r^3]\ne0$.  We therefore grant the proposal
its strongest observer-side form and take $b$ to multiply the on-shell
$\ell=0$ mass mode.}

DS and Simonetti et al. implement an eccentric orbit by evaluating this family
along $A\to A(t)$, treating it as a sequence of monopoles
\cite{DaiStojkovic2019Observing,SimonettiEtAl2021SensitiveSearches}.  For the
resulting purely $\ell=0$ field, the time--radial component of the linearized
Einstein tensor is
\begin{equation}
  \delta G^{r}{}_{t}=\frac{2\dot b}{r_2^{2}},
  \label{eq:momentum-constraint}
\end{equation}
which must vanish in vacuum, forcing $\dot b=0$.  This is a constraint,
binding at each instant and at the observer's location rather than at a distant
boundary.
When a source is present, $\delta G^{r}{}_{t}=8\pi\,\delta T^{r}{}_{t}$ gives
$\dot b=\oint\delta T^{r}{}_{t}\,dS$: the change is supplied by energy flux
through the measuring sphere.%
\footnote{The calculation assumes a timelike cut $R>r_g$ and a uniform
linearization.  Since the mode in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ds-observer-mode} is a mass
perturbation, smallness at the cut requires
$2\mu R/[A(R-r_g)]\ll1$.  Thus the choice $R=r_g$, discussed by DS, lies
outside this perturbative regime.}

\section{First-order gravitational balance}
\label{sec:gravity-charge}
% ============================================================

The gravitational version replaces the Maxwell flux with a linearized Killing
charge and assumes no symmetry of the perturbation.

Let $h_{\alpha\beta}$ denote the metric perturbation, $\delta_h$ the variation
along it, and $\xi$ a vector field with $\delta_h\xi=0$.  For the
Einstein--Hilbert Lagrangian, the covariant phase space construction of
Refs.~\cite{IyerWald1994NoetherCharge,BarnichBrandt2002CovariantTheory}
supplies a linearized surface-charge two-form
$\bm k_\xi[h;\bar g]=\delta_h\bm Q_\xi-\xi\mathbin{\cdot}\bm\theta$, built from
the Noether-charge two-form $\bm Q_\xi$ and the symplectic potential
$\bm\theta$.  Where the background equations hold it satisfies
\begin{equation}
  d\bm k_\xi[h;\bar g]
  =\bm\omega(\bar g;h,\mathcal L_\xi\bar g)-\delta_h\bm C_\xi,
  \label{eq:iw-identity}
\end{equation}
with $\bm\omega$ the symplectic current and the constraint term $\bm C_\xi$
vanishing on shell.  For a background Killing field a charge of this kind can
also be built directly from the linearized field equations
\cite{AbbottDeser1982Stability}; we use the covariant form because it supplies
the sourced version below.  We apply it to a measuring worldtube.

\begin{theorem}[First-order Killing-charge balance]
\label{thm:gravity}
Let a smooth timelike three-surface $\mathcal T$ in an observer-side region
have boundary $S_{t_2}-S_{t_1}$.  Suppose that, in a neighborhood of
$\mathcal T$, the background is on shell and admits an exact Killing field
$\xi$, and $h_{\alpha\beta}$ is a smooth solution of the homogeneous
linearized Einstein equation.  Define
\begin{equation}
  \delta H_\xi[S;h]=\int_S\bm k_\xi[h;\bar g].
  \label{eq:linear-charge}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
  \delta H_\xi[S_{t_2};h]=\delta H_\xi[S_{t_1};h].
  \label{eq:linear-rigidity}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $\mathcal L_\xi\bar g=0$ and the linearized constraint source vanishes,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:iw-identity} gives $d\bm k_\xi=0$.  Applying Stokes' theorem to
the same worldtube geometry as Fig.~\ref{fig:worldtube} gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:linear-rigidity}.
\end{proof}

If a first-order constraint source crosses the measuring worldtube, the same
identity gives instead
\begin{equation}
  \delta H_\xi[S_{t_2};h]-\delta H_\xi[S_{t_1};h]
  =-\int_{\mathcal T}\delta_h\bm C_\xi.
  \label{eq:sourced-balance}
\end{equation}
Thus matter or wormhole-support stress energy can change the charge by carrying
first-order Killing energy through $\mathcal T$.  Such a channel is allowed,
but its magnitude and phase require a dynamical solution.

We use the standard Einstein--Hilbert representative of $\bm\theta$.  When an
asymptotically flat end is available, choosing $\xi$ to approach its unit time
translation makes the charge agree with the ADM mass variation.  It is a
first-order variation; no integrability beyond the linearized theory is
assumed.
Gauge invariance here refers to proper diffeomorphisms that preserve the
measuring surface and any asymptotic frame; a covariantly transformed surface
embedding is equivalent.  Large boundary time rescalings are not gauge
redundancies in this comparison.  The proof is local to $\mathcal T$, and holds
for a single-ended handle.

% ============================================================
\section{Application to the DS monopole}
\label{sec:gravity-application}
% ============================================================

It remains to identify the DS coefficient $b$ with the charge of
Theorem~\ref{thm:gravity}.

On a round background sphere, harmonics with $\ell\ge1$ integrate to
zero, so $\delta H_\xi[S;h]$ sees only the $\ell=0$ part of the perturbation;
and because the linearized Einstein operator commutes with the harmonic
decomposition on a spherically symmetric background, that part satisfies the
monopole equations by itself.  The angular structure of the perturbation is
therefore irrelevant to the monopole: nonsphericity populates $\ell\ge1$
without altering the $\ell=0$ equations.

With the background time normalization fixed, linearized Birkhoff
classification gives
\begin{equation}
  h^{\ell=0}_{\alpha\beta}
  =\delta M\,\partial_M\bar g_{\alpha\beta}
   +\mathcal L_\eta\bar g_{\alpha\beta},
  \label{eq:l0-mass}
\end{equation}
where $\eta$ generates an admissible gauge transformation
\cite{MartelPoisson2005SchwarzschildPerturbations}.  The vacuum field equations
make $\delta M$ constant in a connected first-order solution.  Since
$\partial_M\bar g$ is itself a vacuum perturbation, $d\bm k_\xi=0$ makes
$\int_S\bm k_\xi[\partial_M\bar g;\bar g]$ independent of the round sphere $S$
within the vacuum region.  With the standard Einstein--Hilbert representative
and $\xi=\partial_t$, direct evaluation on any round sphere gives
\begin{equation}
  \left.\bm k_{\partial_t}
  [\delta M\,\partial_M\bar g;\bar g]\right|_{S_r}
  =\frac{\delta M}{4\pi}\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\phi,
  \qquad
  \int_{S_r}\bm k_{\partial_t}=\delta M.
  \label{eq:finite-sphere-mass}
\end{equation}
No sphere at infinity is required; wherever an asymptotically flat end exists,
this agrees with the ADM mass variation.

For $r_g=2M$, $\partial_M\bar g_{tt}=2/r$ and
$\partial_M\bar g_{rr}=2r/(r-r_g)^2$, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:ds-observer-mode} is
\begin{equation}
  h^{\mathrm{our}}_{\alpha\beta}=b\,\partial_M\bar g_{\alpha\beta},
  \qquad
  \delta H_\xi=\delta M=b=\frac{\mu R}{A}.
  \label{eq:ds-is-charge}
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[DS mass-monopole rigidity]
Consider any on-shell completion of the DS construction about the stated
Schwarzschild background that retains
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ds-is-charge} and leaves a neighborhood of a fixed observer-side
measuring worldtube free of first-order constraint sources.  Then $b$ is
constant along the evolution.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:gravity} with the background Schwarzschild time
translation.  Equation~\eqref{eq:ds-is-charge} identifies its surface
functional with $b$.
\end{proof}

The prescription $b(t)=\mu R/A(t)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ds-observer-mode} instead
moves through different mass-charge sectors as the source radius changes.
Separate stationary solutions at different $A$ need not be successive states
of one source-free history.  A mouth changes its mass when a body actually
transits it \cite{FrolovNovikov1990PhysicalEffects}; here the proposed variation
would require equal and opposite mouth-mass changes with no transit.%
\footnote{The revised static coefficients give
$\delta M_{\rm source}=\mu-\mu R/A$ and
$\delta M_{\rm observer}=\mu R/A$, whose sum is $\mu$ when each end uses its
future-directed unit time.  This is static bookkeeping, not a transfer law;
changing either charge requires the appropriate flux.}

There are two relevant ways an on-shell dynamical completion can alter this
conclusion.  If the $A(t)$-dependent part retains the mass-mode form, it changes
$\delta M$ and must be supplied by the source term in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sourced-balance}.  If additional components instead make the
varying coordinate coefficient a gauge contribution, then it is not a mass
observable and the acceleration inferred from $h_{tt}$ alone requires a new
gauge-invariant derivation.  This exhausts the source-free first-order
$\ell=0$ sector in a fixed asymptotic frame; other physical channels remain.

In particular, nonspherical motion may generate $\ell\ge1$ perturbations and
radiative $\ell\ge2$ modes.  These do not contribute to the round-sphere mass
charge, but they may produce other observer-side effects.  In the DS
electrostatic model these carry their own geometric suppression: the matching
gives observer-side multipole coefficients proportional to
$R^{2\ell+1}/A^{\ell+1}$, so at observer radius $r_2$ the
dipole contribution to the potential is smaller than the monopole contribution
by $3R^2/(2Ar_2)$.  The gravitational analogues are not computed in the DS
construction, which treats the monopole alone
\cite{DaiStojkovic2019Observing}.  Vacuum
gravitational-wave energy flux is quadratic in first-order wave amplitude, so
over a fixed orbital time it is $O(\epsilon^2)$ rather than the reversible
$O(\epsilon)$ monopole modulation DS propose
\cite{BondiVanderBurgMetzner1962Gravitational,Sachs1962Asymptotic}.  A
first-order matter or support-field flux is a distinct possibility already
covered by Eq.~\eqref{eq:sourced-balance}.  Nonlinear throat response and
scalar monopoles lie outside the present claim.

% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ============================================================

In Maxwell theory, each mouth carries a flux of its own, and redistribution
requires current across the corresponding measuring surface.  The position
dependence of the electrostatic solution traces to holding both ends at the
same potential; holding the charge instead removes it.  On the
stationary Schwarzschild background, the first-order surface-charge identity
gives the gravitational counterpart without assuming a spherical
perturbation.  For the DS $\ell=0$ ansatz, one component of the linearized field
equations already forbids $A\to A(t)$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:momentum-constraint}; the corrected observer coefficient is the
Schwarzschild mass charge, and the worldtube balance holds it fixed in a
source-free evolution.

The static constructions still contain nonzero cross-mouth fields.  Dynamical
higher multipoles, waves, and explicit matter or support-field responses are
not excluded.  What does not
follow is the proposed position-dependent $O(\mu)$ mass monopole obtained by
the replacement $A\to A(t)$.  Establishing such a signal requires either the
first-order flux that changes the charge or a different gauge-invariant
observable.
