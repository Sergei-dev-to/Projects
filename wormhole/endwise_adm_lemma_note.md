# Endwise ADM Conservation Note

## Purpose

This note records the gravity-side statement in the narrowest form we currently know how to defend.

The intended use is as an appendix-sized argument for the full paper, not as a historical survey.

## Admissibility Lemma

Let `(M,h,\pi)` be asymptotically flat initial data with finitely many asymptotically flat ends `E_1,\dots,E_n`, satisfying the standard Regge--Teitelboim falloff/parity conditions.

For any choice of constants `c_1,\dots,c_n`, there exists a smooth lapse `N` with vanishing shift such that:

1. on each end `E_a`, `N=c_a` outside some compact set;
2. the improved RT Hamiltonian `H[N,0]` is differentiable;
3. on the constraint surface,
   \[
   H[N,0]=\sum_{a=1}^n c_a E_a^{ADM}.
   \]

In particular, for each end `E_a` there exists an end-selecting lapse `N_a` with
- `N_a=1` on the far region of `E_a`,
- `N_a=0` on the far region of every other end,

such that the corresponding improved Hamiltonian
\[
H_a:=H[N_a,0]
\]
reduces on shell to `E_a^{ADM}`.

## Proof of the Lemma

Because `M` has finitely many ends, there is a compact set `K` such that
\[
M\setminus K = \bigsqcup_{a=1}^n U_a,
\]
where each `U_a` lies in end `E_a` and is diffeomorphic to the exterior of a ball in `\mathbb{R}^3`.

Choose slightly smaller end neighborhoods `U_a' \subset U_a` with compact closure in `U_a \cup K`, and choose smooth cutoff functions `\chi_a` such that

- `\chi_a=1` on `U_a'`,
- `\chi_a=0` on `U_b'` for `b\neq a`,
- all derivatives of `\chi_a` are supported in a compact region.

Then
\[
N:=\sum_{a=1}^n c_a \chi_a
\]
is smooth and equals the constant `c_a` on the far region `U_a'` of each end.

Now apply the usual one-end Regge--Teitelboim differentiability argument separately on each end.

- On `U_a'`, the lapse is exactly constant, so the boundary variation coming from the scalar constraint is exactly the same as for an asymptotic time translation with coefficient `c_a`.
- Since `\partial N=0` on each far end, there are no additional asymptotic terms involving derivatives of the lapse.
- All derivatives of `N` are supported in a compact region, so the interpolation region contributes no new asymptotic boundary term.

Therefore the total boundary variation is just the sum of the endwise ADM variations,
\[
\delta B[N,0] = \sum_{a=1}^n c_a\,\delta E_a^{ADM},
\]
and adding the boundary term
\[
B[N,0]=\sum_{a=1}^n c_a E_a^{ADM}
\]
makes the Hamiltonian differentiable.

On the constraint surface the bulk term vanishes, so
\[
H[N,0]=\sum_{a=1}^n c_a E_a^{ADM}.
\]

Taking `c_a=\delta_{ab}` gives the end-selecting Hamiltonians `H_b`.

This proves the lemma.

## Proposition

Let `(M,h,\pi)` be asymptotically flat initial data with finitely many asymptotically flat ends `E_1,\dots,E_n`, satisfying the standard Regge--Teitelboim falloff/parity conditions. Assume:

1. `M` has no additional physical boundary components besides the asymptotic ends.
2. The Einstein evolution under consideration preserves the same asymptotic class and is generated by a Hamiltonian of the form
   \[
   H = \sum_{a=1}^n c_a H_a + H_{\mathrm{gauge}},
   \]
   where the `H_a` are the end-selecting Hamiltonians furnished by the lemma and `H_{\mathrm{gauge}}` is generated by asymptotically trivial lapse/shift data.

Then each endwise ADM energy is conserved weakly:

`dE_a^{ADM}/dt = 0`

on the constraint surface / reduced phase space.

## Proof

Fix two ends `E_a` and `E_b`.

1. By the standard ADM hypersurface-deformation algebra, the Poisson bracket of two scalar generators is a vector generator:
   \[
   \{H[N_a,0],H[N_b,0]\}=H[0,\beta_{ab}],
   \]
   with
   \[
   \beta_{ab}^i = h^{ij}(N_a\partial_j N_b - N_b\partial_j N_a).
   \]

2. Because the `N_a` may be chosen exactly constant on the far region of every end, their gradients have compact support. Therefore `\beta_{ab}` has compact support as well.

3. In the RT formalism, a compactly supported vector generator carries no asymptotic boundary charge. Because assumption (1) excludes additional internal boundary terms, there is no other boundary contribution. Hence
   \[
   \{H_a,H_b\}\approx 0
   \]
   weakly.

4. Also `\{H_a,H_a\}=0` by antisymmetry. Therefore, for the full evolution Hamiltonian,
   \[
   \frac{dH_a}{dt}
   =
   \{H_a,H\}
   =
   \sum_{b=1}^n c_b \{H_a,H_b\} + \{H_a,H_{\mathrm{gauge}}\}
   \approx 0.
   \]

5. By the lemma, `H_a = E_a^{ADM}` on the constraint surface. Therefore
   \[
   \frac{dE_a^{ADM}}{dt}\approx 0.
   \]

This proves the proposition.

## Interpretation

The proposition does not depend on wormhole topology. It is a statement about asymptotic boundary charges in the multi-end RT Hamiltonian framework.

The physical content is:

- each asymptotic end carries its own ADM energy,
- ordinary Einstein evolution preserving the asymptotic structure does not change those endwise energies,
- therefore local bulk motion, including motion near or through a wormhole throat, cannot by itself induce a varying ADM monopole at another asymptotic end.

## Scope

The proposition is intentionally narrow.

- It is a weak Hamiltonian conservation statement, not a PDE/global-existence theorem.
- It is about ADM energy at spatial infinity, not Bondi mass at null infinity.
- It excludes internal boundary terms from horizons, thin shells treated as literal boundaries, or other additional Hamiltonian boundary components. Those do not change the meaning of the ADM charge at infinity, but they do require extra terms in the Hamiltonian proof.
- The only nontrivial technical input beyond standard RT is the end-by-end differentiability argument in the lemma above. The key simplification is that the end-selecting lapses may be chosen exactly constant on each far end, so all interpolation derivatives are compactly supported.

## Practical use in the paper

The safest way to use this result in the main draft is:

1. Present the Maxwell theorem as the main rigorous result.
2. State the gravity proposition in a separate appendix or clearly marked subsection.
3. If desired, promote the admissibility of end-selecting lapses from an assumption to the lemma above, whose proof is just the RT one-end boundary-term argument applied end by end.

That keeps the gravity statement strong while making the remaining technical assumption completely visible.
