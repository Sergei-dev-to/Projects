# Warm traversal of a CTC region — findings

Working note, 2026-08-02. Companion to `groundhog_day_ctc_research_note.md`
(Codex scoping note) and `groundhog_day_ctc_review.md` (review of it).

**Question driving this session.** Can a warm observer retain information about a
chronology-violating region without exotic matter and without passing through
singularities?

---

## 1. What the literature actually establishes

Read in full, not from abstracts.

### Exotic matter is not required — settled

**Krasnikov, gr-qc/9711040**, p. 2: *"the example of the Deutsch-Politzer
spacetime (DPS) shows that to create an NTM we need neither 'dangerous
geodesics', nor 'exotic matter'."* DPS is Minkowski cut and reglued by
translations — vacuum, all energy conditions trivially satisfied.

**Ori, PRD 76, 044002 (gr-qc/0701024)** does it with real matter: vacuum core,
dust envelope of positive energy density, external Schwarzschild, on
asymptotically flat topologically trivial (R³) C^∞ initial data. *"Trivially
satisfies the weak, dominant, and strong energy conditions,"* with a well-posed
initial-value problem.

### A singularity in the spacetime is required — but not on the observer's worldline

**Minguzzi, arXiv:1406.5919, Theorem 19:** under the null convergence condition,
with S a non-compact connected partial Cauchy hypersurface, H⁺(S) cannot be
compactly generated. (He notes Hawking's original proof "was incomplete as he
assumed a C² horizon at two steps.")

**Theorem 20**, and his own reading of it (p. 35): *"without breaking the
spacetime continuum it is impossible, in classical general relativity, to
locally create time machines or to locally change the topology of space."* The
escape is alternative (ii): *"K is non compact and contains an integral curve of
V which escapes every compact set in some direction."*

That alternative is **purely topological**. Minguzzi establishes that breaking
the continuum is the only local escape and says nothing about what *kind* of
break is required.

**Krasnikov's "handy singularities" are that break, minimised.** His three
properties: (1) *"Unlike Misner-type singularities, they make the relevant
region non-compact"* — Minguzzi's (ii) exactly; (2) *"absolutely mild (i.e. all
curvature scalars are bounded)"*; (3) *"of laboratory, rather than of
cosmological nature."* In DPS they are the four removed edge points, and they
are *"irremovable."* They sit at the **rim** — a worldline through the middle of
the cut never meets them.

### Both constructions suppress the traveller, and both authors say so

Krasnikov: *"the problem remains of how to cause a spacetime to evolve in the
appropriate manner."* There is no observer in DPS as a source.

Ori frames his central regularity worry as danger to *"an extended **(test)**
observer attempting to cross the CH."* He also notes that a Cauchy horizon
generated by closed null geodesics is claimed unstable against fragmentation,
then: *"we do not attempt to address issues of stability in this paper."*

**The reduction under this whole literature is: the traveller is a test
particle.** The conclusion asserted past it is that a time machine can be built
and traversed without exotic matter.

### Causal structure, for the record

Entry crosses H⁺(S) — the chronology-violating set is outside D⁺(S) by
definition. **Exit crosses nothing.** H⁺(S) is not a wall around the CTC region;
it is the boundary of the predictable region, and cannot be un-crossed. The
asymmetry is informational, not geometric: everything after entry is not a
function of S. Krasnikov, p. 2: *"In any time machine we encounter extra
unpredictable information as soon as we intersect the Cauchy horizon (by its
very definition) and so for none of TM's the evolution can be completely
controlled from the initial surface."*

## 2. The calculation

`sim/ori_core_pp.py`. Ori's core metric, his Eq. (2):

    ds² = (1-2μ/r) dv² + 2 dv dr + r²(dθ² + sinh²θ dφ²),    v ~ v + l

Pseudo-Schwarzschild — Schwarzschild Wick-rotated θ → iθ, so the transverse
2-space is hyperbolic. Cauchy horizon at r = 2μ. Unlike DPS this is **not** flat
(DPS is Minkowski cut and reglued, Riemann ≡ 0, so no curvature diagnostic can
say anything there at all).

**Premises verified rather than assumed:** g^rr = 0 at r = 2μ so the surface is
null; g(∂_v, ∂_v) = 0 there; and ∇_{∂_v}∂_v = κ ∂_v with κ = −1/(4μ) constant,
so ∂_v is geodesic on the **whole** surface.

**Result — parallel-propagated frame components on r = 2μ:**

| component | boost weight | value |
|---|---|---|
| R_KYKY, R_KZKZ, R_KYKZ | +2 | **0** |
| R_KNKY, R_KNKZ | +1 | **0** |
| R_KYNY | 0 | −1/(8μ²) |
| R_KNKN | 0 | +1/(4μ²) |
| R_YZYZ | 0 | −1/(4μ²) |

**Control:** the same script on Schwarzschild in ingoing Eddington form gives an
identical structure — all +2 and +1 components zero, boost-weight-zero entries
the same magnitudes with signs flipped by the Wick rotation. Kretschmann
48μ²/r⁶ for both, matching the known Schwarzschild value.

**Conclusion: there is no non-scalar curvature singularity at Ori's Cauchy
horizon.** Every component that could diverge as the boost factor q → ∞ vanishes
identically.

## 3. Why, and the criterion that follows

r = 2μ is a **genuine Killing horizon**: ∂_v is Killing, null on the surface, and
geodesic across all of it with constant surface gravity. So B_AB = 0 by the
Killing-plus-hypersurface-orthogonal argument, and the Sachs equation forces
R_KAKB = 0. This is the PG-Schwarzschild control case from
`alcubierre-penrose/paper/main.tex`, reappearing in a different problem.

Alcubierre fails precisely because ∂_t is null on v² = 1 but geodesic **only at
two isolated axial points**, so no Sachs constraint applies and the transverse
wall curvature H_AB survives.

> **Criterion, first form.** A chronology or Cauchy horizon that is a genuine
> Killing horizon is immune to the non-scalar obstruction. The obstruction
> requires the null surface to fail to be a Killing horizon.

**Sharpened, same day, after the Zeno-collar computation** (`sim/zeno_rim.py`,
`sim/zeno_normchar.py`; see `zeno_chronology_transits_review.md`). The first form
is too coarse — there are two distinct ways to fail to be a Killing horizon, with
opposite consequences:

> **Criterion, sharpened.** The non-scalar obstruction requires a surface that is
> **null** *and* **not geodesically generated**. Three cases:
>
> 1. **Null on an OPEN portion, carrying a congruence of geodesic generators**
>    (a Killing horizon): `B_AB = 0`, Sachs forces `R_KAKB = 0`. Immune. — Ori's
>    core; the Zeno product core.
> 2. **Not null at all**: no generators exist, so nothing is unboundedly boosted
>    and the curvature components are irrelevant however large. Immune. — generic
>    points of the Zeno localization's collar, where `B=0` is timelike.
> 3. **Null only on a smaller set, so no generator congruence**: individual
>    orbits may still be pregeodesics and incomplete, Sachs does not apply, and
>    the transverse Hessian survives *and* is amplified.
>    **This is the obstruction.** — Alcubierre, where `∂_t` is null on the whole
>    `v²=1` surface yet geodesic only at the two isolated axial points; and the
>    Zeno collar's *transverse critical points*.

**Correction, 2026-08-02 21:24, from the revised Zeno draft §4.4.** Case 1 was
first written as "null and geodesically generated," which is too loose. The
Sachs argument requires an **open** null hypersurface with a **congruence** of
generators. At an isolated transverse critical point (`B_A = 0`, `B_t ≠ 0`) the
surface is null *and* the closed axial orbit is a pregeodesic, yet there is no
hypersurface and no congruence — so no Sachs constraint, and the draft's
Eq. (4.18)–(4.19) give `R(k,E_A,k,E_B) = -½f²B_AB` diverging as `|λ-λ*|⁻²` with
scalar invariants finite. Those points belong to case 3.

I had concluded from `|dB|²|_{B=0} = (∂_ρB)² + (∂_zB)²` that the collar is
generically timelike and therefore harmless. Generically timelike is right; the
"therefore harmless" skipped the critical points, where the obstruction is real.
The draft's Prop 4.2 removes them by choosing a radial toroidal cutoff with
`∇_⊥B ≠ 0` on all of `𝒵` — **avoidable by design, not absent.**

Case 3 is a narrow band, and both the Alcubierre result and the Zeno collar's
critical points sit in it. The corrected form explains all five computed cases
and gives the search rule: **look for surfaces that stay null on a set too small
to carry a generator congruence.**

This explains both known "mild" time machines structurally rather than by luck:
DPS is flat, so there is no curvature to diverge; Ori built his on a
Wick-rotated Schwarzschild, so Sachs protects it. It also says where to look for
a case that *does* obstruct: a chronology horizon that is not Killing-generated.

## 3a. The criterion's first target: Ori's *earlier* pp-wave core

The criterion says the obstruction needs a null surface that fails to be a
Killing horizon. Ori's current model is immune because its CH is *entirely*
generated by closed null geodesics. But on p. 2 he contrasts this with his own
previous model (his ref. [7]), which used a locally **pp-wave** vacuum core, and
states: *"In the locally-pp metric, on the other hand, the CNGs are generically
isolated."*

That is precisely the flagged structure, and it is the direct analogue of the
Alcubierre case, where ∂_t is null on the whole v²=1 surface but geodesic **only
at two isolated axial points**. Isolated generators ⇒ no Sachs constraint ⇒ the
obstruction can survive.

Ori then raises the danger himself, and cannot settle it:

> "This structure may allow the possibility of a singularity which evolves at the
> future boundary of D⁺(S) ... and extends arbitrarily close to N. In that case,
> N would be a regular CNG ... but would still lack a regular neighborhood in
> D⁺(S). This might be harmful for an extended (test) observer attempting to
> cross the CH ... It is not clear if this potential problem is realized in the
> model [7]. It is hard to say, because the exact solution for the time-evolving
> metric is only known in the internal vacuum core and in the external region,
> not in the envelope."

He flagged it, could not resolve it, and switched to the pseudo-Schwarzschild
core specifically to avoid it. The calculation in §2 confirms the switch worked.

**Target: run the same diagnostic on the pp-wave core of Ori's ref. [7].** It is
a vacuum solution known explicitly in the core, which is exactly the region the
diagnostic needs. Reference [7] still needs identifying from the paper's
bibliography. This is the best lead the thread produced and it came from a
computation, not a screen.

## 4. Where this leaves the warm-observer question

**Ruled out: the tidal answer.** Ori's stated worry about an extended observer
crossing the CH is, at the level of parallel-propagated curvature, unfounded —
his horizon is as regular as Schwarzschild's and an extended body crossing it
feels finite tides.

**What remains** is the Misner-type structure Ori himself names: the horizon is
generated by *closed* null geodesics of finite affine length. That is a
holonomy-and-incompleteness pathology, invisible to every curvature diagnostic
by construction, which is the definition of quasi-regular — the category of
Slobodov 2008.

So if there is an obstruction to warm traversal here, **it is holonomy, not
curvature.** That is the second independent route in this session pointing at
holonomy rather than curvature.

**Is there a path to a non-perturbative result?** Not a short one, either sign.
Every existing no-go keys on *compact generation*, and Ori and Krasnikov both
evade by being compactly *constructed* but not compactly generated — Ori is
explicit that his models "might not be compactly generated." A new negative
theorem would have to bite on non-compactly generated horizons, quantifying over
solutions rather than perturbing one. A positive result means an exact
Einstein–matter construction with a self-gravitating extended body crossing a
Cauchy horizon. Both are programs, not calculations.

## 5. Pre-emptions found, recorded honestly

Ideas generated in this session that turned out to exist already:

- **"Retained memory is either vacuous or Jinn-carrying."** Second horn is
  Krasnikov 1997, quoted above — unpredictable information enters at the horizon
  by definition.
- **Reusability as a closure/fixed-point condition on the machine.** Gavassino,
  *Life on a closed timelike curve*, CQG 2024 (arXiv:2405.18640), states a
  restoration principle as an operator identity and derives spectral
  discretisation from it. His closure is on the traveller, not the region, so
  the distinction survives — but the machinery is his.
- **Warm observer destabilises via blueshift, trading against exotic matter.**
  Fewster–Roman, gr-qc/0510079: the smaller the exotic matter content, the more
  prone the structure is to destabilisation by small amounts of infalling
  positive matter, *"since this matter will be enormously blueshifted by the
  time it reaches the throat."* In print since 2005, in the wormhole setting.
