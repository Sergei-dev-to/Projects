## 1. Krasnikov (2008)

Source:
- Sergei Krasnikov, `Electrostatic interaction of a pointlike charge with a wormhole`
- arXiv:0802.1358
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.1358

Role:
- likely closest Maxwell-side prior art
- may already contain much of the fixed-sector/source-free split

Key abstract points:
- the potential is split into a part that depends on the charge location and a part that does not change under quasistatic motion
- source-free potentials differ by throat flux
- flux can be interpreted as the wormhole “charge”

Exact place to revisit:
- arXiv abstract lines 42-44 from the arXiv page
- TeX source lines 153-183: uniqueness for a wormhole of given charge `Q`
- TeX source lines 204-213: `Q(r)=const` under quasistatic transport
- TeX source lines 543-571: explicit argument that the wormhole part keeps the same `Q` after quasistatic motion
- TeX source lines 217-220: decomposition of force into wormhole part plus self-force

Current assessment:
- clearly supportive rather than adversarial
- very close to the source-free/source-generated split used in the present draft
- stronger judgment after reading the TeX source:
  - Krasnikov already proves uniqueness of the electrostatic solution for fixed wormhole charge `Q`
  - he explicitly argues that `Q` remains unchanged when the particle is quasistatically moved
  - he decomposes the force into a source-free wormhole part and a self-force part
- what still appears not to be present in the same form:
  - the asymptotic-language no-go about varying opposite-end monopole coefficients
  - the explicit `formal solution family` versus `dynamical reachability` diagnosis
  - the targeted application to Dai--Stojkovic and their observational descendants
- revised judgment: Krasnikov 2008 substantially anticipates the Maxwell-side backbone of the paper, so the paper's novelty will need to be framed more as
  - a sharpened asymptotic reformulation,
  - an explicit multipole/no-varying-monopole presentation,
  - and a correction of the DS observational interpretation

## Additional target: Visser / Frolov--Novikov mouth-mass lore

Source:
- Matt Visser, `Lorentzian Wormholes: From Einstein to Hawking` (1995)
- Valery P. Frolov and Igor D. Novikov, `Physical effects in wormholes and time machines` (1990)
- local file: `_frolov_novikov_1990.pdf`

Role:
- likely primary source chain for the standard statement that when matter actually traverses a wormhole, the entry mouth gains mass and the exit mouth loses mass
- important for defining the gravity-side boundary of the paper's claim

Question to resolve:
- are these sources talking about mouth-local / quasi-local bookkeeping, genuine end ADM masses, or an uncareful mix of the two?
- do they explicitly restrict the statement to actual transit through the throat, rather than mere approach / orbit / no-transit motion?

Why this matters:
- if the lore is only about mouth-local bookkeeping under actual transit, it is compatible with the present rigidity thesis about end ADM monopoles
- if the lore is stated as an end-ADM statement, then either the gravity theorem must be narrowed or the classic statement must be corrected

Current assessment:
- secondary traces suggest this is a real and widely repeated piece of wormhole lore
- it appears to concern actual transit, not ordinary no-transit motion near the throat
- it remains a `must-check primary source`, because the paper should not cite the claim from secondary discussion alone

## Additional target: endwise ADM / Hamiltonian charge conservation

Sources:
- Tullio Regge and Claudio Teitelboim, `Role of Surface Integrals in the Hamiltonian Formulation of General Relativity` (1974)
- Piotr T. Chrusciel, `The general relativistic constraint equations` (2020 review)
- Domenico Giulini, `The Hamiltonian formulation of General Relativity` (local extract: `_giulini_hamiltonian_gr.txt`)

Role:
- likely source chain for the gravity-side analogue of the Maxwell theorem
- the key question is whether the literature already states, in essentially finished form, that each asymptotically flat end carries its own Hamiltonian / ADM charge and that these end charges are separately conserved under ordinary evolution

What is clearly standard:
- asymptotically flat initial data with finitely many ends are standard in the modern mathematical literature
- Chrusciel defines AF data by requiring that, outside a compact set, the manifold is a disjoint union of finitely many ends
- ADM energy-momentum is then defined endwise in the asymptotic chart of an end
- Regge--Teitelboim provide the Hamiltonian surface-term machinery that turns asymptotic symmetries into surface charges

Question still unresolved:
- I have not yet found a canonical textbook/paper theorem stated in exactly the desired form:
  `for a multi-ended asymptotically flat spacetime, each end's ADM mass is separately conserved under ordinary evolution`
- The current best assessment is that this is probably an implicit corollary of standard Hamiltonian machinery, not a famous standalone theorem with a standard name

Why this matters:
- if this endwise conservation statement is already standard, then the gravity side of the wormhole paper becomes primarily an application/clarification paper
- if it is only implicit, then part of the value of the project is to formulate the corollary cleanly and apply it to wormhole monopole claims

Current assessment:
- multi-end AF setup: standard
- endwise ADM charges: standard
- separate endwise conservation under evolution: very likely standard in substance, but not yet located as a boxed theorem/corollary in a canonical source
