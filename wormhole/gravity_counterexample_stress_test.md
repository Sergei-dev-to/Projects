# Gravity Counterexample Stress Test

## Target of the stress test

Test the **overstrong** claim:

> An asymptotically flat end's mass can never vary under ordinary motion elsewhere in a two-ended wormhole spacetime.

This is stronger than the claim we likely want for the paper. The purpose of the test is to find at least one clean scenario in which the broad statement fails, and then identify the missing hypothesis.

## Candidate Counterexample 1: Radiative leakage through the observed end

### Setup

- Start with a two-ended asymptotically flat wormhole spacetime.
- Work in a weak-field / perturbative regime around a fixed background wormhole metric.
- Let a small body move nonstationarily in the interior or near the throat.
- Allow the motion to source a weak packet of gravitational radiation that propagates outward to the right asymptotic end.

### What happens

At future null infinity of the right end, the Bondi mass decreases while the wave packet carries energy away. In that sense the right end's radiative asymptotic mass is time-dependent.

This is not an exotic loophole. It is the standard radiative behavior of asymptotically flat gravity: with news, the Bondi mass changes.

### Why this matters

This means the broad statement above is false **if "mass" is understood in the Bondi/null-infinity sense**. It does **not** by itself refute an ADM-mass statement at spatial infinity. So any gravity theorem in the paper has to say explicitly whether it is about

- ADM mass on asymptotically flat Cauchy slices in a quasistatic / no-radiation regime, or
- Bondi mass at null infinity, in which case one must impose no-news / no-flux conditions.

### What breaks

The failing assumption is not topology. The failing assumption is:

- `no gravitational energy flux through the asymptotic end being measured`.

Without that, a changing Bondi mass is allowed. This does not yet provide a counterexample to a claim stated specifically in terms of the end's ADM monopole data.

## Consequence for the paper

This stress test does **not** undermine the restricted claim we actually seem to want if that claim is about ADM monopole data. It only shows that the gravity analogue must be stated with explicit asymptotic-charge language and must not slide between ADM mass and Bondi mass.

The safe theorem template is therefore something like:

> In a fixed two-ended asymptotically flat wormhole background, for sufficiently small perturbations generated by compactly supported matter motion, if the asymptotic structure of a chosen end is held fixed, then the end's ADM monopole mass data are time-independent. If one instead works at null infinity with Bondi mass, one must impose the corresponding no-news / no-flux conditions.

This is narrower than the overstrong claim, but much more defensible.

## Status

We now have at least one genuine counterexample to the **broad** gravity slogan if "mass" is left ambiguous between ADM and Bondi notions. The next task is to look for a genuine counterexample to the sharper ADM-style claim rather than to the looser radiative one.

## Candidate Counterexample 2: A source-position-dependent formal family of end masses

### Setup

- Consider a two-ended asymptotically flat wormhole spacetime, or a family of asymptotically flat initial data sets for such a spacetime.
- Introduce a parameter `A` interpreted heuristically as the position of a small body relative to one mouth.
- Suppose one can solve the exterior field equations for each fixed `A` and obtain an asymptotic expansion at the opposite end whose `1/r` coefficient depends on `A`.

At first sight, this looks like exactly the induced-monopole mechanism one would want: as the body moves, the opposite end appears to acquire a varying mass monopole.

### Why this might fail as a counterexample

The Maxwell diagnosis suggests a more careful reading. A family of formal solutions labeled by `A` does not yet represent the continuous evolution of a single physical system. The varying `1/r` coefficient may instead mean that the family is moving through **different ADM sectors** at the opposite end.

In that reading:

- each fixed `A` labels a distinct asymptotically flat data set,
- the opposite end's ADM mass is different from one member of the family to another,
- but continuous local motion of a small body does not generate that drift in the ADM label.

Then the family is not a counterexample. It is only the gravity analogue of the Maxwell issue already identified in the DS discussion: a formal source-position-dependent monopole family exists as a family of boundary-value problems, but not as one dynamically reachable history at fixed asymptotic sector.

### What would have to be shown for this to become a real counterexample

To defeat the paper's intended claim, one would need more than the existence of the family. One would need to show all of the following:

- the asymptotic frame at the measured end is fixed across the family,
- the ADM mass at that end is genuinely the quantity being read off from the varying `1/r` coefficient,
- the family arises from continuous evolution of one physical system rather than a relabeling of distinct initial data sectors,
- and no hidden boundary condition at infinity is being varied as `A` changes.

Without these extra steps, the candidate is not yet a counterexample.

### Current assessment

This is the most serious loophole still worth testing. It may turn out to be a real problem, but it may equally well collapse into the same `formal family versus dynamical reachability` distinction that already resolves the Maxwell case.

## Concrete Program

To decide whether Candidate 2 is a real counterexample, use the following checklist on an explicit family of wormhole solutions:

1. Identify a one-parameter family of two-ended asymptotically flat solutions or initial data.
2. Write the asymptotic expansion at the measured end and isolate the `1/r` coefficient.
3. Check whether that coefficient is explicitly identified in the source as the end's ADM mass.
4. Determine which parameters the family is said to be uniquely labeled by.
5. Check whether those parameters are asymptotic charges / boundary data or a genuine source-position variable.
6. Look for an evolution law showing that ordinary local motion moves the system through the family.
7. If no such evolution law is given, treat the family provisionally as a family of distinct ADM sectors rather than a dynamical history.

If a candidate family fails at step 6, it does not yet provide a counterexample to the paper's intended claim.

## Toward a Strong Gravity Theorem

The current direction of the project is stronger than the earlier caution in this note.

At the level of `end ADM data`, there may be **no real boundary** between

- ordinary no-transit motion near the throat, and
- actual transit through the throat.

The likely distinction is instead between

- mouth-local or quasi-local bookkeeping, which may change during transit, and
- the ADM mass defined at each asymptotically flat end, which may remain fixed.

If that is right, then the strongest plausible gravity statement is:

> In a two-ended asymptotically flat wormhole spacetime, ordinary local evolution of compact matter, including passage through the throat, does not change the ADM mass attached to either asymptotic end, provided the asymptotic end structure itself is held fixed.

This is the gravity analogue of the Maxwell rigidity claim, with Hamiltonian boundary charges replacing the electric flux/cohomology data.

## Why a proof should exist if the statement is true

If the strong statement is correct, the proof should not depend on whether the worldline stays on one side of the throat or actually crosses it. That strongly suggests that the proof, if it exists, is not local-throat bookkeeping but a global argument based on the asymptotic ends themselves.

The expected structure is:

1. Choose a Cauchy slice with two asymptotically flat ends.
2. Define the Hamiltonian/ADM charge associated with the time translation at each end.
3. Show that local interior motion changes only interior/quasi-local data.
4. Conclude that the boundary charge at each end is unchanged unless the asymptotic sector itself is changed.

This is "topological" only in a broad sense. Unlike the Maxwell case, the relevant invariant is probably not a cohomology class in `H^2(\Sigma)`, but a Hamiltonian boundary charge attached to each end of a multiply-ended manifold.

## Minimal proof program

To turn the strong gravity claim into a theorem, one would need at least the following steps.

### Step 1: Precise arena

Specify a class of initial data sets `(M,g,k)` with

- two asymptotically flat ends,
- a chosen asymptotic time translation at each end,
- sufficient decay to define ADM energy-momentum at each end.

### Step 2: Correct notion of invariant

State clearly that the quantity at issue is the ADM mass of an end, not Bondi mass and not a quasi-local mouth mass.

### Step 3: Evolution statement

Show that for a one-parameter family of such data arising from ordinary evolution, including worldlines that cross the throat, the end Hamiltonians are unchanged unless one alters the asymptotic boundary structure itself.

### Step 4: Reinterpret transit lore

Explain any claimed "mouth mass increase/decrease" as quasi-local bookkeeping and prove that it does not imply a change in the end ADM charge.

## What remains missing

We do **not** yet have the key Step 3 in a rigorous form. So the strong theorem is currently a structural conjecture / research direction, not a proved result.

Still, the program now looks sharper than before:

- the main obstruction is no longer Bondi radiation, because that concerns a different mass notion;
- the main challenge is to convert the strong `mouth bookkeeping versus end ADM` distinction into a genuine Hamiltonian proof.

### What still seems missing

What I do **not** yet have is a ready-made theorem in the literature saying:

> for a two-ended asymptotically flat wormhole, ordinary local evolution, including throat transit, preserves the ADM mass attached to each end.

So the current best assessment is:

- the literature already gives enough machinery to formulate the theorem sharply in Hamiltonian language;
- it likely also gives the conceptual reason the theorem should be true;
- but the final wormhole-specific conservation statement still appears to require assembly or proof, rather than being available off the shelf.

### Practical conclusion

This means we probably do **not** need to invent the formal framework from scratch. We need to do something narrower and more realistic:

- take the standard ADM/Hamiltonian machinery for multiple asymptotic ends,
- adapt it to a two-ended wormhole slice,
- and prove that local throat dynamics alter only interior/quasi-local bookkeeping, not the end boundary terms.

## Candidate proof sketch via end-localized Hamiltonians

The most promising route found so far is to define a separate Hamiltonian for each asymptotic end by a suitable choice of lapse.

### Basic idea

Let `(M,g,\pi)` be asymptotically flat initial data with two ends `End_+` and `End_-`. Choose lapse-shift pairs

- `(\alpha_+, \beta_+)` such that `\alpha_+ -> 1` on `End_+` and `\alpha_+ -> 0` on `End_-`,
- `(\alpha_-, \beta_-)` such that `\alpha_- -> 0` on `End_+` and `\alpha_- -> 1` on `End_-`,

with shifts chosen to vanish asymptotically for simplicity.

If the Regge--Teitelboim boundary analysis goes through endwise, then the corresponding Hamiltonians satisfy schematically

`H_+ = \int_M (\alpha_+ \mathcal{H} + \beta_+^i \mathcal{H}_i) + B_+`

`H_- = \int_M (\alpha_- \mathcal{H} + \beta_-^i \mathcal{H}_i) + B_-`

where `\mathcal{H}` and `\mathcal{H}_i` are the scalar and momentum constraints and `B_\pm` are the surface terms at the corresponding ends.

On-shell, the bulk constraints vanish, so

- `H_+ = B_+ = E_{\mathrm{ADM}}(End_+)`,
- `H_- = B_- = E_{\mathrm{ADM}}(End_-)`.

### Why this is promising

This would immediately separate the two end masses as distinct Hamiltonian boundary charges. Local dynamics in the throat region, including actual transit of matter through the throat, occur in the interior and therefore affect only the bulk fields, not the asymptotic normalization of `\alpha_\pm`.

If the Hamiltonian evolution generated by the Einstein equations preserves the differentiability conditions and asymptotic structure, then each `H_\pm` should be conserved along the flow in the same sense that the total ADM Hamiltonian is conserved.

### What the proof would have to show

To make this rigorous, the following lemmas are needed.

1. **End-localized lapse admissibility**

   Show that lapse functions approaching `1` on one end and `0` on the other are admissible in the asymptotically flat Hamiltonian formalism and lead to differentiable Hamiltonians with only the corresponding end boundary term surviving.

2. **On-shell reduction**

   Show that for solutions of the constraints, the Hamiltonian reduces exactly to the ADM energy of the selected end.

3. **Flow invariance**

   Show that under ordinary evolution with fixed asymptotic end structure, `dH_\pm/dt = 0`.

4. **Transit irrelevance**

   Show that a worldline crossing the throat changes only interior/quasi-local bookkeeping and does not alter the asymptotic boundary data entering `B_\pm`.

### If this works

If these lemmas can be established, then the gravity theorem would read naturally as:

> For two-ended asymptotically flat wormhole initial data with fixed asymptotic structure, the ADM energy attached to each end is separately conserved under ordinary Einstein evolution, including matter transit through the throat.

This would be the precise gravity analogue of the Maxwell rigidity statement, with endwise Hamiltonians playing the role of fixed flux-sector charges.

### Current status

This is still a proof sketch, not a proof. But it is the first concrete route found so far that makes the strong gravity theorem look technically plausible rather than merely intuitive.
