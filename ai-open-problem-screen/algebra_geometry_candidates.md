# Algebra, number theory, topology, and geometry candidates

Status audit: **2026-07-22**.  This is a susceptibility screen, not a ranking of
mathematical importance.  The unit being scored is a concrete attack target:
often an explicit object whose discovery would settle one direction of a
larger conjecture.  Scores use the rubric in [METHODOLOGY.md](METHODOLOGY.md):
artifact compactness (**A**), exact verification (**V**), search
compressibility (**S**), representation leverage (**R**), low saturation
(**L**), and bounded-trajectory fit (**T**), each from 0 to 5.

“Exact verifier” means a realistic acceptance test for a proposed artifact. It
does **not** mean that the search for the artifact is easy.  In particular, a
finite presentation or algebraic curve can be short while the negative or
genericity assertion needed to make it decisive is not.

## Status exclusions made during this pass

The screen deliberately omits problems for which a live 2025--26 resolution
claim makes “currently open” too contentious for an attack inventory, even if
peer review is incomplete.  Examples include the three-dimensional Kakeya
conjecture, the moving-sofa optimum, the cycle-double-cover conjecture, the
circulant-Hadamard conjecture, and the Erdos--Straus conjecture.  It also omits
the Jacobian conjecture, whose new counterexample motivated this methodology.
Claims on personal repositories do not automatically close famous questions,
but they do trigger exclusion when the claim is serious enough that the first
research task would be adjudicating it rather than attacking a clean target.

## Final top six for deep review

The numeric score is a screen, not a calibrated probability.  The ordering
below adds two tie-breakers: whether a successful output settles the named
target without a hidden genericity/nonexistence burden, and whether there is a
specific first experiment rather than merely a suggestive reformulation.

| Rank | Target | Why it survives the screen | Main failure mode | Bounded first experiment and stop rule |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hard four-pentachoron sphere `Q`** | It is one fixed object, the success certificate is only a move sequence, and public exact topology software can replay it. The 2026 exhaustive depth result supplies both training data and a sharply measured frontier. This most closely matches “search a huge structured state space, return a tiny indisputable artifact.” | The shortest path may require an enormous temporary blow-up, or `Q` may be exotic, in which case positive search never terminates. | Reproduce the excess-height-six census; train/search on all smaller hard spheres; run bidirectional and macro-move search to a declared height/node budget. Stop if learned policies do not beat held-out classical search by a prespecified factor before scaling. |
| 2 | **Non-3-colorable great-circle arrangement** | The first unchecked size is close (12 circles), graph non-colorability has standard proof certificates, and realizability can be expressed through oriented-matroid signs. The literature appears to have searched arrangements and colorings more than it has searched the two certificate spaces jointly. | All simple great-circle arrangements may be 3-colorable; generating abstract counterexamples can waste effort on nonrealizable oriented matroids. | Enumerate/canonicalize rank-3 realizable oriented matroids at 12 circles, extract arrangement graphs, and retain DRAT-certified non-3-colorable cases. Stop or move to 13 only after the 12-circle generation is independently completeness-checked. |
| 3 | **A 46-pentachoron K3 triangulation** | This is a finite constructive gap of only eight pentachora with powerful invariant pruning and exact combinatorial output. Even a new 47--53 construction is publishable progress and teaches the search geometry. | Recognizing a candidate as PL-homeomorphic to K3 can be harder than validating the face pairing; the lower bound may not be sharp. | Search 46--53 face pairings under Euler/homology/intersection-form constraints, then require Regina-replayable simplification or Pachner certificates for survivors. Stop a size after canonical augmentation and invariant strata are demonstrably exhausted. |
| 4 | **Markoff uniqueness collision** | The final counterexample is six integers and the checker is elementary. Markoff triples have several exact encodings--tree paths, words, matrices, geodesics, cluster data--so one can search for invariant collisions in a representation humans did not optimize for. | Existing theory may make the conjecture true, and known lower/uniqueness results mean direct enumeration is saturated far beyond naive ranges. | Build a collision-directed generator over compressed word/matrix invariants and benchmark it against ordinary Markoff-tree enumeration at equal cost. Stop if it produces no asymptotic gain in distinct maxima covered or no new near-collision structure. |
| 5 | **Conway's 99-graph** | A 99-vertex adjacency matrix is a perfect construction certificate, much smaller than the missing Moore graph, while spectrum, star complements, common-neighbor equations, and SAT offer several exact coordinate systems. | Recent SAT work may already cover the obvious encodings; existence could be false, for which a complete certificate may be huge. | Reproduce the 2026 SAT baseline, quotient by star-complement/spectral states, and test learned branching or switching-based seed synthesis with proof logging. Stop if the new representation does not reduce proof/search cost on published subinstances. |
| 6 | **Polycube edge-unfolding counterexample** | The geometry becomes entirely discrete on the cubic lattice: cell complexes, boundary dual spanning trees, and integer overlap tests. It is comparatively low-saturation and has a large representation gap between geometric intuition and canonical finite search. | The smallest counterexample may be large, and “no unfolding works” needs an exhaustive certificate whose format has not been standardized. | First build and independently test a proof-logging enumerator for all genus-zero polycubes through a modest cube count and every symmetry class of cut tree. Continue only if canonicalization and overlap certificates remain compact enough to audit. |

### Near misses

- **`u(22)` and 17-square packing** scored highly, but an improved
  construction does not automatically determine the optimum; the lower-bound
  certificate is the difficult half.
- **`AK(3)` trivialization** is an excellent proof-producing benchmark, but
  current automated-reasoning attention makes it more saturated than `Q`.
- **Order-12 projective plane, Moore-57, Hadamard-668, and Hadwiger--Nelson**
  have superb verifiers but enormous, heavily searched spaces. They belong in
  a second wave after a representation earns its keep on a smaller target.
- **MUB-6, SICs, SHGH, and Nagata** have attractive reformulations, but exact
  recognition, inequivalence, or genericity creates a hidden proof burden not
  reflected by the apparent size of a numerical candidate.

## Bottom line

The most promising cluster is not “famous conjectures with computers.” It is
**finite artifact synthesis with a replayable certificate, a known search
frontier, and at least two genuinely different state representations**.  `Q`
is the cleanest pure instance; great-circle arrangements offer the strongest
counterexample-and-SAT shape; K3 offers incremental publishable milestones;
Markoff is the best microscopic Diophantine witness; the 99-graph is the best
moderate-size algebraic design; and polycubes are the best low-saturation
representation bet.
