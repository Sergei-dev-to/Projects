\begin{abstract}
Can a traveler in an Alcubierre warp bubble pass into a larger spacetime?
A diagram that leaves out the sideways directions cannot decide this.
We prove that the spherical bubbles studied here have no smooth extension,
wherever an attachment might be attempted.  We give a criterion for
continuation within a class of fixed-shape bubbles and construct a compact
bubble that meets it.  Through its flat cap, freely falling travelers enter
an added region in finite time on their own clocks.  The spherical and
capped bubbles can have identical geometry along the direction of travel,
despite these different outcomes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Alcubierre drive \cite{Alcubierre1994} describes faster-than-light travel
through a moving distortion of spacetime.  Where can a traveler in this
spacetime actually go?  The standard coordinates may cover only part of a
larger geometry.

For an eternal bubble of fixed shape, the axial Penrose diagram keeps only
time and the direction of travel.  Its reduced geometry has a smooth
continuation beyond a boundary approached as the original time coordinate
tends to infinity \cite{FinazziLiberatiBarcelo2009,BarceloEtAl2022}.  We ask
whether this passage exists in the full four-dimensional spacetime and
whether a freely falling traveler can cross it.

We study stationary Alcubierre geometries with a smooth nonpositive scalar
shift along the direction of travel and a constant or exponentially
approached superluminal exterior.  Extensions are required to be Hausdorff,
with a twice continuously differentiable (\(C^2\)) metric.  No field equation
or energy condition is imposed.

For regular walls, we prove that an extension exists if and only if the
wall contains an open transverse planar horizon patch with constant nonzero
surface gravity.  The necessity statement concerns any extension, without
assuming where it is attached.  Without wall regularity, we still prove
inextendibility whenever the stationary-limit set contains no open
transverse planar disk.  This excludes the spherical bubbles studied here,
including those with a degenerate stationary-limit level.

We realize the criterion with a smooth compact bubble whose cap admits an
open family of timelike geodesics from the core into an attached region.
The spherical and capped bubbles can have identical axial metrics,
as illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:reduced-patch}.  Their different
four-dimensional extendibility therefore cannot be read from that shared
diagram.  At the smooth cap rim, we also exhibit generators with bounded
parallel-propagated curvature but no regular endpoint: curvature bounds
fail in every transverse neighborhood.

Pfenning and Ford computed curvature in a freely falling observer's frame
\cite[Eq.~(18)]{PfenningFord1997}.  Full-metric geodesics, parallel transport,
and optical deviation are studied in
Refs.~\cite{ClarkHiscockLarson1999,MullerWeiskopf2012,McMonigalEtAl2012}.
Barcel\'o et al. determine finite-affine axial limits and the effect of front
shape on nearby geodesics \cite[Secs.~3.1--3.3]{BarceloEtAl2022}.

Gonz\'alez-D\'iaz discusses warp-drive extensions, including a
four-dimensional metric expression
\cite[Sec.~II]{GonzalezDiaz2000}, while Finazzi et al. give the reduced
diagram used here \cite[Sec.~II~A]{FinazziLiberatiBarcelo2009}.
R\'acz--Wald supplies the local continuation of a suitable Killing horizon
\cite{RaczWald1992,RaczWald1996}.  Here the capture and rigidity arguments
force such a horizon patch from an arbitrary four-dimensional extension.
The compact construction attaches its continuation to the full bubble.

Section~\ref{sec:diagram} recalls the reduced passage and the transverse
dynamics that motivate the question.  Section~\ref{sec:maximality} proves
the extension criterion and spherical inextendibility.
Section~\ref{sec:compact-cap} constructs the compact crossing and proves
the rim obstruction.  Detailed proofs are in the appendix.

\section{Reduced conformal geometry}
\label{sec:diagram}

On \(M=\mathbb R^4\), consider the stationary scalar-shift metric
\begin{equation}
  ds^2=-dt^2+[dx-v(\boldsymbol{x})dt]^2+dy^2+dz^2,
  \qquad \boldsymbol{x}=(x,y,z),\quad r=|\boldsymbol{x}|.
  \label{eq:general-scalar-shift}
\end{equation}
For an Alcubierre bubble, the lab-frame shift \(v_s f\) becomes
\(v=v_s(f-1)\) under
\(x=X-v_st\).  Thus the bubble center has \(v=0\) and the exterior approaches
\(v=-v_s<-1\).  The precise tail and wall hypotheses appear in
Theorem~\ref{thm:extension-rigidity}.

For a radial profile, the axis \(y=z=0\) is totally geodesic.  Writing
\(\xi=x\) there gives the reduced metric used in
Ref.~\cite[Sec.~II]{FinazziLiberatiBarcelo2009}:
\begin{equation}
  ds^2_{1+1}=-dt^2+[d\xi-v(\xi)dt]^2 .
  \label{eq:reduced-metric}
\end{equation}
The two null families satisfy \(d\xi/dt=v\pm1\), so the retarded and advanced
null coordinates are
\begin{equation}
  u=t-\int\frac{d\xi}{v+1},\qquad
  w=t-\int\frac{d\xi}{v-1}.
  \label{eq:null-coords}
\end{equation}
They give \(ds^2_{1+1}=-(1-v^2)\,du\,dw\).
For an even profile with two nondegenerate roots \(v=-1\), at
\(\xi_1<0<\xi_2\), the bubble interior II lies between two exterior branches
I and III.  Figure~\ref{fig:reduced-patch}(a) shows this common axial
geometry for the spherical and capped profiles in panels (b,c).  Their
shifts agree exactly on the axis.  The amber continuation is available to
the capped bubble in four dimensions.  The matched profiles are constructed
in Section~\ref{sec:compact-cap}; plotting data are supplied in the
Supplement and conformal coordinates in Appendix~\ref{app:cap-attachment}.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/bubble_extension_comparison.pdf}

  (a) Both bubbles share the original three-region patch.  Amber shows the

  \(B=C^+\); null lines are at \(45^\circ\).
  (b,c) Finite-time spatial wall sections, at the same scale with matching
  axial shifts.  The spherical spacetime is \(C^2\)-inextendible; the cap
  permits a regular passage.  Circles locate the rim: its generators have
  no regular limiting endpoints, although the finite-time wall is smooth.}
  \label{fig:reduced-patch}
\end{figure}

For a simple root, exponential null coordinates remove the reduced metric's
coordinate singularity, giving the continuation described in
Ref.~\cite[Sec.~II~A]{FinazziLiberatiBarcelo2009}.  In particular, the
stationary null generators at \(\xi_2\) and \(\xi_1\) have regular future
and past endpoints, respectively, in the reduced extension.  These are
\(B=C^+\) and \(C^-\) in Figure~\ref{fig:reduced-patch}.
The full extension must also preserve the transverse geometry, which the
axial metric does not determine.

The finite-affine axial limits and the transverse dynamics near rounded,
flat, and concave fronts were obtained in
Ref.~\cite[Secs.~3.1--3.3]{BarceloEtAl2022}.  In particular, rewriting its
linearized transverse equation in affine time gives inverse-square tidal
growth near a nondegenerate spherical front.  A flat front removes that
transverse term, but does not by itself make the full metric flat.

These local facts do not decide whether the original four-dimensional
spacetime admits any regular extension: an attachment could in principle
occur elsewhere.  The next result addresses that global question.

\section{Extension criterion for stationary scalar shifts}
\label{sec:maximality}

An extension is a smooth, time-orientation-preserving isometric embedding
of \((M,g)\) as a proper open subset of a connected, time-oriented smooth
Hausdorff manifold with a \(C^2\) Lorentzian metric.  We identify \(M\)
with its image.

\begin{samepage}
\begin{theorem}[Global extension rigidity for scalar shifts]
\label{thm:extension-rigidity}
Let \(M=\mathbb R^4\) carry the metric
\eqref{eq:general-scalar-shift}, where \(v\) is smooth and \(v\leq0\).
Assume that, for some
\(v_s>1\), the exterior is either exactly constant or exponentially
short-range:
\begin{equation}
  v=-v_s\quad(r\geq R_0),
  \qquad\text{or}\qquad
  |v+v_s|+|\nabla v|\leq Ce^{-\sigma r}
  \quad(r\geq R_0),
  \label{eq:general-scalar-tail}
\end{equation}
for some \(R_0,C,\sigma>0\).  Suppose also that
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma=\{\boldsymbol{x}:v(\boldsymbol{x})=-1\}
  \label{eq:general-wall}
\end{equation}
is regular (\(dv\ne0\) on \(\Sigma\)).

Then \((M,g)\) admits a Hausdorff \(C^2\) Lorentzian extension if and only
if there are
\(x_H\in\mathbb R\), a nonempty open disk
\(D_0\subset\mathbb R^2_{y,z}\), and a constant \(\alpha\ne0\) such that
\begin{equation}
  v(x_H,y,z)=-1,
  \qquad v_x(x_H,y,z)=\alpha
  \quad ((y,z)\in D_0).
  \label{eq:planar-cap-criterion}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\end{samepage}

The tail condition also makes \(v\) and \(\nabla v\) bounded and
\(\Sigma\) compact.
Condition~\eqref{eq:planar-cap-criterion} says that, for the stationary
Killing field \(\chi=\partial_t\), the wall
worldvolume contains the open Killing-horizon patch
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal H=\mathbb R_t\times\{x_H\}\times D_0,
  \qquad
  \nabla_\chi\chi=\alpha\chi
  \quad\hbox{on }\mathcal H .
  \label{eq:forced-killing-horizon}
\end{equation}
When the condition holds, for every disk \(D\) with
\(\overline D\subset D_0\) there is a proper smooth Hausdorff extension
continuing the corresponding finite-affine stationary generators through a
bifurcate Killing horizon over \(D\).

The two conditions constrain different features of the wall.  Planarity
fixes its position \(x=x_H\) and makes \(v_A=v_{AB}=0\) on the patch.
The normal derivative \(v_x\), the signed surface gravity for
\(\chi=\partial_t\), could still vary across it.  The rigidity proof uses
transverse curvature to force planarity and mixed curvature to force
\(v_{xA}=0\), hence constant surface gravity
(Appendix~\ref{app:global-proof}).

Higher normal derivatives remain free, so a regular crossing can have
nonzero curvature and finite tidal forces.

Sufficiency uses the local R\'acz--Wald continuation mechanism
\cite{RaczWald1992,RaczWald1996}.  Appendix~\ref{app:cap-attachment}
constructs the extension and verifies its attachment to the whole bubble.
The sign of \(\alpha\) determines the temporal direction: \(\alpha<0\) gives a future
attachment and \(\alpha>0\) a past attachment.

\paragraph{Proof outline.}
If a proper extension exists, global hyperbolicity and
Ref.~\cite[Theorem~2]{GallowayLingSbierski2018} supply a timelike geodesic
leaving \(M\) in finite proper time.  The capture proposition shows that
this geodesic becomes increasingly boosted and approaches the stationary
generator locus.  Curvature must remain uniformly bounded on a small
transverse disk in the extension.  The transverse-disk lemma uses that
neighborhood bound to force the wall to be planar and \(v_x\) to be
constant there, giving Eq.~\eqref{eq:planar-cap-criterion}.

Appendix~\ref{app:global-proof} proves the capture and transverse-rigidity
steps.

\begin{corollary}[Inextendibility without a planar wall patch]
\label{cor:alcubierre-maximality}
Let \(v\leq0\) be smooth and satisfy \eqref{eq:general-scalar-tail} for
some \(v_s>1\).
If \(\{v=-1\}\) contains no nonempty open disk in any plane
\(x=\mathrm{constant}\), the corresponding spacetime is
\(C^2\)-inextendible.  No regularity of \(\{v=-1\}\) is assumed.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Suppose that a proper extension exists.  As in
Appendix~\ref{app:global-proof}, take a boundary-reaching timelike geodesic,
after time reversal and reflection of \(x\) if necessary.
Proposition~\ref{prop:capture} and Lemma~\ref{lem:transverse-disk-rigidity}
require no wall regularity and force an open planar disk in \(\{v=-1\}\),
a contradiction.
\end{proof}

In particular, if \(v=v(r)\) and \(\{v=-1\}=\{r=R_F\}\), the spacetime
is inextendible without monotonicity or nondegeneracy assumptions.  This
includes the sech family of Ref.~\cite{FinazziLiberatiBarcelo2009} and
smooth compact-transition profiles, including \(v_r(R_F)=0\).


\section{Compact bubbles with planar caps}
\label{sec:compact-cap}

The following compact smooth bubble has planar front and rear caps with
constant nonzero surface gravity.  Freely falling travelers can cross from
the bubble core through its front cap into a future extension; the rear
cap admits the time-reversed passage.

\begin{samepage}
Put \(\rho^2=y^2+z^2\), choose \(0<a<b\) and \(L,d>0\), and define
\begin{equation}
 \eta(u)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_0^u e^{-1/z}\,dz&u>0,\\0&u\leq0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \psi(s)=L^2\frac{\eta((s-a^2)/d)}{\eta((b^2-a^2)/d)}.
 \label{eq:explicit-cap-shape}
\end{equation}
This \(\psi\) vanishes to all orders at \(s=a^2\), satisfies
\(\psi',\psi''>0\) for \(s>a^2\), grows without bound, and has
\(\psi(b^2)=L^2\).
\end{samepage}
The level set
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma=\{\Phi=0\},
  \qquad
  \Phi=x^2+\psi(\rho^2)-L^2,
  \label{eq:compact-flat-cap-wall}
\end{equation}
is smooth, compact, and connected.  It has the exact planar caps
\(x=\pm L\), \(\rho<a\), and closes at \(x=0\), \(\rho=b\).  Regularity is
immediate where \(x\ne0\); where \(x=0\) on \(\Sigma\), one has \(\rho=b\)
and \(\psi'(b^2)>0\).

Choose a smooth nonincreasing \(W\) with \(W(0)=-1\), \(W'(0)=-c<0\), and
\(W(\phi)=-1\) only for \(\phi=0\).  Take it to be zero sufficiently far
inside, equal to \(-v_s<-1\) sufficiently far outside, and affine near zero.
Then
\begin{equation}
  v_{\rm cap}=W(\Phi),\qquad v_{\rm sph}=W(r^2-L^2).
  \label{eq:matched-figure-profiles}
\end{equation}
Both profiles satisfy the global hypotheses and are exactly constant outside a compact
set.  On \(\rho<a\), \(v_{\rm cap}\) depends only on \(x\).  At the front cap,
\(v_x=-2cL\), and at the rear cap \(v_x=2cL\), both constant over the disk.
Theorem~\ref{thm:extension-rigidity} therefore gives a proper smooth
extension through either cap interior.

Replacing \(\psi(\rho^2)\) by \(\rho^2\) gives the spherical profile with the
same axial shift.  Corollary~\ref{cor:alcubierre-maximality} makes that spacetime
\(C^2\)-inextendible.

Figure~\ref{fig:reduced-patch}(a) shows the attachment on the upper-right edge
of the original diagram.  The traveler approaches \(x=L\) as
\(t\to+\infty\) and crosses beside the black front-generator endpoint.
This finite-position null boundary becomes an internal surface of the
extension, distinct from ordinary null infinity.  Panel (c) locates the
cap and its rim on the finite-time wall.


\subsection{Timelike crossing through the cap interior}

The aperture is accessible from the bubble core by ordinary timelike
geodesics.  Within \(\rho<a\), the example is a product with shift \(v=v(x)\).
Choose its flat core to contain \(x=x_0<L\).

\begin{proposition}[A finite bundle of crossing trajectories]
\label{prop:timelike-cap-crossing}
Launch a unit-mass timelike geodesic from the flat core with axial momentum
\(P_0>0\), transverse momentum \(Q=(p_y,p_z)\), and transverse radius
\(\rho_0\).  If
\begin{equation}
  \rho_0+|Q|\frac{L-x_0}{P_0}<a,
  \label{eq:cap-aperture}
\end{equation}
then it stays within the cap cylinder and crosses a smooth cap extension in
finite proper time, with
\(\tau_*-\tau_0\leq(L-x_0)/P_0\).
The condition holds for an open set of initial data and hence for a sufficiently
small compact bundle with nonzero spatial extent.
\end{proposition}

Conservation of Killing energy gives a lower bound on the outward velocity,
\(\dot x\geq P_0\), while the transverse momentum remains constant.
Condition~\eqref{eq:cap-aperture} ensures that the traveler reaches the
cap before drifting to its rim.  Appendix~\ref{app:timelike-crossing}
proves the bound and smooth continuation of the tangent through the passage.

The traveler crosses in finite proper time at infinite stationary coordinate
time.  The comoving center remains complete; crossing requires outward motion.
The bundle describes a finite test body with gravitational backreaction
neglected.

The crossing neighborhood can also be chosen exactly flat by inserting
a stationary specialization of the vacuum shift equation in
Ref.~\cite{SantosPereiraEtAl2020}.  Appendix~\ref{sec:flat-crossing}
describes its embedding in the compact cap.

\subsection{Cap-rim endpoints}

At the seam, all metric derivatives along a rim generator agree with
those on the neighboring cap.  A regular endpoint would also require a
neighborhood extending to both sides of the seam.

\begin{proposition}[The cap-rim endpoint obstruction]
\label{prop:flat-cap-rim}
Each circular cap-rim generator is incomplete at finite affine parameter and
has bounded parallel-propagated curvature, but it has no endpoint in any
\(C^2\) Lorentzian extension.
\end{proposition}


The proof in Appendix~\ref{app:compact-cap} finds unbounded curvature on
fixed-length transverse geodesics just outside the cap.  Extension requires
curvature bounds in a neighborhood of the endpoint \cite{Racz2010}.

\section{Discussion}

Within the stationary scalar-shift class studied here, with regular walls
and the stated exterior behavior, any \(C^2\) extension forces an open
planar horizon patch with constant nonzero surface gravity.  This necessity
result controls attachments anywhere on the extension boundary.  The
R\'acz--Wald continuation of that patch
\cite{RaczWald1992,RaczWald1996}, attached to the whole original spacetime,
establishes sufficiency.
The spherical and capped bubbles constructed here have identical axial
metrics, but only the capped bubble meets the criterion.  Their reduced
Penrose diagrams therefore cannot determine four-dimensional extendibility.

The compact example admits freely falling travelers from the bubble core.
Its rim generators have bounded propagated curvature but no regular
endpoints, because neighborhood curvature bounds fail there.
The construction attaches a local crossing region to the whole original
spacetime; its maximal continuation and the eventual fate of travelers
remain open.

Reduced quantum-field calculations find growing stress near warp horizons
\cite{FinazziLiberatiBarcelo2009},
while higher-dimensional geodesic estimates identify flat fronts as
susceptible to radiation accumulation \cite[Sec.~3.3]{BarceloEtAl2022}.
Whether supporting matter and quantum fields preserve our four-dimensional
passage remains open.

\appendix

\section{Proof of the extension criterion}
\label{app:global-proof}

\subsection{Frame conventions}

Use the curvature convention
\((\nabla_c\nabla_d-\nabla_d\nabla_c)V^a=R^a{}_{bcd}V^b\) and the
stationary orthonormal frame
\begin{equation}
  e_0=\partial_t+v\partial_x,\qquad e_1=\partial_x,
  \qquad e_A=\partial_A,\quad A\in\{y,z\}.
  \label{eq:stationary-frame}
\end{equation}
On \(\Sigma=\{v=-1\}\), the stationary Killing field
\(\chi=\partial_t\) obeys
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_\chi\chi=v_x\partial_t+v_y\partial_y+v_z\partial_z.
  \label{eq:not-whole-sphere}
\end{equation}
Its null pregeodesic orbits therefore lie precisely on
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal G=\{p\in\Sigma:v_y(p)=v_z(p)=0\}.
  \label{eq:generator-locus}
\end{equation}
There \(\chi=e_0+e_1\).  The curvature identities used to force a full
transverse disk are displayed in the rigidity lemma below.

\subsection{Boundary-reaching timelike geodesics}

The inverse metric has \(g^{tt}=-1\), so \(t\) is a smooth temporal function.
If a future-directed causal curve is parametrized by \(t\), its causal
inequality is
\begin{equation}
  \left|\frac{d\boldsymbol{x}}{dt}-v(\boldsymbol{x})e_x\right|\leq1,
  \qquad
  \left|\frac{d\boldsymbol{x}}{dt}\right|
  \leq1+\|v\|_\infty=:V .
  \label{eq:causal-speed}
\end{equation}
The spatial slices are complete Euclidean spaces, the lapse is one, and
the shift is uniformly bounded.  The standard criterion of
Ref.~\cite[Theorem~2.1]{ChoquetBruhatCotsakis2002} therefore makes every
constant-\(t\) slice Cauchy and \((M,g)\) globally hyperbolic.  The explicit
speed bound \eqref{eq:causal-speed} will also be used in the capture argument.

Suppose that a proper Hausdorff \(C^2\) extension exists.
The extension-boundary
theorem for globally hyperbolic spacetimes supplies a timelike geodesic in
\(M\), future- or past-directed, with an endpoint on the extension boundary
\cite[Theorem~2]{GallowayLingSbierski2018}.  We first treat the future case.
Its proper time is finite: a final segment lies in a temporal coordinate
chart, where causal lengths are bounded by a constant times the
coordinate-time interval.  Parametrize it as
\begin{equation}
  \gamma:[0,\tau_*)\longrightarrow M,
  \qquad \tau_*<\infty,
  \qquad u=\dot\gamma .
  \label{eq:extension-geodesic}
\end{equation}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:endpoint-frames}, its tangent has a finite limit in the
extension, and the Riemann tensor of the
\(C^2\) extension is continuous.  Curvature contractions with vectors having
uniformly bounded extension-chart components must therefore remain bounded.

\begin{proposition}[Capture of finite-time exits]
\label{prop:capture}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:extension-rigidity}, without
regularity of \(\Sigma\), let \(\gamma\) be
a future-directed unit timelike geodesic with finite proper-time endpoint that
cannot be continued in \(M\).  Put \(P=p_x\), \(Q_A=p_A\), and
\begin{equation}
  n=\frac{(P,Q_y,Q_z)}{\dot t}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
  t\to+\infty,\qquad \dot t\to+\infty,\qquad
  v\to-1,\qquad n\to e_1,
  \label{eq:capture-limits}
\end{equation}
the spatial image of \(\gamma\) is precompact, and there are
\(t_n\to+\infty\) and \(p\in\mathcal G\) such that
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{x}(t_n)\longrightarrow p.
  \label{eq:capture-subsequence}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Capture of finite-proper-time exits}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:capture}]\mbox{}\par\smallskip
\noindent\emph{First integrals and divergent Killing time.}

Let \(E=-p_t\) be the conserved Killing energy.  For a unit timelike
geodesic the Hamiltonian equations read
\begin{equation}
  \dot t=E-vP,
  \qquad
  \dot t^2=P^2+Q_AQ_A+1,
  \label{eq:global-first-integrals}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  \dot P=-v_xP\dot t,
  \qquad
  \dot Q_A=-v_AP\dot t .
  \label{eq:global-momentum-equations}
\end{equation}
Here a dot denotes \(d/d\tau\), and repeated transverse indices are summed.
Since \(t\) is temporal, \(\dot t>0\).

The geodesic cannot exit with \(t\) bounded.  In that case
Eq.~\eqref{eq:causal-speed} would give a finite spatial limit, while, using
\(t\) as parameter,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{dP}{dt}=-v_xP,
  \qquad
  \frac{dQ_A}{dt}=-v_AP
  \label{eq:t-momentum-equations}
\end{equation}
would keep the momenta bounded on the resulting compact coordinate set.  The
geodesic data would converge in \(TM\), and the smooth geodesic equation would
continue \(\gamma\) past \(\tau_*\).  Therefore a future exit requires
\(t\to+\infty\).

The sign of \(P\) is fixed by Eq.~\eqref{eq:global-momentum-equations}.  If
\(P\leq0\), then \(v\leq0\) gives \(0<\dot t\leq E\), which is incompatible
with \(t\to+\infty\) in finite proper time.  Hence \(P>0\).  Moreover,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{d\log P}{dt}=-v_x
  \label{eq:log-p}
\end{equation}
has a globally bounded right-hand side.  If \(P\) returned below a fixed bound
at arbitrarily large values of \(t\), this logarithmic derivative would give
infinitely many disjoint \(t\)-intervals of uniform width on which \(P\), and
therefore \(\dot t=E-vP\), stayed bounded.  Each interval would contribute a
fixed positive amount to \(d\tau=dt/\dot t\), contradicting
\(\tau_*<\infty\).  Thus
\begin{equation}
  P\longrightarrow+\infty,
  \qquad
  \dot t\longrightarrow+\infty .
  \label{eq:p-diverges}
\end{equation}

For the spatial direction \(n\) defined in Proposition~\ref{prop:capture},
Equations~\eqref{eq:global-first-integrals} and \eqref{eq:p-diverges} give
\begin{equation}
  |n|\longrightarrow1,
  \qquad
  1+vn_x=\frac{E}{\dot t}\longrightarrow0.
  \label{eq:n-limit}
\end{equation}

\medskip\noindent\emph{Spatial infinity is not an exit.}

The exclusion of spatial infinity does not require radial symmetry.  Put
\begin{equation}
  \Pi=(P^2+Q_AQ_A)^{1/2},\qquad
  m=\frac{(P,Q_y,Q_z)}{\Pi},\qquad
  \beta=\frac{\Pi}{\sqrt{\Pi^2+1}} .
\end{equation}
Since \(P\to\infty\), also \(\Pi\to\infty\).  The exact energy and velocity
relations are
\begin{equation}
  vm_x=\frac{E}{\Pi}-\sqrt{1+\Pi^{-2}},
  \qquad
  \frac{d\boldsymbol{x}}{dt}=ve_x+\beta m .
  \label{eq:infinity-projective}
\end{equation}
Choose \(0<c<v_s-v_s^{-1}\).  By
\eqref{eq:general-scalar-tail} and \eqref{eq:infinity-projective}, there are
\(T,R\) such that whenever \(t\geq T\) and \(r\geq R\),
\begin{equation}
  \frac{dx}{dt}\leq-c<0.
  \label{eq:x-speed-infinity}
\end{equation}

Consider a connected exterior excursion
\([t_1,t_2]\subset\{t\geq T:r(t)\geq R\}\) that enters and later returns
through \(r=R\).  At both endpoints \(-R\leq x\leq R\), while
\eqref{eq:x-speed-infinity} gives
\begin{equation}
  c(t_2-t_1)\leq x(t_1)-x(t_2)\leq2R.
\end{equation}
The causal speed bound \eqref{eq:causal-speed} therefore bounds the radius of
every returning excursion by
\begin{equation}
  r(t)\leq R+\frac{2RV}{c}.
  \label{eq:returning-excursion-bound}
\end{equation}

If the spatial trajectory were unbounded,
\eqref{eq:returning-excursion-bound} would force one exterior component to be
nonreturning.  Equation~\eqref{eq:x-speed-infinity} would then imply
\begin{equation}
  x(t)\leq-ct+O(1),\qquad r(t)\geq ct-O(1).
  \label{eq:linear-exterior-escape}
\end{equation}
In an exactly constant exterior all spatial momenta are constant, contrary to
\(\Pi\to\infty\).  In the exponentially short-range case, the momentum
equations give
\begin{equation}
  \left|\frac{d\log\Pi}{dt}\right|
  =|m_x\,m\cdot\nabla v|
  \leq|\nabla v|.
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:general-scalar-tail} and
\eqref{eq:linear-exterior-escape} make the right-hand side integrable, so
\(\log\Pi\) has a finite limit, again a contradiction.  Every
finite-proper-time exit therefore has bounded spatial image.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Every bounded exit approaches the stationary-generator set.}

On a bounded exit, Eq.~\eqref{eq:n-limit} first implies
\(|v|\geq1-o(1)\).  The exact longitudinal velocity is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{dx}{dt}
    =v+\frac{P}{E-vP}
    =v-\frac1v+O(P^{-1}).
  \label{eq:bounded-x-speed}
\end{equation}
The positive part of this velocity is \(O(P^{-1})\).  Indeed,
\(\dot t=E-vP\geq P\) implies \(v\leq-1+E/P\), while
\(P/\dot t\leq1\).  Hence the positive part is bounded by
\(\max\{E,0\}/P\).  It is integrable because
\begin{equation}
  \int^\infty\frac{dt}{P}
    =\int^{\tau_*}\frac{\dot t}{P}\,d\tau<\infty .
  \label{eq:inverse-p-integrable}
\end{equation}
Here \(\dot t/P=E/P-v\) is bounded on the late tail, while the remaining
proper time is finite.
Because \(x\) is bounded, its negative variation is then finite as well, so
\(x(t)\) has finite total variation and a limit.  The same estimate forces
\(v(\boldsymbol{x}(t))\to-1\).  Otherwise, for some \(\epsilon>0\), there
would be arbitrarily late times with \(v\leq-1-\epsilon\).  On the bounded
spatial tail, \(v\) has bounded gradient and the coordinate speed is bounded,
so \(v(\boldsymbol{x}(t))\) is uniformly continuous.  Each such occurrence
would produce a time interval of uniform width on which
\eqref{eq:bounded-x-speed} has a fixed negative upper bound, contradicting the
finite negative variation.

Equation~\eqref{eq:n-limit} now gives
\begin{equation}
  v\longrightarrow-1,\qquad
  n_x\longrightarrow1,
  \qquad n_A\longrightarrow0,
  \qquad \frac{d\boldsymbol{x}}{dt}\longrightarrow0.
  \label{eq:spatial-direction-limit}
\end{equation}

It remains to locate the spatial limit set.  Put
\(\varepsilon=P^{-1}\) and \(c_A=Q_A/P=n_A/n_x\); then
\(\varepsilon,c_A\to0\).  Together with \(\boldsymbol{x}\), these variables
obey the smooth autonomous system
\begin{equation}
  \frac{d\boldsymbol{x}}{dt}
    =ve_x+\frac{(1,c_y,c_z)}{\sqrt{1+|c|^2+\varepsilon^2}},
  \qquad
  \varepsilon'=v_x\varepsilon,
  \qquad
  c_A'=-v_A+v_xc_A.
  \label{eq:compactified-exit-flow}
\end{equation}
The trajectory is precompact, so its \(\omega\)-limit set is nonempty, compact, and
invariant.  Every point in it has
\(\varepsilon=0\), \(c_A=0\), and \(v=-1\).  Invariance keeps \(c_A=0\) along
the orbit of any such point; the last equation in
\eqref{eq:compactified-exit-flow} therefore forces \(v_A=0\).  Thus the spatial
\(\omega\)-limit set lies in \(\mathcal G\), and
\(\operatorname{dist}(\boldsymbol{x}(t),\mathcal G)\to0\).

Spatial precompactness now gives the subsequence in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:capture-subsequence}; convergence of the full trajectory is not
needed.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Transverse rigidity at the extension boundary}

\begin{lemma}[Transverse-disk rigidity]
\label{lem:transverse-disk-rigidity}
Let \(\gamma:[0,\tau_*)\to M\), with \(\tau_*<\infty\), be a
future-directed unit timelike geodesic of \eqref{eq:general-scalar-shift}
converging to \(q_*\) on the boundary of \(M\) in a Hausdorff \(C^2\)
extension.  Suppose that for a sequence \(\tau_n\uparrow\tau_*\), with
\(t_n=t(\tau_n)\), its spatial positions tend to \(p\in\mathcal G\), its
factors \(\Gamma_n=\dot t(\tau_n)\) tend to infinity, and its unit spatial
momentum directions \(\widehat n_n\), expressed in the stationary frame,
tend to \(e_1\).  Then a nonempty open transverse
disk centered at \((p_y,p_z)\) satisfies
\(v(p_x,y,z)=-1\) and \(v_x(p_x,y,z)=\alpha\) for a constant \(\alpha\).
No regularity of \(\Sigma\) is assumed, and \(\alpha\) may vanish.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We compare data at their actual points near \(q_*\) and at points
translated to a fixed stationary slice.  Let \(\Phi_a\) be the isometry
\(\Phi_a(t,\boldsymbol{x})=(t+a,\boldsymbol{x})\) of \(M\), and put
\begin{equation}
  q_n=\gamma(\tau_n),\qquad
  \bar q_n=\Phi_{-t_n}(q_n)=(0,\boldsymbol{x}_n)\longrightarrow(0,p).
  \label{eq:actual-translated-points}
\end{equation}
For a vector \(W_n\) at \(q_n\), write
\(\bar W_n=(\Phi_{-t_n})_*W_n\).  Extension bounds will be taken at the
actual points \(q_n\); stationary translations are used only within \(M\).
By Lemma~\ref{lem:endpoint-frames}, \(u_n=\dot\gamma(\tau_n)\) has a finite
nonzero limit at \(q_*\).  At \(q_n\), write
\begin{equation}
  u_n=\Gamma_n(e_0+\beta_n\widehat n_n),
  \qquad
  \Gamma_n=\dot t(\tau_n),\qquad
  \beta_n=\sqrt{1-\Gamma_n^{-2}},\qquad
  \widehat n_n\longrightarrow e_1 .
  \label{eq:exit-boost-form}
\end{equation}
Choose spatial orthonormal vectors \(s_{2,n},s_{3,n}\) at \(q_n\),
perpendicular to \(\widehat n_n\), whose translates satisfy
\(\bar s_{A,n}\to e_A\) at \((0,p)\).  Complete the observer frame by
\begin{equation}
  s_{1,n}=\Gamma_n(\beta_ne_0+\widehat n_n).
\end{equation}
Then \((u_n,s_{1,n},s_{2,n},s_{3,n})\) is orthonormal.  Put
\begin{equation}
  L_n=u_n+s_{1,n}=A_n\ell_n,
  \qquad
  N_n=\frac12(u_n-s_{1,n})=A_n^{-1}\nu_n,
  \label{eq:observer-null-frame}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
  A_n=\Gamma_n(1+\beta_n)\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
  \ell_n=e_0+\widehat n_n,\qquad
  \nu_n=\frac12(e_0-\widehat n_n).
\end{equation}

Work in a relatively compact convex normal neighborhood of \(q_*\).
There \(u_n\) converges, and the unit spheres in \(u_n^\perp\) form a compact
family.  After taking a
subsequence, the observer frames in Eq.~\eqref{eq:observer-null-frame}
therefore converge in an extension chart.  The coordinate vectors \(e_A\)
need not be bounded there; this is why the observer vectors \(s_{A,n}\) are
used below.

Choose a closed disk \(B_\delta\subset\mathbb R^2_\zeta\) so small that the
limiting extension data \((q_*,\zeta^As_{A,*})\), \(\zeta\in B_\delta\), lie
in the time-one domain of the geodesic flow and their geodesics remain in the
normal neighborhood.  This domain is open, while the disk of limiting data is
compact.  Hence the same \(\delta\) works for all large \(n\), and continuous
dependence of the geodesic and parallel-transport flows gives uniformly
bounded transported observer frames.

In \(M\), the translated initial data
\((\bar q_n,\zeta^A\bar s_{A,n})\) converge uniformly for
\(\zeta\in B_\delta\) to \(((0,p),\zeta^Ae_A)\).  The limiting geodesic is exactly
\begin{equation}
  r\longmapsto (0,p_x,p_y+r\zeta^2,p_z+r\zeta^3),
  \label{eq:limiting-transverse-geodesic}
\end{equation}
because \(\Gamma^\mu{}_{AB}=0\).  Shrinking \(\delta\) if necessary, smooth
dependence in the original metric gives the same common parameter interval.
Translate these geodesics back by \(\Phi_{t_n}\).  For every large
\(n\) and every \(\zeta\in B_\delta\), the resulting original geodesic
from \(q_n\) exists for the whole interval \(0\leq r\leq1\).  Its image in
the extension solves the same initial-value problem as the extension geodesic,
so uniqueness identifies them on that whole interval.  Their transported
observer frames and curvature contractions are therefore uniformly bounded
for \(\zeta\in B_\delta\) as \(n\to\infty\).

For \(\ell=e_0+e_1\) and \(\nu=(e_0-e_1)/2\), parallel transport along
the limiting transverse lines obeys
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_A\ell=\frac{v_A}{2}\ell,
  \qquad
  \nabla_A\nu=-\frac{v_A}{2}\nu,
  \qquad
  \nabla_Ae_B=0.
  \label{eq:transverse-null-transport}
\end{equation}
Thus the limits of the translated, transported vectors
\(\ell_n,\nu_n\) differ from \(\ell,\nu\) only by finite nonzero
reciprocal factors.  Use hats for parallel transport along the transverse
geodesics from the actual points \(q_n\).
Bounded observer-frame curvature and \(A_n\to\infty\) give
\begin{align}
 R(\widehat\ell_n,\widehat s_{A,n},\widehat\ell_n,\widehat s_{B,n})
 &=A_n^{-2}R(\widehat L_n,\widehat s_{A,n},\widehat L_n,\widehat s_{B,n})
 \longrightarrow0, \notag\\
 R(\widehat\ell_n,\widehat s_{A,n},\widehat\ell_n,\widehat\nu_n)
 &=A_n^{-1}R(\widehat L_n,\widehat s_{A,n},\widehat L_n,\widehat N_n)
 \longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:scaled-curvature-limits}
\end{align}
Stationary translation preserves these curvature contractions.  On the
fixed disk, the translated geodesics and transported normalized vectors
\(\overline{\widehat\ell}_n\), \(\overline{\widehat\nu}_n\), and
\(\overline{\widehat s}_{A,n}\) converge in the original geometry.
Passing to these limits and removing the finite nonzero transport factors
therefore yields
\begin{equation}
  R(\ell,e_A,\ell,e_B)=0,
  \qquad
  R(\ell,e_A,\ell,\nu)=0
  \label{eq:positive-boost-vanishing}
\end{equation}
throughout that disk.

Direct evaluation in the scalar-shift metric gives
\begin{align}
  R(\ell,e_A,\ell,e_B)&=v_{AB}-v_Av_B,\notag\\
  R(\ell,e_A,\ell,\nu)&=\tfrac12(v-1)v_{xA}+v_xv_A.
  \label{eq:transverse-rigidity-curvature}
\end{align}
For \(f=e^{-v}\), the first equation in
\eqref{eq:positive-boost-vanishing} is therefore \(f_{AB}=0\).  Hence \(f\) is
affine on the disk.  At its center \(p\in\mathcal G\), one has \(v=-1\) and
\(v_A=0\), so the affine gradient vanishes and
\begin{equation}
  v(p_x,y,z)=-1
  \label{eq:forced-planar-wall}
\end{equation}
throughout the disk.  There \(v_A=0\), and the second equation in
\eqref{eq:positive-boost-vanishing} reduces to
\begin{equation}
  R(\ell,e_A,\ell,\nu)=-v_{xA}=0.
  \label{eq:forced-constant-kappa}
\end{equation}
Thus \(v_x\) is constant on the disk, proving the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Necessity in Theorem~\ref{thm:extension-rigidity}]
For a future extension, the boundary-reaching timelike geodesic supplied in
Appendix~\ref{app:global-proof} satisfies the capture proposition.
Lemma~\ref{lem:transverse-disk-rigidity} then gives a planar disk with constant
\(v_x\).  Regularity of \(\Sigma\) makes this constant nonzero, establishing
Eq.~\eqref{eq:planar-cap-criterion}.

For a past extension, set \(T=-t\), \(X=-x\), and
\(\widehat v(X,Y,Z)=v(-X,Y,Z)\).  This preserves the metric form and all
hypotheses, so the future argument gives the same criterion.  This proves
necessity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Planar-cap extension}
\label{app:cap-attachment}

\begin{proof}[Sufficiency in Theorem~\ref{thm:extension-rigidity}]
Assume Eq.~\eqref{eq:planar-cap-criterion} holds on \(D_0\), fix an open disk
\(D\) with \(\overline D\subset D_0\), and put \(\kappa=-\alpha\).  Then
\(\mathcal N=\mathbb R_t\times D\) at \(x=x_H\) is a Killing horizon whose
Killing orbits are diffeomorphic to \(\mathbb R\), with smooth cross-section
\(D\) and constant nonzero inaffinity.  Locally, its regular bifurcate extension
is therefore the constant-surface-gravity branch of the R\'acz--Wald theorem
\cite{RaczWald1992}.  The following coordinates realize this continuation.

Put \(s=x-x_H\).  Hadamard's lemma with \((y,z)\) as parameters gives a
smooth function \(d\) such that
\begin{equation}
  v=-1-\kappa s+s^2d(s,y,z)
  \label{eq:cap-hadamard-form}
\end{equation}
on a neighborhood of \(s=0\) over \(D\).  Define
\begin{equation}
  U=e^{-\kappa t},\qquad s=UV.
  \label{eq:cap-kruskal-coordinates}
\end{equation}
The original region has \(U>0\).  Substitution into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:general-scalar-shift} gives
\begin{align}
 ds^2={}&U^2dV^2
 +\left[-\frac{2}{\kappa}
 +\frac{2U^2V^2}{\kappa}d(UV,y,z)\right]dU\,dV \notag\\
 &+\left[-\frac{2V^2}{\kappa^2}d(UV,y,z)
 +\frac{U^2V^4}{\kappa^2}d(UV,y,z)^2\right]dU^2
 +dy^2+dz^2 .
 \label{eq:cap-extended-metric}
\end{align}
Every coefficient is smooth at \(U=0\), and the determinant of the
\((U,V)\) block is exactly \(-\kappa^{-2}\).  The metric therefore extends
smoothly and nondegenerately to \(U<0\).

To attach this patch to all of \(M\), take a small product neighborhood
\(P\) in \((U,V,y,z)\) crossing \(U=0\) and glue its \(U>0\) part by
\begin{equation}
  (U,V,y,z)\longmapsto
  \left(-\kappa^{-1}\log U,\ x_H+UV,\ y,\ z\right).
  \label{eq:cap-gluing-map}
\end{equation}
The gluing graph is closed.  Indeed, a sequence with \(U\to0\) has
\(|t|\to\infty\) and cannot converge in \(M=\mathbb R^4\); all other possible
limits are handled by continuity.  Since the quotient map is open, the glued
manifold is Hausdorff.  It is a proper extension because it contains \(U<0\).
The product patch is time-orientable, and its orientation can be chosen to
agree with that of \(M\) on the connected overlap.
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t=\kappa(V\partial_V-U\partial_U),
\end{equation}
so \(U=0\) and \(V=0\) are the two branches of a bifurcate Killing horizon
and \(U=V=0\) is a bifurcation disk.
\end{proof}

For the product cap in Section~\ref{sec:compact-cap}, the null coordinates
\(u,w\) of Eq.~\eqref{eq:null-coords} also give the conformal diagram in
Figure~\ref{fig:reduced-patch}(a).  With \(s=x-L\), choose matching integration
constants on its two sides and put
\begin{equation}
  p=\operatorname{sgn}(-s)e^{\kappa u},\qquad
  q=e^{-\kappa w},\qquad
  pq=-c_0s e^{h(s)},\qquad c_0>0,
  \label{eq:cap-conformal-coordinates}
\end{equation}
where \(h\) is smooth.  The inverse function theorem gives a smooth
\(s=s(pq)\), and
\(ds^2_{(2)}=C(pq)\,dp\,dq\), where
\(C=(1-v^2)/(\kappa^2pq)\), has a positive smooth coefficient at
\(pq=0\).  The original region has \(q>0\), its bubble side has \(p>0\),
and future causal curves have increasing \(p\) and decreasing \(q\).
The added half \(q<0\) is the same attachment as \(U<0\) above.

To display it together with the original three-region patch,
Figure~\ref{fig:reduced-patch}(a) uses the compactification
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal A=\frac23
  \begin{cases}
    \pi/4+\arctan(e^{-\kappa u}) & \text{I},\\
    \arctan(e^{\kappa u})-\pi/4 & \text{II},\\
    -\arctan(e^{\kappa u})-\pi/4 & \text{III},
  \end{cases}
  \qquad \mathcal B=\arctan[\sinh(\kappa w)],
  \label{eq:cap-global-compactification}
\end{equation}
with \(X=(\mathcal B-\mathcal A)/2\), \(T=(\mathcal B+\mathcal A)/2\).
Here the two cap slopes have equal magnitude \(\kappa\).
The original patch has \(|\mathcal A|,|\mathcal B|<\pi/2\).
Near the front, both sides of the attachment share the smooth formulas
\[
  \mathcal A=\tfrac23\arctan p-\pi/6,\qquad
  \mathcal B=\pi/2-2\arctan q.
\]
The two-dimensional metric is conformal to \(-dT^2+dX^2\), with positive
factor \(3C(pq)(1+p^2)(1+q^2)/4\), including at \(q=0\).
Every finite \(p>0\) at \(q=0\) gives a crossing on the upper-right edge
\(X+T=\pi/2\), above and to the left of \(B\), which has \(p=0\).
The segment \(\mathcal B=\pi/2\), \(\mathcal A>\pi/6\), belongs instead
to ordinary future null infinity in region I; the figure labels a portion
of that segment separately from the cap crossing.  The shaded attachment
is a finite chart neighborhood, not a proposed maximal extension.

\subsection{A flat profile inside the compact cap}
\label{sec:flat-crossing}

Flat bubble cores are standard: a spatially constant shift gives an
exactly flat region \cite[Corollary~1.6]{Natario2002}.  For a nonconstant
product shift, Santos-Pereira et al. derived the vacuum equation
\(\beta_t+\tfrac12(\beta^2)_x=h(t)\)
\cite[Eq.~(4.12)]{SantosPereiraEtAl2020}.  Its stationary branch with
\(h=\kappa\) includes the flat profile
\begin{equation}
  v(s)=-\sqrt{1+2\kappa s},\qquad s=x-L,
  \label{eq:flat-cap-germ}
\end{equation}
up to the sign convention for the shift.  We use it on a smaller cap disk
to obtain an exactly flat crossing neighborhood and its regular continuation.

Putting \(\kappa=2cL\) matches the wall value and first normal derivative
of the compact example.  A smooth collar interpolation embeds the profile:
the difference of the two shifts is \(O(s^2)\), so an interpolation supported
near the wall preserves its value, nonzero normal derivative, and unique
stationary-limit level.  Taking the transverse support strictly inside the
cap leaves the rim construction unchanged.

\subsection{Timelike crossing from the bubble core}
\label{app:timelike-crossing}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:timelike-cap-crossing}]
Put \(\mu^2=1+|Q|^2\).  Conservation of \(Q\) and Killing energy
\(E=\sqrt{P_0^2+\mu^2}\) gives the outgoing branch
\begin{equation}
  \dot x=\sqrt{E^2-\mu^2(1-v^2)}
        =\sqrt{P_0^2+\mu^2v^2}\geq P_0.
  \label{eq:cap-timelike-velocity}
\end{equation}
Thus the cap is reached within the stated proper time, and the transverse
displacement, \(|Q|(\tau-\tau_0)\), obeys \eqref{eq:cap-aperture}.
Near \(x=L\), put \(\kappa=2cL\) and \(\delta=L-x\).
The smooth first integrals give \(\dot x=E+O(\delta)\),
\(\dot t=E/(\kappa\delta)+O(1)\), and hence
\(d\delta/dt=-\kappa\delta+O(\delta^2)\).
For small \(\delta\) this gives exponential decay and
\(d\log(\delta e^{\kappa t})/dt=O(\delta)\), an integrable remainder.
Consequently \(\delta e^{\kappa t}\to C\in(0,\infty)\).
In the regular coordinates \eqref{eq:cap-kruskal-coordinates}, the endpoint
therefore has \(U=0\) and \(V=-C\), strictly inside the aperture.
Lemma~\ref{lem:endpoint-frames} gives a finite nonzero limiting tangent, and
local geodesic existence continues the trajectory across \(U=0\).
Strictness of \eqref{eq:cap-aperture} permits a compact bundle of nearby data
whose trajectories remain a positive distance from the rim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Endpoint frames and the cap-rim obstruction}
\label{app:compact-cap}

\begin{lemma}[Endpoint frames]
\label{lem:endpoint-frames}
If a future-directed affinely parametrized causal geodesic
\(\gamma:[0,\lambda_*)\to M\), where \(\lambda_*<\infty\), converges to \(q\)
in a \(C^2\) extension, then its affine tangent and every parallel vector along
it have finite limits at \(q\), and the tangent limit is nonzero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In a relatively compact extension chart with temporal coordinate \(T\), put
\(K=\dot\gamma\) and \(f=dT(K)>0\).  Compactness of the causal cone section
\(dT=1\) gives \(|f'|=|\nabla^2T(K,K)|\leq Cf^2\), whereas
\(\int_\lambda^{\lambda_*}f(\mu)\,d\mu
  =T(q)-T(\gamma(\lambda))<\infty\).
If \(f(\lambda_j)\to\infty\), then \(|(1/f)'|\leq C\) implies
\(1/f(\lambda)\leq C(\lambda_* -\lambda)\).  This would make
\(\int^{\lambda_*}f\,d\lambda\) diverge.  Thus \(K\) is bounded.  Standard
coordinate ODE estimates now make \(K\) Lipschitz; the linear
parallel-transport equation and Gr\"onwall's inequality do the same for every
parallel vector.  Hence all have limits, and uniqueness of the geodesic equation
excludes a zero tangent limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:flat-cap-rim}]
Consider the front rim at \((x,y,z)=(L,a,0)\); rotational symmetry covers its
circle, and the rear is its time reverse.
There \(\kappa=-v_x=2cL\ne0\), and an affine tangent to the stationary null
orbit is \(K=q(e_0+e_1)\), where \(q=q_0e^{\kappa t}\); hence the orbit reaches
finite affine parameter as \(t\to+\infty\).  Since \(\psi\) is flat at
\(a^2\), all derivatives of the metric along the rim orbit agree with those of
the planar product germ.  In the parallel null frame
\(K,N=q^{-1}(e_0-e_1)/2,e_2,e_3\), the only potentially nonzero independent
curvature component is therefore
\(R(K,N,K,N)=v_{xx}-v_x^2\), which is stationary and finite.  The generator's
parallel-propagated curvature is consequently bounded.

Suppose nevertheless that it converges to an endpoint in a \(C^2\) extension.
Lemma~\ref{lem:endpoint-frames} gives limits for \(K\) and the parallel vector
\(e_2\).  The open domain and continuous dependence of the \(C^2\) geodesic
flow \cite[Sec.~2.3]{Minguzzi2019} then supply a fixed \(\delta>0\), independent
of sufficiently large \(t\), for the transverse geodesics
\(\sigma_t(r)=(t,L,a+r,0)\), since \(\Gamma^\mu{}_{yy}=0\).  Their transported
frames converge, so their curvature components must remain bounded for
\(|r|<\delta\).

Along these geodesics,
\(\nabla_{e_2}(e_0+e_1)=\tfrac12v_y(e_0+e_1)\), so transport from the rim gives
\begin{align*}
 \widehat K(r)&=q e^{-[v(L,a+r,0)+1]/2}(e_0+e_1),\\
 R(\widehat K,e_2,\widehat K,e_2)
 &=q^2e^{-[v(L,a+r,0)+1]}(-c\phi_{yy}-c^2\phi_y^2),
 \qquad \phi(y)=\psi(y^2).
\end{align*}
For every sufficiently small fixed \(r>0\), one has
\(\phi_{yy}=2\psi'(y^2)+4y^2\psi''(y^2)>0\).  The last contraction therefore
diverges as \(t\to+\infty\), contradicting curvature continuity.  Hence the
rim generator has no \(C^2\) endpoint.
\end{proof}
