\begin{abstract}
We study whether the extra regions visible in conformal diagrams of eternal
superluminal Alcubierre bubbles belong to the full four-dimensional spacetime.
We first compute the reduced \(1+1\)-dimensional diagram for a smooth
hyperbolic-secant profile.  Its front and rear horizon rays end after finite
affine distance, and the reduced metric continues beyond them.  Restoring the
two suppressed spatial directions changes the result.  The radial spacetimes
in our class admit no Hausdorff extension with a twice continuously
differentiable (\(C^2\)) metric.  At nondegenerate walls, freely falling tidal
curvature diverges at the endpoints even though scalar curvature measures
remain finite.

We then extend the analysis beyond radial symmetry and determine when a
four-dimensional continuation is possible.  The wall must contain an open,
exactly flat patch that already forms a stationary horizon, with the same
nonzero horizon scale everywhere on it.  We construct the continuation through
such a patch, showing that the criterion is sharp.  Thus the reduced diagram
lifts to four dimensions only where a whole open patch---not just one horizon
ray---remains regular.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A conformal diagram computed after suppressing spatial dimensions need not
describe an extension of the full spacetime.  Superluminal Alcubierre bubbles
\cite{Alcubierre1994} provide a concrete test: their reduced diagrams contain
front and rear horizon
rays ending at apparently traversable corners.  Scalar curvature measures stay
finite on smooth Alcubierre walls \cite{MattinglyEtAl2021,Rodal2023}, so they do
not decide whether these corners survive in four dimensions.

We first compute the reduced diagram and its finite-affine endpoints exactly
for a smooth hyperbolic-secant (sech) profile.  Restoring the transverse
directions reveals unbounded tidal curvature in a freely falling frame at a
nondegenerate rounded front and rear.  Our global result then shows that, within the class
studied here, an extension exists exactly when the wall contains an open planar
patch that is already a stationary horizon with a constant nonzero horizon
scale.  Smooth radial walls have no such patch and are \(C^2\)-inextendible;
the planar case admits a Kruskal-like extension.  In a compact smooth example,
bounded curvature on a cap-edge ray is defeated by divergence on arbitrarily
nearby rays.  Thus the issue hidden by dimensional reduction is regularity
across a transverse neighborhood, not merely along one ray.

\paragraph{Relation to earlier work.}
Alcubierre's metric realizes apparent superluminal motion at the cost of
violating the usual energy conditions
\cite{Alcubierre1994,PfenningFord1997,LoboVisser2004}.  Its null geodesics,
horizons, and infinite-blueshift loci have been studied
\cite{ClarkHiscockLarson1999,BarceloEtAl2022}, as have quantum effects in
reduced models \cite{Hiscock1997,FinazziLiberatiBarcelo2009}.  Earlier work also
gave a Misner-space continuation \cite{GonzalezDiaz2000} and the standard
reduced diagram \cite{FinazziLiberatiBarcelo2009}.  A broader classification of
warp-drive geometries and their energy-condition properties is given in
Ref.~\cite{BarzegarBuchertVigneron2026}.  Here we address classical
four-dimensional extendibility.

\section{The Reduced Conformal Diagram}
\label{sec:diagram}

Write \(\xi=x\) for the signed axial coordinate.  In the bubble-comoving
convention, the usual lab-frame shift \(v_s f\)
becomes \(v=v_s(f-1)\) under \(x=X-v_st\), so the center has \(v=0\) and the
asymptotic exterior \(v=-v_s\).

Suppressing the transverse directions, the stationary comoving geometry reduces
to
\begin{equation}
  ds^2_{1+1}=-dt^2+[d\xi-v(\xi)dt]^2
    =-(1-v^2)dt^2-2v\,dt\,d\xi+d\xi^2 .
  \label{eq:reduced-metric}
\end{equation}
The two null families satisfy \(d\xi/dt=v\pm1\), so the retarded and advanced
null coordinates are
\begin{equation}
  u=t-\int\frac{d\xi}{v+1},\qquad
  w=t-\int\frac{d\xi}{v-1},
  \label{eq:null-coords}
\end{equation}
and give the doubly null form
\begin{equation}
  ds^2_{1+1}=-(1-v^2)\,du\,dw .
  \label{eq:double-null}
\end{equation}
The horizons are the simple roots of \(1-v^2\).  For the even profile used
below, \(v\) decreases monotonically from \(0\) at the center to
\(-v_s<-1\) on each outward branch, so there are exactly two, at
\(\xi=\xi_1<0\) and \(\xi=\xi_2>0\) with \(v=-1\).  They divide the
reduced spacetime into the bubble interior, region II with \(|v|<1\), and the
two exterior branches, regions I and III with \(|v|>1\).

For the representative profile, written in the two-parameter form
\(v(\xi)=\alpha[\operatorname{sech}(\xi/a)-1]\) with \(\alpha>1\), both
integrals in Eq.~\eqref{eq:null-coords} are elementary.  With
\(\beta=\alpha/(\alpha-1)\), the horizons are
at \(\xi_{1,2}=\mp a\operatorname{arcosh}\beta\), and the local peeling scale is
\(\kappa=(\alpha/a)\sqrt{\beta^2-1}/\beta^2\).  The closed-form
antiderivatives are recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:sech-chart}.  To display all
three regions in one compact chart, define
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal U=
  \begin{cases}
    \tfrac12+e^{-\kappa u} & \text{I},\\[2pt]
    \tfrac12\tanh(\kappa u/2) & \text{II},\\[2pt]
    -\tfrac12-e^{\kappa u} & \text{III},
  \end{cases}
  \quad
  \mathcal W=w/a,
  \qquad
  \bar{\mathcal U}=\arctan\mathcal U,
  \quad
  \bar{\mathcal W}=\arctan\mathcal W,
  \label{eq:regularized}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  X=\frac{\bar{\mathcal W}-\bar{\mathcal U}}2,
  \qquad
  T=\frac{\bar{\mathcal W}+\bar{\mathcal U}}2.
  \label{eq:diagram-coordinates}
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:reduced-patch} plots these compactified coordinates for
\(\alpha=2\) and \(a=1\).

\begin{figure}[!t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.82\linewidth]{figures/calculated_three_region_endpoint_patch.pdf}
  \caption{Compactified conformal diagram of the reduced \(1+1\) patch for
  \(v(\xi)=2[\operatorname{sech}\xi-1]\).  Blue short-dashed horizons separate
  the interior II from the exterior branches I and III; gray solid and
  long-dashed curves have constant \(\xi\) and \(t\), respectively, and the
  orange curve is \(\xi=0\).  Black points mark the finite-affine past end of
  the \(\xi_1\) generator and future end of the \(\xi_2\) generator.  The
  reduced metric extends through these points; the usual rounded
  four-dimensional geometry does not.}
  \label{fig:reduced-patch}
\end{figure}

Near the future endpoint at \(\xi_2\), write \(s=\xi-\xi_2\).  Since
\(v=-1-\kappa s+O(s^2)\), one has
\(1-v^2=-2\kappa s+O(s^2)\) and
\(u=t+\kappa^{-1}\ln|s|+O(1)\), \(w=t+O(1)\).  The local chart
\begin{equation}
  U=
  \begin{cases}
    e^{\kappa u} & \text{region II},\\
    -e^{\kappa u} & \text{region III},
  \end{cases}
  \qquad V=e^{-\kappa w}
  \label{eq:endpoint-chart}
\end{equation}
then gives \(ds^2_{1+1}=[(1-v^2)/(\kappa^2UV)]\,dU\,dV\), whose prefactor has a
finite nonzero limit because \(UV\propto-s\).  The front generator is \(U=0\)
and its future endpoint is \(U=V=0\), at finite affine parameter; the
past-rear corner is analogous.  The chart is regular there, so the reduced
metric continues through the corner.  For the radial four-dimensional
application, the profile above is its axial restriction: \(r=|\xi|\),
\(\xi_{1,2}=\mp R_F\), and \(\kappa=|v_x|\) at the two generators.

\section{When Does the Four-Dimensional Spacetime Extend?}
\label{sec:maximality}

The marked rays do not exclude an extension elsewhere.  The result below does:
for the scalar shifts considered here, any extension requires an open planar
patch that is already a stationary horizon.

Throughout, a Hausdorff \(C^k\) Lorentzian extension of \((M,g)\) means a
connected time-oriented smooth Hausdorff manifold \(\widetilde M\) with a
\(C^k\) Lorentzian metric \(\widetilde g\), together with a smooth
time-orientation-preserving isometric embedding
\(\iota:M\to\widetilde M\) whose image is a proper open subset.  We identify
\(M\) with its image.  The spacetime is \(C^k\)-inextendible if no such
extension exists.

\begin{theorem}[Global extension rigidity for scalar shifts]
\label{thm:extension-rigidity}
Let \(M=\mathbb R^4\) carry the scalar-shift metric
\begin{equation}
  ds^2=-dt^2+[dx-v(\boldsymbol{x})dt]^2+dy^2+dz^2,
  \qquad \boldsymbol{x}=(x,y,z),\quad r=|\boldsymbol{x}|,
  \label{eq:general-scalar-shift}
\end{equation}
where \(v\) is smooth, bounded, and \(v\leq0\).  Assume that, for some
\(v_s>1\), the exterior is either exactly constant or exponentially
short-range:
\begin{equation}
  v=-v_s\quad(r\geq R_0),
  \qquad\text{or}\qquad
  |v+v_s|+|\nabla v|\leq Ce^{-\sigma r}
  \quad(r\geq R_0),
  \label{eq:general-scalar-tail}
\end{equation}
for some \(R_0,C,\sigma>0\).  Suppose also that
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma=\{\boldsymbol{x}:v(\boldsymbol{x})=-1\}
  \label{eq:general-wall}
\end{equation}
is compact and regular (that is, \(dv\ne0\) on \(\Sigma\)).  Its
stationary-generator locus is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal G=\{p\in\Sigma:v_y(p)=v_z(p)=0\}.
  \label{eq:generator-locus}
\end{equation}
The sign condition makes \(\Sigma=\{|v|=1\}\) the entire stationary-limit
set; it is not a normalization for profiles taking both signs.  Regularity
implies \(v_x\ne0\) on \(\mathcal G\).

If \((M,g)\) admits a Hausdorff \(C^2\) Lorentzian extension, then there are
\(x_H\in\mathbb R\), a nonempty open disk
\(D_0\subset\mathbb R^2_{y,z}\), and a constant \(\alpha\ne0\) such that
\begin{equation}
  v(x_H,y,z)=-1,
  \qquad v_x(x_H,y,z)=\alpha
  \quad ((y,z)\in D_0).
  \label{eq:planar-cap-criterion}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, for the stationary Killing field \(\chi=\partial_t\), the wall
worldvolume contains the open Killing-horizon patch
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal H=\mathbb R_t\times\{x_H\}\times D_0,
  \qquad
  \nabla_\chi\chi=\alpha\chi
  \quad\hbox{on }\mathcal H .
  \label{eq:forced-killing-horizon}
\end{equation}
In particular, if the wall worldvolume contains no such patch, \((M,g)\) is
\(C^2\)-inextendible.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Sharpness of the rigidity condition]
\label{prop:planar-cap-extension}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:extension-rigidity}, suppose
there are \(x_H\), a nonempty open disk \(D_0\), and a constant
\(\alpha\ne0\) satisfying Eq.~\eqref{eq:planar-cap-criterion}.  For every open
disk \(D\) with \(\overline D\subset D_0\), \((M,g)\) admits a proper smooth
Hausdorff Lorentzian extension which continues the corresponding finite-affine
stationary generators through a bifurcate Killing horizon over \(D\).
\end{proposition}

Within this class, an extension exists exactly when an open flat wall patch has
a uniform horizon scale.

\paragraph{Why the usual horizon endpoints are singular.}
For any Killing field \(\chi\), the Killing equation and Ricci identity give
\begin{equation}
  R(\chi,X,\chi,Y)
   =g(\nabla_X\chi,\nabla_Y\chi)
    -\frac12(\nabla^2\chi^2)(X,Y).
  \label{eq:killing-tidal-identity}
\end{equation}
With
\((\nabla_c\nabla_d-\nabla_d\nabla_c)V^a=R^a{}_{bcd}V^b\), this follows from
\(\nabla_a\nabla_b\chi_c=R_{cbad}\chi^d\) by differentiating \(\chi^2\).
For the scalar shift, take \(\chi=\partial_t\) and introduce the stationary
orthonormal frame
\begin{equation}
  e_0=\partial_t+v\partial_x,\qquad e_1=\partial_x,
  \qquad e_A=\partial_A.
  \label{eq:stationary-frame}
\end{equation}
On \(v=-1\),
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_{\partial_t}\partial_t
    =v_x\partial_t+v_y\partial_y+v_z\partial_z .
  \label{eq:not-whole-sphere}
\end{equation}
Thus \(\mathcal G\) is precisely the locus of null pregeodesic stationary
orbits, and \(e_0+e_1=\chi\) there.  At \(p\in\mathcal G\), the transverse,
or screen, components of the Killing identity reduce to
\begin{equation}
  H_{AB}(p):=v_{AB}(p),
  \qquad
  R_{tAtB}=H_{AB},
  \qquad R_{KAKB}=H_{AB}q^2
  \quad\text{for }K=q\partial_t .
  \label{eq:screen-general}
\end{equation}

Let the null orbit through \(p\) be parametrized by Killing time and suppose
\(\nabla_\chi\chi=\alpha\chi\), with \(\alpha\ne0\).  At one end of the orbit,
its affine tangent \(K=q\chi\) obeys
\(|q|=[|\alpha|\,|\lambda-\lambda_*|]^{-1}\).  Thus a nonzero transverse tidal
form produces a parallel-propagated curvature singularity proportional to
\(|\lambda-\lambda_*|^{-2}\).  The Killing flow preserves the screen metric
and tidal form, so a parallel screen frame changes only its orthonormal basis.
Lemma~\ref{lem:endpoint-frames} therefore excludes a \(C^2\) endpoint.  The
marked corners of Figure~\ref{fig:reduced-patch} are consequently
four-dimensional curvature boundaries.

\paragraph{From the local singularity to a global result.}
If a proper extension existed, global hyperbolicity and
Ref.~\cite[Theorem~2]{GallowayLingSbierski2018} would supply a timelike geodesic
leaving \(M\) in finite proper time.  The capture proposition sends a boosted
subsequence toward \(p\in\mathcal G\).  Uniform curvature bounds on a small
transverse disk in the extension then force
\(v_{AB}-v_Av_B=0\) and \(v_{xA}=0\).  Thus \(e^{-v}\) is affine on the disk;
its gradient vanishes at \(p\), so \(v=-1\) and \(v_x\) is constant
there.  This is exactly Eq.~\eqref{eq:planar-cap-criterion}.

Appendix~\ref{app:global-proof} proves the capture and transverse-rigidity
steps and constructs the proper Hausdorff attachment.

\paragraph{A practical geometric test.}
The set \(\mathcal G\) is precisely the critical set of the height function
\(x|_\Sigma\), and an extension requires \(x|_\Sigma\) to be constant on an
open subset of \(\Sigma\).  A Morse height function has isolated critical
points, so it rules out an extension immediately.

\begin{corollary}[Radial Alcubierre inextendibility]
\label{cor:alcubierre-maximality}
Suppose \(v=v(r)\) is smooth and radially nonincreasing, with
\[
  v(0)=0,\qquad -v_s\leq v\leq0,\qquad
  v=-1\Longleftrightarrow r=R_F,
\]
and satisfies \eqref{eq:general-scalar-tail}.  Then the corresponding spacetime
is \(C^2\)-inextendible.  No nondegeneracy is assumed at \(R_F\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Suppose that a proper extension exists.  Proposition~\ref{prop:capture} and the
transverse-rigidity argument through Eq.~\eqref{eq:forced-planar-wall} use
compactness of \(\Sigma\), but not its regularity.  They force an open planar
disk in \(\{v=-1\}\).  Here that level set is the sphere \(r=R_F\), which
contains no such disk, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

The corollary includes the sech family of Section~\ref{sec:diagram} and smooth
compact-transition profiles satisfying its hypotheses, including the
degenerate case \(v_r(R_F)=0\).


\section{What Causes the Alcubierre Obstruction?}

\paragraph{Tidal forces at the endpoint.}
For a nondegenerate radial wall in Corollary~\ref{cor:alcubierre-maximality},
put \(\kappa=-v_r(R_F)>0\).  The
stationary field is null on \(v^2=1\), but the
four-dimensional wall is generally not null: there
\begin{equation}
  g^{ab}\partial_a(v^2-1)\partial_b(v^2-1)=4(v_y^2+v_z^2).
  \label{eq:wall-normal-norm}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:not-whole-sphere} therefore selects only the two axial
orbits.  At the future front, \(v_x=-\kappa\), so
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_{\partial_t}\partial_t=-\kappa\partial_t .
  \label{eq:nonaffine}
\end{equation}
The transverse vectors \(Y=\partial_y\) and \(Z=\partial_z\) are parallel
along this orbit, and the wall Hessian is
\begin{equation}
  H_{AB}=-\frac{\kappa}{R_F}\delta_{AB}.
  \label{eq:radial-hessian}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:screen-general}, with
\(q=[\kappa(\lambda_*-\lambda)]^{-1}\), gives
\begin{equation}
  R(K,Y,K,Y)=R(K,Z,K,Z)
  =-\frac{1}{\kappa R_F(\lambda_*-\lambda)^2}.
  \label{eq:main-blowup}
\end{equation}
The isometry \((t,x,y,z)\mapsto(-t,-x,y,z)\) gives the past-rear result.  These are
parallel-propagated curvature singularities in the standard sense
\cite{EllisSchmidt1977}, although the stationary coordinate curvature and
scalar invariants remain finite.  Finite affine length alone is insufficient:
the analogous screen tensor vanishes in Painlev\'e--Gullstrand Schwarzschild,
whose Kruskal extension is regular \cite{LemosSilva2021}.

\paragraph{Wall shape and matter source.}
Write a regular front locally as \(x=F(y,z)\).  Differentiating its wall
equation twice at a stationary generator gives
\begin{equation}
  F_{AB}=\frac{H_{AB}}{\kappa}.
  \label{eq:wall-graph-hessian}
\end{equation}
In principal transverse coordinates for a smooth convex front,
\begin{equation}
  F_{yy}=-C_1,\qquad F_{zz}=-C_2,\qquad C_i>0,
\end{equation}
and the spherical result is \(C_1=C_2=1/R_F\).  An isolated zero of \(H\) is
insufficient: Theorem~\ref{thm:extension-rigidity} requires an open planar cap
with constant \(\kappa\).  Appendix~\ref{app:compact-cap} constructs such a
compact smooth wall and analyzes its rim.

If the Einstein equation is imposed, the same wall curvature determines the
null energy component along the increasingly boosted light direction (in
geometrized units):
\begin{equation}
  8\pi T_{KK}=G_{KK}=q^2\operatorname{tr}H,
  \qquad
  G_{KK}=-\kappa(C_1+C_2)q^2<0
  \quad\text{at a convex future front}.
  \label{eq:null-source}
\end{equation}
Thus the transverse wall curvature that produces the tidal obstruction also
gives an unbounded negative null energy component at a convex front.  No energy
condition enters the extension proof: at a saddle point the full tidal tensor
may be nonzero even when its trace, and hence this source component, vanishes.

\section{Discussion}

For radial Alcubierre profiles, the extra regions of the stand-alone \(1+1\)
diamond do not lift to another regular four-dimensional sheet.  A planar cap
admits a finite transverse Kruskal aperture; in the compact example of
Appendix~\ref{app:compact-cap}, its rim does not extend because neighboring
rays develop unbounded curvature.  Strictly subluminal profiles form no
stationary-limit wall, and the bubble-center worldline remains complete.

\paragraph{Why the rounded wall is not an ordinary horizon.}
The name ``warp horizon'' can mislead.  On a rounded wall the stationary field
becomes null, but the four-dimensional wall itself is not null, and
\(\partial_t\) is geodesic only on isolated stationary generators.  It is not
an ordinary Killing-horizon extension problem \cite{RaczWald1992}.  The planar
cap is instead a genuine constant-inaffinity Killing horizon with the expected
bifurcate extension.  The stationary-limit wall is not a Cauchy horizon: the
bounded-shift argument in Appendix~\ref{app:global-proof} gives global
hyperbolicity \cite[Theorem~2.1]{ChoquetBruhatCotsakis2002}, since
\(g^{-1}(dt,dt)=-N^{-2}\) independently of whether \(\partial_t\) is timelike.
Kerr likewise has a stationary-limit surface in a globally hyperbolic domain
\cite[Secs.~8.3 and 12.3]{Wald1984}.

Away from the planar-cap equality case, \(\kappa=-v_x\) is a local peeling and
inaffinity scale rather than a global Killing-horizon surface gravity
\cite[Secs.~2 and 4.5]{CroppLiberatiVisser2013}.  General vector shifts lie
outside Theorem~\ref{thm:extension-rigidity}.  At \(C^0\) regularity, where
curvature is not classically available, different tools are required
\cite{Sbierski2018}.

\section{Closed-Form Sech Chart}
\label{app:sech-chart}

For the profile
\[
  v(\xi)=\alpha[\operatorname{sech}(\xi/a)-1],\qquad \alpha>1,
\]
put \(\beta=\alpha/(\alpha-1)\), \(\gamma=\alpha/(\alpha+1)\), and
\(E_i=e^{-(\xi-\xi_i)/a}\), where
\(\xi_{1,2}=\mp a\operatorname{arcosh}\beta\).  Direct integration of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:null-coords} gives
\begin{equation}
  u=t+\frac{\xi}{\alpha-1}
    -\frac{a\beta}{(\alpha-1)\sqrt{\beta^2-1}}\,\ln\Lambda,
  \qquad
  \Lambda=
  \begin{cases}
    (E_1-1)/(E_2-1) & \text{region I},\\
    (1-E_1)/(E_2-1) & \text{region II},\\
    (1-E_1)/(1-E_2) & \text{region III},
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:u-closed}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  w=t+\frac{\xi}{\alpha+1}
    +\frac{2a\gamma}{(\alpha+1)\sqrt{1-\gamma^2}}
     \arctan\frac{e^{\xi/a}-\gamma}{\sqrt{1-\gamma^2}} .
  \label{eq:w-closed}
\end{equation}
The regionwise form in Eq.~\eqref{eq:u-closed} keeps the logarithm real and
positive on each branch.  The coordinate \(u\) diverges logarithmically at both
horizons; the regionwise cases in Eq.~\eqref{eq:regularized} absorb this
divergence.
In region II, \(\mathcal U\to\pm\tfrac12\) as \(u\to\pm\infty\).  Thus the
future \(\xi_2\) generator has
\(\bar{\mathcal U}=-\arctan\tfrac12\) and terminates at
\(\bar{\mathcal W}=\pi/2\), while the past \(\xi_1\) generator has
\(\bar{\mathcal U}=\arctan\tfrac12\) and terminates at
\(\bar{\mathcal W}=-\pi/2\).
Equation~\eqref{eq:diagram-coordinates} then gives the marked points in
Figure~\ref{fig:reduced-patch}.

\section{Proof of Global Rigidity and Sharpness}
\label{app:global-proof}

\subsection{From an extension to a captured exit}

The inverse metric has \(g^{tt}=-1\), so \(t\) is a smooth temporal function.
If a future-directed causal curve is parametrized by \(t\), its causal
inequality is
\begin{equation}
  \left|\frac{d\boldsymbol{x}}{dt}-v(\boldsymbol{x})e_x\right|\leq1,
  \qquad
  \left|\frac{d\boldsymbol{x}}{dt}\right|
  \leq1+\|v\|_\infty=:V .
  \label{eq:causal-speed}
\end{equation}
If the \(t\)-range of an inextendible causal curve had a finite upper or lower
endpoint, Eq.~\eqref{eq:causal-speed} would make its spatial coordinates
Cauchy and hence convergent in \(\mathbb R^3\).  The curve could then be
continued in the smooth metric, a contradiction.  Thus \(t\) ranges from
\(-\infty\) to \(+\infty\) on every inextendible causal curve, every
constant-\(t\) slice is Cauchy, and \((M,g)\) is globally hyperbolic.

Suppose, for contradiction, that a proper Hausdorff \(C^2\) extension exists.
The extension-boundary
theorem for globally hyperbolic spacetimes supplies a timelike geodesic in
\(M\), future- or past-directed, with an endpoint on the extension boundary
\cite[Theorem~2]{GallowayLingSbierski2018}.  We first treat the future case.  In a
\(C^2\) extension the theorem's causal maximizer may be restricted near its
first boundary point to a convex normal neighborhood.  That final
positive-length maximizing segment is the unique timelike extension geodesic
through the boundary point.  Its physical portion may therefore be
parametrized by proper time as
\begin{equation}
  \gamma:[0,\tau_*)\longrightarrow M,
  \qquad \tau_*<\infty,
  \qquad u=\dot\gamma .
  \label{eq:extension-geodesic}
\end{equation}
Its tangent has a finite limit in the extension, and the Riemann tensor of the
\(C^2\) extension is continuous.  Curvature contractions with vectors having
uniformly bounded extension-chart components must therefore remain bounded.
The remaining model-specific dynamical statement is isolated below.

\begin{proposition}[Capture of finite-time exits]
\label{prop:capture}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:extension-rigidity}, except that
\(\Sigma\) need only be compact, let \(\gamma\) be
a future-directed unit timelike geodesic with finite proper-time endpoint that
cannot be continued in \(M\).  Put \(P=p_x\), \(Q_A=p_A\), and
\begin{equation}
  n=\frac{(P,Q_y,Q_z)}{\dot t}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
  t\to+\infty,\qquad \dot t\to+\infty,\qquad
  v\to-1,\qquad n\to e_1,
  \label{eq:capture-limits}
\end{equation}
the spatial image of \(\gamma\) is precompact, and there are
\(t_n\to+\infty\) and \(p\in\mathcal G\) such that
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{x}(t_n)\longrightarrow p.
  \label{eq:capture-subsequence}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Proof of the capture proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:capture}]\mbox{}\par\smallskip
\noindent\emph{First integrals and divergent Killing time.}

Let \(E=-p_t\), \(P=p_x\) and \(Q_A=p_A\) be the momenta conjugate to the
coordinates.  For a unit timelike geodesic the Hamiltonian equations read
\begin{equation}
  \dot t=E-vP,
  \qquad
  \dot t^2=P^2+Q_AQ_A+1,
  \label{eq:global-first-integrals}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  \dot P=-v_xP\dot t,
  \qquad
  \dot Q_A=-v_AP\dot t .
  \label{eq:global-momentum-equations}
\end{equation}
Here a dot denotes \(d/d\tau\), \(E=-p_t\) is conserved, and repeated
transverse indices are summed.  Since \(t\) is temporal, \(\dot t>0\).

The geodesic cannot exit with \(t\) bounded.  In that case
Eq.~\eqref{eq:causal-speed} would give a finite spatial limit, while, using
\(t\) as parameter,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{dP}{dt}=-v_xP,
  \qquad
  \frac{dQ_A}{dt}=-v_AP
  \label{eq:t-momentum-equations}
\end{equation}
would keep the momenta bounded on the resulting compact coordinate set.  The
geodesic data would converge in \(TM\), and the smooth geodesic equation would
continue \(\gamma\) past \(\tau_*\).  Therefore a future exit requires
\(t\to+\infty\).

The sign of \(P\) is fixed by Eq.~\eqref{eq:global-momentum-equations}.  If
\(P\leq0\), then \(v\leq0\) gives \(0<\dot t\leq E\), which is incompatible
with \(t\to+\infty\) in finite proper time.  Hence \(P>0\).  Moreover,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{d\log P}{dt}=-v_x
  \label{eq:log-p}
\end{equation}
has a globally bounded right-hand side.  If \(P\) returned below a fixed bound
at arbitrarily large values of \(t\), this logarithmic derivative would give
infinitely many disjoint \(t\)-intervals of uniform width on which \(P\), and
therefore \(\dot t=E-vP\), stayed bounded.  Each interval would contribute a
fixed positive amount to \(d\tau=dt/\dot t\), contradicting
\(\tau_*<\infty\).  Thus
\begin{equation}
  P\longrightarrow+\infty,
  \qquad
  \dot t\longrightarrow+\infty .
  \label{eq:p-diverges}
\end{equation}

For the spatial direction \(n\) defined in Proposition~\ref{prop:capture},
Equations~\eqref{eq:global-first-integrals} and \eqref{eq:p-diverges} give
\begin{equation}
  |n|\longrightarrow1,
  \qquad
  1+vn_x=\frac{E}{\dot t}\longrightarrow0.
  \label{eq:n-limit}
\end{equation}

\medskip\noindent\emph{Spatial infinity is not an exit.}

The exclusion of spatial infinity does not require radial symmetry.  Put
\begin{equation}
  \Pi=(P^2+Q_AQ_A)^{1/2},\qquad
  m=\frac{(P,Q_y,Q_z)}{\Pi},\qquad
  \beta=\frac{\Pi}{\sqrt{\Pi^2+1}} .
\end{equation}
Since \(P\to\infty\), also \(\Pi\to\infty\).  The exact energy and velocity
relations are
\begin{equation}
  vm_x=\frac{E}{\Pi}-\sqrt{1+\Pi^{-2}},
  \qquad
  \frac{d\boldsymbol{x}}{dt}=ve_x+\beta m .
  \label{eq:infinity-projective}
\end{equation}
Choose \(0<c<v_s-v_s^{-1}\).  By
\eqref{eq:general-scalar-tail} and \eqref{eq:infinity-projective}, there are
\(T,R\) such that whenever \(t\geq T\) and \(r\geq R\),
\begin{equation}
  \frac{dx}{dt}\leq-c<0.
  \label{eq:x-speed-infinity}
\end{equation}

Consider a connected exterior excursion
\([t_1,t_2]\subset\{t\geq T:r(t)\geq R\}\) that enters and later returns
through \(r=R\).  At both endpoints \(-R\leq x\leq R\), while
\eqref{eq:x-speed-infinity} gives
\begin{equation}
  c(t_2-t_1)\leq x(t_1)-x(t_2)\leq2R.
\end{equation}
The causal speed bound \eqref{eq:causal-speed} therefore bounds the radius of
every returning excursion by
\begin{equation}
  r(t)\leq R+\frac{2RV}{c}.
  \label{eq:returning-excursion-bound}
\end{equation}

If the spatial trajectory were unbounded,
\eqref{eq:returning-excursion-bound} would force one exterior component to be
nonreturning.  Equation~\eqref{eq:x-speed-infinity} would then imply
\begin{equation}
  x(t)\leq-ct+O(1),\qquad r(t)\geq ct-O(1).
  \label{eq:linear-exterior-escape}
\end{equation}
In an exactly constant exterior all spatial momenta are constant, contrary to
\(\Pi\to\infty\).  In the exponentially short-range case, the momentum
equations give
\begin{equation}
  \left|\frac{d\log\Pi}{dt}\right|
  =|m_x\,m\cdot\nabla v|
  \leq|\nabla v|.
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:general-scalar-tail} and
\eqref{eq:linear-exterior-escape} make the right-hand side integrable, so
\(\log\Pi\) has a finite limit, again a contradiction.  Every
finite-proper-time exit therefore has bounded spatial image.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Every bounded exit approaches the stationary-generator set.}

On a bounded exit, Eq.~\eqref{eq:n-limit} first implies
\(|v|\geq1-o(1)\).  The exact longitudinal velocity is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{dx}{dt}
    =v+\frac{P}{E-vP}
    =v-\frac1v+O(P^{-1}).
  \label{eq:bounded-x-speed}
\end{equation}
The positive part of this velocity is \(O(P^{-1})\).  Indeed,
\(\dot t=E-vP\geq P\) implies \(v\leq-1+E/P\), while
\(P/\dot t\leq1\).  Hence the positive part is bounded by
\(\max\{E,0\}/P\).  It is integrable because
\begin{equation}
  \int^\infty\frac{dt}{P}
    =\int^{\tau_*}\frac{\dot t}{P}\,d\tau<\infty .
  \label{eq:inverse-p-integrable}
\end{equation}
Here \(\dot t/P=E/P-v\) is bounded on the late tail, while the remaining
proper time is finite.
Because \(x\) is bounded, its negative variation is then finite as well, so
\(x(t)\) has finite total variation and a limit.  The same estimate forces
\(v(\boldsymbol{x}(t))\to-1\).  Otherwise, for some \(\epsilon>0\), there
would be arbitrarily late times with \(v\leq-1-\epsilon\).  On the bounded
spatial tail, \(v\) has bounded gradient and the coordinate speed is bounded,
so \(v(\boldsymbol{x}(t))\) is uniformly continuous.  Each such occurrence
would produce a time interval of uniform width on which
\eqref{eq:bounded-x-speed} has a fixed negative upper bound, contradicting the
finite negative variation.

Equation~\eqref{eq:n-limit} now gives
\begin{equation}
  v\longrightarrow-1,\qquad
  n_x\longrightarrow1,
  \qquad n_A\longrightarrow0,
  \qquad \frac{d\boldsymbol{x}}{dt}\longrightarrow0.
  \label{eq:spatial-direction-limit}
\end{equation}

It remains to locate the spatial limit set.  Put
\(\varepsilon=P^{-1}\) and \(c_A=Q_A/P=n_A/n_x\); then
\(\varepsilon,c_A\to0\).  Together with \(\boldsymbol{x}\), these variables
obey the smooth autonomous system
\begin{equation}
  \frac{d\boldsymbol{x}}{dt}
    =ve_x+\frac{(1,c_y,c_z)}{\sqrt{1+|c|^2+\varepsilon^2}},
  \qquad
  \varepsilon'=v_x\varepsilon,
  \qquad
  c_A'=-v_A+v_xc_A.
  \label{eq:compactified-exit-flow}
\end{equation}
The trajectory is precompact, so its \(\omega\)-limit set is nonempty, compact, and
invariant.  Every point in it has
\(\varepsilon=0\), \(c_A=0\), and \(v=-1\).  Invariance keeps \(c_A=0\) along
the orbit of any such point; the last equation in
\eqref{eq:compactified-exit-flow} therefore forces \(v_A=0\).  Thus the spatial
\(\omega\)-limit set lies in \(\mathcal G\), and
\(\operatorname{dist}(\boldsymbol{x}(t),\mathcal G)\to0\).

Spatial precompactness now gives the subsequence in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:capture-subsequence}; convergence of the full trajectory is not
needed.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Transverse rigidity at an exit}

We now prove Theorem~\ref{thm:extension-rigidity}.  Let \(p\in\mathcal G\) and
\(t_n\) be as
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:capture-subsequence}, and translate the data at \(t_n\) to
the slice \(t=0\) by the stationary isometry.  The translated points converge
to \((0,p)\).  In the stationary frame \eqref{eq:stationary-frame}, write
\begin{equation}
  u_n=\Gamma_n(e_0+\beta_n\widehat n_n),
  \qquad
  \Gamma_n=\dot t_n,\qquad
  \beta_n^2=1-\Gamma_n^{-2},\qquad
  \widehat n_n\longrightarrow e_1 .
  \label{eq:exit-boost-form}
\end{equation}
Choose spatial orthonormal vectors \(s_{2,n},s_{3,n}\) perpendicular to
\(\widehat n_n\), with \(s_{A,n}\to e_A\), and complete the observer frame by
\begin{equation}
  s_{1,n}=\Gamma_n(\beta_ne_0+\widehat n_n).
\end{equation}
Then \((u_n,s_{1,n},s_{2,n},s_{3,n})\) is orthonormal.  Put
\begin{equation}
  L_n=u_n+s_{1,n}=A_n\ell_n,
  \qquad
  N_n=\frac12(u_n-s_{1,n})=A_n^{-1}\nu_n,
  \label{eq:observer-null-frame}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
  A_n=\Gamma_n(1+\beta_n)\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
  \ell_n=e_0+\widehat n_n,\qquad
  \nu_n=\frac12(e_0-\widehat n_n).
\end{equation}

This is where the extension topology enters.  Work in the final convex normal
neighborhood of Eq.~\eqref{eq:extension-geodesic}.  There \(u_n\) converges,
and the unit spheres in \(u_n^\perp\) form a compact family.  After taking a
subsequence, the observer frames in Eq.~\eqref{eq:observer-null-frame}
therefore converge in an extension chart.  The coordinate vectors \(e_A\)
need not be bounded there; this is why the observer vectors \(s_{A,n}\) are
used below.

Choose a closed disk \(B_\delta\subset\mathbb R^2_\zeta\) so small that the
limiting extension data \((q_*,\zeta^As_{A,*})\), \(\zeta\in B_\delta\), lie
in the time-one domain of the geodesic flow and their geodesics remain in the
normal neighborhood.  This domain is open, while the disk of limiting data is
compact.  Hence the same \(\delta\) works for all large \(n\), and continuous
dependence of the geodesic and parallel-transport flows gives uniformly
bounded transported observer frames.

On the original side, stationary translation sends the initial data to a
family converging uniformly on \(B_\delta\) to
\(((0,p),\zeta^Ae_A)\).  The limiting geodesic is exactly
\begin{equation}
  r\longmapsto (0,p_x,p_y+r\zeta^2,p_z+r\zeta^3),
  \label{eq:limiting-transverse-geodesic}
\end{equation}
because \(\Gamma^\mu{}_{AB}=0\).  Shrinking \(\delta\) if necessary, smooth
dependence in the original metric gives the same common parameter interval.
For each \(n\), uniqueness identifies the original and extension geodesics
while they lie in \(M\).  Consequently, at every point of the fixed transverse
disk, all curvature components in the transported observer frames remain
bounded as \(n\to\infty\).

Parallel transport along the limiting transverse lines is elementary.  For
\(\ell=e_0+e_1\), \(\nu=(e_0-e_1)/2\),
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_A\ell=\frac{v_A}{2}\ell,
  \qquad
  \nabla_A\nu=-\frac{v_A}{2}\nu,
  \qquad
  \nabla_Ae_B=0.
  \label{eq:transverse-null-transport}
\end{equation}
Thus the transported limits of \(\ell_n,\nu_n\) differ from \(\ell,\nu\)
only by finite nonzero reciprocal factors.  Since the transported
\(L_n,N_n,s_{A,n}\) are extension-bounded, Eq.~\eqref{eq:observer-null-frame}
implies on the whole disk
\begin{equation}
  R(\ell,e_A,\ell,e_B)=0,
  \qquad
  R(\ell,e_A,\ell,\nu)=0;
  \label{eq:positive-boost-vanishing}
\end{equation}
otherwise the corresponding observer-frame components would grow like
\(A_n^2\) or \(A_n\).

Direct evaluation in the scalar-shift metric gives
\begin{equation}
  R(\ell,e_A,\ell,e_B)=v_{AB}-v_Av_B.
  \label{eq:transverse-rigidity-curvature}
\end{equation}
For \(f=e^{-v}\), the first equation in
\eqref{eq:positive-boost-vanishing} is therefore \(f_{AB}=0\).  Hence \(f\) is
affine on the disk.  At its center \(p\in\mathcal G\), one has \(v=-1\) and
\(v_A=0\), so the affine gradient vanishes and
\begin{equation}
  v(p_x,y,z)=-1
  \label{eq:forced-planar-wall}
\end{equation}
throughout the disk.  There \(v_A=0\), and the second equation in
\eqref{eq:positive-boost-vanishing} reduces to
\begin{equation}
  R(\ell,e_A,\ell,\nu)=-v_{xA}=0.
  \label{eq:forced-constant-kappa}
\end{equation}
Thus \(v_x\), and hence \(\kappa=-v_x\), is constant on the disk.  Regularity
of \(\Sigma\) gives \(v_x(p)\ne0\), so \(\kappa\ne0\).  This proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:planar-cap-criterion} for every future extension.

For a past extension, set \(T=-t\), \(X=-x\), and
\(\widehat v(X,Y,Z)=v(-X,Y,Z)\).  This preserves the metric form and all
hypotheses, so the future argument gives the same criterion.  This proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:extension-rigidity}.
\hfill\(\square\)

\subsection{Sharpness: the planar-cap extension}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:planar-cap-extension}]
Assume Eq.~\eqref{eq:planar-cap-criterion} holds on \(D_0\), fix an open disk
\(D\) with \(\overline D\subset D_0\), and put \(\kappa=-\alpha\).  Then
\(\mathcal N=\mathbb R_t\times D\) at \(x=x_H\) is a Killing horizon whose
Killing orbits are diffeomorphic to \(\mathbb R\), with smooth cross-section
\(D\) and constant nonzero inaffinity.  Locally, its regular bifurcate extension
is therefore the constant-surface-gravity branch of the R\'acz--Wald theorem
\cite{RaczWald1992}.  We give the coordinates explicitly because proper
Hausdorff attachment to the full spacetime remains to be established.

Put \(s=x-x_H\).  Hadamard's lemma with \((y,z)\) as parameters gives a
smooth function \(d\) such that
\begin{equation}
  v=-1-\kappa s+s^2d(s,y,z)
  \label{eq:cap-hadamard-form}
\end{equation}
on a neighborhood of \(s=0\) over \(D\).  Define
\begin{equation}
  U=e^{-\kappa t},\qquad s=UV.
  \label{eq:cap-kruskal-coordinates}
\end{equation}
The original region has \(U>0\).  Substitution into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:general-scalar-shift} gives
\begin{align}
 ds^2={}&U^2dV^2
 +\left[-\frac{2}{\kappa}
 +\frac{2U^2V^2}{\kappa}d(UV,y,z)\right]dU\,dV \notag\\
 &+\left[-\frac{2V^2}{\kappa^2}d(UV,y,z)
 +\frac{U^2V^4}{\kappa^2}d(UV,y,z)^2\right]dU^2
 +dy^2+dz^2 .
 \label{eq:cap-extended-metric}
\end{align}
Every coefficient is smooth at \(U=0\), and the determinant of the
\((U,V)\) block is exactly \(-\kappa^{-2}\).  The metric therefore extends
smoothly and nondegenerately to \(U<0\).

To attach this patch to all of \(M\), take a small product neighborhood
\(P\) in \((U,V,y,z)\) crossing \(U=0\) and glue its \(U>0\) part by
\begin{equation}
  (U,V,y,z)\longmapsto
  \left(-\kappa^{-1}\log U,\ x_H+UV,\ y,\ z\right).
  \label{eq:cap-gluing-map}
\end{equation}
The gluing graph is closed.  Indeed, a sequence with \(U\to0\) has
\(|t|\to\infty\) and cannot converge in \(M=\mathbb R^4\); all other possible
limits are handled by continuity.  Since the quotient map is open, the glued
manifold is Hausdorff.  It is a proper extension because it contains \(U<0\).
The product patch is time-orientable, and its orientation can be chosen to
agree with that of \(M\) on the connected overlap.
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t=\kappa(V\partial_V-U\partial_U),
\end{equation}
so \(U=0\) and \(V=0\) are the two branches of a bifurcate Killing horizon
and \(U=V=0\) is a bifurcation disk.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A compact flat-cap example}
\label{app:compact-cap}

Put \(\rho^2=y^2+z^2\), choose \(0<a<b\) and \(L>0\), and let \(\psi\) be a
standard infinitely flat smooth function which vanishes for \(s\leq a^2\),
satisfies \(\psi',\psi''>0\) for \(s>a^2\), and has \(\psi(b^2)=L^2\).  The level set
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma=\{\Phi=0\},
  \qquad
  \Phi=x^2+\psi(\rho^2)-L^2,
  \label{eq:compact-flat-cap-wall}
\end{equation}
is smooth, compact, and connected.  It has the exact planar caps
\(x=\pm L\), \(\rho<a\), and closes at \(x=0\), \(\rho=b\).  Regularity is
immediate where \(x\ne0\); where \(x=0\) on \(\Sigma\), one has \(\rho=b\)
and \(\psi'(b^2)>0\).

Choose a smooth nonincreasing \(W\) with \(W(0)=-1\), \(W'(0)=-c<0\), and
\(W(\phi)=-1\) only for \(\phi=0\).  Take it to be zero sufficiently far
inside, equal to \(-v_s<-1\) sufficiently far outside, and affine near zero.
Then
\begin{equation}
  v(x,y,z)=W(\Phi(x,y,z))
\end{equation}
satisfies all the global hypotheses and is exactly constant outside a compact
set.  On \(\rho<a\), it depends only on \(x\).  At the front cap,
\(v_x=-2cL\), and at the rear cap \(v_x=2cL\), both constant over the disk.
Proposition~\ref{prop:planar-cap-extension} therefore gives a proper smooth
extension through either cap interior.

At the infinitely flat seam, every metric jet along the rim generator agrees
with the neighboring extendible generators.  Its obstruction is transverse.

\begin{lemma}[Endpoint frames]
\label{lem:endpoint-frames}
If a future-directed affinely parametrized causal geodesic
\(\gamma:[0,\lambda_*)\to M\), where \(\lambda_*<\infty\), converges to \(q\)
in a \(C^2\) extension, then its affine tangent and every parallel vector along
it have finite limits at \(q\), and the tangent limit is nonzero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In a relatively compact extension chart with temporal coordinate \(T\), put
\(K=\dot\gamma\) and \(f=dT(K)>0\).  Compactness of the causal cone section
\(dT=1\) gives \(|f'|=|\nabla^2T(K,K)|\leq Cf^2\), whereas
\(\int_\lambda^{\lambda_*}f(\mu)\,d\mu
  =T(q)-T(\gamma(\lambda))<\infty\).
If \(f(\lambda_j)\to\infty\), then \(|(1/f)'|\leq C\) implies
\(1/f(\lambda)\leq C(\lambda_* -\lambda)\).  This would make
\(\int^{\lambda_*}f\,d\lambda\) diverge.  Thus \(K\) is bounded.  Standard
coordinate ODE estimates now make \(K\) Lipschitz; the linear
parallel-transport equation and Gr\"onwall's inequality do the same for every
parallel vector.  Hence all have limits, and uniqueness of the geodesic equation
excludes a zero tangent limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The infinitely flat cap rim]
\label{prop:flat-cap-rim}
Each circular cap-rim generator is incomplete at finite affine parameter and
has bounded parallel-propagated curvature, but it has no endpoint in any
\(C^2\) Lorentzian extension.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Consider the front rim at \((x,y,z)=(L,a,0)\); rotational symmetry covers its
circle, and the rear is its time reverse.
There \(\kappa=-v_x=2cL\ne0\), and an affine tangent to the stationary null
orbit is \(K=q(e_0+e_1)\), where \(q=q_0e^{\kappa t}\); hence the orbit reaches
finite affine parameter as \(t\to+\infty\).  Since \(\psi\) is flat at
\(a^2\), all derivatives of the metric along the rim orbit agree with those of
the planar product germ.  In the parallel null frame
\(K,N=q^{-1}(e_0-e_1)/2,e_2,e_3\), the only potentially nonzero independent
curvature component is therefore
\(R(K,N,K,N)=v_{xx}-v_x^2\), which is stationary and finite.  The generator's
parallel-propagated curvature is consequently bounded.

Suppose nevertheless that it converges to an endpoint in a \(C^2\) extension.
Lemma~\ref{lem:endpoint-frames} gives limits for \(K\) and the parallel vector
\(e_2\).  The open domain and continuous dependence of the \(C^2\) geodesic
flow \cite[Sec.~2.3]{Minguzzi2019} then supply a fixed \(\delta>0\), independent
of sufficiently large \(t\), for the transverse geodesics
\(\sigma_t(r)=(t,L,a+r,0)\), since \(\Gamma^\mu{}_{yy}=0\).  Their transported
frames converge, so their curvature components must remain bounded for
\(|r|<\delta\).

They do not.  Since
\(\nabla_{e_2}(e_0+e_1)=\tfrac12v_y(e_0+e_1)\), transport from the rim gives
\begin{align*}
 \widehat K(r)&=q e^{-[v(L,a+r,0)+1]/2}(e_0+e_1),\\
 R(\widehat K,e_2,\widehat K,e_2)
 &=q^2e^{-[v(L,a+r,0)+1]}(-c\phi_{yy}-c^2\phi_y^2),
 \qquad \phi(y)=\psi(y^2).
\end{align*}
For every sufficiently small fixed \(r>0\), one has
\(\phi_{yy}=2\psi'(y^2)+4y^2\psi''(y^2)>0\).  The last contraction therefore
diverges as \(t\to+\infty\), contradicting curvature continuity.  Hence the
rim generator has no \(C^2\) endpoint.
\end{proof}
