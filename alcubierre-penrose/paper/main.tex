\title{A Transverse Curvature Obstruction to the Alcubierre Horizon Extension}

\author{Sergei Slobodov\\\texttt{sergei@slobodov.com}}

\date{\today}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The Alcubierre warp drive is a standard toy model for apparent faster-than-light
motion in general relativity.  Its causal structure is often studied after
reducing the geometry to time plus the direction of travel.  In the eternal
superluminal case, that reduced 1+1-dimensional diagram contains a horizon
boundary that can be formally continued beyond the original stationary
warp-drive patch, much as one extends familiar black-hole coordinate patches.
This formal continuation does not produce a twice-differentiable
four-dimensional extension of a finite Alcubierre bubble.
The obstruction appears when the transverse directions are restored.  The
front axial null generator reaches the boundary of the reduced stationary
patch at finite affine parameter.  For a smooth finite bubble with a
finite-curvature convex front wall, the corresponding generator in the full
3+1 geometry has a parallel-propagated transverse curvature component that
diverges at that endpoint, while scalar polynomial curvature invariants remain
finite.  The endpoint is therefore a non-scalar null curvature singularity.  In
the same affine frame, the classical source tensor has an unbounded negative
null component, sharpening the standard energy-condition violation of the
Alcubierre source.  The rear axial generator has the corresponding
time-reversed finite-affine endpoint and curvature divergence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Alcubierre's warp-drive geometry provides an explicit spacetime in which the
center of a compact distortion can move with arbitrarily large apparent
velocity relative to asymptotic observers, at the cost of stress-energy that
violates the usual energy conditions, including the null energy condition
\cite{Alcubierre1994,PfenningFord1997,LoboVisser2004}.  The present result
sharpens this classical energy-condition statement at the finite-affine
endpoint: in the affine frame of the relevant null generator, the required
source has an unbounded negative null component.  Null geodesics
and horizon structure in superluminal warp bubbles were studied in
Refs.~\cite{ClarkHiscockLarson1999,BarceloEtAl2022}; quantum stress-energy
effects in reduced warp-drive models were analyzed in
Refs.~\cite{Hiscock1997,FinazziLiberatiBarcelo2009}.
Scalar polynomial curvature invariants of Alcubierre and related warp-drive
metrics were computed in Ref.~\cite{MattinglyEtAl2021}.  The present result is
complementary: those scalar invariants remain finite at the endpoint considered
here, while a parallel-propagated curvature component diverges.
The same stationary shift geometry is closely related to Painlev\'e-Gullstrand
and acoustic-horizon metrics, where horizon-regular extensions are standard
\cite{Visser1998,LemosSilva2021}; analogue warp geometries have also been
constructed in acoustic systems \cite{FischerVisser2003}.

Finazzi, Liberati, and Barcel\'o draw the eternal \(1+1\) warp-drive diagram
as a non-maximal patch and identify null boundaries at which the reduced
geometry can be formally extended, while noting that a maximal extension is not
uniquely fixed by the original matter distribution
\cite{FinazziLiberatiBarcelo2009}.  The question addressed here is whether
that reduced continuation is also a \(C^2\) continuation of the original
four-dimensional Alcubierre metric.  For a finite bubble the answer is no:
the endpoint corresponding to the front axial null generator is a null
parallel-propagated (PP) curvature singularity.

By the \(1+1\) axial reduction we mean the geometry obtained by keeping time
and the longitudinal direction while suppressing the two transverse spatial
directions.  In the eternal superluminal case, this reduced spacetime contains
an original stationary patch bounded by front and rear null horizons.  The
front axial null generator reaches the boundary of this patch at finite
affine parameter; equivalently, it is an incomplete null geodesic of the
stationary patch.  The question is whether that same endpoint is regular in a
four-dimensional \(C^2\) extension of the original Alcubierre metric.
Figure~\ref{fig:reduced-patch} shows the reduced stationary patch and the
finite-affine endpoints.  Only the marked horizon generators reach those
compactification corners at finite affine parameter; other curves approach the
same corners only at infinite affine parameter or infinite proper time.

The obstruction comes from the transverse geometry.  Assume that, near the
front axial point, the \(v=-1\) surface is smooth, has finite curvature, and is
convex in the transverse directions.  A parallel-propagated frame along the
front axial null generator then measures a transverse Riemann component that
diverges at the endpoint reached at finite affine parameter.  The usual scalar
polynomial curvature
invariants remain finite, making this a non-scalar curvature singularity.  A
\(C^2\) extension through the endpoint would require all Riemann components
measured in a parallel-propagated frame along the incomplete generator to
remain finite.  The divergent transverse component therefore rules out such an
extension.

The blow-up is controlled by the local geometry of the front wall.  Finite
transverse data of the bubble wall are amplified by the affine tangent to the
front null generator, which diverges at the finite endpoint.  For a convex
front these transverse data are the wall curvatures themselves; transverse
variation of the non-affinity gives a divergent mixed component with one power
of the affine factor.  A perfectly planar product wall removes
these transverse terms.  The resulting geometry has an infinite planar front,
which is an exceptional product spacetime.  Once a finite flat patch reaches a
rim or transition, the product continuation no longer gives a regular
four-dimensional neighborhood metric.

The Riemann curvature divergence found here is purely classical.  It occurs in
the four-dimensional Alcubierre metric before any quantum field is introduced.
The bubble-center worldline remains timelike and complete in the stationary
patch; the divergent component is measured along the front axial null generator.

\section{Stationary Geometry and Axial Generators}
\label{sec:geometry}

The relevant curve is the front null generator, which reaches the boundary of
the reduced stationary patch at finite affine parameter.  The full
four-dimensional calculation requires its local data, its affine normalization,
and a frame parallel transported along it.

The stationary comoving metric is
\begin{equation}
  ds^2=-dt^2+[dx-v(r)dt]^2+dy^2+dz^2,
  \qquad r=(x^2+y^2+z^2)^{1/2}.
  \label{eq:metric}
\end{equation}
We use the bubble-comoving convention.  Starting from the usual Alcubierre
shift \(v_s f(r)\), with \(f(0)=1\) and \(f(\infty)=0\), the coordinate
change \(x=X-v_s t\), where \(X\) is the standard lab-frame coordinate, gives
\begin{equation}
  v(r)=v_s[f(r)-1].
\end{equation}
The bubble center has \(v=0\), while the asymptotic exterior has
\(v=-v_s\).  For \(v_s>1\) the \(+x\) axis contains one point where \(v=-1\),
the front axial point; by reflection symmetry, the \(-x\) axis contains the
rear axial point.  The front point supplies the local data for the endpoint
analysis.

Subscripts on \(v\) denote partial derivatives, while \(v_r\) denotes the
radial derivative of the profile \(v(r)\).  Let the front axial point sit at
\(r=R_F\), so its coordinates are \((R_F,0,0)\).  Define
\(\kappa=-v_r(R_F)>0\) and \(\mu=v_{rr}(R_F)\).  For a spherical profile, the
Taylor data at the front point are
\begin{equation}
  v=-1,\qquad v_x=v_r=-\kappa,\qquad
  v_y=v_z=0,\qquad
  v_{yy}=v_{zz}=\frac{v_r}{R_F}=-\frac{\kappa}{R_F},\qquad
  v_{xx}=\mu .
  \label{eq:front-data}
\end{equation}
Here \(\mu\) enters finite curvature components but not the leading divergent
transverse tidal coefficient.

The stationary field \(\partial_t\) is null wherever \(v^2=1\).  This is not
enough to make the four-dimensional surface a Killing horizon: the integral
curves of \(\partial_t\) must also be null geodesics.  At \(v=-1\),
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_{\partial_t}\partial_t
    = v_x\partial_t+v_y\partial_y+v_z\partial_z .
  \label{eq:not-whole-sphere}
\end{equation}
Therefore \(\partial_t\) is geodesic on the \(v=-1\) surface only where
\(v_y=v_z=0\).  For a spherical profile this selects the two points
\((\pm R_F,0,0)\).  Thus the four-dimensional \(v^2=1\) surface is not a
Killing horizon generated by \(\partial_t\); only the two axial integral
curves of \(\partial_t\) are also null geodesics.  At the front point,
\(\partial_t\) is tangent to the future
front axial null generator and satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_{\partial_t}\partial_t=-\kappa\,\partial_t .
  \label{eq:nonaffine}
\end{equation}

The Killing parameter \(t\) is not an affine parameter on this generator.
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:nonaffine} and following
the standard rescaling of a non-affinely parametrized null generator
\cite[Sec.~3.3]{Wald1984}, let \(\lambda\) be an affine parameter on this
generator, with \(\lambda_*\) its finite value as \(t\to+\infty\).  With
\(q=dt/d\lambda\), the affine tangent is \(K=dx^a/d\lambda=q\,\partial_t\).
The affine condition gives
\begin{equation}
  0=\nabla_KK=q\left(\frac{dq}{dt}-\kappa q\right)\partial_t ,
\end{equation}
so \(dq/dt=\kappa q\).  Since \(q=dt/d\lambda\), this integrates to
\begin{equation}
  q=\frac{1}{\kappa(\lambda_*-\lambda)},
  \label{eq:q-affine}
\end{equation}
after fixing the affine normalization.  Thus \(t\to+\infty\) is reached at the
finite affine parameter \(\lambda=\lambda_*\).

The curvature obstruction will be detected in a frame carried by this geodesic.
We use capital letters as frame labels: \(K\) is the affine null tangent, \(N\)
is the auxiliary null vector normalized against \(K\), and \(Y,Z\) are the two
transverse spacelike directions.  A convenient parallel null frame along the
generator is
\begin{equation}
  K=q\partial_t,\qquad
  N=q^{-1}\left(\frac12\partial_t-\partial_x\right),\qquad
  Y=\partial_y,\qquad Z=\partial_z,
  \label{eq:frame}
\end{equation}
with \(g(K,N)=-1\) and \(g(Y,Y)=g(Z,Z)=1\).  A direct connection calculation
shows that \(K,N,Y,Z\) are parallel transported along \(K\).  The next section
evaluates the Riemann tensor in this frame.

\section{Parallel-Propagated Curvature Divergence}

The curvature is evaluated in two frames.  The coordinate components at the
front axial point are finite.  The divergent component appears when the same
curvature is evaluated in the frame carried by the affine null generator of
Sec.~\ref{sec:geometry}.  The tangent vector \(K=q\partial_t\) is increasingly
boosted relative to the stationary coordinates as \(\lambda\) approaches
\(\lambda_*\).

We use the convention
\begin{equation}
  (\nabla_c\nabla_d-\nabla_d\nabla_c)V^a
    =R^a{}_{bcd}V^b .
\end{equation}
With this convention, the ADM/Gauss-Codazzi calculation in
Appendix~\ref{app:adm} gives the transverse coordinate-basis components
\begin{equation}
  R_{tyty}=R_{tztz}=-\frac{\kappa}{R_F}.
  \label{eq:rtyty}
\end{equation}
These are finite components at a fixed point of the stationary metric.  The
coefficient is fixed by two pieces of local geometry: the first radial
derivative of the shift, \(\kappa=-v_r(R_F)\), and the radius \(R_F\) of the
spherical wall.

Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:q-affine} and the parallel frame \eqref{eq:frame},
\begin{equation}
  R(K,Y,K,Y)=q^2R_{tyty}
    =-\frac{\kappa q^2}{R_F}
    =-\frac{1}{\kappa R_F(\lambda_*-\lambda)^2}.
  \label{eq:main-blowup}
\end{equation}
The \(Z\)-component is identical.  The finite coordinate-basis tidal
component in Eq.~\eqref{eq:rtyty} is converted into an unbounded
parallel-propagated component by the affine normalization of \(K\).
A closed-form profile check of these coefficients is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:checks}.
Equation~\eqref{eq:main-blowup} also identifies the geometric mechanism.  The transverse curvature
of the wall supplies a finite coordinate-basis tidal component, while the
affine tangent to the null generator is increasingly boosted relative to the
stationary frame.  Since the measured tidal component contains two factors of
\(K\), the boost contributes the factor \(q^2\).  The \(1+1\) reduction suppresses the
transverse directions in which this wall-curvature term appears.

Frame labels are used as tensor indices; for example,
\(R_{KYKY}\equiv R_{abcd}K^aY^bK^cY^d\), and similarly for the other
components.
The independent nonzero Riemann components in the full parallel null frame are
\begin{equation}
  R_{KNKN}=\mu-\kappa^2,\qquad
  R_{KYKY}=R_{KZKZ}=-\frac{\kappa q^2}{R_F},\qquad
  R_{NYNY}=R_{NZNZ}=\frac{\kappa}{4R_Fq^2}.
\end{equation}
The transverse \(K\)-components are the divergent components at the future
endpoint.  The finite component \(R_{KNKN}\) contains \(\mu=v_{rr}(R_F)\), while
the divergent coefficient in Eq.~\eqref{eq:main-blowup} is independent of
\(\mu\).  The blow-up is controlled by the transverse wall curvature.

It is important that finite affine parameter is not by itself the obstruction.
A useful control case is Painlev\'e-Gullstrand Schwarzschild, which has exactly
the shift form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:metric} with a curved transverse metric,
\begin{equation}
  ds^2=-dt^2+[dr-v(r)dt]^2+r^2d\Omega^2,
  \qquad v(r)=-\left(\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{1/2}.
  \label{eq:pg-schwarzschild}
\end{equation}
Here \(v=-1\) at \(r=2M\), the field \(\partial_t\) is null there, and it is
non-affinely geodesic with \(\nabla_{\partial_t}\partial_t=v_r\partial_t\) and
\(|v_r(2M)|=1/4M\), the surface gravity.  The affine rescaling of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:q-affine} applies verbatim, so the horizon generator again reaches
a finite affine parameter.  Nevertheless the boost-weight \(+2\) components
vanish identically on that surface,
\begin{equation}
  R_{KAKB}=0
  \qquad\text{on } r=2M,
  \label{eq:pg-vanishing}
\end{equation}
so no \(q^2\) blow-up occurs and the Kruskal extension is permitted.

The reason is structural, and it is exactly what fails for a finite bubble.  In
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pg-schwarzschild} the field \(\partial_t\) is geodesic on the
\emph{whole} surface \(r=2M\), which is therefore a null hypersurface ruled by a
congruence of null geodesics.  Write \(\xi=\partial_t\).  Since \(\xi\) is
Killing, \(\nabla_a\xi_b\) is antisymmetric, so the screen projection
\(B_{AB}\) of \(\nabla_b\xi_a\) is antisymmetric; but \(\xi\) is normal to the
horizon, so that congruence is hypersurface orthogonal, its twist vanishes and
\(B_{AB}\) is symmetric.  Hence \(B_{AB}=0\), and the Sachs equation
\(\partial_\lambda B_{AB}=-B_A{}^CB_{CB}-R_{KAKB}\) forces
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pg-vanishing}.  For the Alcubierre metric \eqref{eq:metric} the
surface \(v^2=1\) carries no such congruence: by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:not-whole-sphere}, \(\partial_t\) is geodesic only at the two
isolated axial points.  No Sachs constraint applies, and the transverse wall
curvature \(H_{AB}=v_{AB}\) survives to give
Eq.~\eqref{eq:main-blowup}.  The obstruction is therefore the transverse
geometry of the wall, not the finiteness of the affine parameter, and the
diagnostic \(R_{KAKB}\) separates the two cases.

The rear axial generator is obtained by time reversal.  At \(x=-R_F\) one has
\begin{equation}
  v=-1,\qquad v_x=+\kappa,\qquad
  \nabla_{\partial_t}\partial_t=+\kappa\,\partial_t .
\end{equation}
The incomplete generator is then the past-directed one, with \(t\to-\infty\)
at finite affine parameter, and the same parallel-propagated curvature blow-up
is obtained after time reversal.

\section{Inextendibility and Source Tensor}

Equation~\eqref{eq:main-blowup} has three immediate consequences.  First, it
rules out a \(C^2\) extension through the endpoint reached by the affine
generator.  Second, the singularity is invisible to ordinary scalar polynomial
curvature invariants.  Third, if the metric is treated as a classical solution
of Einstein's equation, the source tensor has an unbounded null component in
the same affine frame.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\(C^2\)}{C2} inextendibility}

Suppose a \(C^2\) extension existed through the future front endpoint.  In the
extended spacetime the incomplete null generator would acquire a future
endpoint.  Continuity of the Riemann tensor, together with a parallel frame
along the geodesic, would force \(R_{abcd}K^aY^bK^cY^d\) to have a finite
limit there.  This is the standard parallel-propagated curvature obstruction
to a smooth extension \cite{EllisSchmidt1977}.  Equation~\eqref{eq:main-blowup}
violates that requirement, so no \(C^2\) extension exists through this
endpoint.  This statement does not rule out extensions of lower regularity,
for example \(C^{1,1}\), \(C^{0,1}\), \(C^0\), or distributional extensions,
which would require separate formulations of the curvature obstruction
\cite{Sbierski2018,GallowayLingSbierski2018}; the argument here uses only the
standard \(C^2\) criterion.

\subsection{Scalar polynomial invariants}

The singularity is non-scalar.  The metric is stationary and the generator
stays at the fixed spatial point \((R_F,0,0)\), so every zeroth-order scalar
polynomial curvature invariant is constant along the generator and equal to
its value at that point.  Scalar invariants of Alcubierre and related
warp-drive metrics have been studied explicitly in
Ref.~\cite{MattinglyEtAl2021}; for the local spherical data used here, writing
\(\mathcal R=g^{ab}R_{ab}\) for the Ricci scalar and \(C_{abcd}\) for the Weyl
tensor gives
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal R=2(\kappa^2-\mu),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  R_{ab}R^{ab}
   =\frac{2[(R_F\kappa^2-R_F\mu)^2-\kappa^2]}{R_F^2},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  R_{abcd}R^{abcd}
   =\frac{4[(R_F\kappa^2-R_F\mu)^2-\kappa^2]}{R_F^2},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  C_{abcd}C^{abcd}=\frac{4(\kappa^2-\mu)^2}{3}.
\end{equation}
These are signed Lorentzian contractions and need not be positive.

These examples reflect a general zeroth-order fact.  The coordinate-basis
curvature components at \((R_F,0,0)\) are finite and stationary, and scalar
polynomial invariants contract those components with the finite inverse metric.
Equivalently, in the parallel null frame, divergent
\(K\)-components have positive boost weight, while scalar polynomial
contractions have total boost weight zero.  Under a null boost
\(K\to cK\), \(N\to c^{-1}N\), each \(K\) index contributes one positive power
of \(c\) and each \(N\) index one negative power; scalar contractions are boost
invariant, so the \(q\)-factors cancel between \(K\) and \(N\) legs.  The same
boost-weight argument applies to polynomial
invariants built from the Riemann tensor and its covariant derivatives, whose
coordinate-basis components are finite for a smooth stationary metric.

\subsection{Classical source tensor}

In the same frame, the Einstein tensor components relevant to the classical
source are
\begin{equation}
  G_{KK}=-\frac{2\kappa q^2}{R_F},\qquad
  G_{NN}=\frac{\kappa}{2R_Fq^2},\qquad
  G_{YY}=G_{ZZ}=\mu-\kappa^2 .
\end{equation}
If the metric is imposed as a classical solution of Einstein's equation, then
\begin{equation}
  T_{KK}=\frac{G_{KK}}{8\pi}
    =-\frac{1}{4\pi\kappa R_F(\lambda_*-\lambda)^2}.
  \label{eq:tkk}
\end{equation}
This divergence belongs to the classical source tensor required by the imposed
\(3+1\) Alcubierre geometry.  In the affine frame, the standard finite
energy-condition violation of the Alcubierre source is sharpened to an
unbounded negative null component, \(T_{KK}\).  It is distinct from the
renormalized stress-energy tensor of a test quantum field in the \(1+1\)
problem.

For a nonspherical wall, mixed longitudinal-transverse derivatives can also
produce subleading affine-frame source divergences,
\begin{equation}
  G_{KY}=-v_{xy}q,\qquad G_{KZ}=-v_{xz}q.
\end{equation}
These terms diverge only as \(q\), slower than the leading \(q^2\) behavior of
\(G_{KK}\).

\section{General Front Surfaces and the Planar Limit}
\label{sec:general}

The spherical assumption is not essential.  Near a regular point of the front
\(v=-1\) surface, choose \(x\) as the longitudinal coordinate and
\(A\in\{y,z\}\) as the transverse coordinates.  A stationary generator at such
a point has \(v_A=0\), and with \(\nabla v\ne0\) it has a simple longitudinal
root \(v_x\ne0\).  The two remaining alternatives to check are an imposed
degenerate longitudinal root, \(v_x=0\), and a point with \(v_A\ne0\), which
lies on no stationary generator.  At a simple finite-affine front boundary,
transverse dependence of the wall produces positive-boost-weight Riemann
components.  The exact transverse product geometry is the exceptional case in
which those components vanish; it is not a finite bubble, and a finite flat cap
loses the product structure at its rim.

\subsection{Planar product fronts}

In the exceptional product case, \(v\) depends only on the longitudinal
coordinate throughout a transverse neighborhood of the front endpoint.  The
metric then splits as
\begin{equation}
  ds^2=ds^2_{1+1}+dy^2+dz^2 .
  \label{eq:product-split}
\end{equation}
The \(1+1\) factor is the Painlev\'e-Gullstrand form of a stationary horizon
metric.  Away from \(1-v^2=0\), the time coordinate
\begin{equation}
  dT=dt+\frac{v\,dx}{1-v^2},
\end{equation}
puts it in the diagonal form
\begin{equation}
  ds^2_{1+1}=-(1-v^2)dT^2+\frac{dx^2}{1-v^2}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, with
\begin{equation}
  x_*=\int\frac{dx}{1-v^2},\qquad
  u=T-x_*,\qquad w=T+x_*,
\end{equation}
one has \(ds^2_{1+1}=-(1-v^2)du\,dw\).  A simple root of \(1-v^2\) is
regularized by the usual exponential null coordinates, as in
Painlev\'e-Gullstrand and acoustic horizons
\cite{Visser1998,LemosSilva2021}.  The direct product with the flat
transverse plane is then a smooth (in particular \(C^2\)) four-dimensional
extension of this local product spacetime.

\subsection{Breakdown at a finite rim}

This product construction cannot be a global continuation of a finite bubble.
A finite flat cap must either bend or pass into a transition region.  Near the
planar horizon, with \(x_H\) denoting the horizon location, write
\begin{equation}
  s=x-x_H,\qquad v=-1-\kappa s+O(s^2),\qquad
  U=e^{-\kappa t},\qquad s=UV .
\end{equation}
Here \(V\) is the conjugate null coordinate defined by \(s=UV\).  If the front
surface bends or enters a transition region, write it locally as
\(x=F(y)+s\), suppressing the second transverse coordinate.  Then
\begin{equation}
  dx=V\,dU+U\,dV+F_y\,dy,\qquad
  dt=-\frac{dU}{\kappa U},
\end{equation}
and the metric in this regularized null chart contains
\begin{equation}
  g_{Uy}=-\frac{F_y}{\kappa U}+O(U).
  \label{eq:rim-regularized-null}
\end{equation}
For continuity of the lifted metric in a neighborhood of \(U=0\), the
coefficient \(F_y/U\) must have a path-independent finite limit.  This forces
\(F_y=0\) throughout an open transverse neighborhood.  A \(C^\infty\)
bump-function rim can make \(F_y\) vanish to all orders at the exact edge, but
if the product patch ends, there are still points \(y_n\to0\) with
\(F_y(y_n)\ne0\).  Along paths \(U_n=F_y(y_n)^2\),
\eqref{eq:rim-regularized-null} is unbounded.  The product construction gives a
regular four-dimensional continuation only where the product structure holds
on an open transverse neighborhood.

\subsection{Stationary generators on general front surfaces}

The same obstruction has an invariant local form at stationary finite-affine
generators.  Let
\(A,B\in\{y,z\}\), and assume at such a generator
\begin{equation}
  v=-1,\qquad v_x=-\kappa,\qquad v_A=0,\qquad
  B_A=v_{xA},\qquad H_{AB}=v_{AB}.
\end{equation}
The potentially divergent Riemann components in the parallel frame are
\begin{equation}
  R_{KNKA}=-B_Aq,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  R_{KAKB}=H_{AB}q^2,
  \label{eq:screen-general}
\end{equation}
with \(R_{KNKN}\) finite.  The corresponding Einstein-frame components include
\begin{equation}
  G_{KK}=q^2(H_{yy}+H_{zz}),\qquad
  G_{KA}=-B_Aq .
\end{equation}
Since \(\kappa=-v_x\), \(B_A=-\partial_A\kappa\) is the transverse gradient of
the non-affinity, while \(H_{AB}\) records the transverse curvature data of
the front \(v=-1\) surface.  Nonzero \(B_A\) gives a \(q\)-divergent mixed
component; nonzero \(H_{AB}\) gives the stronger \(q^2\) screen-tidal
component.  A product patch has \(B_A=H_{AB}=0\) throughout the transverse
neighborhood, which is why the reduced extension lifts there.

For a regular front \(v=-1\) surface written locally as \(x=F(y,z)\), the
stationary-generator condition is \(v_A=0\).  Differentiating
\(v(F(y,z),y,z)=-1\) twice gives
\begin{equation}
  F_{AB}=\frac{H_{AB}}{\kappa}.
\end{equation}
In principal transverse coordinates for a smooth convex front,
\begin{equation}
  F_{yy}=-C_1,\qquad F_{zz}=-C_2,\qquad C_i>0,
\end{equation}
where \(F_{AA}<0\) means that the front bends back into the bubble interior as
one moves transversely away from the axis.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
  R_{KYKY}=-\frac{C_1}{\kappa(\lambda_*-\lambda)^2},
  \qquad
  R_{KZKZ}=-\frac{C_2}{\kappa(\lambda_*-\lambda)^2},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  G_{KK}=-\frac{C_1+C_2}{\kappa(\lambda_*-\lambda)^2}.
\end{equation}
The spherical result is the special case \(C_1=C_2=1/R_F\).

\subsection{Degenerate roots and non-generator points}

This subsection treats those two alternatives: an imposed profile with
degenerate longitudinal root \(v_x=0\) at the axial point, and a point of the
\(v^2=1\) surface with \(v_A\ne0\), which lies on no stationary generator.
Neither evades the \(C^2\) obstruction derived above.  By
\eqref{eq:not-whole-sphere}, a stationary geodesic generator of a regular
front surface requires \(v_A=0\).  With \(\nabla v\ne0\), this also forces
\(v_x\ne0\), so the longitudinal root is simple.  If a degenerate axial root is
imposed anyway,
\begin{equation}
  v(s,y)=-1-a s^m+\frac12 H y^2+\cdots,\qquad m\ge2,\qquad a\ne0,
\end{equation}
then the exact stationary generator is not incomplete at finite affine
parameter, but crossing causal geodesics on the branch \(t\to+\infty\) have
\begin{equation}
  \dot t\sim \frac{E}{1+v}\sim -\frac{E}{a s^m},\qquad
  \dot s\to E .
\end{equation}
On the branch that approaches the root, \(E\) has the sign for which \(s\to0\);
since \(\dot s\) tends to a finite nonzero limit, \(s=0\) is reached at finite
affine or proper parameter.
At \(v_y=0\),
\begin{equation}
  R_{tyty}=-vH,\qquad R_{tyxy}=\frac{H}{2},\qquad R_{xyxy}=0,
\end{equation}
and therefore, with \(\dot\gamma\) denoting the tangent to the crossing
geodesic, and using that \(\Gamma^a{}_{ty}=\Gamma^a{}_{xy}=0\) wherever
\(v_y=v_z=0\), so that the unit vector \(\partial_y\) is parallel propagated
along any geodesic confined to the axis,
\begin{equation}
  R(\dot\gamma,Y,\dot\gamma,Y)\sim H\dot t^2
    \sim \frac{H E^2}{a^2s^{2m}} .
\end{equation}
Degeneracy of the longitudinal root does not rescue a transversely
curved front.

Points with \(v_A\ne0\) fail outright: they do not define stationary null
geodesic generators and do not provide the finite-parameter causal endpoint of
the reduced diagram.  Let \(E=-p_t\), \(P=p_x\), and \(Q_A=p_A\).  With
\(\epsilon=0\) for null geodesics and \(\epsilon=1\) for timelike geodesics,
the Hamiltonian equations give
\begin{equation}
  \dot t=E-vP,\qquad
  \dot t^2=P^2+Q_AQ_A+\epsilon,
  \label{eq:rim-causal-constraint}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  \dot P=-v_xP\dot t,\qquad
  \dot Q_A=-v_AP\dot t .
  \label{eq:rim-momenta}
\end{equation}
If a causal geodesic reached a simple point \(p\) with
\(v(p)=-1\), \(v_x(p)\ne0\), and some \(v_A(p)\ne0\), while
\(t\to+\infty\) at finite affine or proper parameter, then
\eqref{eq:rim-causal-constraint} would force \(P^2+Q_AQ_A\to\infty\).  If
\(P\) remained bounded, the equations for \(Q_A\) would have locally bounded
coefficients and at most linear growth in \(Q_A\), so by Grönwall's inequality
\(Q_A\) could not blow up at finite parameter.  Hence \(P\to\infty\).  Then
\eqref{eq:rim-momenta} gives, for each transverse direction,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{dQ_A}{dP}=\frac{v_A}{v_x}
  \longrightarrow \frac{v_A(p)}{v_x(p)} ,
\end{equation}
so, for each transverse index \(A\) with \(v_A(p)\ne0\), one has
\(Q_A=c_AP+o(P)\) with \(c_A\ne0\).  The constraint then gives
\(\dot t\sim |P|\sqrt{1+\sum_A c_A^2}\),
and \(\dot P=-v_xP\dot t\) implies \(|P|\sim(\lambda_*-\lambda)^{-1}\) near a
finite-parameter endpoint.  Since \(\dot y^A=Q_A\), at least one transverse
coordinate diverges logarithmically.  This contradicts the assumption that the
geodesic reaches the finite spatial point \(p\).  The endpoint present in the
reduced diagram is a regular four-dimensional endpoint only in the transverse
product case; a finite bubble with a rim cannot supply such a neighborhood.

\section{Strength of the Singularity}

For the convex case, the \(q^2\) screen-tidal term dominates.  This leading
term determines the strength of the singularity in the usual curvature-integral
and Jacobi-field senses, and classifies the endpoint reached by the generator
analyzed here.

Write
\begin{equation}
  s=\lambda_*-\lambda,\qquad
  R_{KYKY}=-\frac{\mathcal A_y}{s^2},\qquad
  R_{KZKZ}=-\frac{\mathcal A_z}{s^2}.
\end{equation}
For a spherical profile \(\mathcal A_y=\mathcal A_z=1/(\kappa R_F)\).  For a
general convex front \(v=-1\) surface \(\mathcal A_i=C_i/\kappa>0\).

In one transverse channel, write \(\mathcal A\) for either
\(\mathcal A_y\) or \(\mathcal A_z\).  Then
\begin{equation}
  \int^1_\epsilon \frac{\mathcal A}{s^2}\,ds \sim \frac{\mathcal A}{\epsilon},
  \qquad
  \int^1_\epsilon du\int^1_u \frac{\mathcal A}{s^2}\,ds
    \sim \mathcal A\log(1/\epsilon).
\end{equation}
For a transverse Jacobi field component \(\eta\), the leading Jacobi equation is
\begin{equation}
  \eta''=\frac{\mathcal A}{s^2}\eta ,
\end{equation}
with powers
\begin{equation}
  p_\pm=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1+4\mathcal A}}{2}.
\end{equation}
For \(\mathcal A>0\), one power is greater than one and the other is negative.  One
solution shrinks while the independent solution diverges, so generic
transverse separations are infinitely stretched as the endpoint is approached.

If \(\mathcal A<0\), the PP component still diverges as \(1/s^2\) when
\(\mathcal A\ne0\), but the Jacobi behavior changes.  For
\(-1/4<\mathcal A<0\) both powers are real and positive; for
\(\mathcal A<-1/4\) the powers are complex and the solutions oscillate
logarithmically with an overall \(s^{1/2}\) amplitude.  If \(\mathcal A=0\),
the leading inverse-square term vanishes and the next nonzero term in the
local wall expansion must be analyzed separately.

The convex-front endpoint is therefore a strong null PP singularity in the
curvature-integral and deformational sense
\cite{Tipler1977,ClarkeKrolak1985}.  For the convex case \(\mathcal A>0\), if the phrase
``Tipler-strong'' is reserved strictly for generic crushing of every transverse
area element, the more precise description is stretching-strong.  For the
concave range \(-1/4<\mathcal A<0\), both Jacobi modes vanish as \(s\to0\), so
the transverse area collapses; this is the Tipler-crushing regime.

\section{Discussion}

The formal continuation of the eternal \(1+1\) warp-drive diagram is not a
\(C^2\) four-dimensional extension of a finite Alcubierre bubble.  At a smooth
curved front, the obstruction is the parallel-frame component displayed in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:screen-general}; for a convex front this gives
\(R_{KYKY}=-C_1/[\kappa(\lambda_*-\lambda)^2]\) and the analogous
\(Z\)-component.  These components diverge even though the zeroth-order scalar
polynomial invariants remain finite.

The flat-front calculation identifies the exceptional geometry in which the
reduced continuation really does lift: the product metric
\eqref{eq:product-split} on an open transverse product neighborhood.
That is the geometry of an infinite planar wall or of a local planar patch,
not a finite bubble.  When a finite flat cap reaches a rim or transition, the
metric in the product regularized null chart fails to define a continuous
four-dimensional neighborhood metric unless the product structure persists on
an open transverse neighborhood.  At points where \(v_A\ne0\), the
finite-parameter causal endpoint of the reduced diagram does not occur.

This conclusion is compatible with extension theorems for Killing horizons.
The R\'acz-Wald theorem applies, in its standard form, to a smooth Killing
horizon with constant surface gravity and gives a local extension to a regular
bifurcate Killing horizon \cite{RaczWald1992}.  The four-dimensional
Alcubierre surface \(v^2=1\) fails the corresponding geometric premise:
although \(\partial_t\) is null on that surface, it is geodesic there only at
the axial points \eqref{eq:not-whole-sphere}.  The Painlev\'e-Gullstrand
Schwarzschild control case \eqref{eq:pg-schwarzschild} shows the contrast
concretely: there the premise holds, the boost-weight \(+2\) components vanish
by \eqref{eq:pg-vanishing}, and the extension exists despite the horizon
generator having finite affine parameter.  Those isolated axial generators
are precisely where the reduced \(1+1\) diagram has finite-affine endpoints,
and embedding them in a finite transverse wall produces the PP curvature
obstruction derived here.  This is consistent with the R\'acz-Wald theorem:
the calculation identifies why the Alcubierre horizon surface is not a regular
Killing horizon of the type covered by that theorem.

The analysis is for the eternal stationary Alcubierre geometry.  A
finite-lifetime bubble has additional creation and destruction regions, so its
global conformal diagram is a different problem.  The obstruction found here is
local to a stationary finite-affine front or rear endpoint.  Any dynamical
configuration that is locally \(C^2\) close to such a stationary endpoint
inherits this obstruction.  A fully dynamical endpoint with no stationary
limit requires a separate calculation.

The same calculation separates the present classical effect from the
semiclassical instability of reduced warp-drive models.  Hiscock's
two-dimensional quantum stress-energy divergence and the Finazzi-Liberati-
Barcel\'o \(1+1\) conformal diagram concern the reduced geometry.  Here the
obstruction is already present in the classical \(3+1\) metric: in the
parallel affine frame approaching the endpoint, the source required by
Einstein's equation has the unbounded negative null component given in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:tkk}.  The front and rear axial points are precisely where the
reduced \(1+1\) conformal-diagram boundary lifts to a four-dimensional
curvature singularity.  The bubble-center
observer remains timelike and complete in the stationary patch; the obstruction
belongs to the attempted horizon continuation, not to the center worldline.

\section{ADM Derivation of the Transverse Curvature}
\label{app:adm}

This appendix gives the short derivation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:rtyty}.  It fixes the
sign convention used in the main text and shows explicitly where the factor
\(-\kappa/R_F\) enters.

The ADM form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:metric} is
\begin{equation}
  ds^2=-\mathcal N^2dt^2+\gamma_{ij}(dx^i+\beta^i dt)(dx^j+\beta^j dt),
  \qquad
  \mathcal N=1,\quad \gamma_{ij}=\delta_{ij},\quad \beta^i=(-v,0,0).
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}
  n=\partial_t-\beta^i\partial_i,\qquad
  \mathcal K_{ij}=\frac12(D_i\beta_j+D_j\beta_i).
\end{equation}
Here \(n\) is the future unit normal to the \(t=\mathrm{const.}\) slices,
\(\mathcal K_{ij}\) is their extrinsic curvature in this sign convention, and
\(D_i\) is the covariant derivative compatible with \(\gamma_{ij}\).
At the front axial point, the data \eqref{eq:front-data} give
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal K_{xx}=-v_x=\kappa,\qquad
  \mathcal K_{xy}=-\frac12v_y=0,\qquad
  \mathcal K_{yy}=0,\qquad
  D_y\mathcal K_{xy}=-\frac12v_{yy}=\frac{\kappa}{2R_F}.
\end{equation}
With the Riemann convention used in the main text,
\begin{equation}
  R_{ijkl}=\mathcal K_{il}\mathcal K_{jk}
    -\mathcal K_{ik}\mathcal K_{jl},\qquad
  R_{nijk}=D_j\mathcal K_{ik}-D_k\mathcal K_{ij}.
\end{equation}
The components entering the transverse tidal term are
\begin{equation}
  R_{xyxy}=0,\qquad
  R_{nyxy}=-\frac{\kappa}{2R_F},\qquad
  R_{nyny}=0 .
\end{equation}
Since \(\beta^x=-v=1\) at the front axial point,
\(\partial_t=n+\partial_x\), and multilinearity gives
\begin{equation}
  R_{tyty}=R_{nyny}+2R_{nyxy}+R_{xyxy}
    =-\frac{\kappa}{R_F}.
\end{equation}
The \(z\)-component is identical by spherical symmetry.

\section{Closed-Form Check Profile}
\label{app:checks}

As a closed-form check, take the smooth profile
\begin{equation}
  v(r)=2(\operatorname{sech}r-1),
\end{equation}
for which the front axial point and local Taylor coefficients are
\begin{equation}
  R_F=\operatorname{arcosh}2,\qquad
  \kappa=\frac{\sqrt3}{2},\qquad
  \mu=\frac12 .
\end{equation}
The leading frame coefficients are then
\begin{equation}
  \frac{R_{KYKY}}{q^2}
    =-\frac{\kappa}{R_F}
    =-\frac{\sqrt3}{2\operatorname{arcosh}2},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  \frac{G_{KK}}{q^2}
    =-\frac{2\kappa}{R_F}
    =-\frac{\sqrt3}{\operatorname{arcosh}2}.
\end{equation}
The scalar polynomial invariants are finite at the same point:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal R=\frac12,\qquad
  R_{ab}R^{ab}=\frac18-\frac{3}{2\operatorname{arcosh}^2 2},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  R_{abcd}R^{abcd}=\frac14-\frac{3}{\operatorname{arcosh}^2 2},
  \qquad
  C_{abcd}C^{abcd}=\frac1{12}.
\end{equation}
These expressions were checked by direct symbolic curvature evaluation in
coordinate and parallel frames, by the ADM calculation of
Appendix~\ref{app:adm}, and by the local Taylor expansions used in
Sec.~\ref{sec:general}.  A numerical finite-difference curvature calculation
for the same profile gives the same coefficients.
