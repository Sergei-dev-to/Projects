# Contribution and citation boundaries

Reviewed 5 November 2026. This records the current editorial scope; it does
not certify priority or replace independent review of the global proofs.

The abstract should tell the physical story to readers familiar with the
Alcubierre drive but unfamiliar with extension theory. Put precise hypotheses
and the full criterion in the introduction and theorem. Do not expand the
abstract into a compressed theorem to accommodate referee comments.

## Established ingredients

- Reduced warp-drive continuations and diagrams: González-Díaz (2000),
  Finazzi–Liberati–Barceló (2009). González-Díaz also writes a four-dimensional
  expression; do not claim that all earlier extension work was exclusively
  two-dimensional or that nobody considered four-dimensional extensions.
- Curvature measured by freely falling observers: Pfenning–Ford (1997),
  Eq. (18). Full-metric geodesics, parallel transport, and optical deviation:
  Clark–Hiscock–Larson, Müller–Weiskopf, and McMonigal–Lewis–O'Byrne.
- Finite-affine axial limits and transverse shape effects: Barceló et al.
  (2022), published Secs. 3.1–3.3. Their rounded-front transverse equation,
  expressed in affine parameter, implies the leading inverse-square tidal
  coefficient used in our spherical discussion. This is a deduction from
  their equations, not an explicit global inextendibility theorem in that
  paper. Do not claim first discovery of the local divergence.
- Flat interiors: Natário (2002), Corollary 1.6. A spatially constant shift
  gives an exactly flat region.
- Flat product shift: Santos-Pereira–Abreu–Ribeiro (2020), published Eq. (4.12),
  gives beta_t + (beta^2)_x/2 = h(t). Its stationary h=kappa branch contains
  our square-root flat germ. The specialization is ours to identify, but
  it is not a new vacuum branch. The earlier paper does not explicitly
  construct our compact cap or its extension.
- Continuation of a suitable constant, nonzero surface-gravity Killing
  horizon, and the obstruction from transverse variation of surface gravity:
  Rácz–Wald (1992, 1996). Do not claim a new general horizon-extension method.

An additional explicit numerical tidal calculation appears in An T. Le,
arXiv:2602.18023v1, Sec. 5.1, for a subluminal bubble. This section is absent
from the inspected v6; cite the version if this example is used. The older
Pfenning–Ford calculation suffices for the manuscript's background citation.

## Results retained as the paper's contribution

1. In the stated stationary scalar-shift class, capture of every possible
   finite-proper-time exit and transverse-disk rigidity force an open planar
   constant-inaffinity wall patch from any Hausdorff C2 extension. Together
   with standard sufficiency this gives the criterion. The separate
   no-planar-disk corollary does not require a regular wall.
2. A smooth compact capped realization, attached to the whole original
   spacetime, with an open family of core-launched timelike crossings. A
   spherical and capped profile can have identical axial metrics and
   different four-dimensional extendibility.
3. A smooth cap-rim example with bounded propagated curvature along each
   incomplete rim generator but no regular endpoint, because neighborhood
   bounds fail.

The construction is a proper local attachment to the full original bubble.
It does not establish a maximal global continuation or stability under
matter or quantum backreaction. No absolute firstness claim is made.
