# Paper Style Reference

Use Leutheusser and Van Raamsdonk, *Tensor Network Models of Unitary Black
Hole Evaporation* (arXiv:1611.08613), as the model for the abstract and
introduction.

The target is not generic simplification or popularization.  It is
contribution-led precision:

1. Open with the physical or conceptual problem, not a literature inventory.
2. State the model and the scope before introducing proof machinery.
3. Use declarative result sentences: “We compute,” “We prove,” and “We
   construct.”
4. Make assumptions and qualifications visible where they limit the claim.
5. Present the strongest concrete result first, then its converse or sharpness
   statement, then the conceptual interpretation.
6. Keep equations and proof vocabulary out of the abstract unless essential to
   state the result.
7. Move detailed comparison with earlier work after the paper's own
   contributions are clear.
8. Prefer concrete physical language, but retain standard technical terms when
   they carry information that a paraphrase would lose.

For this paper, the opening sequence is:

1. the reduced diagram really continues;
2. ordinary radial four-dimensional bubbles do not;
3. an open planar stationary-horizon patch is the exact extendible case;
4. transverse uniformity is the common reason.
